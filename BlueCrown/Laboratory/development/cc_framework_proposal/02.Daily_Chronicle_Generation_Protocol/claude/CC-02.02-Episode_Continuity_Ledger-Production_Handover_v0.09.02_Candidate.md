## 1. Contract goals, scope and invariants

An ordinary two-week episode has up to ten individually commissioned weekday chronicles. The ledger preserves: the approved calendar and identity; actual story revisions; source observations and claims; consent and licensing limits; what **accepted** events changed the fictional world; what **proposed** events sit unaccepted in drafts; each character's knowledge and location; Edubeam mission deployment; storyline openings and closures; organizational roles and verified real work; public challenges **without answers**; derivative provenance; owner and editor decisions; actual quality checks; and a self-contained next-day handover. Original story history and the reasons for corrections are preserved without confusing a proposal with an approved fictional fact.

The persistent `CC_AI_DESK` holds reusable prompt, schema and catalog definitions, routing and approved shared canon. It does **not** automatically own an episode's research, sensitive diary, restricted key or changing mission state; approved episode archives own those. Parent-level rollups cite contributing accepted episodes. Canon changes need a distinct authorized promotion, never an automatic consequence of writing a story.

**Core invariants**

1. One authoritative accepted projection per episode revision.
2. No accepted state is derived from draft events.
3. Corrections append or supersede with evidence; history is never silently erased.
4. Public, internal and restricted data stay isolated.
5. B6 is a rights-filtered portable projection, not a substitute for source records and not a copy of private documents.
6. A logical record definition never proves that a repository or database row exists.
7. No archive write is asserted without verified readback.
8. No derivative is labeled current when its upstream revision mismatches.
9. No phantom family rows are created to satisfy a count.
10. No unapproved expansion from a daily commission to an episode or media commission.

## 2. Security, partitioning and custody

Use field- and artifact-level classes `PUBLIC`, `INTERNAL`, `RESTRICTED`; the Studio's actual policy may be narrower. `PUBLIC` describes genuinely approved output, not a draft that merely looks suitable. Retrieval, internal use, character portrayal, adaptation, public release, cross-platform transfer, retention and revocation are **separate rights**; a public GitHub file or a model-created canon note does not prove permission to publish a diary, face, voice or identity.

| Class | Examples | Default custody |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PUBLIC` | Human-approved A, the exact public question wording, approved public attribution | Approved public destination, after separate release authorization |
| `INTERNAL` | B1–B8, drafts, accepted and proposed event data, project and role records, source assessments, non-revealing consent statuses | Approved private episode or editor workspace, permission-filtered |
| `RESTRICTED` | Challenge answer and rationale, private diaries, identity documents, credentials, sensitive consents | Purpose-specific access-controlled custody — **never** generic chat, ordinary B, public repositories, shared archives, image prompts, alt text, file names or error messages |

`challenge_answer_key` is a restricted logical family, not an instruction to instantiate it. An ordinary daily run never stores or prints its body. B7 and B8 hold only non-revealing custody states and opaque references. If custody is absent, B8 records exactly `RESTRICTED OUTPUT NOT CREATED; HUMAN SECURE CUSTODY REQUIRED`; the draft A remains permissible while competitive release awaits a separate editor decision.

Retention, revocation, lawful erasure, emergency containment and privacy restrictions outrank a naive "append forever" rule. Authorized custodians apply audit-preserving corrections, redaction or tombstones under the actual law and policy, and downstream public or derivative exports are re-reviewed. Forbidden content is never preserved merely because it once appeared in an event record.

## 6. Calendar, three-clock and source-snapshot contracts

For a daily `episode_context_snapshot` store: the requested production local date and the separate acquisition timestamp; the approved timezone name; the authority reference; phase start and end and the inclusive/exclusive convention; the first-Monday anchor; the **whole** Monday–second-Friday episode fit; non-negative `offset` and `block_day`; the authorized episode 1–4, chronicle 1–10 and chapter workday 1–40; target weekday; the accepted ID and legacy crosswalk; duplicates; exception state. A weekend, phase gap or unsupported partial episode gets **no** invented ordinary number. Resolve from the approved calendar, never from telemetry alone.

For each clock P, H and F store: the source URL or record; the original offset-aware ISO timestamp if actually supplied; parse status; converted dates and times; the actual captured-at timestamp; freshness; source-field missing reasons; the **independently determined location**. Clock P on a retrospective request is the requested story date while today's retrieval is audited separately; Clock H is an historical-reference value, not proof of present presence; Clock F is a probe time, not access to future newspapers. Track separately HQ and control, the actual or unknown current Enka, the historical-reference Enka, the Edubeam lab, the probe destination and each active team member.

The `source_snapshot_register` records `iso`, `probe_iso`, `now_iso`, `phase_start_iso`, `phase_end_iso`, `phase_index`, oscillation and amplitude **only where actually returned** from the endpoints, plus HTTP errors, parse failures and partial responses. Fresh probe telemetry supports present observations but neither authorizes a chapter calendar nor describes conditions on an old story date. A user-supplied fallback keeps its original acquisition time and limitations and is never rebranded as newly fetched.

## 7. Append-only events, accepted-state projection and canon promotion

### 7.1 Event semantics

A `continuity_event_log` event records the fields in §5.1. Lifecycle: `PROPOSED` → `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` by an authenticated decision. Editing an accepted past event in place is forbidden; use a separately recorded, approved correction or compensating event; the original stays historically traceable subject to lawful retention. Distinguish *revision to a narrative's wording* from *an in-world event changing state*. Multiple accepted viewpoints may disagree without contradiction when the speakers have different information. An accepted in-world event is franchise truth only within its scope, not proof that a human performed an action in reality.

### 7.2 Accepted-state projection algorithm

1. Select the authorized episode and its last **accepted** event head; check parent carry-forward references and current effective canon; preserve the actual `base_revision`.
2. Read events in unambiguous accepted revision and sequence order, keeping each event's fictional effective time as a separate attribute. Sorting flashback timestamps must not reverse a later editorial correction.
3. Exclude proposed, rejected, revoked or unapproved events. Validate every accepted predecessor; unresolved broken causality becomes a logged conflict, not guessed world state.
4. Apply each effective accepted delta to the correctly scoped character, mission, storyline and episode projections using approved supersession and reversal semantics without erasing audit history. If an old event is corrected, recompute dependent later state and escalate any invalid downstream prerequisite.
5. Record the projection's accepted revision and the exact contributing event references. A proposed new chapter is *hypothetical state only* until an authorized editor accepts it. Before a write, compare the accepted head again.
6. On revoked usage rights or lawful erasure, execute custodial redaction or tombstoning and invalidate affected public exports under actual policy; do not disclose restricted history to preserve the append-only abstraction.

**Causal invariants.** A stranded person stays stranded until an accepted return, contact or transport event changes that state. A character cannot know a revelation that exists only in an unaccepted draft. An episode or chapter boundary never manufactures a team retrieval, a healed injury, a resolved romance, a completed project or a challenge release.

### 7.3 Acceptance and canon are different actions

Accepting A's prose revision, accepting proposed continuity deltas, promoting cross-episode canon, approving a real person's portrayal, granting a repository write and authorizing public release are **distinct** decisions with their own scopes and effective times. Accepting only story text does not accept rights, reward transactions, mission-technology powers or an altered character canon. A character-specific approved era file outranks a generic unratified Edubeam suggestion. Missing approval evidence leaves the item pending.

## 8. Characters, relationships and mission state

Maintain two complementary records for each featured character: a concise **Current Accepted State**, and dated **append-only interaction and relationship events** (scene, affected person, cause, emotional or professional change, uncertainty, acceptance status). Every newly drafted change is `PROPOSED` until an authorized editorial decision; never silently erase an argument because the next writer prefers harmony. The owner welcomes suggestions for per-character behavioral and relationship files appended after each chronicle or episode: record these as proposed deltas in B3 and B5 and as `proposed_changes` in `character_state_record`. Private diaries inform authorized writing only within consent, quotation and audience limits.

Resolve canon from `CC_AI_DESK/04_CANON/CHARACTERS/{CHARACTER_ID}/{CHARACTER_ID}_character_cannon.md` (the literal suffix preserved). Missing canon: re-check the exact path and folder images; request formal creation for a major new character; if still unavailable continue and record the gap in B3 and B8. A recurring figure appearing in more than three episodes of a chapter without canon is flagged for explicit review. A lead character without a `visual_collage_url` is recorded as `VISUAL_REFERENCE_GAP`; creating a reference collage is a separately commissioned image task and never implies it was added to the repository.

Mission records follow the pre/post check in `CC-02.01` §8 for every active participant. Duplicate-self paradoxes, unaccounted objects, silent coordinate jumps and knowledge-before-discovery are logged as conflicts. Record the accepted causality model (self-consistent, branching or mutable history) before depicting interventions that affect earlier events; never mix mutually exclusive time models in one scene.

## 9. Real work versus story work

Never promote or reassign an actual person in a story and call it a real appointment. Use the six master project statuses in `role_project_log`. Legacy mapping: `ACTUAL_VERIFIED` → one of `VERIFIED_PLANNED` / `VERIFIED_IN_PROGRESS` / `VERIFIED_COMPLETED` according to evidence; `FICTIONAL_STORY` → `FICTION_ONLY`; `PROPOSAL_ONLY` → `PROPOSED_REAL_ACTION` (or `FICTION_ONLY` if never put to the organization). Only independent organizational evidence supports a `VERIFIED_*` status. A V6 story can dramatize a recommendation but cannot update a plan, execute procurement, complete a task or submit a report. Process improvements credited to field discoveries remain proposals until adoption is evidenced.

## 10. Public challenge, custody and the challenge ladder

The **one** public question is the literal final content of A. Its exact approved wording is held in `challenge_public_register` with the parent A revision, accessibility and fairness status, the public source and the release state. Never put an answer, correct letter, rationale, grading rubric that reveals the answer or a hint into B1–B8, story metadata, comic or media prompts, captions, alt text or file names. `challenge_answer_key` exists only through a separate secure operation with verified custodial permissions; without that store, do **not** generate C.

Release timing is an owner decision. The inherited default (pending owner confirmation) is the following episode's authorized release, with a chapter-final exception controlled by the owner; no answer appears the next *day* by default.

**Challenge ladder** (owner standing design): daily challenges unlock tips, hints or tools for the episode challenge, which unlock help for the chapter challenge. The optional **`CHALLENGE_TIER_B`** (eight intermediate tasks, such as research, clue matching, critical reading, voluntary social interaction, creative submissions) and the older "43 Clues", Palmier/myCred, Buzzjuice Score, points, badges, QR or Buzz Codes, team-ups and rewards are *future integrations* requiring a validated ruleset, prize and point economics, integration authority, age and eligibility rules, privacy policy, anti-abuse controls, moderation and capability tests. A fictional points award creates no real transaction. Tier B tasks need accessible alternatives and may never require public personal-data disclosure, unsafe stunts or unconsented filming. The label **Challenge Tier C** means the private answer key and verification rubric — it is unrelated to the B1–B8 section labels.

## 12. Derivative dependency graph and stale state

**Chain:** approved calendar, canon and rights → accepted story revision → challenge revision → comic and media plan → assets → release QA → publication.

`production_manifest` binds any expressly commissioned comic, image, collage, audio, video or podcast to the exact source A revision and optionally the public challenge version, comic page and panel or media scene, shot and clip, the visual canon version, rights, the approved audience and the provider's actually verified settings. Track whether an item is a *plan*, *prompt*, *rendered asset*, *assembled file*, *validated export* or *published artifact*; a storyboard is not a rendered comic and a text-to-video prompt is not a playable export. Include checksum, duration, dimensions or commit reference **only if measured**.

Asset status values: `NOT_COMMISSIONED`, `PLANNED`, `STORYBOARDED`, `PROMPTS_CREATED`, `ASSETS_RENDERED_VERIFIED`, `ASSEMBLED_VERIFIED`, `QA_PASSED`, `APPROVED`, `STALE_SOURCE`. Changing A, canon, rights, challenge wording, appearance or mission state invalidates the relevant downstream work until re-verified; mark exactly which pages, sheets, dialogue, stills, shots, audio, subtitles and exports are stale and redo only what is necessary plus neighbors carrying visual state. Revoked consent can invalidate media even when the text is unchanged. Do not infer deletion, regeneration, external credits or verified files. Series identity: never combine the Koware logo with the Classies Chronicles title as a composite lockup (`CC-01.01` §41).

`service_card_register` is optional: only a separately commissioned, evidence-backed, permission-reviewed and accessible card is recorded as a product; a suggestion is a proposal.

## 13. Editorial decisions, exceptions, validation and statuses

`editorial_decision_log` distinguishes **proposal**, **approval**, **rejection**, **deferral**, **revision request**, **canon promotion**, **storage migration**, **publication** and **challenge release**. Each entry has the actual scope, the human authorizer and evidence when genuine, effective time, impacted IDs and revisions, and the follow-up owner. A model suggestion stays a proposal. Authorization of a narrowly stated draft operation is not a licence, third-party consent or persistent rights clearance.

`exception_conflict_register` tracks `ADVISORY`, `MATERIAL` and `BLOCKING` issues, the conflicting source versions or observations, the affected day or artifact, missing evidence, the responsible owner, a safe workaround and an `OPEN` / `RESOLVED` disposition. Typical issues: a phase gap or short fourth episode, probe misuse as calendar, an incomplete reading anchor, a false verse-provider attribution, a wrong role-day, an era or canon conflict, an unauthorized diary, insecure challenge custody, a duplicate A, two simultaneous ledger editors, stale media and unclear release rights. A material contradiction is never hidden behind a generic pass.

`validation_report` holds each exact gate: `Q01_SCOPE`, `Q02_CALENDAR`, `Q03_THREE_CLOCKS`, `Q04_IDENTIFIERS`, `Q05_CANON`, `Q06_ROLE_PROJECT`, `Q07_STUDY`, `Q08_VERSE_HS`, `Q09_RESEARCH`, `Q10_RIGHTS`, `Q11_STORY`, `Q12_CHALLENGE`, `Q13_RECORDS`, `Q14_DERIVATIVES`, `Q15_INTEGRITY`, `Q16_BOUNDARIES`, each with the run timestamp, checker, evidence, result (`PASS`, `FAIL`, `NOT_RUN`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`), explanation and remediation. Legacy `BLOCKED` and `NOT_CHECKED` remain observable and never count as a pass. Comic C01–C22 and media M01–M30 are specialist checks recorded only when commissioned and performed.

Track the master's five status axes (process, editorial, persistence, release, derivative) separately for each record and handover; never compress them into "complete".

## 14. Safe writes, concurrency, migration and rollback

**A daily generation instruction is not an archive write grant.** For an explicitly approved archive change:

1. Verify the owner-approved storage implementation, write privilege, ACL and classification; preserve existing IDs and actual paths.
2. Read the latest authorized head and `base_revision`; assemble the exact A and B drafts, source and rights evidence and the proposed append or supersession deltas; recheck revoked permissions.
3. Compare the store's current head with `base_revision`. If it changed, set `CONFLICT_REQUIRES_RECONCILIATION`, inspect the intervening work and require an approved merge or rebase; never force-overwrite accepted text. Fork or rebase only with approved conflict resolution.
4. Validate downstream impacted character, object, role, mission, challenge and episode manifests; submit the smallest change through a supported atomic or change-controlled operation; preserve history and a documented rollback; keep restricted and private records out of public destinations. Never save restricted challenge solutions in a public repository.
5. Verify actual readback of the destination, content or hash, revision and audience permissions. Mark `COMMITTED_VERIFIED` only on proof; otherwise `DRAFTED_NOT_COMMITTED` or `WRITE_FAILED`. Publication, approval and canon promotion still need distinct evidence. Store the commit or record ID.
6. **Migration:** inventory all approved old records, old B3 and B7 headings and `calendar_probe_record`; prepare a reversible field-by-field source-to-destination mapping with checks for null reasons, offsets, old pointers, consent and restricted data; test dual-read, conflict resolution and rollback before an approved cutover. A document-version upgrade alone never upgrades the deployed `schema_version`. Reject an unapproved mass rename.

**Rollback:** keep original archive IDs, old source snapshots, historical reviews, committed revisions and exact permissions. After a release or privacy incident follow human-controlled containment, takedown and lawful erasure. A failed pilot becomes `REQUIRES_REVISION`, never an invented successful migration.

## 15. Compilation, rollups and cross-episode continuity

`EPISODE_COMPILATION` is separately commissioned **after** every required component exists in the specified accepted revisions (ten distinct dates, or an owner-approved exception or compression crosswalk). Check for missing or unreviewed days, corrections, open storylines and challenge obligations. Never generate absent days under a compilation command. A documented historical compression (for example three final dated entries presented as one Part 8) keeps the underlying dated operational coverage and a source-to-part mapping.

Compilation edits duplicated morning routines, repeated sermons, peripheral headlines and redundant transitions while retaining each dated contribution to cause and effect. Integrate, as **episode-level payoffs** rather than new unsourced morals: one significant previously documented Eternal Dreamfall study insight (referenced as a resource in the story), one accurately attributed previously used Verse of the Day, and one practical Health & Safety issue. Respect returning-team findings, unresolved mission records, role rotation and prior challenges. A brief next-episode preview and any credit or disclosure come **before** the final item, which is the **episode challenge**. The actual episode title is finalized only after editorial completion; chapter and season titles follow their own completions; provisional names stay visibly provisional and stable IDs are never renamed retroactively. An edited episode's historical target is about twenty minutes (acceptable roughly 15–30), a different metric from source segment runtimes: ten 160-second segments total 26 minutes 40 seconds before editing, eight total 21 minutes 20 seconds, ten 200-second pilots 33 minutes 20 seconds.

**Rollup chain:** accepted daily events → accepted episode ledger → approved chapter ledger → season ledger → proposed Series Master Continuity Ledger. Each level derives only from accepted lower-level records, cites the source range it covers, and requires independent approval; there is no automatic write or canon promotion. Missions, deployed characters, unresolved relational consequences and reveal obligations persist across weekends and chapter transitions until accepted actions change them.

For an expressly requested comic or media derivative, supply only the permitted exact A revision (accepted, or an expressly commissioned draft), approved canon, visual and rights state, the public challenge wording without an answer, story-to-panel or story-to-shot provenance and stale flags. Specialist requirements stay in the comic and media companions; the ledger manages trace and lifecycle and does not itself commission, generate, edit or certify a playable file.
