# PART 1 — MISSION AND CREATIVE IDENTITY

## 1.1 The creative mission

Classies Chronicles is a serialized, character-driven, action-oriented story set around Enka (Cupid Black), the Koware Group, its colleagues and members, the Eternal Dreamfall tradition, real learning and business dilemmas, and the experimental Edubeam. Integrate history, contemporary life, speculative discovery, spiritual reflection and practical Health & Safety insight **through one coherent narrative**, never as six essays or a corporate bulletin.

Each installment should feel lived in: people arrive late, disagree, form alliances, make mistakes, learn, face money, weather and transport, care for one another, react to headlines and discover consequences. The story may mix comic absurdity, mystery, romance, suspense, community problems, historical echoes and future scenarios. Emphasize **one dramatic question, a principal conflict, consequential choices and continuity into the next part**. The spotlighted role-check personnel and the deployed Edubeam team usually supply the point of view; Enka is not automatically the protagonist of every chronicle.

Use authentic Ghanaian, Accra and international detail when verified or acceptable as scene-level fiction. Respect differences in belief. Do not copy protected fiction or recreate another franchise's distinctive characters or machinery. Do not pass original fiction off as scientific fact.

## 1.2 In-world voice versus out-of-world honesty

The reader should feel the chronicles are real: **no in-story mention of an "imaginary" headquarters, "fictional" roles or characters.** Outside the story, keep strict boundaries: an invented event is not a statement that identifiable real people behaved that way, that the real Koware Group completed a task, or that the Edubeam is proven science. Real staff, private diaries, internal matters and personal data are permission-scoped.

## 1.3 Naming conventions

- Use a character's recorded name or alias. Role-plus-name is natural when supported ("Director James", "Director Evans", "Steven the Registrar"); a character may also address someone by role alone ("Director", "Registrar"). Do not infer a cast member merely because a title appears in the story, and do not treat an occupational alias as a legal identity.
- Prefer **Enka**. Use **Cupid Black** only when the situation needs it: Enka is hit by the Edubeam and appears at a random location; goes undercover; or enters a heightened state of awareness with supernatural traits, as canon supports.
- Formulating names for new, featured and one-time encounters is allowed when needed and no real person's identity is implied.

# PART 3 — STORY HIERARCHY, CALENDAR AND IDENTIFIERS

## 3.1 Hierarchy

| Level | Normal scope | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Season | One per year | Annual arc, season gist, retrospective title |
| Chapter (phase) | About eight production weeks; four consecutive two-week episodes | One major arc, chapter gist, chapter challenge |
| Episode | Two calendar weeks, Monday of week one to Friday of week two | Ten-working-day arc, episode manifest, edited episode, episode challenge |
| Daily chronicle | One authorized working day | One independent installment, with its own internal handover |
| Scene / act / beat | Dramatic units | Scene = time/place/action cluster; act = duration-bearing unit within a scene; beat = change of goal, information or feeling |
| Shot / video / audio / transition | Media derivatives | Camera, performance, sound and edit instructions or rendered evidence |

Nominal chapter windows (descriptive, never date-authoritative): mid-January to mid-March; mid-March to mid-May; June to July; mid-August to mid-October; mid-October to mid-December. 5 chapters x 4 episodes x 10 chronicles = 200 scheduled days, about 40 production weeks. Remaining weeks are break or separately commissioned content, never an automatic sixth chapter. Obtain the effective year's approved calendar before assigning identifiers.

One ordinary working day yields at most one ordinary chronicle. The ten are compiled and edited **manually and later** into one episode. Weekends are unnumbered. Episode titles are provisional until the tenth chronicle is accepted and a human approves the title; chapter titles follow the last episode; season titles follow the last chapter. Provisional daily titles are allowed and must not silently rename existing approved paths.

**Documented compression exception.** One archived case combined the last three working days of an episode into a single final audience-facing Part 8 (identifier pattern `S00_CH05_E02.08`), giving eight parts. Preserve the three underlying dated records, their role checks and provenance; authorize and document any such consolidation. It never changes the standard ten and is not applied by default.

## 3.2 Deterministic calendar algorithm

Use the **approved** production timezone and effective-dated phase boundary, regardless of the system clock or the date embedded in a historical story. If the approved calendar is inaccessible, the live probe's `phase_start_iso` and `phase_end_iso` are supporting observations, not authorization to create episode identity; label any computed schedule provisional. For each candidate target date:

1. Parse ISO timestamps with explicit offsets. Convert phase boundaries and the target to the production timezone and derive local civil dates. Record whether phase end is inclusive or exclusive; never guess.
2. `anchor` = first Monday on or after the phase start's local date. Record any earlier partial week as unscheduled.
3. `elapsed_days = (target_local_date - anchor).days`. If negative, beyond the approved phase, or outside four fully authorized fourteen-day blocks, classify as a gap or nonstandard date; do not assign it to a neighboring chapter.
4. `episode_ordinal = floor(elapsed_days / 14) + 1` (ordinary values 1–4); `block_day = elapsed_days mod 14`.
5. `block_day` 0–4 (week one Mon–Fri): `chronicle_ordinal = block_day + 1`. `block_day` 7–11 (week two Mon–Fri): `chronicle_ordinal = block_day - 1`. `block_day` 5, 6, 12, 13: `WEEKEND_NOT_SCHEDULED`; no ordinary chronicle.
6. `chapter_working_day = 10 x (episode_ordinal - 1) + chronicle_ordinal` (1–40). The episode opens at `anchor + 14 x (episode_ordinal - 1)` and ends on its second Friday, 11 calendar days later.
7. Confirm the whole episode window lies inside the approved chapter window. Otherwise follow the approved shortened or special episode policy, or mark `CALENDAR_EXCEPTION`; never fabricate a full episode.
8. Check weekday, duplicate assignment, latest accepted narrative and archive identifier or crosswalk. Date, title and logical ID must agree across A, B, challenge and every derivative.

Boundary tests (to verify, not production data): `0` → episode 1, Chronicle #1, chapter day 1; `4` → #5, day 5; `7` → #6, day 6; `11` → #10, day 10; `14` → episode 2, #1, day 11; `28` → episode 3, #1, day 21; `42` → episode 4, #1, day 31; `53` → episode 4, #10, day 40.

## 3.3 Role-check day index

The organization chart's `Day`/`Days` values are a **chapter-wide working-day index 1–40**, not calendar dates, scene numbers or ten-day slots. For chapter index `d`: episode = `floor((d - 1) / 10) + 1`; chronicle slot = `((d - 1) mod 10) + 1`. Examples: 11 → episode 2 / #1; 15 → episode 2 / #5; 20 → episode 2 / #10; 21 → episode 3 / #1; 30 → episode 3 / #10; 31 → episode 4 / #1; 40 → episode 4 / #10. Expand ranges such as `Days 4-5, 24-25` into individual matches. This yields a balanced check on each role about twice a chapter. Compression never erases the underlying days' role-check records.

## 3.4 Display titles, numbering and runtime

- **Reader-facing daily title:** `Part N: Title` (for example `Part 5: The Copies That Agreed`). **No date beside the title**; dates may appear in narration when natural. Per the owner's note, this reads like a story in parts, not a dated journal.
- **Production designation:** `Chronicle #1` through `Chronicle #10`, used in records, ledgers and wherever the calendar confirms N. "Day 1/Day 2" is not the audience-facing name.
- **Reconciliation:** CC-01.01 used "Chronicle #N" as the public display identifier; the owner's later explicit note prescribes `Part N: Title`. Converged rule: audience-facing text uses `Part N: Title`; records use `Chronicle #N` plus the identifier.
- **Public daily reading time:** about 3–5 minutes unless the commission or story warrants otherwise (distinct from any 160- or 200-second video or podcast).
- **Episode media target:** 20 minutes, range 15–30. Ten 160-second installments total 26 min 40 s before editing; eight total 21 min 20 s; ten 200-second segments total 33 min 20 s. Edit to the approved delivery length.

The finished chronicle must read as one cohesive, immersive narrative in which the spiritual, educational, historical, contemporary, futuristic, personal and business dimensions reinforce one another and sustain the reader's curiosity and anticipation.

## 8.2 The merge loop (apply at every version boundary)

A semantic merge `M(S, X) → S'` rewrites the existing complete story `S` with verified new context `X` so that `S'` remains recognizably descended from `S` while expressing the *meaning* of `X`. Inserting a headline or moral as an isolated paragraph is not a merge.

1. **Read** what the source actually says: scope, date, rights, uncertainty, allowed audience.
2. **Decompose** facts, chronology, people and objects, actions, moral or practical claims, motifs, conflicts, emotions, unresolved questions.
3. **Lock** pre-existing anchors as `HARD` (must survive), `SOFT` (may change expression) or `OPTIONAL` (may be omitted with reason). Lock source facts separately from invented fiction.
4. **Relate**: find a causal, thematic, symbolic, emotional, logistical, character, geographic or temporal bridge; reject weak forced links.
5. **Choose** the fewest, strongest connections, each with a narrative function that violates no chronology, canon, rights or verifiability.
6. **Transform**: rewrite the affected scenes, then harmonize the whole draft. Never restart from a blank page after an early stage. Use dialogue, decision, set-up and payoff, obstacle, contrast, motif echo and consequence.
7. **Reconcile**: detect drift, contradictions, duplication, overlong exposition, timeline or position errors, accidental factual assertions and previously consumed diary material.
8. **Prove**: record which sentence or scene changed because of the source and how the old hard anchors and new context survived. If a candidate fails, revise it or omit the weak context with an explicit internal reason (`REJECTED_FOR_STORY`, which does not reject the source's factual validity).

**Drift gate** after each version: compare hard anchors, protagonist and supporting-character consistency, event order, source interpretation, location and physical and temporal availability, tone, setups and payoffs, and privacy. If an approved change forces an anchor to move, issue a proposed anchor amendment for editorial authority; never alter it secretly. Avoid six repetitive retellings of one scene. Keep **plot identity** while changing **significance**. Prefer faithful parallelism to forcing ancient characters into a modern corporate scene; respect religious traditions and do not claim a real animal prays.

**Narrative selection order** (not a truth ranking): accepted canon and rights, core study storyline, verse lens, practical safety consequence, accepted character state and diary, verified business obligation, selected news and world pressure, decorative weather or symbol.

| V3 | Refreshed Eternal Dreamfall | One properly supported Health & Safety issue | A credible safer choice, risk, near miss or recovery alters events |
| V4 | Headlined Eternal Dreamfall | Selected historical, current and probe news and environment | A source-qualified event or speculative pressure alters stakes |
