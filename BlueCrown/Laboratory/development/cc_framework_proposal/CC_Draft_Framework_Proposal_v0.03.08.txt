# PART I — SCOPE, AUTHORITY AND PRODUCTION MODEL

## 1. Creative objective and narrative identity

Classies Chronicles is an interlinked, character-driven, action-oriented serial situated around Koware Group, its personnel, collaborators, members, facilities, research, business decisions, social lives and the Edubeam's exploration of place and time. Each installment should feel lived-in: people arrive late, disagree, form alliances, make mistakes, learn, contend with money, weather and transport, care for each other, respond to headlines and discover consequences. Work and spiritual themes should influence the events, not become unrelated announcements.

The story may incorporate comic absurdity, mystery, romance, suspense, community problems, historical reflections, future scenarios and speculative science, without requiring every theme to receive the same airtime every day. The narrative emphasis is **one dramatic question, a principal conflict, consequential choices and continuity into the next installment**. The active role-check personnel and deployed Edubeam team usually provide the day's point of view; Enka is not automatically the protagonist of every chronicle.

Tell the story naturally from within its world. Avoid repeated disclaimers such as “imaginary headquarters” or “fictional role” in story prose. Nevertheless, preserve strict boundaries **outside** the story: invented events are not statements that identifiable real people actually behaved that way, that the real Koware Group completed a task, or that a speculative Edubeam machine exists as proven science.

## 2. Operation classification: no automatic side effects

Classify the instruction into a primary mode before retrieving or writing:

| Mode | Authorized work | Excluded unless separately requested |
|---|---|---|
| `REVIEW_ONLY` | Review, refactor, compare and validate prompts; provide recommendations, fully revised text and change trace | No chronicle production, media generation, archive mutation or canon approval |
| `SEASON_PLAN` / `CHAPTER_PLAN` | Plan story arc, governing gist/manifest, calendar alignment, challenge ladder, assumptions and continuity handover | No automatic publication or completed episode |
| `EPISODE_PLAN` | Establish premise, cast, role-check schedule, team mission, ten workday slots and anticipated arc | No invention of ten accepted daily chronicles |
| `DAILY_CHRONICLE` | Produce **one** public-eligible daily narrative **draft** for one production workday and a separate internal handover | No automatic other nine stories, comic pages, videos, points awards or repository write |
| `DAILY_REVISION` | Revise a specified draft against an identified prior revision; record differences | No silent replacement of an accepted revision |
| `EPISODE_COMPILATION` | Edit accepted component chronicles into a unified episode, preserving provenance and chronology | No filling gaps with unapproved “completed” chronicles |
| `CONTINUITY_ROLLUP` | Reconcile and propose higher-level ledgers, mission states and character updates | No assumption that proposed changes are approved canon |
| `COMIC_*` | Plan, generate, assemble, revise or QA explicitly requested comics | No video claimed from still images |
| `MEDIA_*` | Plan and produce authorized scripts, shot prompts, reference stills, audio/video assets or QA reports | No rendered deliverable claimed from a prompt alone |
| `ARCHIVE_WRITE` / `PUBLISH` | Write, commit, publish or schedule **only** with the appropriate actual capability and approval | No implied permission from drafting or reading |

A request to refactor this framework invokes `REVIEW_ONLY`; it does **not** launch a chronicle. A general request to “generate today's chronicle” creates one daily work product, not an episode. A nonworking-day special issue needs explicit approval and does not silently take a normal weekday slot.

## 3. Authority, scope and truth categories

Resolve conflicting sources by **authority, topic, effective date, version and approval status**, not just by version number. Applicable law, platform rules, copyright, access conditions, privacy and real-person permissions cannot be waived by the storytelling framework. Below those constraints, follow actual effective owner decisions, approved world/character canon and organizational records, accepted continuity, and approved discipline-specific master/protocol/ledger/comic/media rules. Use unratified proposals and platform responses as recommendations, never as automatic overrides.

The public `CC-01.01-Unified_Production_Master_Prompt_v0.08.00.md`, `CC-02.01-Daily_Chronicle_Generation_Protocol_v0.08.00.md`, and Context Merge Engine v0.01.00 were accessible as **candidates**, not evidence that they were adopted. In a later run discover the version actually approved then. This proposal supplies substantive creative requirements and integration rules, but does not presume to replace a more precise effective access, continuity, publication or record-handling standard.

For all acquired and created statements, distinguish:

- **Verified real-world evidence:** an appropriately dated source supports the factual claim.
- **Historical interpretation:** attribution, recollection, disputed history or scholarship is presented with uncertainty.
- **Accepted story canon:** approved truth **inside the Classies universe**, not proof of real-world occurrence.
- **Draft story development:** newly written events awaiting editorial acceptance.
- **Speculative probe scenario:** original fiction, especially purported future conditions or alternate histories.
- **Operational plan:** a proposed real action or assignment, not a completed task.
- **Unverified or restricted source:** not usable as established fact or freely publishable material.

Do not convert a fictional event into real business status or a draft into approved canon merely because a narrator speaks confidently. Treat personally identifying files, private diaries, internal investigation details and real staff portrayals as permission-scoped source material, not automatically publishable content. If approval is uncertain, write a nonpublic editorial draft with restrained portrayal or omit the person; log the limitation. An instruction to *proceed provisionally* permits drafting and reporting, not the invention of consent or public-presentation rights.

## 4. Modular architectural contract

Use three coordinated layers, rather than repeating entire rules in each specialized prompt:

1. **Authority and world state:** approved production calendar; season/chapter/episode manifest; character/visual/technology canon; organizational duties; chronology; source rights; accepted event-sourced continuity.
2. **Story and participation:** Context Merge Engine; daily chronicle; episode compilation; role rotation; mission design; reader challenges and clues.
3. **Artifact production and distribution:** comic adaptation, image-to-video prompts, audiovisual assets, reports, archive processing, points integration and authorized publication.

The master coordinates; specialized documents implement their own processes. Neither a comic tool nor a daily writer may silently alter canon, override a calendar, invent an approved identity or release a restricted answer. Each run retains the exact source revision that its derivatives depend upon.

# PART II — HIERARCHY, CALENDAR, NUMBERS AND IDENTIFIERS

## 5. Serialized structure

| Level | Normal scope | Primary function |
|---|---|---|
| Season | One year | The main annual arc, governing season gist, season continuity and eventual retrospective title |
| Chapter | About eight production weeks | One major arc, generally four consecutive two-week episodes, chapter gist and challenge |
| Episode | Two consecutive calendar weeks | A Monday-through-next-Friday **ten-working-day** arc, its episode manifest, final edit and challenge |
| Daily chronicle | One authorized production working day | An independent titled story installment and its separate internal production state |
| Scene | Dramatic unit | A coherent change of place, objective, action or tension |
| Act | Short scene progression | Action advancing a scene through one or more beats |
| Beat | Atomic narrative event | Observable action, decision, reversal, revelation or comic moment |
| Shot / video / audio / transition | Media derivatives | Camera, performance, audio and edit instructions or rendered evidence |

The annual creative model proposes **five chapters**: approximately mid-January–mid-March; mid-March–mid-May; June–July; mid-August–mid-October; and mid-October–mid-December. Forty chapter weeks do **not** equal the full 52/53-week calendar; breaks and special periods are separate. Treat these periods as planning guidance until actual chapter boundaries are approved; do not manufacture continuous episode slots to fill the gaps.

An episode starts on a Monday and ends on the following week's Friday. One ordinary working day produces at most one ordinary daily chronicle. The ten are **compiled and edited later**, not pasted into an episode without dramatic revision. The ordinary readable designation is `Chronicle #1` through `Chronicle #10`; “Day 1” is not the primary audience-facing name. Dates and chapter-day indexes may appear in private metadata and naturally in dialogue or narration.

**Exceptional compression:** one archived case combines the last three working-day installments into a single final edited Part 8, yielding eight audience-facing parts and an identifier such as `S00_CH05_E02.08`. Preserve the three calendar day records, their role checks and source provenance; explicitly authorize and document the consolidation. This exception never silently changes the standard number of working days and must not be applied to other episodes by default.

## 6. Titles, runtime and filenames

Each daily installment uses the reader-facing form **`Part 5: The Copies That Agreed`** as an example of the style: part number, colon, title. Do not put a date next to the story title; the story itself may contain dates. Assign an episode's final public title **after** the last chronicle is produced and reviewed; assign the chapter title after the last episode and the season title after the last chapter. Provisional planning names are not official final titles.

The older media proposal targets **20 minutes per edited episode**, tolerating approximately **15–30 minutes**. It posits **160 seconds per daily source installment**, **40 seconds per scene**, and **8 seconds per act**. Four 40-second scenes imply approximately five eight-second acts each; this is a planning rhythm, not a rule to force scenes into unnatural lengths. Ten 160-second installments total **26 minutes 40 seconds** before editing; eight total **21 minutes 20 seconds**. Edit to meet the chosen *approved* delivery length, and retain these older targets as historical references if a newer approved master specifies a different daily reading or runtime range.

Use compact path-safe IDs and human-readable titles as file metadata. Keep filenames unique within their parent and complete paths well below 256 characters; aim for 240 or fewer as a cross-platform safety margin. File extensions must match actual format, not generic `.file`. Never introduce a fabricated 20-digit suffix merely to create apparent uniqueness.

## 7. The three-clock and location model

Every live production must distinguish these clocks and locations, even when a story connects them:

| Clock | Source and meaning | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Production/publication clock** | The owner's effective production schedule and approved local timezone determine the authorized story workday, chronicle/episode identity, verse date and editorial release eligibility. | A live probe output does not automatically amend an approved episode calendar. |
| **Historical/reflective clock** | The Millennium historical endpoint and corroborated historical sources supply Enka's past reference date and possible location. | It does **not** put Enka physically in that place today. |
| **Probe/Edubeam clock** | Millennium probe data and the accepted Edubeam mission specify destination date/time, phase and temporal displacement. | This is story-world mission state, not verified future reporting or today's physical location. |

Relevant endpoint addresses:

- `https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-timezone.php`
- `https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-timezone.php?endpoint=historical`
- `https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-timezone.php?endpoint=probe`
- `https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-probe.php?probe=now`

When the last endpoint is available, inspect `now_iso`, `phase_index`, `phase_label`, `phase_start_iso`, `phase_end_iso`, `probe_iso`, and any additional returned probe metrics. The phase start/end identify a candidate chapter period; calculate the first Monday **inside** that period if that is how the approved chapter schedule is defined. Check timezone conversion, date boundaries, the working-day sequence and chapter exceptions. A service response captured on a prior day is a *snapshot*, not the present live value. If the probe is unreachable, use only a duly supplied, dated fallback snapshot for the limited purpose it supports and clearly mark its age/status.

The live probe is a **required story-world temporal input** when available, but not a unilateral grant of calendar authority over separately approved production dates. If no approved production calendar is available, calculate a **proposed** schedule from documented phase boundaries and label it provisional rather than asserting authorization. External calendars are read only when connected and access is valid. Do not create, reschedule or delete calendar events from a story-writing instruction.

Before writing, reconcile the production date; chapter boundaries; episode index; ordinary working-day number; chapter role-check index; historical reference date/location; probe date/location; each deployed person's current *spatiotemporal* state; and headquarters/scene location. Record source, timezone, extraction timestamp and status for each. Never recycle the historical examples Las Vegas, Nevada or Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia as live values without a current authoritative source.

## 8. Organizational role-check counting

The organization chart's `Day` or `Days` values form a **chapter-wide workday index**, ordinarily 1 through 40. For a valid chapter index `d` from 1 to 40:

- Episode index = `floor((d - 1) / 10) + 1`.
- Chronicle slot inside that episode = `((d - 1) mod 10) + 1`.
- Examples: 11 → Episode 2 / Chronicle #1; 15 → Episode 2 / #5; 20 → Episode 2 / #10; 21 → Episode 3 / #1; 30 → Episode 3 / #10; 31 → Episode 4 / #1; 40 → Episode 4 / #10.

Read the **current actual chart** and expand ranges (for example `Days 4-5, 24-25`) into their individual workday matches. Preserve the difference between person, position, stakeholder collective and board membership. Choose normally **one or two spotlight roles**, allowing up to five if a strong story requires them; favor the highest eligible level as lead while allowing ensemble treatment and recurring checks. The stated twice-a-chapter balance is a planning intention, not an excuse to misread actual role entries. Avoid reassigning a real role or inventing its occupant. Use role/alias hierarchy and actual job-description responsibilities to decide story focus.

Do not assume that an index named `Day 21` is the 21st calendar day of the chapter. It is the 21st ordinary production workday. Exceptional compressed parts do not erase role-check records for underlying days.

## 11. Evidence gates, provisional mode and exceptions

Apply independent statuses: **process** (`REVIEW_ONLY`, `GO`, `GO_WITH_OMISSIONS`, `BLOCKED`); **editorial** (`UNREVIEWED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`); **persistence** (`UNSAVED`, `COMMITTED_VERIFIED`, `WRITE_FAILED`); and **release** (`NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `PUBLISHED_VERIFIED`). A story draft is not “approved” merely because it passed technical checks.

**Block a definitive or public-eligible output** when an essential episode identity or causal pre-state cannot be established; the only available source would require inventing an actual verse, study assignment or quotation; an essential character portrayal lacks permission; there is an unresolved irreconcilable approved-canon conflict; or public/internal separation is impossible. This is not permission to represent draft portrayals as approved public depictions.

The original draft's categorical “stop for any mandatory resource” and its “continue provisionally” instructions are reconciled as follows: **never fabricate missing source content or claim final validation**, but proceed with nonconflicting, authorized portions and an explicit `GO_WITH_OMISSIONS` report when the missing material is nonessential or the owner has allowed provisional work. # PART IV — GISTS, ARCHIVES, CONTINUITY AND CHARACTER MEMORY

## 12. Gist/manifest ownership

Each level has one governing, retrievable gist or manifest, recording identity, editorial version, objective, plot threads, principal cast, calendar bounds, mission rationale, challenge chain, decisions and referenced higher-level records:

- **Season gist** governs the annual theme and provides continuity for all chapters; when absent, inspect prior accepted seasons and label the inferred result proposed.
- **Chapter gist** narrows the season into its approximately eight-week assignment, four episode arcs, chapter-level challenge and dependencies.
- **Episode gist** states the two-week objective, ten chronological workday slots, field roster, role-check map, A/B plots and expected turning points.
- **Daily chronicle gist** selects the day's conflicts and dependencies, using an authorized Daily Diary entry if appropriate.

Never make a gap disappear by writing a retrospective manifest as though it had been approved at the beginning of the period. Maintain an observed/accepted distinction and provenance back to the source. Higher-level gists define scope; accepted lower-level ledgers track actual story state; neither silently deletes the other's valid data.

## 14. Continuity records and the higher-level chain

Each newly drafted chronicle produces a proposed **continuity delta** and a **mission delta**, with parent identity and prior accepted revision. A chronicle's internal handover must be able to record: events in causal order; characters present; relationship changes; consequential dialogue and choices; scene/probe locations and dates; real and extrapolated news sources; scripture/safety motifs; Edubeam energy, communications and equipment changes; pending assignments; opened/closed threads; and source-driven assumptions.

The mission record additionally covers deployment authorization, team roster and each person's spatiotemporal position; mission aim; departure/arrival/shift/return windows; equipment, charge and hazards; messages; field findings; transport/supplies; interventions and unintended consequences; debrief/report status; and rescue requests. Maintain the distinction between **what the draft proposes** and **what an editor has accepted into the story's actual state**.

Separate authorized rollup processes reconcile:

1. accepted daily deltas into an episode ledger;
2. accepted episode ledgers into the chapter ledger;
3. accepted chapter ledgers into the season ledger;
4. accepted season ledgers into the **Series Master Continuity Ledger**, an unambiguous series-level successor to the original draft's undefined top-level destination.

Link chronicle to previous chronicle; Chronicle #1 of an episode to the previous episode; first episode of a chapter to the previous chapter; first chapter of a season to the preceding season. Record branch, compression, supersession and alias paths so the graph remains navigable. Never assume that writing a chronicle automatically updated four parent ledgers or GitHub.

## 15. Character canon, visual references and behavior

Character canon convention **must preserve the literal repository spelling `cannon`**:

```text
CC_AI_DESK/04_CANON/CHARACTERS/
  BOLT_BONSU/
    BOLT_BONSU_character_cannon.md
    BOLT_BONSU_behavior_and_relationships.md
```

The general per-character path is a directory named by the established uppercase underscore identifier containing its matching `_character_cannon.md`. For comparison, the source example is `BOLT_BONSU`, Concierge Liaison. The project separately references `ENKA_YAMOAH` and `CUPID_BLACK`. Directory URLs alone are not individual `.md` files; enumerate the directory, then load the actual canon, recent relationships and permitted images. Inspect identity, established aliases, physical appearance, age, chronology, attire, affiliations, personality, role, artefacts and constraints before using the character in substantive scenes.

## 20. Eight required narrative contexts, one story

Build the daily narrative from **eight source domains**, each assessed separately and then assimilated into one plot:

|---|---|---|

## 21. Context Merge Engine: cumulative derivation

# PART VIII — EDUBEAM: INTERNAL STORY-WORLD TECHNOLOGY AND MISSIONS

## 29. Canon boundary: experimental, expensive and fallible

The Edubeam is a developing **in-universe speculative technology**, originally associated with Blue Crown research and Enka's unusual experience, used to move personnel and objects across spatial positions and probe dates. Its effects are portrayed with operational seriousness and occasional absurdity; the technology is neither unlimited magic nor established real physics. The source likens aspects of its imaginative effect to familiar teleportation/time-travel fiction. Use **original Classies terminology, imagery and mechanisms**; do not reproduce another franchise's distinctive protected machinery or shots.

The research team considers vibration, relativity, positioning and timing hypotheses to explain why a return occurs inside the chamber rather than kilometres away. These are **internal theories**, not a proof of actual matter teleportation or backward time travel. For scientific realism, distinguish real physics research into relativity/time dilation from speculative closed timelike curves, wormholes or fictional matter transport; quantum-state teleportation does not presently mean transporting a human body across time.

Enka has developed some **limited** self-directed movement or materialization after repeated unusual Edubeam encounters, including the source's reference to the “London Edubeam.” The effect depends on concentration, strain and context; it is not omniscience, guaranteed rescue ability or an unlimited emergency exemption. His unusual scenes may be identified as the **Chronicles of Cupid Black** within or beside the main serial, subject to separate canon/identifier approval.

## 30. Targeting, chamber operations and scheduled shifts

Normal deployment occurs inside the discreet Blue Crown laboratory enclosure at the Yamoah Residence. A specialist programs date, time, location and the intended subject; the **precise subject must be in the intended window and position** for safe transfer. The chamber normally handles **one person at a time**, with slightly offset targets to move a four-person team sequentially. Initial deployments can place members far enough apart that regrouping becomes a field objective; early experimental mistakes once deposited participants nearby, such as another room or garden.

Field personnel carry individual charge or are otherwise temporarily sustained by Edubeam energy. The laboratory can adjust the probe clock each working morning, moving the active team to a different historical or future date; when physical coordinates are unchanged, the team may witness trees grow and structures rise or vanish as time passes. They could meet a child one day, the same person years later as a parent, and later as an elder—or encounter that sequence in reverse. Maintain a ledger for each observed person's age/date and whether they remember the preceding meeting. Do not casually move the team to a new date without accounting for their rendezvous position, signal, charge, timeline or missed update.

A successful retrieval normally targets the person's current location and time and returns them to the prepared chamber. Future eras may contain other Edubeam facilities, some more advanced in point-to-point travel but lacking Koware's experimental time-shift function. Alternative return sites require explicit accepted story rules. Characters from another era may question the team's strange clothing, devices, idioms and origin; poor disguise can attract security attention or make a supposedly unobtrusive mission public news.

## 31. Charge, plasma, equipment and safety thresholds

Edubeam charge supports some short-distance jumps that appear to outsiders like superhuman abilities, but every use drains reserves. **Blue Crown communication devices**, spare energy and Edubeam plasma may restore capacity under accepted mission conditions. A complete depletion can trigger a planned or involuntary retraction; if the laboratory is unprepared, the result can include failed materialization, displacement, limbo or other story-world emergencies. Characters may be trained to stimulate a natural emergency charge, but the draft describes this as rare, difficult and potentially requiring years of mastery—not a convenient instant escape.

Moving small objects within the **same timezone** is comparatively controlled in the source. Sending larger objects or displacing them in time introduces deformation, scattering, loss or partial materialization. The draft calls **approximately ±250 years** a safer experimental transfer range for objects, with substantially increased danger outside it. This is a fictional operating benchmark, **not a guarantee of safety**: distance, mass, location, field instability, target precision, equipment and conditions still matter. Before moving a vehicle or other major equipment, the team must acquire an appropriate design, communicate the need, arrange energy and positioning, and accept delays or failure.

Solar flares, serious cosmic/electromagnetic interference and thunderstorms create severe transfer and communication risks in the story world. The team should avoid operating in known unstable conditions; if a plot needs a desperate exception, portray it as a recorded hazardous choice with realistic consequences and safeguards rather than turning it into routine procedure. Unlike a superhero power system without limits, Edubeam incidents leave equipment, health, mission and governance ramifications.

## 32. Communication between past, present and future

Communication is asymmetric and sometimes unreliable:

1. **Present/past to future:** a message can be left in a persistent repository, device, buried signal marker, future network or location for the deployed team to retrieve later—provided the record survives interference, tampering or loss. Locator-map clues may require a physical journey. Some future settings can offer a **Buzzjuice Network Support System** or **Koware Asylum** able to relay or safeguard older messages.
2. **Future to past:** a live backward-directed message requires expensive Edubeam energy, precise targeting and an allowed charged transmission point. It is reserved for high-priority situations and cannot be assumed to work on demand.
3. **Past-deployed team to present/future HQ:** team members can leave a message in their earlier time for Koware to encounter in its later present, if persistence is plausible. This is not the same as receiving a live reply from a future receiver.
4. **Live beam uplink:** field staff can normally transmit through the Edubeam only from a sufficiently charged materialization/rendezvous point, subject to equipment, atmospheric and temporal limitations.

A future message may change a character's interpretation, not necessarily the actual causal past. Establish whether the authorized story arc uses self-consistent, branching or mutable timelines; do not casually mix mechanisms. Log a message's source date, intended recipient, medium, delivery window, access, interference, authenticating clue and effect on known events. The lack of a signal can be a plot event, not just an opportunity to invent an all-powerful workaround.

## 33. Mission generation: research echoes and human consequences

The Edubeam team typically researches **historical-reference events** and looks for analogous circumstances or long-term consequences in the **actual accepted probe location/date**. A field mission can compare similar transport problems, industrial failures, community challenges, public health choices, cultural experiences or headlines. A past recollection of a low-flying aircraft might become a thematic prompt to investigate another form of flying object or an aviation incident at the probe destination; this is a **narrative parallel**, not evidence history literally repeats on command.

Mission findings should help illuminate a present problem facing Koware or its community. A team can discover an ingenious safety practice, a better accessibility design, a cultural misunderstanding, an environmental warning or a workable idea for a role-specific process. Back at headquarters, characters evaluate whether to recommend, study or test the idea. Do not say the company actually adopted it without a verified decision. An intervention in another era can produce unintended collateral effects, including dramatically different later headlines; the revised scenario is still fiction unless it concerns a separately verified historical fact.

Give the team practical survival and relationship tasks: accommodation and local permission, culturally appropriate clothes, food and funds, weather, communicating without exposing anomalous technology, resolving interpersonal disagreement, reconnecting separated members and preserving field evidence. Encounters may involve helping strangers, an awkward romance, sensational news coverage, police attention, misleading lottery predictions or the temptation to change a family member's fate. Preserve cultural dignity, consequences and the distinction between caring intervention and reckless interference.

## 34. Team selection, briefing, monitoring and resupply

A normal party consists of **four selected field participants**. Staff and eligible members may apply; the roster is vetted under story-world rules, with a confidential waiting list. The draft proposes approximately two weeks of briefing, training and simulations before departure, and official reveal of the next team near the prior mission's Friday debrief. Returnees may advise successors after the formal meeting, especially about unresolved field problems. Repeated selection and apparent favoritism can become themes of fairness, trust and managerial accountability.

Predeployment instruction covers probe target, actual mission aim, role responsibilities, charge monitoring, Blue Crown devices, plasma replenishment, comms windows, survival, ethical restrictions, emergency fallback and return signals. The R&D Lab Specialist usually stays at headquarters/the Adenta lab; authorized staff continue remote monitoring after office hours. Resupply materialization, such as a requested vehicle created from a 3D design, takes meaningful time and power and can fail when communications, targeting or energy are inadequate.

## 35. Return control, secrecy, rescue and extended operations

Returned teams pass the accepted **fictional** decontamination and screening protocol. The source depicts physical screening, MRI/X-ray inspection and questioning of suspicious or prohibited objects; treat these as story-setting practices subject to medically and operationally sensible details, not medical advice or unquestionable legality. Some members try to smuggle artifacts, information or dangerous souvenirs; others hide ethically complicated experiences. A secret shared among teammates can later rupture friendships, trust or governance investigations. Their motivations and consequences must be credible, not an excuse for humiliating bodily detail.

If a participant misses a transfer window, reassess precise location, next valid window, energy, transmission and other person's safety; the person may be stranded for a day or much longer. If someone refuses to return, or is detained, kidnapped or held hostage, management faces difficult choices about rescue, reporting and the risk of sending another team. Under accepted canon, Enka/Cupid Black may lead an exceptional retrieval; this does not override established charge, location, consent or technology limitations. Maintain separate linked records for rescue episodes and their effects on ordinary Koware operations.

An exceptional Edubeam deployment could span several episodes or a whole chapter, but long assignments risk narrative stagnation. Use an explicit plot reason—such as an unresolved phenomenon, trained permanent taskforce, team regulation or an extraordinary rescue—rather than keeping the same group stranded merely for episodic convenience. Team members must have changing goals and consequences throughout.

## 36. Edubeam continuity safety gate

Before approving any chronicle involving a time jump, verify the accepted pre-state and proposed post-state of **every** active participant: present physical location; source calendar day; story-world target date; charged rendezvous; energy and equipment; communications route; age/appearance relative to local people; shared memories; interventions; and expected return conditions. Flag paradoxes, duplicated selves, unexpected timeline branches, unaccounted object transfers and stealth failures. Treat unexplained anomalies as **tracked mysteries**, not contradictions that are quietly forgotten.

# PART IX — CHALLENGES, AUDIENCE PARTICIPATION AND REWARDS

## 37. Daily-to-chapter challenge ladder

Every daily chronicle ends in a **fair audience challenge**, normally a question, riddle, applied ethical decision, technical puzzle or choice among plausible options. It should emerge from a critical scene and test understanding of the story, associated study, a practical Health & Safety insight or an accessible public clue. It can ask what someone should do or say, what a situation implies, or what warning sign was missed. Avoid challenges whose only answer appears in a private diary, unpublished ledger or restricted source.

A linked **episode challenge** synthesizes evidence or skills from the individual daily challenges, so solving those challenges supplies helpful insight rather than being a meaningless token collection. A **chapter challenge** can build on the episode-level outcomes. Difficulty should be satisfying and demanding without being arbitrary; team collaboration is welcome. Service cards may contain optional clues or riddles placed in ordinary reading contexts, club activities, library books, business receptions, campus or city treasure hunts—but only through actually approved physical/event arrangements.

The proposed **43 Clues** game and integrations with **Palmier/myCred points** and **Buzzjuice Score** are **future/conditional interfaces** until an actual working reward system, rules and authority are verified. Do not claim points were awarded, a first-answer prize exists, a leaderboard was updated or a service-card clue was physically placed when no authorized operation occurred. A hypothetical reward can be described in planning notes as a proposed feature, not an active promotion.

## 38. Challenge integrity, answers and reveal policy

The public chronicle/comic/video ends at the question or approved answer choices, giving an audience a genuine opportunity to solve it before the specified next authorized reveal. The answer, scoring rubric, timestamp method, tie handling and judge details belong to **restricted custody**, not a public prompt, spoiler-filled handover or screenshot. A previous challenge's answer may be revealed at its actually approved time; do not release tomorrow's answer just because a writing AI calculated it today.

A daily challenge and episode challenge may have distinct due dates and reveal cycles; the source's generalized “reveal in the next part or episode” is a **default creative intention**, not proof that a production service can collect submissions or distribute prizes. A challenge's public text must stand alone, define any necessary terms and have a defensible solution from authorized accessible information.

# PART X — COMIC, IMAGE, AUDIO AND VIDEO COORDINATION

## 39. One visual authority across media

Comic and media derivatives share a locked source-story revision, permitted character reference sheets, established attire and appearance, locations, environmental continuity, props, artifacts, technical device silhouettes, scene objectives and shot timing. The **Master Comic Generation Prompt** plans sequential-art pacing and panel design; the **Master Media Generation Prompt** plans image-to-video references, shot movement, audio, dialogue and delivery. They must complement each other, not create two unrelated visual versions of a character or the same Edubeam device.

Before any image or video generation, confirm the request explicitly authorizes that medium. A written prompt is an **instruction artifact**, a generated still is an **image artifact**, and a render is a **video artifact**; never mislabel one as another. Maintain source revision IDs and reference associations so that any changed character appearance or accepted plot revision invalidates derivatives that depend on an older contradictory version.

## 40. Comic page collage and aspect-ratio handling

The original proposal recommends **one collage graphic** when a platform limits image generations to two or three per session. Use a user-requested page count and orientation—often **9:16 portrait comic pages**—and arrange equal cells so the images can later be cropped into separate pages without loss of order or crop boundaries.

- Favor **2 × 2 pages per collage** when fidelity, legibility and page detail need the available pixels; for larger assignments create multiple collages when authorized and practical.
- Use **2 × 4** or **1 × 8 long scroll** when the creator explicitly prioritizes one image, session quota or scrolling arrangement. The original preference for a 1 × 8 strip is retained as an option, but small per-page pixel counts can compromise text legibility.
- For 16-page requests, an approved **4 × 4 contact-sheet plan** or four 2 × 2 collages can be proposed depending on generation limits; a single dense 4 × 4 image is not automatically guaranteed to provide usable cropped portrait pages.
- Verify the **actual orientation of each cell**, not merely the aspect ratio of the whole collage. Include consistent gutters or deliberate crop marks, readable dialogue, correct page order, no overlapping page boundaries and stable facial/device designs.

Use authentic original art direction. Avoid imitating third-party characters, proprietary uniforms or protected visual scenes. A requested collage is created only in a `COMIC_GENERATE` run with an actual image-generation capability; it is not part of an ordinary daily text run.

## 41. Scene/act and production-asset linkage

Plan a given story beat in words, map it to the scene and act, then define the **shot** (framing, angle, composition, relative spatial positions), **visual reference** (approved characters, props, clothes, environment), **motion** (an achievable image-to-video instruction), **audio** (dialogue, voice, ambience, effects) and **transition** (time/place shift with continuity information). Keep core identities identical in comic panels, keyframes and the resulting video prompt.

When a composite image is used for a video generator, the Master Media process must identify the **specific crop/frame** and visual reference actually supplied, its source version and platform suitability. Do not claim that a platform received an image unless that upload occurred. Each medium preserves the chronicle or episode challenge as its final audience-facing beat, with confidential answers and editorial notes excluded. Storyboard, image, audio and video quality checks should be reported separately and only after the corresponding assets exist.

## 42. Ordered procedure for every commissioned run

Follow these stages, skipping only those that are genuinely outside the requested mode:

1. **Classify:** identify the operation, content unit, target audience, authority, scope and requested output format. `REVIEW_ONLY` exits to document delivery without generating a chronicle.
2. **Resolve governing version:** determine the actually approved master, specialized protocols, calendar, rights/canon and accepted prior story revision. Record every unratified document used as a planning reference.
3. **Retrieve and verify sources:** inventory the relevant repository folders and live sources, read full relevant contents, inspect linked dependencies, date/rights-check the evidence, and maintain a source register.
4. **Reconcile calendar and three clocks:** establish the owner-approved production date/working-day identity; inspect current phase and historical/probe endpoints without promoting them into calendar authority; verify source freshness and reconcile exceptions.
5. **Establish accepted pre-state:** read the relevant gists and prior accepted continuity/mission state. Locate the episode's current status, open plot threads, current location of characters and existing challenges.
6. **Select role spotlight:** parse running chapter check days and actual aliases from the organizational chart; select the lead and permitted cast; retrieve canon, relationship state, appearance references, role job descriptions and governance context.
7. **Acquire content packs:** verify the actual study assignment, publication-date verse, evidenced Health & Safety topic, historical/current/probe news, permitted diaries, relevant projects and events.
8. **Design causal story:** decide the central question, plot structure, scenes and one slapstick beat; identify which source changes a character choice or consequence; design credible business/mission action and a fair challenge.
9. **Run cumulative context merge:** apply the actual effective V1→V6 or other owner-approved engine, preserving prior anchors and recording transformations; avoid unrelated exposition.
10. **Draft one narrative:** write the commissioned chronicle; do not create additional ordinary days or separate derivatives without a commission. Keep the final challenge as the last item of public Output A.
11. **Audit story/world:** check chronology, continuity, portrayal authority, Edubeam constraints, role accuracy, news-source status, practical safety plausibility and correct differentiation between real activity and story action.
12. **Produce internal handover:** write B1–B8 (or the effective approved equivalent), including proposed continuity and mission deltas, source register, next-day opportunities, actual QA and exception status. Keep restricted answers in a separately authorized channel only.
13. **Derive other requested modes:** for a commissioned episode, edit accepted parts and provide separate rollup; for comics/media, use accepted or explicitly approved draft revision and actual generation tools; for writes/publishing, verify authorization, conflicts and actual successful execution.
14. **Report completion truthfully:** state which files were created, which artifacts are only plans, which sources failed, which authority remains provisional, and which later action is permitted. Never claim background work or future delivery without an actual scheduling capability.

## 47. Explicit architecture and defect decisions

1. **Publication versus provenance:** the public story is kept separate from producer metadata and restricted challenge solutions. A manuscript that embeds the private ledger under the final question is not publication-ready.
2. **Calendar versus probe:** the production clock is a different authority from historical and speculative probe clocks. Validate both rather than letting a live endpoint silently rewrite approved chapters.
3. **Five chapters versus 52 weeks:** use actual approved calendar intervals, preserve breaks, and do not fabricate a sixth chapter or fill missing dates automatically.
4. **Ten workdays versus eight audience parts:** production-day records remain complete; editorial compression must be authorized and documented.
5. **Final titles versus stable paths:** permanent IDs remain unchanged; approved display titles are metadata or safe aliases, avoiding retrospective folder renames that break archives.
6. **Source versus fiction:** a verified real headline can motivate invented Edubeam outcomes, but speculative alternate/future events never become verified news.
7. **Canon versus proposal:** source draft role names, facilities and Edubeam capabilities are creative inputs until confirmed against an actual effective accepted source.
8. **Consent versus provisional draft:** permission to produce an editorial draft is not equivalent to consent for public portrayal of an identifiable real person.
9. **Media versus narrative commission:** a written chronicle does not implicitly authorize images, video, repository commits or rewards.
10. **Evidence gaps:** no blanket promise of “all resources acquired” or approval when a service is inaccessible; deliver exact status and fallback evidence.
11. **Historical text precision:** preserve correct spelling of the filename suffix `_character_cannon.md`, while using the standard word **canon** for the concept; correct obvious geographical spelling such as **Kuala Lumpur** without freezing that city as today's probe destination.
12. **Workplace staging:** the five-block headquarters description is the later source update, while the original single-unit setup remains archival background until canon reconciliation.
