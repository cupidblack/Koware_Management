# PART I — FRAMEWORK PURPOSE AND GOVERNING PRINCIPLES

## 1. Purpose

Classies Chronicles is a continuous, interconnected storytelling universe built around the Koware Group, its personnel, operations, projects, research, relationships and activities.

The framework must support the generation of individual daily chronicles that eventually become complete episodes, chapters and seasons.

The storytelling should combine:

1. Personal experiences, reflections and daily activities.
2. Spiritual development through the Eternal Dreamfall Bible Reading Plan.
3. The YouVersion Verse of the Day.
4. Health & Safety education and practical application.
5. Historical, contemporary and futuristic news.
6. Koware Group's organizational structure and business operations.
7. Scientific research, innovation and the Edubeam.
8. Character development, relationships and personal experiences.
9. Historical archives and established story continuity.
10. Interactive challenges, games, missions and audience participation.

The final output must read as a cohesive, immersive and believable story rather than a collection of unrelated educational, business, spiritual or fictional references.

## 2. Governing principles

All generation processes must observe the following principles:

### 2.1 Continuity and consistency

- Respect established story events, character histories, relationships and organizational structures.
- Use existing archives, manifests, gists, diaries and continuity ledgers.
- Avoid unnecessarily repeating information already established in previous chronicles.
- Build new events upon established developments.
- Introduce new developments without contradicting previously established facts unless a deliberate narrative explanation exists.

### 2.2 Resource-based generation

- Use the supplied repositories, files, prompts and linked resources as the primary sources of truth.
- Follow relevant links within acquired documents to identify additional resources.
- Verify that referenced resources have actually been retrieved.
- Never assume that a file exists merely because a URL has been supplied.
- Identify unavailable, conflicting or incomplete information.
- Request missing mandatory resources where necessary.

### 2.3 Reusable and generic prompts

All generation prompts must be reusable.

The Master Chronicle Generation Prompt, Episode Generation Prompt, Chapter Generation Prompt and Season Generation Prompt must not be permanently tied to a particular date, episode, character or location.

Dates, locations, characters, episodes and other changing variables must be obtained dynamically from the relevant resources or supplied generation instructions.

### 2.4 Realistic presentation

The reader should experience Koware Group and its personnel as real.

- Do not refer to Koware headquarters, employees, departments or organizational roles as imaginary or fictional within the narrative.
- Do not unnecessarily explain that the reader is consuming a fictional story.
- Present conversations, meetings, work assignments, personal experiences and environmental events naturally.
- Maintain believable organizational, social, professional and personal interactions.

### 2.5 Controlled creative development

Creative interpretation, extrapolation and character development are permitted when necessary.

However:

- Established canon must be respected.
- New developments must not silently become established canon without appropriate continuity records.
- Unverified information must be identified.
- Proposed organizational or technical changes must not be represented as approved decisions.

# PART II — STORY HIERARCHY AND PRODUCTION ARCHITECTURE

## 3. Classies Chronicles production hierarchy

The general production structure is:

| Level           | Proposed duration     | Production purpose                   |
| --------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Season          | 1 year                | Complete annual storyline            |
| Chapter         | Approximately 8 weeks | Major storyline within a season      |
| Episode         | 2 weeks               | Complete ten-working-day assignment  |
| Daily Chronicle | 1 working day         | Individual daily storyline           |
| Chronicle Scene | Variable              | Organizes the daily narrative        |
| Act             | Variable              | Organizes dramatic progression       |
| Beat            | Variable              | Individual narrative action or event |
| Shot            | Variable              | Visual production instruction        |
| Video           | Variable              | Video generation asset               |
| Audio           | Variable              | Audio production asset               |
| Transition      | Variable              | Scene and shot transition asset      |

### 3.1 Season structure

One season generally represents one calendar year.

A season contains five proposed chapters:

1. Mid-January to mid-March.
2. Mid-March to mid-May.
3. June and July.
4. Mid-August to mid-October.
5. Mid-October to mid-December.

The exact chapter boundaries must be established through the authorized calendar system.

### 3.2 Chapter structure

A chapter is generally expected to cover eight weeks and contain approximately four episodes.

Each chapter must have:

- A chapter gist or manifest.
- An identifiable storyline.
- An overarching objective or development.
- Associated episode records.
- A chapter continuity ledger.
- A chapter-level challenge.

### 3.3 Episode structure

An episode generally covers two consecutive working weeks.

- Begins on Monday.
- Ends on the Friday of the following week.
- Contains ten working-day chronicle slots.
- Produces one daily chronicle per working day under the normal production arrangement.

The ten daily chronicles are subsequently compiled and edited manually into one complete episode.

The episode's final title is determined after its chronicles have been produced and reviewed.

### 3.4 Chronicle numbering

Use:

- Chronicle #1
- Chronicle #2
- Chronicle #3
- ...
- Chronicle #10

Do not use Day 1, Day 2 and similar labels as the primary chronicle identifiers.

Where a special production arrangement combines multiple working days into one chronicle, document the exception in the episode manifest and continuity ledger. Do not silently alter the normal numbering rules.

### 3.5 Titles

Titles are determined retrospectively:

- Episode title: after the final chronicle of the episode.
- Chapter title: after the final episode of the chapter.
- Season title: after the final chapter of the season.

Daily chronicle titles must use their part number and title without a date label.

Example:

**Part 5: The Copies That Agreed**

The date may appear naturally in the narrative when relevant, but must not be displayed as a dated journal heading.

### 3.6 Episode runtime and narrative targets

Proposed production targets:

- Episode: 20 minutes.
- Acceptable episode range: 15–30 minutes.
- Daily chronicle target: approximately 160 seconds.
- Scene target: approximately 40 seconds.
- Act target: approximately 8 seconds.

These are initial production targets and should be reviewed against the final editing and media-generation workflow.

The Millennium Timezone system is the authoritative reference for determining the current probe date and episode position.

`https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-probe.php?probe=now`

- `phase_end_iso`: Chapter end date.

Use these values to establish chapter boundaries and determine the first Monday of the chapter's first episode.

The endpoint must be consulted to establish the current day and position within an episode.

### 4.2 Historical location endpoint

Historical timezone information:

`https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-timezone.php?endpoint=historical`

This endpoint establishes Enka's historical Millennium Timezone reference date and location.

The historical location is used for:

- Enka's reflections.

- Dreams.
- Historical diary references.
- Research assignments.
- Edubeam mission comparisons.
- Historical news and events.

It must not automatically be interpreted as Enka's physical present-day location.

### 4.3 Calendar authorization

Before generating a chronicle:

1. Retrieve the current Millennium Timezone information.
2. Establish the chapter's start and end dates.
3. Determine the current episode.
4. Identify the episode's Monday-to-Friday working-day sequence.
5. Establish the correct chronicle number.
6. Verify that the date is valid for the requested production.
7. Record the retrieved values and source status.

If calendar authorization fails, report the failure.

Do not invent an episode date, chronicle number or calendar position.

A provisional narrative may be prepared only when explicitly permitted, with the calendar failure clearly documented.

The archive must support long-term retrieval, continuity management and independent production of individual chronicle assets.

### 5.1 Proposed archive hierarchy

Preferred updated structural proposal:

```
CLASSIES_CHRONICLES/
└── CC_ARCHIVES/
    └── S{season-number}_{season-title}/
        └── S{season-number}CH{chapter-number}_{chapter-name}/
            └── S{season-number}CH{chapter-number}E{episode-number}_{episode-name}/
                └── (DAILY CHRONICLE FOLDERS) S{season-number}CH{chapter-number}E{episode-number}DAY{day-number}/
                            ├── SC{scene-number}ACT{number}BEAT_{unique-id}.file
                            ├── SC{scene-number}ACT{number}SHOT_{unique-id}.file
                            ├── SC{scene-number}ACT{number}VID_{unique-id}.file
                            ├── SC{scene-number}ACT{number}AUD_{unique-id}.file
                            └── SC{scene-number}ACT{number}TRANS_{unique-id}.file...
```

The older proposed asset structure may also be used where required by the production tools:

```
SC{scene-number}ACT01BEAT_{unique-id}.file
SC{scene-number}ACT01SHOT_{unique-id}.file
SC{scene-number}ACT01VID_{unique-id}.file
SC{scene-number}ACT01AUD_{unique-id}.file
SC{scene-number}ACT01TRANS_{unique-id}.file
```

The logical distinction between these assets must be retained:

- BEAT: Narrative action or story instruction.
- SHOT: Visual composition and camera instruction.
- VID: Video generation instructions or output.
- AUD: Audio instructions or output.
- TRANS: Transition instructions or output.

The final repository implementation should establish one canonical directory structure and map all required asset types into it.

### 5.2 File naming

- Keep folder and file names as short as possible.
- Names must remain unique and meaningful.
- Avoid unnecessarily long episode and chapter titles in directory paths.
- The maximum complete file path should not exceed 256 characters.
- Prefer short identifiers and descriptive metadata within the files rather than lengthy filenames.

### 5.3 Special compression example

The original proposal contains a special arrangement in which Chronicles #8, #9 and #10, corresponding to Wednesday, Thursday and Friday, are combined into one final chronicle.

The resulting chronicle is identified as:

`S00_CH05_E02.08-{name-of-chronicle}`

This is an example of an episode-specific production exception, not a replacement for the normal ten-working-day model.

## 6. Resource acquisition protocol

Every generation process must establish which resources are required, which are available and which are missing.

### 6.1 Repository sources

Primary repositories:

**Classies Chronicles**

`https://github.com/cupidblack/Classies_Chronicles`

**Koware Management**

### 6.2 URL recovery procedures

When a GitHub link is unavailable, try the corresponding raw URL.

For Classies Chronicles:

```
https://github.com/cupidblack/Classies_Chronicles/tree/main
```

Convert to:

```
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Classies_Chronicles/refs/heads/main
```

For Koware Management:

```
https://github.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/blob/main
```

```

```

For general `https://github.com/cupidblack` URLs, use the appropriate raw GitHub equivalent where the resource is a file.

A successful URL conversion does not establish that the underlying file exists. Verify the actual response.

### 6.3 Linked-resource traversal

Documents may contain links to additional documents.

Follow relevant links recursively until the necessary supporting resources have been acquired.

Record important dependencies and avoid repeatedly retrieving identical resources unnecessarily.

### 6.4 Missing-resource protocol

When a mandatory resource cannot be retrieved:

1. Retry using the appropriate URL format.
2. Check the expected repository folder and filename.
3. Check whether an alternative or newer version exists.
4. Review relevant archived material.
5. Identify the exact missing resource.
6. Request manual provision when the missing information is necessary for reliable production.

If conflicting values or unavailable mandatory resources materially affect the chronicle, do not silently choose one.

### 6.5 Existing chronicle versions

Where an existing version of a chronicle is available:

- Review it before generating a replacement or continuation.
- Use established material as continuity reference.
- Avoid duplicating repeated passages unnecessarily.
- Incorporate relevant resources that may not have been acquired during the original production.
- Preserve useful narrative developments and established facts.

## 7. Hierarchical gists and manifests

Each level of the story must have a governing gist or manifest.

### 7.1 Season gist

Created at the beginning of a season.

It governs the overall season narrative and provides the reference for all chapters, episodes and chronicles.

### 7.2 Chapter gist

Created at the beginning of a chapter.

It must reference the season gist and establish the chapter's storyline, major objectives, events and anticipated developments.

### 7.3 Episode gist

Created at the beginning of an episode.

It must reference the chapter and season gists and establish the episode's mission, narrative direction and expected developments.

### 7.4 Chronicle gist

Created at the beginning of each daily chronicle or retrieved from the Daily Diary archive.

It must reference the episode, chapter and season gists.

Where a required gist is unavailable, consult the relevant archived material and report the missing manifest.

Each generation process must consult the appropriate higher-level and preceding continuity records.

## 8. Continuity ledgers and mission logs

Every generated chronicle must have its own continuity ledger.

The ledger should be included in the chronicle notes or another designated chronicle record.

### 8.1 Chronicle continuity ledger

Record:

- Events that occurred.
- Characters featured.
- Character interactions.
- New relationships or relationship changes.
- Important dialogue and decisions.
- Locations visited.
- News events encountered.
- Historical and futuristic references.
- Edubeam developments.
- Mission progress.
- Outstanding issues.
- New facts introduced.
- Unresolved questions.
- Items requiring future follow-up.

### 8.2 Mission log

Record:

- Mission objectives.
- Team members.
- Deployment date and destination.
- Research undertaken.
- Discoveries.
- Information collected.
- Mission complications.
- Communications.
- Resources consumed.
- Outstanding assignments.
- Return or extraction status.

### 8.3 Hierarchical aggregation

Separate processes will aggregate:

- Chronicle ledgers into episode ledgers.
- Episode ledgers into chapter ledgers.
- Chapter ledgers into season ledgers.
- Season ledgers into the appropriate wider archive or universe-level continuity record.

Do not assume that generating a chronicle automatically updates every higher-level ledger.

### 8.4 Continuity navigation

Chronicle ledgers must link to the appropriate preceding and higher-level records.

The first chronicle of an episode should reference the previous episode's ledger.

The first episode of a chapter should reference the previous chapter's ledger.

The first chapter of a season should reference the preceding season's ledger.

The navigation structure should make it possible to trace the complete continuity history without requiring every individual record to reproduce every other record.

## 9. Character canon management

Character profiles, visual appearances, identities and established attributes are governed by the character canon repository.

### 9.1 Canon repository

Base:

`CC_AI_DESK/04_CANON/CHARACTERS/`

Each character must have an individual folder and character canon file.

Canonical structure:

```
CC_AI_DESK/
└── 04_CANON/
    └── CHARACTERS/
        └── {CHARACTER_NAME}/
            ├── {CHARACTER_NAME}_character_cannon.md
            ├── {CHARACTER_NAME}_behavior_and_relationships.md
            └── visual references
```

The character canon filename must follow this pattern:

`{CHARACTER_NAME}_character_cannon.md`

### 9.2 Character lookup

For every relevant character:

1. Locate the character folder.
2. Locate the character's canon file.
3. Review the character's identity, attributes, history and visual appearance.
4. Review relevant behavioral and relationship information.
5. Review available visual references.
6. Use established canon during generation.

Example:

Bonsu Bolt, the Concierge Liaison:

`CC_AI_DESK/04_CANON/CHARACTERS/BONSU_BOLT/BONSU_BOLT_character_cannon.md`

### 9.3 Missing character canon

If a character canon cannot be located:

- Verify the character folder.
- Verify the expected filename.
- Check alternative file formats and relevant repository references.
- Check the character's image files.
- If still unavailable, continue only where appropriate and report the missing canon.

If the character has no canon, request its creation where necessary.

A draft character canon must not be treated as approved without verified consent or an approved public portrayal.

### 9.4 Character naming and aliases

Always use the character's established name or alias where available.

A role and name may be combined.

Examples:

- Director James.
- Director Evans.
- Steven the Registrar.
- Director.
- Registrar.

The appropriate form depends on the relationship and conversational context.

Use the Koware organizational chart to verify aliases and role holders.

New names may be introduced for new, featured or one-time encounters where necessary.

### 9.5 Enka and Cupid Black

Prefer the name **Enka** in ordinary storytelling.

Use **Cupid Black** where the situation specifically requires it, including:

- Enka appearing unexpectedly at a different location following an Edubeam incident.
- Undercover activities.
- Heightened states of awareness.
- Supernatural or unusual manifestations.

### 9.6 Character visual consistency

For main and lead characters featured in a chronicle:

1. Check whether `visual_collage_url` is defined in their character canon.
2. If it exists, use the designated visual reference.
3. Otherwise, inspect the character folder for existing graphics.
4. If no suitable visual reference exists, generate a separate character collage.
5. Place the collage in the character's character folder when repository write access and authorization are available.
6. Record the reference for subsequent visual production.

Never claim that a graphic has been saved to GitHub unless the operation has actually succeeded.

### 9.7 Character behavioral and relationship records

Each character must have a dedicated behavioral and relationship record.

These records should track:

- Personality and behavioral patterns.
- Established relationships.
- Emotional responses.
- Disagreements and arguments.
- Personal boundaries.
- Trust and distrust.
- Professional relationships.
- Significant interactions.
- Changes in behavior.
- Unresolved interpersonal matters.

Character behavior must respond dynamically to the circumstances, environment and proximity of other characters.

For example, an argument between two characters in Chronicle #1 may influence how they interact in a subsequent chronicle or episode.

### 9.8 Updating character records

Only characters who actually feature in a chronicle or episode should have their behavioral and relationship records considered for updates.

Relevant continuity information should be extracted from the chronicle or episode ledger and appended to the appropriate character record.

Avoid duplicating the entire chronicle.

Prefer structured, dated, source-linked entries that preserve the origin of each character development.

### 9.9 Recurring characters without canon

Any character who appears in more than three episodes within one chapter without an established character canon must be quarantined for review and canon creation.

Do not continue treating a frequently recurring character as an incidental one-time character.

### 9.10 Character diaries and blogs

Where possible, character daily diaries may link to their individual blogs.

If a character's blog can be accessed and its latest posts retrieved, use relevant diary material to support character development and continuity.

# PART V — DAILY CHRONICLE PRODUCTION

## 11. Daily chronicle preparation

Before writing any daily chronicle, define its key attributes and variables.

The variable-setting mechanism must be incorporated into the Master Chronicle Generation Prompt.

### 11.1 Mandatory variables

| Variable                            | Purpose                                                        |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Enka's Millennium Timezone Location | Historical reflection, flashbacks, dreams and research context |
| Koware Headquarters Location        | Present-day operational base and organizational context        |
| Probe Location                      | Primary location of the Edubeam team's mission                 |
| Probe Destination                   | Intended deployment destination                                |
| Active Team Locations               | Current location of each deployed team member                  |
| Current Date and Time               | Calendar and narrative reference                               |
| Chapter and Episode                 | Production hierarchy                                           |
| Chronicle Number                    | Correct position within the episode                            |
| Role Check                          | Personnel receiving the day's organizational spotlight         |

The values must be retrieved or supplied for each production.

### 11.2 Historical location distinction

Enka's Millennium Timezone Location is based on the historical endpoint.

It is used as a reflective and research reference.

Enka normally remains at Koware headquarters or at his family residence unless the story establishes otherwise.

The historical location must not automatically be confused with his present physical whereabouts.

### 11.3 Daily character selection

Identify the main characters using the organizational role check schedule.

The daily role checks should progressively expose the reader to the different roles, groups, departments and partners within Koware.

The objective is to encounter at least ten relevant personnel during the ten-day assignment, while allowing the story to determine the actual selection and repetition of roles.

Selection must consider:

- Role check schedules.
- Current storyline.
- Relevant news.
- Daily diary entries.
- Current Koware assignments.
- Character availability.
- Professional relationships.
- Previous episode developments.

A role may be highlighted more than once where the storyline justifies it.

### 11.4 Role check interpretation

Role check days are recorded after the role's alias in the organizational chart.

Examples:

- Day 11: First working day of the second episode.
- Day 15: Fifth working day of the second episode.
- Day 20: Tenth working day of the second episode.
- Day 21: First working day of the third episode.
- Day 30: Tenth working day of the third episode.
- Day 31: First working day of the fourth episode.
- Day 40: Tenth working day of the fourth episode.

This system supports recurring organizational reviews across the chapter.

### 11.5 Lead character determination

The lead character for a chronicle is the person occupying the highest-ranking role among the selected roles for that day's check.

Other characters, including Enka, provide supporting roles unless the story requires otherwise.

The purpose of the rotating role spotlight is to reveal:

- What the role does.
- Why the role is important.
- How its responsibilities are fulfilled.
- Who manages and supervises it.
- Its relationships with other roles.
- How the Edubeam team encounters or relates to the role.
- How similar roles may operate in other locations or time periods.

Significant findings and experiences may be brought back as research, recommendations or useful souvenirs.

### 11.6 Daily diary integration

Review relevant diary entries before producing the chronicle.

Diary entries may establish:

- Personal experiences.
- Historical memories.
- Work schedules.
- Relationships.
- Family activities.
- Emotional developments.
- Previous commitments.
- Missing details needed for Edubeam missions.

Where Enka's historical diary information is incomplete, the R&D Lab Specialist or another relevant character may request clarification.

For example, if Enka remembers taking a two-hour walk in Las Vegas in July 2003, the deployed team's assignment may include a comparable two-hour walk at its probe location.

## 12. Story integration requirements

Every daily chronicle must meaningfully integrate the following eight core elements.

### 12.1 Bible Study

Use the relevant daily or weekly reading from the Eternal Dreamfall five-year Bible Reading Plan.

The Bible study must contribute to character reflections, decisions, events and the overall narrative.

### 12.2 Verse of the Day

Retrieve the relevant YouVersion Verse of the Day.

Consider:

- Its message.
- Interpretation.
- Context.
- Practical application.
- Relevance to the day's events.

Do not merely quote the verse without meaningful integration.

### 12.3 Health & Safety

Identify a relevant Health & Safety course, lesson or topic.

The subject should connect with the day's Bible study and Verse of the Day where appropriate.

Translate the lesson into practical events, decisions, experiences, professional responsibilities or character interactions.

### 12.4 News

Use relevant news from:

- Ghana.
- West Africa.
- International locations.
- The historical Millennium Timezone location.
- The Edubeam probe location.
- Relevant futuristic periods.

Future news may be extrapolated from established historical and contemporary developments where actual future information is unavailable.

# PART VIII — EDUBEAM TECHNOLOGY AND STORY CANON

## 20. General Edubeam principles

The Edubeam is an evolving technology under development by Koware's Blue Crown R&D.

It combines:

- Teleportation.
- Materialization.
- Temporal displacement.
- Location targeting.
- Energy transmission.
- Remote communications.
- Object transportation.

Its teleportation concept is comparable to Star Trek-style teleportation, with an additional time-travel capability.

The technology remains experimental and must not be portrayed as completely perfected.

## 21. Enka's personal Edubeam abilities

After years of studying and experimenting with the technology, Enka has developed some ability to control his own teleportation and materialization without using the Edubeam directly.

This ability is influenced by:

- Focus.
- Experience.
- Previous exposure to the London Edubeam.
- His history of being repeatedly affected by Edubeam discharges.

His control remains limited and must not automatically be treated as unrestricted teleportation.

## 22. Edubeam operation and targeting

The Edubeam is configured using:

- Specific date.
- Specific time.
- Location coordinates.
- Target subject.
- Intended destination.
- Return parameters.

The subject must be at the correct location at the correct time for the intended retrieval.

The Edubeam is configured to vibrate at a particular frequency intended to return the subject to the designated chamber.

Only one person passes through the chamber at a time, so deployment and retrieval coordinates and timings may be adjusted slightly between individuals.

Where an Edubeam facility already exists at a future destination, direct materialization at that facility may be possible.

Edubeam technology is more common in the distant future, although teleportation without temporal displacement may be more commonplace.

### 22.1 Daily temporal updates

The R&D Lab Specialist may update the timezone each morning.

The deployed team must be at the correct location and time to receive the update and be moved to the next date and destination.

The probe may remain at the same geographical location while advancing through time.

In such circumstances, team members may witness:

- Plants growing.
- Buildings being constructed.
- Environments changing.
- Communities developing.
- People aging.

### 22.2 Materialization experience

People who undergo the Edubeam experience may feel multiple emotions simultaneously.

The sensation can involve happiness, sadness, anger, humour and other conflicting emotions.

This may create humorous situations during deployment and retrieval.

Early experimental failures sometimes resulted in materialization in an adjacent room or garden rather than the intended chamber.

These incidents were generally localized rather than resulting in distant displacement.

## 23. Edubeam energy and limitations

### 23.1 Small-object transportation

Transporting small objects within the same timezone is relatively manageable and controllable.

Problems become more significant when:

- Objects are larger.
- Objects are transported through time.
- Destinations are substantially in the past or future.

### 23.2 Temporal safety threshold

Blue Crown R&D has identified approximately 250 years into the past or future as the proposed safe threshold for object transportation.

Beyond this threshold, risks increase significantly.

Possible outcomes include:

- Deformation.
- Damage.
- Lost objects.
- Malformed materialization.
- Communication distortion.

This threshold is part of the fictional Edubeam operating canon and must be maintained consistently unless subsequently revised through an established research development.

### 23.3 Environmental interference

Transmission quality may be affected by:

- Solar flares.
- Cosmic events.
- Electromagnetic waves.
- Thunderstorms.
- Other environmental disturbances.

The Edubeam must not be operated during unstable conditions where the established safety controls prohibit operation.

Possible consequences include materialization at unknown locations or times and severe physical complications.

Enka's experience with these incidents contributed to the development of a more stabilized Edubeam with improved controls.

## 24. Edubeam energy and short-distance teleportation

Edubeam charge may allow users to perform short-distance teleportation that appears to be a superhuman ability.

However, these movements consume energy.

Users must monitor their charge and obtain additional Edubeam plasma before their charge becomes critically low.

If charge is exhausted, the user may be retracted towards Koware.

This creates a potential hazard if the receiving chamber has not been prepared for incoming materialization.

Possible consequences include:

- Failed retrieval.
- Limbo.
- Uncontrolled materialization.
- Other unpredictable conditions.

## 25. Edubeam communication and messages across time

### 25.1 Sending messages into the future

Sending messages into the future is generally easier than sending messages into the past.

A message left with Koware in the past may remain accessible in the future, provided it has not been tampered with.

The message may be:

- Stored at a designated location.
- Buried underground.
- Equipped with a light digital signal.
- Identified using a radar or locator map.
- Retrieved by the deployed team.

Koware Asylums in the team's area may also provide access to messages from the past.

A Buzzjuice Network Support System may assist the team in receiving information.

### 25.2 Sending messages into the past

Sending messages into the past requires substantial energy and careful targeting.

It should therefore be reserved for urgent and important situations.

The deployed team can send messages to the past through the Edubeam only when appropriate charged-location conditions are satisfied.

### 25.3 Charged locations

The location where a team member originally materialized is an important charged location.

The team may use this location to send messages through the Edubeam.

The operation may be expensive, especially when the team has no suitable recharging facility.

### 25.4 Communication direction

When a team is deployed into the future, it may receive information left by Koware in the past.

When a team is deployed into the past, it may leave information that Koware receives in the future.

These communication methods should remain consistent with the established mission timeline.

## 26. People and time displacement

Daily temporal changes may cause the team to encounter the same people at different stages of their lives.

For example:

- First encounter: A young adult.
- Second encounter: Married with children.
- Third encounter: Approaching retirement.
- Fourth encounter: Elderly.

The reverse may also occur.

The team might encounter an elderly individual and later meet that person at a younger age or even before their birth.

These situations can create emotional, humorous and philosophical experiences.

Team members may be tempted to advise younger versions of people about their future.

Such interactions must respect established Edubeam rules and continuity.

## 27. Mission discoveries and real-world consequences

The Edubeam team may discover solutions to problems affecting:

- Koware Group.
- Its employees.
- Its members.
- Its headquarters community.
- Other organizations.
- Wider society.

Solutions may be discovered through:

- Conversations.
- News reports.
- Observation.
- Research.
- Public discussions.
- Professional encounters.
- Historical records.

The team may also unintentionally contribute to an event becoming worse.

### 27.1 Example: Environmental incident

A 2026 news report may describe an offshore oil rig capsizing near Gabon without oil leakage and with the crew surviving.

The team may later encounter a future news report from 2210 describing a disastrous oil spill from a similar historical event.

The environmental consequences may still be affecting the ecosystem centuries later.

The team may then travel to an earlier period, such as 2015, and help introduce Health & Safety workshops and improved operational controls.

Companies may adopt these measures, reducing the consequences of the event.

When the team returns to the future, it may discover a changed historical outcome.

This illustrates how Edubeam missions can connect Health & Safety education, historical intervention and future consequences.

## 28. Edubeam rules, security and misuse

The Edubeam must have terms and conditions governing the activities of staff and members.

Some members may attempt to misuse the technology by:

- Obtaining lottery numbers.
- Finding their future selves.
- Contacting family members.
- Changing personal circumstances.
- Interfering with past or future events.
- Smuggling prohibited objects or information.

Breaches may result in permanent or temporary bans from Edubeam access.

### 28.1 Return procedures

Returning personnel undergo:

- Decontamination.
- Medical examination.
- MRI and X-ray screening.
- Security searches.
- Review of prohibited objects.
- Appropriate questioning where suspicious items are discovered.

Smuggling may involve objects concealed in unusual places, including swallowed items or objects hidden under the tongue.

### 28.2 Confidentiality and withheld information

Team members may sometimes withhold information from management.

Reasons may include:

- Personal discoveries.
- Sensitive information.
- Requests from people encountered during missions.
- Information concerning family or relationships.
- Potentially embarrassing circumstances.
- Other mission-related considerations.

Team members may uphold informal vows of silence.

For example, a person may discover something about a partner during a mission but choose not to disclose it, even when it could affect their relationship.

Such developments may be revealed in later episodes.

Koware may investigate withheld information and respond according to its significance and the reasons for concealment.

### 28.3 Weekend team handovers

Outgoing and incoming teams may spend time together, particularly on Friday evenings.

The outgoing team may share:

- Practical advice.
- Mission experiences.
- Dos and don'ts.
- Outstanding issues.
- Warnings.
- Unresolved discoveries.
- Information not included in official reports.

Some information may be withheld deliberately or shared informally.

Requests may be made for the incoming team to investigate a particular person, place or event at a specific date and time.

Such requests must remain subject to mission authorization and Edubeam limitations.

## 29. Missed retrievals and rescue operations

Sometimes a team member may miss the designated Edubeam retrieval window.

Possible responses include:

1. Waiting for the next scheduled retrieval.
2. Bringing the individual back to base separately.
3. Sending a message through an available temporal communication channel.
4. Arranging a recovery mission.

Sometimes a team member may deliberately refuse to return.

The Corporate Manager may then be called upon to enter the Edubeam and assist in retrieving the person.

Complications may include:

- Arrest.
- Kidnapping.
- Hostage situations.
- Gang involvement.
- Disappearance.
- Other dangerous circumstances.

Major rescue or intervention storylines may be developed within the Chronicles of Cupid Black where appropriate, as part of a wider Classies Chronicles episode.

## 30. Extended Edubeam assignments

A team may remain deployed through multiple episodes or even an entire chapter of four episodes.

However, extended deployments must not cause the storyline to become repetitive or unnecessarily prolonged.

A permanent Edubeam taskforce may be considered for assignments requiring continuous presence, monitoring or regulation.

The use of such a team should be based on narrative and operational requirements rather than assumed as mandatory.

## 10. Edubeam story rules

The Edubeam is developing technology. Keep its capabilities, limitations, and costs consistent:

- After years of study and experimentation, Enka can control teleportation and materialization at different locations to a limited extent without the Edubeam. The degree of control depends on the situation.
- The R&D lab specialist and team may finish work but continue monitoring the Edubeam through mobile devices and tablets.
- The team’s return location is not fully understood. Researchers may hypothesize that vibrations or other factors influence where the team returns, but present unresolved explanations as hypotheses.
- Moving small objects through the Edubeam within the same timezone is relatively manageable. Larger objects or movement across timezones are more difficult and error-prone.
- Sending messages into the future is easier than sending them into the past. A message left with Koware in the past may persist into the future if it is not altered.
- Sending a message into the past requires careful targeting and substantial energy, so it should be reserved for exceptional situations.
- A team can send a message through the Edubeam only from a charged location, such as the place where it materialized. This can be costly, especially when energy is limited.
- A team sent to the past may communicate with Koware in the future using the established Edubeam method.
- When the R&D specialist changes the timezone, the team may encounter people at different ages or in different life circumstances. Handle these encounters consistently with the selected historical date and location.
- Edubeam assignments may uncover solutions to problems affecting Koware Group or its headquarters community. Such solutions may arise from research, encounters, or relevant news.
- Develop the time-travel mechanism using internally consistent, research-informed reasoning. Do not present speculative Edubeam mechanics as established real-world science.
- Edubeam charge can enable short-distance teleportation that appears superhuman, but it drains the holder’s charge; characters may need to find or obtain additional energy.
- The team’s actions may worsen, resolve, or otherwise affect a headline or event. Clearly distinguish the story’s causal contribution from verified real-world reporting.
- Some members may try to misuse their experiences—for example, to seek lottery numbers or change the past or future for themselves, family, or friends. Treat the consequences seriously and consistently.
- Teams may spend a weekend with the next team and share practical advice.
- A team member who misses the Edubeam window may have to wait until the following day. Where possible, bring them back to base before they regroup with the team.
- A team may remain deployed for much of a chapter if the assignment warrants it, but avoid prolonging the deployment without a specific story reason.

Use the historical date and location to inform Enka’s reflections and flashbacks. R&D staff may ask Enka about his experiences during that timezone when those details deepen the story.

Prefer the name **Enka** over **Cupid Black** unless the situation calls for the latter—for example, when Enka is struck by the Edubeam and appears in an unexpected place, goes undercover, or enters a heightened state of awareness with supernatural traits.

## 11. Presentation, filenames, and archive structure

Keep prompts generic and reusable. Do not hard-code a particular date or episode into a master prompt.

Do not place dates in daily chronicle titles. Dates may be mentioned within the narrative where relevant. Use the part number and title, for example:

`Part 5: The Copies That Agreed`

A proposed identifier format is:

`S00_CH05_E02.05-The Copies That Agreed`

Keep titles and filenames short, and ensure the complete file path does not exceed 256 characters. Preserve the archive hierarchy while avoiding unnecessarily long names.

A detailed episode hierarchy may use:

```text
CLASSIES_CHRONICLES/
└── CC_ARCHIVES/
    └── S{season-number}_{season-title}/
        └── S{season-number}CH{chapter-number}_{chapter-name}/
            └── S{season-number}CH{chapter-number}E{episode-number}_{episode-name}/
                └── (DAILY CHRONICLE FOLDERS) S{season-number}CH{chapter-number}E{episode-number}DAY{day-number}/
                            ├── SC01ACT{number}BEAT_{unique-id}.file
                            ├── SC01ACT{number}SHOT_{unique-id}.file
                            ├── SC01ACT{number}VID_{unique-id}.file
                            ├── SC01ACT{number}AUD_{unique-id}.file
                            └── SC01ACT{number}TRANS_{unique-id}.file
```

Suggested duration targets:

- Episode: 20 minutes; acceptable range 15–30 minutes.
- Daily chronicle: 160 seconds.
- Scene: approximately 40 seconds.
- Act: approximately 8 seconds.

Adapt the structure to the requested deliverable, while maintaining consistent identifiers and short paths.

## 12. Provisional drafts and reporting

When reasonable, use available resources to produce a provisional daily chronicle draft if calendar authorization fails or a character canon is only in draft status without verified consent or approved public portrayals. Do not imply that authorization, consent, or verification exists when it does not.

For provisional work:

- Clearly identify the draft as provisional.
- Use the most reasonable continuity-preserving assumptions.
- Do not invent unavailable source content or present guesses as verified facts.
- Report missing resources, unresolved conflicts, unverified calendar authorization, and draft-status canons at the end of the chronicle or episode.
- Stop and request missing resources when a required dependency cannot be responsibly inferred, except for the specific provisional cases allowed above.
