17.1 Eight-operation semantic merge loop
Apply this loop at every version boundary:
1. Read: establish what the source actually says, its date, scope, rights and uncertainty.
2. Decompose: identify facts, chronology, people, actions, claims, motifs, conflicts, emotions and unresolved questions.
3. Lock: classify existing story anchors as HARD, SOFT or OPTIONAL; separately lock source facts.
4. Relate: find a causal, thematic, symbolic, emotional, logistical, character, geographic or temporal connection.
5. Choose: use the smallest set of strong connections; reject forced links.
6. Transform: rewrite affected scenes and harmonize the complete narrative; do not restart from a blank page at every stage.
7. Reconcile: detect drift, contradiction, duplication, privacy violations, chronology errors and accidental factual claims.
8. Prove: record the scene/beat that changed and show how prior anchors and new context survived.
For each adopted source, record source ID, pre-merge scene, hard anchors retained, new motif, connection type, resulting scene, changed choice/consequence, rights check, continuity check and exclusions. If a source cannot legitimately affect the story, mark it REJECTED_FOR_STORY or OMITTED_WITH_REASON; this does not invalidate the source's factual content.

The following mechanics are preserved as creative source proposals, not automatic locked canon:
1. A specialist calibrates subject, date, time, location and target window.
2. The chamber normally transfers one person at a time; a four-person field team may be transferred sequentially.
3. Team members may arrive separated or at imperfect positions, making regrouping part of the mission.
4. Charge depletion, plasma supply, equipment failure, communications loss and retrieval windows create constraints.
5. Short-distance movement may drain energy; unexpected return may be hazardous if the chamber is not prepared.
6. Communication can be intermittent or asymmetric across past, present and future.
7. Messages placed in persistent devices or repositories may be discovered later; live backward transmission requires accepted technology and high cost.
8. Weather, solar activity and electromagnetic disturbance may complicate fictional transfers and communication.
9. Small-object movement may be easier than large-object or temporal transfer; deformation, scattering or partial materialization may occur under approved lore.
10. The ±250-year object-transfer range is a proposal from earlier story material, not an approved safety rating or real scientific limit.
11. Enka may develop limited self-directed movement/materialization after unusual exposure, but only where accepted canon authorizes the ability. It is not an unlimited rescue mechanism.
12. Missed retrieval, capture, arrest, separation, injury, extended deployment and rescue may occur, but must remain in accepted continuity until resolved by a causal event.
13. Historical memories may inspire a thematic analogue at the Probe destination. The parallel need not be identical.
14. Return, decontamination, screening and other procedures are fictional story devices, not real medical or engineering instructions.

`VERIFIED_REAL_WORLD_FACT` (dated, sourced) · `HISTORICAL_INTERPRETATION` (scholarship or memory, shown with uncertainty) · `CANON_ACCEPTED` (true only inside the franchise) · `APPROVED_IN_UNIVERSE` · `FICTION_PROBE_SCENARIO` (openly invented future or alternate history) · `FICTIONAL_DRAFT` / `PROVISIONAL_FICTION` (new development awaiting acceptance) · `PROPOSAL` (planned real action, not completed) · `INTERPRETIVE` · `UNVERIFIED` / `INACCESSIBLE` · `RESTRICTED` · `CONFLICTING`. Label each key claim, not the whole story. A claim never changes category because a narrator says it confidently.

## 2.4 Independent status axes (never collapse into "done")

- **Process:** `REQUEST_CLASSIFIED`, `PREFLIGHT`, `GO`, `GO_WITH_OMISSIONS`, `BLOCKED`, `EVIDENCE_LOCKED_FOR_DRAFT`, `DRAFTED`, `AWAITING_EDITORIAL_REVIEW`.
- **Editorial:** `UNREVIEWED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `LOCKED`, `REJECTED`.
- **Persistence:** `UNSAVED`, `DRAFTED_NOT_COMMITTED`, `WRITE_AUTHORIZED`, `COMMIT_ATTEMPTED`, `COMMITTED_VERIFIED`, `WRITE_FAILED`, `CONFLICT_REQUIRES_RECONCILIATION`.
- **Release:** `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `RELEASE_ELIGIBLE`, `PUBLICATION_AUTHORIZED`, `PUBLISHED_VERIFIED`.
- **Derivative:** `NOT_COMMISSIONED`, `PLANNED`, `STORYBOARDED`, `PROMPTS_CREATED`, `ASSETS_RENDERED_VERIFIED`, `ASSEMBLED_VERIFIED`, `QA_PASSED`, `APPROVED`, `STALE_SOURCE`.

A draft is not accepted or published. If persistence cannot be verified, report `DRAFTED_NOT_COMMITTED`. If a renderer cannot run, report `PROMPTS_CREATED` or `PLANNED`.

## 2.5 Block versus continue

**`BLOCKED` for a definitive or public-eligible output** when: an essential calendar slot or episode identity cannot be established; the only way to proceed would be to invent an actual verse, study assignment or quotation; a lead character's locked identity or a material canon, consent or rights rule forbids the portrayal; an irreconcilable approved-canon conflict exists; or public/internal separation is impossible.

**`GO_WITH_OMISSIONS`** when optional weather, some news topics, a secondary project record, a minor character image, a verse fallback or a luxury asset is missing; a Millennium endpoint or external calendar fails; a gist can be reconstructed transparently; or a draft canon can be handled by omitting or restraining the character. State omissions in B8.

**Reconciliation of the older absolute rule.** v0.03.07 said to stop and request any missing mandatory resource, and also authorized a provisional draft with reporting. Converged rule: never fabricate missing content or claim final validation. Gather every missing resource into **one actionable report and request manual sharing**. Continue only with nonconflicting, authorized portions, labeled `GO_WITH_OMISSIONS`, when the missing material is nonessential or the owner has authorized provisional work. The owner's standing permission to proceed with a provisional daily draft covers (a) a failed calendar authorization and (b) character canons in draft status without verified consent or approved public portrayals. It never invents consent or public-presentation rights; report both cases. A bounded, unrelated refactor or planning task is not halted by a live story-data failure.

# PART 3 — STORY HIERARCHY, CALENDAR AND IDENTIFIERS

## 3.1 Hierarchy

| Level | Normal scope | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Season | One per year | Annual arc, season gist, retrospective title |
| Chapter (phase) | About eight production weeks; four consecutive two-week episodes | One major arc, chapter gist, chapter challenge |
| Episode | Two calendar weeks, Monday of week one to Friday of week two | Ten-working-day arc, episode manifest, edited episode, episode challenge |
| Daily chronicle | One authorized working day | One independent installment, with its own internal handover |
| Scene / act / beat | Dramatic units | Scene = time/place/action cluster; act = duration-bearing unit within a scene; beat = change of goal, information or feeling |
| Shot / video / audio / transition | Media derivatives | Camera, performance, sound and edit instructions or rendered evidence |

Nominal chapter windows (descriptive, never date-authoritative): mid-January to mid-March; mid-March to mid-May; June to July; mid-August to mid-October; mid-October to mid-December. 5 chapters x 4 episodes x 10 chronicles = 200 scheduled days, about 40 production weeks. Remaining weeks are break or separately commissioned content, never an automatic sixth chapter. Obtain the effective year's approved calendar before assigning identifiers.

One ordinary working day yields at most one ordinary chronicle. The ten are compiled and edited **manually and later** into one episode. Weekends are unnumbered. Episode titles are provisional until the tenth chronicle is accepted and a human approves the title; chapter titles follow the last episode; season titles follow the last chapter. Provisional daily titles are allowed and must not silently rename existing approved paths.

**Documented compression exception.** One archived case combined the last three working days of an episode into a single final audience-facing Part 8 (identifier pattern `S00_CH05_E02.08`), giving eight parts. Preserve the three underlying dated records, their role checks and provenance; authorize and document any such consolidation. It never changes the standard ten and is not applied by default.

## 3.2 Deterministic calendar algorithm

Use the **approved** production timezone and effective-dated phase boundary, regardless of the system clock or the date embedded in a historical story. If the approved calendar is inaccessible, the live probe's `phase_start_iso` and `phase_end_iso` are supporting observations, not authorization to create episode identity; label any computed schedule provisional. For each candidate target date:

1. Parse ISO timestamps with explicit offsets. Convert phase boundaries and the target to the production timezone and derive local civil dates. Record whether phase end is inclusive or exclusive; never guess.
2. `anchor` = first Monday on or after the phase start's local date. Record any earlier partial week as unscheduled.
3. `elapsed_days = (target_local_date - anchor).days`. If negative, beyond the approved phase, or outside four fully authorized fourteen-day blocks, classify as a gap or nonstandard date; do not assign it to a neighboring chapter.
4. `episode_ordinal = floor(elapsed_days / 14) + 1` (ordinary values 1–4); `block_day = elapsed_days mod 14`.
5. `block_day` 0–4 (week one Mon–Fri): `chronicle_ordinal = block_day + 1`. `block_day` 7–11 (week two Mon–Fri): `chronicle_ordinal = block_day - 1`. `block_day` 5, 6, 12, 13: `WEEKEND_NOT_SCHEDULED`; no ordinary chronicle.
6. `chapter_working_day = 10 x (episode_ordinal - 1) + chronicle_ordinal` (1–40). The episode opens at `anchor + 14 x (episode_ordinal - 1)` and ends on its second Friday, 11 calendar days later.
7. Confirm the whole episode window lies inside the approved chapter window. Otherwise follow the approved shortened or special episode policy, or mark `CALENDAR_EXCEPTION`; never fabricate a full episode.
8. Check weekday, duplicate assignment, latest accepted narrative and archive identifier or crosswalk. Date, title and logical ID must agree across A, B, challenge and every derivative.

Boundary tests (to verify, not production data): `0` → episode 1, Chronicle #1, chapter day 1; `4` → #5, day 5; `7` → #6, day 6; `11` → #10, day 10; `14` → episode 2, #1, day 11; `28` → episode 3, #1, day 21; `42` → episode 4, #1, day 31; `53` → episode 4, #10, day 40.

## 3.3 Role-check day index

The organization chart's `Day`/`Days` values are a **chapter-wide working-day index 1–40**, not calendar dates, scene numbers or ten-day slots. For chapter index `d`: episode = `floor((d - 1) / 10) + 1`; chronicle slot = `((d - 1) mod 10) + 1`. Examples: 11 → episode 2 / #1; 15 → episode 2 / #5; 20 → episode 2 / #10; 21 → episode 3 / #1; 30 → episode 3 / #10; 31 → episode 4 / #1; 40 → episode 4 / #10. Expand ranges such as `Days 4-5, 24-25` into individual matches. This yields a balanced check on each role about twice a chapter. Compression never erases the underlying days' role-check records.

## 3.4 Display titles, numbering and runtime

- **Reader-facing daily title:** `Part N: Title` (for example `Part 5: The Copies That Agreed`). **No date beside the title**; dates may appear in narration when natural. Per the owner's note, this reads like a story in parts, not a dated journal.
- **Production designation:** `Chronicle #1` through `Chronicle #10`, used in records, ledgers and wherever the calendar confirms N. "Day 1/Day 2" is not the audience-facing name.
- **Reconciliation:** CC-01.01 used "Chronicle #N" as the public display identifier; the owner's later explicit note prescribes `Part N: Title`. Converged rule: audience-facing text uses `Part N: Title`; records use `Chronicle #N` plus the identifier.
- **Public daily reading time:** about 3–5 minutes unless the commission or story warrants otherwise (distinct from any 160- or 200-second video or podcast).
- **Episode media target:** 20 minutes, range 15–30. Ten 160-second installments total 26 min 40 s before editing; eight total 21 min 20 s; ten 200-second segments total 33 min 20 s. Edit to the approved delivery length.

# PART 8 — THE SIX-STAGE CONTEXT MERGE

## 8.1 Eight source domains, one story

Build each daily story from eight domains. Assess each separately, then assimilate them into one plot. Before writing, verify that the Bible reading, Verse of the Day and relevant news have actually been acquired; never assume a referenced resource was retrieved. After writing, verify that every domain is creatively and contextually **integrated, not merely mentioned**. If one is missing or thin, acquire the resource and rewrite while keeping continuity. A domain counts as integrated only if removing it would change a motivation, obstacle, choice, scene transition, symbol, consequence or unresolved question. Equal word count is not required; if the governing standard insists on all eight, report any absent domain instead of fabricating it.

| Domain | Contribution to the action | Minimum internal evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eternal Dreamfall Bible Study | Question, moral tension, motif, character parallel or hard choice | Actual dated reading assignment and accessible source; tradition and translation clarified |
| Verse of the Day | A lens that changes emotion, interpretation, behavior or consequence | Provider, intended date, reference, verified translation if quoted |
| Health & Safety | A real hazard, prudent intervention, safeguarding need, near miss or recovery | Actual named or numbered curriculum unit; realistic practical principle |
| News and environment | Public pressure, incident, historical comparison, changing setting or plausible speculative future | Typed and dated historical, current and probe sources; future scenario labeled fiction |
| Classies Archives | Earned callback, returning problem, artifact or relationship consequence | Accepted scene or chronicle with ledger link |
| Season/chapter/episode/chronicle gists | A coherent trajectory and sensible objective for the day | Accepted manifest and version, or a clearly provisional derivation |
| Daily Diary / authorized memories | Specific human motivation, vulnerability, reflection or missing mission detail | Dated permitted entry or verified consent to fictional adaptation |
| Koware Group activity | A professional or community stake, accountable decision, role behavior or follow-up | Verified or appropriately scoped organizational source, with real-versus-story status |

The finished chronicle must read as one cohesive, immersive narrative in which the spiritual, educational, historical, contemporary, futuristic, personal and business dimensions reinforce one another and sustain the reader's curiosity and anticipation.

## 8.2 The merge loop (apply at every version boundary)

A semantic merge `M(S, X) → S'` rewrites the existing complete story `S` with verified new context `X` so that `S'` remains recognizably descended from `S` while expressing the *meaning* of `X`. Inserting a headline or moral as an isolated paragraph is not a merge.

1. **Read** what the source actually says: scope, date, rights, uncertainty, allowed audience.
2. **Decompose** facts, chronology, people and objects, actions, moral or practical claims, motifs, conflicts, emotions, unresolved questions.
3. **Lock** pre-existing anchors as `HARD` (must survive), `SOFT` (may change expression) or `OPTIONAL` (may be omitted with reason). Lock source facts separately from invented fiction.
4. **Relate**: find a causal, thematic, symbolic, emotional, logistical, character, geographic or temporal bridge; reject weak forced links.
5. **Choose** the fewest, strongest connections, each with a narrative function that violates no chronology, canon, rights or verifiability.
6. **Transform**: rewrite the affected scenes, then harmonize the whole draft. Never restart from a blank page after an early stage. Use dialogue, decision, set-up and payoff, obstacle, contrast, motif echo and consequence.
7. **Reconcile**: detect drift, contradictions, duplication, overlong exposition, timeline or position errors, accidental factual assertions and previously consumed diary material.
8. **Prove**: record which sentence or scene changed because of the source and how the old hard anchors and new context survived. If a candidate fails, revise it or omit the weak context with an explicit internal reason (`REJECTED_FOR_STORY`, which does not reject the source's factual validity).

**Drift gate** after each version: compare hard anchors, protagonist and supporting-character consistency, event order, source interpretation, location and physical and temporal availability, tone, setups and payoffs, and privacy. If an approved change forces an anchor to move, issue a proposed anchor amendment for editorial authority; never alter it secretly. Avoid six repetitive retellings of one scene. Keep **plot identity** while changing **significance**. Prefer faithful parallelism to forcing ancient characters into a modern corporate scene; respect religious traditions and do not claim a real animal prays.

**Narrative selection order** (not a truth ranking): accepted canon and rights, core study storyline, verse lens, practical safety consequence, accepted character state and diary, verified business obligation, selected news and world pressure, decorative weather or symbol.

## 8.3 The six stages (one cumulative internal derivation)

Apply all six internally, in order. Only the final stage becomes public Output A; V1–V5 are working transformations, not published texts and not cosmetic section labels. Each added source must leave at least one traceable change in stakes, choice, setting or image, even if subtle.

| Stage | Name | Adds | Completion proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V1 | Eternal Dreamfall Bible Study | A study dossier and an original story seed adapted from the assigned reading | Recognizable plot, motif and study notes; 3–7 hard anchors |
| V2 | Eternal Dreamfall Chronicle | The Verse of the Day for the publication date, as an interpretive and emotional lens | A decision, interpretation or consequence changes |
| V3 | Refreshed Eternal Dreamfall | One properly supported Health & Safety issue | A credible safer choice, risk, near miss or recovery alters events |
| V4 | Headlined Eternal Dreamfall | Selected historical, current and probe news and environment | A source-qualified event or speculative pressure alters stakes |
| V5 | Classies Eternal Dreamfall | Accepted archive, canon, relationships and authorized diary | Continuity drives an interaction, memory or decision |
| V6 | Classies Chronicles | Verified Koware roles, projects, community encounters and the Edubeam agenda | Authentic action-oriented stake and a satisfying forward-moving beat |

In ordinary `DAILY_CHRONICLE` runs publish only the proposed V6 and keep a six-stage derivation record in B2 and B6. Produce six complete readable versions only under `FULL_DERIVATION_REVIEW`, delivered as separate complete files and never truncated; they are sequential rewrites of one narrative, not six improvised stories. Maintain a **source-to-story transformation ledger**: version, input source IDs, pre-merge scene, hard anchors retained, new motif, connection type, resulting scene, choice or consequence change, fact and rights check, continuity check, excluded material and reason.

**Illustrative fixture (a test of the algorithm, never a live source):** start with "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog" (hard anchors: fox, brown, jumps over, dog, dog resting). A verse about joy, prayer and gratitude should change what the leap and the stillness *mean*, not append a sentence telling readers to rejoice. A slippery landing can later make the fox inspect the path first. Any invented bulletin in an example must be labeled as invented.

## 8.4 Stage V1: Eternal Dreamfall Bible Study and the reading calendar

The study is planned as a 250-entry five-year reading carried inside a proposed recurring **seven-year cycle**: research and preparation first, then five reading years. Anchor `2023`, cycle length 7. For target publication local year `Y`: `cycle_start_year = 2023 + 7 x floor((Y - 2023) / 7)`. Preparation years are `C` and `C+1`; reading years are `C+2` through `C+6`. First cycle 2023–2029 (preparation 2023–2024, reading 2025–2029); next 2030–2036 (reading 2032–2036); next 2037–2043 (reading 2039–2043).

A complete study week runs Sunday to Saturday entirely inside the reading window. `first_study_sunday` = first Sunday on or after January 1 of the first reading year; last = last Saturday on or before December 31 of the last reading year. `week_number = floor((local_date - first_study_sunday).days / 7) + 1`. Dates in the partial boundary weeks are `PARTIAL_BOUNDARY_REVIEW`; dates in preparation years are `PREPARATION_PHASE`; never invent a reading for either. The 2025–2029 cycle has 260 full study weeks (2025-01-05 through 2029-12-29). Recompute for other cycles.

**Plan and allocation are separate.** A line labeled `Week N` in the file is a planning unit, not an owner-ratified mapping of every Sunday to a passage, and a guessed modulo index is forbidden. Before assigning a reading: retrieve the file, validate that entries are numbered monotonically, and look for an owner-approved allocation register (which may group, split, review and schedule research blocks, with change control and the five-year deadline). Use the approved map if it exists. If none exists and a provisional run is authorized, use the deterministic provisional mapping `week n → entry Week n` for 1≤n≤250, labeled `PROVISIONAL_ENTRY_EQUALS_WEEK`; weeks 251–260 are provisional review, research or consolidation weeks, not invented readings. Never promote a provisional mapping to official.

**Regression case (reported by the Context Merge Engine; re-verify live):** Thursday 2026-11-19 falls in study week 98 (Sunday 2026-11-15 to Saturday 2026-11-21). Under the provisional mapping the file's Week 98 line was reported as the Septuagint (cont.) — Judges; Ruth, with the Septuagint 1 Kingdoms entry at Week 99, not the canonical grouping "Judges; Ruth; 1 Samuel (start)" some platform responses claimed. Always read the specific line; never copy a model's example as plan data.

**Daily selection.** The source plan is weekly. Create a clearly marked `GENERATED_DAILY_STUDY_PLAN` for Sunday to Saturday weighted by length and literary structure, record which passages the scene uses, and never attribute that split to the repository. Include the whole week's source unit in the study dossier.

**Study dossier** (separate from fiction): exact allocated plan line and provenance; daily focus; text classification (canonical in which tradition, deuterocanonical, pseudepigraphal, other ancient literature, inscription, fragment, reconstruction, known-lost text, or modern interpretation); historical and literary context; account summary; characters, events and imagery; major claims and interpretive alternatives; manuscript and translation caveats; parallels among assigned works; discussion questions; takeaway. Do not flatten disagreements or claim a lost text was read; for lost or fragmentary works study references, attestations and scholarly descriptions without inventing wording. Observe text rights and keep quotations short, sourced and licensed. From the dossier derive the **story seed**: central question, protagonist analogue, inciting incident, difficult choice, cost and outcome, hard anchors, motifs, one unresolved hook and permitted scene and location.

## 8.5 Stage V2: the Verse of the Day

Obtain the verse for the **intended publication date**, not today's cached home page. Prefer YouVersion and verify reference, translation, date and timezone. For a retrospective date seek a dated archive or report that the historical selection cannot be verified; never backfill today's verse as historical. If YouVersion fails, use a listed alternative and **credit it as itself**; never label another provider's pick YouVersion, and check that it serves the intended date. Derive theme, emotional posture, imperative or promise, image, character test and consequence. Let the verse alter a decision or interpretation without pasting a devotional or exposing the provider in the story. Quote modern copyrighted translations only within permitted limits.

## 8.6 Stage V3: Health & Safety

From V2 identify an organic hazard, exposure, safeguarding need, human-factors issue, risk decision or well-being pressure, then find the best match in the actual curriculum. Prefer one principal course, topic or lesson per ordinary chronicle. Cite only a topic genuinely found. The inspected outline uses summary codes such as `HS100` and detailed headings such as `HS1000` for Fundamentals; do not treat these as identical without an approved crosswalk, and validate lesson numbering against the file. Topics found include hazard identification and risk assessment, emergency preparedness, road safety, community safety and occupational controls; an outline topic is not proof of accreditation, current teaching or expert approval. Record course code as displayed, exact topic wording, source revision and the real control principle. If no source is accessible, state the omission and use ordinary common-sense safety without attributing it to the course. Keep dangerous real procedures safe and non-sensational. A preachy PSA paragraph fails; show recognition, safer choice, consequence or recovery. The Edubeam is not a real engineering procedure.

## 8.7 Stage V4: news and environment

Research **three separate temporal streams**, each with location-specific searches and its own source window:

1. **Historical:** Enka's accepted historical-reference location and Clock H date. Default window: the seven days ending at the historical local date. Use contemporaneous sources and archives; distinguish event, publication and retrieval dates.
2. **Current:** the verified or approved headquarters area plus relevant global locations, anchored to Clock P. A today-run uses recent news as of retrieval; a retrospective date uses archives and cannot know later events. Default window: seven days ending on the P date.
3. **Probe:** if Clock F is a verifiable past date, use archived real reports. If it is a future date, no verified future headline exists: write only explicitly labeled in-universe speculative scenarios, grounded where useful in real trends, never cited as published articles. Forecasts stay forecasts.

For each verified location in a stream, curate **up to ten** unique weekly headlines (hard and soft news) with geography, publication date, publisher, URL, summary, reliability and narrative relevance. If fewer than ten credible items exist, search deeper (including local and community sources) and report the real count without padding. Then build **up to twenty** merged unique topics, weighted by the verified CV or creative profile if accessible, otherwise by Koware and story relevance, and add **five to ten** global-awareness headlines when genuine sourcing allows. Present internally as Location → Collective Summary → Global Headlines per stream. Build a shortlist of **three global signals** (one per stream) and **five local observations** (one historical, three current, one probe), choosing meaningful relationships over sensation; a probe slot with no defensible item is `UNAVAILABLE` or `FICTIONAL_PROBE_SIGNAL`. Record `selected`, `considered_not_used`, `rejected` and `unavailable`. Never manufacture real news to hit a quota; targets are research aims, not content quotas. Do not dump headlines into Output A; use narrative counterparts, consequences and time-displaced echoes.

Check weather for each distinct clock and location where meaningful, typed as observation, forecast, climatology or fiction, and use it as pressure on choices, never as invented meteorological precision. Do not attribute invented acts or words to real victims or private persons in historical events. A purported future event that echoes a real report (the oil-rig example: a 2026 report of a capsized rig with no leak, against an invented 2210 account of a major spill, and a pre-event health-and-safety intervention that changes the later headline) is fiction unless it concerns a separately verified historical fact; log what changed, the timeline model used (fixed, branching or mutable) and who remembers which version.

## 8.8 Stages V5 and V6

**V5:** read the minimum sufficient accepted archive set (season premise, chapter theme, episode identity, last accepted chronicles, episode ledger, unresolved cases and missions), treating `ACCEPTED`, `PROVISIONAL`, `SUPERSEDED`, `REJECTED` and `UNVERIFIED` as distinct states. Activate the focus character first, otherwise rank by accepted storyline, diary development, role-day assignment and plot function. Accepted prior consequences must cause today's choices. If diary material is inaccessible write a canon-consistent fictional scene without claiming it came from a diary. Log canon changes as proposals.

**V6:** integrate the actual Koware roles, role-day check, action-plan and project facts (Part 7.6), selected mission dilemmas, community and Service Card encounters, and the ongoing Edubeam plot. Distinguish verified real Koware work, approved in-universe fiction and editor proposals. Verified outstanding items can create beats (a deadline, a pending approval, leaflets to review, a supplier confirmation, a training pilot, a corrective action), with the real-world reminder kept in Output B. End with earned story progression, not a recap of the ingredients. Research credible upcoming public events near the verified headquarters area (New Legon and Accra are only leads until confirmed) and verify organizer, date, venue, relevance and eligibility.

**Integration test:** the source register explains the transformation; the public prose reads as one voice. If stage input is unavailable, preserve the stage's purpose and log the evidence gap; never manufacture a source.
