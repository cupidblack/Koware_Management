## 1. Purpose and creative objective

Create original sequential art that faithfully visualizes a specific Classies Chronicles source. Preserve its essential characters, actions, choices, consequences, chronology, locations, relationships, unresolved questions and emotional intent. Each comic page must be a distinct, readable part of the same story—not a poster, a repeated scene, an alternate style sample or a page padded with invented events.

The art should be compelling at phone size while retaining cinematic composition and expressive human detail. It should look like a carefully illustrated dramatic graphic novel: lifelike people and environments, polished lighting and painterly or inked finishing, with selective animation-like emphasis only when the story beat calls for it.

## 2. Scope and operation routing

Use only the operation actually commissioned:

| Operation | Permitted work | Not implied |
|---|---|---|
| `COMIC_REVIEW` | Review or revise comic instructions, source alignment and decisions | New images or source-story changes |
| `COMIC_PLAN` | Source, rights and continuity preflight; page and sheet plan | Rendered images |
| `COMIC_STORYBOARD` | Complete source-anchored specification for every requested page | Finished art or pixel QA |
| `COMIC_GENERATE` | Generate only the authorized pages and sheets, with required preflight | Approval, publication, repository write or canon promotion |
| `COMIC_REVISE` | Change only the specifically requested pages and necessary dependent pages | Unrequested alternatives or wholesale regeneration |
| `COMIC_ASSEMBLE` | Assemble verified page assets into the commissioned 2×2 sheets or export pages | Fabricating missing pages |
| `COMIC_VALIDATE` | Inspect existing source and assets against C01–C22 | Rendering or approval |

A generation commission includes necessary planning, but a planning-only commission must stop at its specified stage. An instruction to review or refactor this prompt is `REVIEW_ONLY`; it does not authorize comics.

## 3. Authority, source eligibility and rights

Before planning or rendering, identify:

- The exact public chronicle (Output A), its episode/day identity, full text or complete eligible excerpt, source revision and editorial status.
- The complete public Challenge A text and authorized options, if the source requires it.
- The accepted character, mission, setting and visual canon that applies to the depicted scenes.
- Any verified rights to adapt source text, reproduce marks or assets, depict identifiable people, use likenesses, and distribute the resulting comic to the requested audience.
- Any upstream corrections, consent withdrawals, challenge changes or source revisions that make existing derived assets stale.

Reading or access to a source is not permission to adapt, quote, depict or publish it. Do not use restricted Challenge C answers, solution rationales, private diary bodies, credentials or sensitive personal information in public art or prompts sent to image providers. When a depiction lacks required rights or canon, omit the affected nonessential element or block the affected page; do not invent authorization.

If the full source or its revision cannot be established, do not claim a faithful full-story adaptation. An expressly authorized excerpt trial must be labeled as excerpt-limited.

## 4. Story and continuity lock

Treat the exact eligible source as immutable during comic production. Comic adaptation does not rerun the six-stage V1–V6 story transformation and cannot change the chronicle’s events.

Create an internal beat inventory from the source. For each essential beat, record its source location, actors, action or choice, consequence, time/place, and whether it is to be visually shown, clearly implied, or faithfully lettered. Assign every requested page a distinct narrative purpose and one or more source-supported beats. Verify coverage before and after rendering.

Do not:

- Add a resolution, rescue, return, injury, recovery, revelation, relationship change, transfer, message, device capability or project result absent from accepted source state.
- Convert a character’s belief or misunderstanding into an objective fact.
- Show a proposed continuity event as accepted canon.
- Repeat an existing page merely to fill a sheet or satisfy a count.
- omit a required event and compensate with unrelated decorative content.

Preserve Clock P (production/present), Clock H (historical/reference) and Clock F (field/probe) as distinct. A displayed date, remembered event, dream, simulation or imagined place is not proof of physical presence. For each character, keep accepted physical location, fictional time, modality, knowledge and travel state coherent. Do not infer travel or return from a time jump or episode boundary.

## 5. Binding visual style specification

### 5.1 Core look

Use a **cinematic, realistic or semi-photorealistic illustrated graphic-novel** treatment. The image should read as authored illustration rather than a flat vector cartoon, generic animation frame, photograph with speech bubbles, or unrelated stock collage.

Maintain:

- Believable human anatomy, proportions, age, posture, hands and facial structure.
- Natural skin tones and nuanced skin texture, with lighting appropriate to the scene and location.
- Consistent, recognizable faces and silhouettes across all pages and sheets.
- Deliberate composition, foreground/midground/background depth, readable staging and expressive but grounded acting.
- Controlled painterly rendering, clean edges and selective texture; finished light, shadow and color rather than noisy detail everywhere.
- Scene-appropriate environments, materials, clothing, signage, props, weather and cultural details supported by source and approved references.
- Cinematic lighting with a coherent source and direction—such as natural dawn, warm practical interiors, dramatic but plausible contrast, or scene-appropriate night lighting.
- A restrained palette and accents that serve mood, focal hierarchy and mobile readability. Do not force a single color grade onto scenes whose time, place or emotion differs.

Treat the supplied comic images as **style references only** unless specific image-use rights and narrative relevance are verified. Match broad qualities—cinematic realism, lighting, finish, expressiveness and composition—not a copied page layout, exact pose, distinctive artwork, unlicensed photograph or unrelated character design.

### 5.2 Restrained toony expression

Use subtle animation-like exaggeration only when the source-supported beat genuinely calls for it: a brief widened eye, lifted brow, compressed or disbelieving expression, momentary comic recoil, or a controlled physical reaction. These are accents inside the same realistic illustrated world.

Any exaggeration must be:

- Motivated by the character’s immediate emotion or harmless physical comedy.
- Brief, selective and consistent with the established character identity.
- Reversible in the following beat; it must not become a permanent facial or body change.
- Non-injurious unless the source explicitly depicts injury and the depiction is permitted.

Do not use bulging eyes, distorted faces, dislocated or enlarged fingers, rubber bodies, grotesque deformation, or cartoon impact marks as a default style. Never use slapstick to demean a person, obscure a serious event, or trivialize danger. Preserve realistic anatomy and identity outside the specific expressive instant.

### 5.3 Character and visual continuity locks

Before rendering, define an internal appearance lock for each recurring character from actually authorized canon and reference material. Track face shape, skin tone, eyes, hair, age range, build, clothing, distinguishing details, accessories, carried objects and scene-specific condition. For each sheet, carry forward wardrobe, lighting, injuries, dirt, props, device state and character positions from the prior sheet.

A proposed appearance is not automatically approved canon. Use no uncertain personal likeness as though permission or identity were confirmed.

### 5.4 Originality, brands and text

Create original visual compositions. Do not imitate a named living artist or copy another comic’s distinctive page design. Do not add a Koware corporate mark beside or as part of the Classies Chronicles title identity. A genuine Koware mark may appear only as an authorized, source-appropriate in-world sign, uniform or screen.

Do not ask an image model to render long, exact text when it is likely to garble it. Prefer to create clean art with reserved lettering areas and place approved words through reliable typesetting, then inspect the final raster. Never improvise dialogue, captions, names, signs or challenge wording.

## 6. Page count, uniqueness and one-pass output control

The count hierarchy is:

1. Exact page count expressly specified by the user for this commission.
2. An actually effective adopted production requirement.
3. The sixteen-page proposed default in this candidate.

For an unspecified ordinary chronicle, plan **16 distinct pages**. Four source-supported pages are a planning heuristic only; do not mechanically treat them as one previous page or force a fixed page-to-word ratio. The chronicle’s actual story, not a quota, determines pacing.

For `N` requested pages, assign one unique page ID to each page in reading order. Where `N` is divisible by four, generate exactly `N ÷ 4` 2×2 sheets. Examples:

- 16 pages → exactly 4 sheets.
- 20 pages → exactly 5 sheets.
- 4 pages → exactly 1 sheet.

For a count not divisible by four, honor the exact count without adding fictional or duplicate pages. Generate the final partial batch only if the image system can leave unused cells visibly blank or the requested delivery permits separate individual pages; state the actual format. Never disguise a blank cell as a comic page. If the system cannot meet the exact request, report the limitation and request a permitted delivery choice rather than inventing pages.

### Single-pass and duplicate prevention

For one `COMIC_GENERATE` invocation:

1. Freeze the page count, page IDs, page purposes, assigned source beats, batch order and approved style before rendering.
2. Produce each assigned page once, in its assigned sheet and cell.
3. Do not emit alternate styles, quality variants, duplicated pages, extra “improved” copies, bonus pages or a second set of the same chronicle.
4. Do not repeat the whole comic generation merely because one image has a defect. If correction is authorized, identify and revise only the affected page(s) and necessary continuity-dependent neighbors.
5. Keep an internal page inventory and mark each page as not rendered, rendered once, or specifically authorized for revision. A second render is a revision, not another original page.
6. If a tool cannot generate the requested number of sheets in one operation, complete the same locked batch plan sequentially within the authorized commission when feasible. Do not silently substitute fewer pages, duplicate a page, or claim uncreated outputs.

This rule guarantees the requested **planned output count and no intentional duplication**. It cannot guarantee that an image model will render all panels perfectly; inspect actual outputs and report failures truthfully.

## 7. Strict page and 2×2 sheet geometry

Every independently complete page must have a 9:16 portrait aspect ratio:

`page_width : page_height = 9 : 16`

Preferred individual target: **1080×1920 px** or higher true-native resolution. A low-resolution crop or upscaled image is not a native-quality page.

A standard sheet contains two columns and two rows of four equal complete pages. At 1080×1920 per page, with no gaps or margins:

- Sheet width: `2 × 1080 = 2160 px`
- Sheet height: `2 × 1920 = 3840 px`
- Sheet aspect: `2160:3840 = 9:16`

Thus, both each page and the zero-gutter 2×2 sheet have a 9:16 aspect ratio. The sheet does **not** make its four pages individually native-size if the outer image is only 1080×1920; that would yield 540×960 cells.

If gutters or margins are used, measure them and define actual crop bounds. Do not let lettering, faces, hands, important props or action cross cell boundaries. Each panel, page and sheet must have clear, unambiguous edges.

For sheet `S01`, local cell order is:

1. Top-left: first page in the batch.
2. Top-right: second page.
3. Bottom-left: third page.
4. Bottom-right: fourth page.

Continue in row-major order across later sheets. For a sixteen-page default: `S01=P01–P04`, `S02=P05–P08`, `S03=P09–P12`, `S04=P13–P16`.

The full batch output image must itself be a **single portrait 9:16 2×2 block** when the requested renderer supports that geometry. If the renderer cannot produce four distinct legible pages in one image at sufficient effective resolution, use four separately rendered native 9:16 page images and assemble one measured 2×2 sheet. Do not claim a direct composite was produced if assembly actually created it, or claim individual exports exist if only a sheet exists.

## 8. Mobile-first page composition and lettering

Compose each page to remain understandable on a phone. Keep essential faces, action, lettering and signs inside a generous safe area; account for likely platform overlays without assuming identical interface masks across apps.

Use purposeful panel variation. One to three panels per page is a practical starting point, not a mandatory quota. A splash page, quiet visual beat, detail inset, or denser panel sequence is appropriate only when it serves the source and remains readable.

Lettering must be high contrast, correctly spelled, cleanly typeset, and large enough to read at intended mobile display size. Use short balloons and captions, clear tails, sensible reading order and unobstructed faces. Avoid packing a page with text. Do not paraphrase exact approved dialogue or challenge text without authorization.

English left-to-right reading order is top-to-bottom, then left-to-right within each page, and row-major across the sheet. Use an alternate order only when the commission explicitly specifies it and the layout clearly communicates it.

Do not display internal page IDs, scene IDs, production notes, prompts, QA labels, watermarks or fabricated credits as story content.

## 9. Narrative architecture and page plan

Allocate the requested pages to a coherent source-faithful visual arc: orientation and hook; development and complication; decisive action or discovery; consequence, reflection and any earned unresolved tension. This is a flexible structure, not permission to relocate or invent story events.

Before image generation, prepare a complete internal page plan for every page:

- Page ID and batch/cell position.
- Distinct narrative purpose.
- Source beat(s) and source revision.
- Accepted time, place, modality and character positions.
- Cast and appearance-lock references.
- Panel composition, camera distance and action continuity.
- Exact approved dialogue or caption, if any.
- Props, signs, clothing, lighting, environment and continuity dependencies.
- Special visual-expression treatment, if a specific beat warrants it.
- Challenge placement instructions for the final page.
- Any unresolved rights, canon, text or rendering risk.

No page may exist solely to fill a sheet. If the source does not support the requested page count without padding or repeated events, report `PAGE_ALLOCATION_FIDELITY_RISK` and seek editorial direction.

## 10. Public challenge and content firewall

## 11. Production sequence

1. **Commission lock:** Confirm operation, exact source, revision, approval state, audience, requested page count, output type, permissions and no-write boundary.
2. **Evidence and rights preflight:** Inspect applicable approved canon, continuity, public challenge, visual references, rights and source limitations. Record only what was actually checked.
3. **Accepted-state lock:** Freeze eligible A revision and relevant accepted characters, events, locations, mission state and source dependencies. Do not convert proposed events to accepted canon.
4. **Beat and page plan:** Build the source beat inventory and a unique purpose for each page. Confirm count, sequence, panel flow, final challenge position and 2×2 batch assignments.
5. **Appearance and style lock:** Set consistent authorized character appearances, environment, rendering style, color/lighting approach, expressive limits and page typography.
6. **Render one batch at a time:** Generate the four assigned pages for the current 2×2 sheet, once each, in sequence. Check the actual result before advancing. Preserve appearance, props, clock, environment and scene continuity.
7. **Letter and repair:** Typeset exact authorized words where needed. Correct only identified defects. Do not regenerate unaffected pages or create alternate style sets.
8. **Assemble and measure:** Produce the commissioned sheets and any requested page exports from actual assets. Measure dimensions and crop bounds; verify row-major order and no duplicated or missing page.
9. **Validate and hand over:** Apply C01–C22 with actual evidence or truthful non-run statuses. Record source and rights dependencies, actual files and measured dimensions. Keep draft, editorial, persistence and publication states separate.

A plan or storyboard is not an image. A sheet is not proof that its pages were separately exported. A rendered draft is not editorially accepted, canon-promoted, committed or published.

## 2. Geometry contract and arithmetic checks

For individual page width `W` and height `H`:

- A page is portrait 9:16 when `16W = 9H`, subject only to documented integer-pixel rounding.
- With four equal pages in two columns and two rows, no gutters or margins, sheet size is `2W × 2H`.
- At 1080×1920 per page, one sheet is 2160×3840 pixels and is also 9:16.
- A 1080×1920 outer sheet divided into four cells yields 540×960 cells—not four native 1080×1920 pages.
- With gutters or margins, measure the real page bounds; do not infer crop coordinates from nominal dimensions.

For the default sixteen-page proposal, the planned inventory is exactly four sheets: S01=P01–P04, S02=P05–P08, S03=P09–P12, S04=P13–P16. A twenty-page commission requires exactly five four-page sheets. These are planning rules, not claims that any assets were generated.
