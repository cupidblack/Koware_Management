## **3. Important production improvements**

I would make three additional provisions before integrating this into the main comic-generation workflow.

**A. Reference hierarchy**

Not all reference images should have equal authority.

I recommend this hierarchy:

1. Approved master visual references — establish the overall style.
2. Approved character reference sheets — establish character identity.
3. Approved scene references — establish scene-specific visual continuity.
4. Previously approved panels — establish immediate narrative continuity.
5. New generation instructions — determine what changes in the current scene.

This prevents an individual scene from accidentally redefining the entire visual identity.

**B. Separate artwork from lettering**

For professional production, artwork and text should be independently controllable.

The image generator should concentrate on illustration, composition and character rendering. A subsequent compositing stage should handle exact dialogue, captions, titles and other text.

This significantly reduces the risk of malformed lettering.

**C. Introduce a visual regression test**

Every new generation should be compared against a small collection of approved reference images.

The comparison should evaluate:

- Character identity consistency.
- Rendering and texture consistency.
- Colour and lighting consistency.
- Composition quality.
- Overall artistic resemblance.

This turns visual consistency into an ongoing production requirement rather than relying entirely on subjective approval.

## 8. World and setting continuity

### Koware Group headquarters

The Koware Group occupies five single-story, three-bedroom apartment blocks clustered so that the blocks share a boundary line. The headquarters/base is associated with New Legon, Accra, Ghana, and functions as the control center for the Classies Chronicles. Use the current setting information in the relevant source if it provides a more specific or updated description.

Top-level managers may work from home; some second-level managers split their time between home and the base. Staff may arrive by different means of transport and at different times. When relevant, show the neighborhood noticing the arrivals and the practical challenge of getting to work on time.

The R&D department—including the R&D director, lab specialist, and R&D liaison—is based at the founder’s family home, the Yamoah Residence in Adenta.

## 9. Daily and episode production outline

Use this outline as a flexible story framework, not as a requirement to include every routine in every chronicle.

### Daily chronicle planning

1. Set and record the required variables from Section 3.
2. Identify the date, episode position, role-check day, lead character, supporting cast, and applicable gists.
3. Review the context-merge resources and relevant continuity, character, governance, action-plan, diary, Bible-study, Health & Safety, news, and event materials.
4. Determine the Edubeam team’s mission and how it relates to historical-date events and news at the probe location.
5. Draft the day’s story, integrating relevant work, personal, spiritual, educational, and business threads.
6. End with the daily challenge, then add the updated mission log and continuity ledger.
7. Perform the integration, continuity, resource, and quality checks in Section 7.

### Two-week episode rhythm

Use the following recurring rhythm where it fits the episode’s story. Morning routines and work schedules can vary by character, job, day, and location.

**Monday of Week 1 — episode opening**

- Establish the current date and time, early-morning atmosphere, and the episode’s main characters.
- Use the role-check schedule to select the day’s focus.
- A scene may begin outside a character’s home and move indoors to a bedside alarm. A 4:00 a.m. alarm may prompt a brief review of the Eternal Dreamfall five-year Bible-reading plan. A 4:30 a.m. alarm may lead to unfinished domestic tasks; a 5:00 a.m. alarm may precede fitness preparation. Characters may snooze alarms or change their routine.
- On some Tuesdays and Saturdays, and occasionally Thursdays, a character may exercise until sunrise or skip the session.
- During weekdays, characters may freshen up and have breakfast around 6:00 a.m. At about 6:30 a.m., the team may coordinate travel to headquarters. Travel can involve buses, trains, planes, traffic, or other locally appropriate conditions.
- Minor disputes during travel may be resolved through a character’s service card when appropriate.
- By 9:00 a.m., staff generally join a secure online briefing. The corporate manager may cover annual goals, relevant news, and Koware’s timezone.
- Review reports and data from the returning Edubeam team. Staff may apply for future assignments.
- Brief the selected four-person Edubeam team on its mission, monitoring energy levels, charging Blue Crown devices, and using Edubeam particles to recharge.
- The next team should be selected in advance of the current team’s return so members have time to prepare. Reveal or confirm the selection as appropriate.
- The R&D lab specialist monitors equipment, feedback signals, and team safety. Team members may communicate needs such as transportation or supplies through Blue Crown devices; the lab specialist may arrange for objects to materialize where feasible.

**Tuesday of Week 1**

- Activate the day’s Eternal Dreamfall Bible reading and Verse of the Day; identify a related Health & Safety lesson.
- The R&D lab specialist fine-tunes the Koware timezone. The deployed team is updated and may be teleported to the revised probe time.
- Focus on the Edubeam team’s research, discoveries, encounters, and involvement in events and headlines.
- No regular meeting briefing is required.

**Wednesday of Week 1**

- Continue the daily Bible reading, Verse of the Day, Health & Safety integration, and morning routines.
- The R&D specialist fine-tunes the Edubeam and updates the team.
- Focus on the team’s research, discoveries, encounters, and consequences.
- No regular meeting briefing is required.

**Thursday of Week 1**

- Continue the daily study, Verse of the Day, Health & Safety integration, and morning routines.
- The R&D specialist fine-tunes the Edubeam and updates the team.
- Hold a follow-up briefing on Monday’s meeting when appropriate.
- Continue the team-focused story.

**Friday of Week 1**

- Continue the daily study, Verse of the Day, Health & Safety integration, and morning routines.
- Focus on the team’s mission and discoveries.
- Around 2:00 p.m., staff may begin weekend departures, lock-up, and security checks. The schedule can change if members stay to socialize, watch films, play games, or work on affiliate membership plans. Clubs and group activities may continue into the evening.
- Domestic management, fitness or walks, dinner, contact check-ins, and an evening review of Eternal Dreamfall may appear when relevant.

**Monday of Week 2**

- Continue the daily study, Verse of the Day, Health & Safety integration, and morning routines.
- Hold a follow-up briefing on the previous week’s briefing when appropriate.
- The R&D specialist fine-tunes the Edubeam and updates the deployed team.
- Continue the team’s research, discoveries, encounters, and mission.

**Tuesday and Wednesday of Week 2**

- Continue the daily study, Verse of the Day, Health & Safety integration, and morning routines.
- The R&D specialist updates the Edubeam team as needed.
- No regular meeting briefing is required.
- Focus on the team’s mission and developments.

**Thursday of Week 2**

- Continue the daily study, Verse of the Day, Health & Safety integration, and morning routines.
- The R&D specialist prepares the Edubeam to bring the deployed team back.
- Hold a follow-up briefing as appropriate. Returning staff may settle in, compile reports, and prepare to present them.

**Friday of Week 2 — episode close**

- Continue the daily study, Verse of the Day, Health & Safety integration, and morning routines.
- Hold a briefing with returning staff, review their reports, and incorporate relevant findings into Koware’s operations, action plans, and workflow.
- Confirm the next Edubeam team, selected in advance, and its preparation needs.
- Around 2:00 p.m., staff may disperse for the weekend, complete security checks, or continue with member activities. Use the flexible evening routine described for the first Friday when appropriate.
- Close the episode’s active threads or carry unresolved ones forward.
- End with the challenging episode game or problem, the next-episode preview, and the updated episode mission log and continuity ledger.

## 10. Edubeam story rules

The Edubeam is developing technology. Keep its capabilities, limitations, and costs consistent:

- After years of study and experimentation, Enka can control teleportation and materialization at different locations to a limited extent without the Edubeam. The degree of control depends on the situation.
- The R&D lab specialist and team may finish work but continue monitoring the Edubeam through mobile devices and tablets.
- The team’s return location is not fully understood. Researchers may hypothesize that vibrations or other factors influence where the team returns, but present unresolved explanations as hypotheses.
- Moving small objects through the Edubeam within the same timezone is relatively manageable. Larger objects or movement across timezones are more difficult and error-prone.
- Sending messages into the future is easier than sending them into the past. A message left with Koware in the past may persist into the future if it is not altered.
- Sending a message into the past requires careful targeting and substantial energy, so it should be reserved for exceptional situations.
- A team can send a message through the Edubeam only from a charged location, such as the place where it materialized. This can be costly, especially when energy is limited.
- A team sent to the past may communicate with Koware in the future using the established Edubeam method.
- When the R&D specialist changes the timezone, the team may encounter people at different ages or in different life circumstances. Handle these encounters consistently with the selected historical date and location.
- Edubeam assignments may uncover solutions to problems affecting Koware Group or its headquarters community. Such solutions may arise from research, encounters, or relevant news.
- Develop the time-travel mechanism using internally consistent, research-informed reasoning. Do not present speculative Edubeam mechanics as established real-world science.
- Edubeam charge can enable short-distance teleportation that appears superhuman, but it drains the holder’s charge; characters may need to find or obtain additional energy.
- The team’s actions may worsen, resolve, or otherwise affect a headline or event. Clearly distinguish the story’s causal contribution from verified real-world reporting.
- Some members may try to misuse their experiences—for example, to seek lottery numbers or change the past or future for themselves, family, or friends. Treat the consequences seriously and consistently.
- Teams may spend a weekend with the next team and share practical advice.
- A team member who misses the Edubeam window may have to wait until the following day. Where possible, bring them back to base before they regroup with the team.
- A team may remain deployed for much of a chapter if the assignment warrants it, but avoid prolonging the deployment without a specific story reason.

Use the historical date and location to inform Enka’s reflections and flashbacks. R&D staff may ask Enka about his experiences during that timezone when those details deepen the story.

Prefer the name **Enka** over **Cupid Black** unless the situation calls for the latter—for example, when Enka is struck by the Edubeam and appears in an unexpected place, goes undercover, or enters a heightened state of awareness with supernatural traits.

## 11. Presentation, filenames, and archive structure

Keep prompts generic and reusable. Do not hard-code a particular date or episode into a master prompt.

Do not place dates in daily chronicle titles. Dates may be mentioned within the narrative where relevant. Use the part number and title, for example:

`Part 5: The Copies That Agreed`

A proposed identifier format is:

`S00_CH05_E02.05-The Copies That Agreed`

Keep titles and filenames short, and ensure the complete file path does not exceed 256 characters. Preserve the archive hierarchy while avoiding unnecessarily long names.

One proposed archive layout is:

```text
CLASSIES_CHRONICLES/
└── CC_EPISODE_ARCHIVES/
    └── CC_S00_FOUNDATION_PHASE/
        └── S00_CH01_E01/
            └── S00_CH01_E01.01/
                └── SCENE_001/
                    └── ACT_001/
```

A more detailed episode hierarchy may use:

```text
CLASSIES_CHRONICLES/
└── CC_ARCHIVES/
    └── CC_S{season-number}_{season-title}/
        └── S{season-number}CH{chapter-number}_{chapter-name}/
            └── S{season-number}CH{chapter-number}E{episode-number}_{episode-name}/
                └── DAILY CHRONICLE {number}/
                    └── SCENE {number}/
                        └── ACT {number}/
                            ├── ACT{number}BEAT_{unique-id}.file
                            ├── ACT{number}SHOT_{unique-id}.file
                            ├── ACT{number}VID_{unique-id}.file
                            ├── ACT{number}AUD_{unique-id}.file
                            └── ACT{number}TRANS_{unique-id}.file
```

Suggested duration targets:

- Episode: 20 minutes; acceptable range 15–30 minutes.
- Daily chronicle: 160 seconds.
- Scene: approximately 40 seconds.
- Act: approximately 8 seconds.

Adapt the structure to the requested deliverable, while maintaining consistent identifiers and short paths.

## 12. Provisional drafts and reporting

When reasonable, use available resources to produce a provisional daily chronicle draft if calendar authorization fails or a character canon is only in draft status without verified consent or approved public portrayals. Do not imply that authorization, consent, or verification exists when it does not.

For provisional work:

- Clearly identify the draft as provisional.
- Use the most reasonable continuity-preserving assumptions.
- Do not invent unavailable source content or present guesses as verified facts.
- Report missing resources, unresolved conflicts, unverified calendar authorization, and draft-status canons at the end of the chronicle or episode.
- Stop and request missing resources when a required dependency cannot be responsibly inferred, except for the specific provisional cases allowed above.

## 13. Final quality-control checklist

Before delivering the requested material, verify that:

- The required variables, date, location, role-check day, and lead character are identified.

- Filenames and paths are concise and within the stated length limit.
