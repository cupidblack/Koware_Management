## **4. Recommended outreach architecture**

The programme should operate through five interconnected stages.

## **Hopeful Way Oxford House outreach funnel**

1. **Institutional engagement**

2. **Supervisor awareness**

Your original proposal mentions obtaining consent from clients' sponsors.

## **10. Oxford House charter and institutional credibility**

This is a particularly important part of the campaign because you want supervisors, sponsors and prospective residents to understand the difference between an Oxford House and an ordinary privately operated halfway house.

### **10.1 What the Oxford House charter establishes**

The official model identifies three fundamental conditions:

**Self-run**

Residents govern their own house and participate in its decisions.

**Self-supporting**

Residents share the expenses and responsibilities of maintaining the house.

**Drug- and alcohol-free**

Maintaining a substance-free residence is a fundamental condition.

Based on the official Oxford House model and charter information.

The Oxford House organisation also provides training and technical assistance to its network.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.oxfordhouse.org\&sz=32)

oxfordhouse.org+1



### **10.2 How to explain the difference**

The campaign should not make unverified accusations about other halfway houses.

Instead, use this comparison:

| Area           | Oxford House model                        | Conventional externally managed residence |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Management     | Resident self-governance                  | Depends on operator                       |
| Decisions      | Democratic resident participation         | Depends on management                     |
| House finances | Shared resident expenses                  | Depends on operator                       |
| Membership     | House interview and resident decision     | Depends on operator                       |
| Accountability | Peer accountability                       | Depends on operating model                |
| Charter        | Oxford House charter standards            | Depends on licensing and accreditation    |
| Duration       | Can be long-term while conditions are met | Depends on the residence                  |

This is a comparison of operating models, not a claim that every other residence is inferior or unsafe.

### B. Presentation approval and house charter verification are separate

Your confirmation that the presentation letter has been signed is sufficient to proceed with internal preparations.

However, before describing the Ashiaman residence publicly as an *officially chartered Oxford House*, the Foundation should verify the document establishing that specific house's current charter status.

There is genuine historical evidence of Hopeful Way's involvement with the Oxford House model in Ghana. Recovery Africa documents collaboration beginning in 2009. [RECOVERY AFRICA](https://www.recoveryafrica.org/oxford-houses?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The campaign should use that history positively without suggesting that every proposed or existing residence automatically holds the same charter.

### C. New resident admission requires special voting rules

The official Oxford House model specifies **80% approval by existing house members for a new member's admission**. Ordinary democratic house decisions generally follow majority voting. [Oxford House](https://www.oxfordhouse.org/faq?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Therefore, the proposed admission procedure has been revised to distinguish:

- Preliminary interview by the designated team.
- In-house interview by residents.
- Resident membership vote.
- Acceptance, practical arrangements and move-in.

No supervisor, sponsor or Foundation representative should guarantee admission before the house's decision.

### D. Adult consent must remain independent of sponsor consent

I recommend a distinction between two different permissions.

**Applicant consent** establishes whether the adult freely wishes to be considered for residence and contacted about the process.

**Sponsor agreement** establishes whether another person has agreed to assist with expenses or practical arrangements.

A sponsor's consent does not replace the applicant's own consent.

The initial form therefore collects only information necessary to establish interest and make safe contact. More sensitive information should be requested later, only when necessary and appropriately protected.

Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843), and Data Protection Commission requirements are particularly relevant because the programme concerns people recovering from substance-use difficulties. [Ghalii](https://ghalii.org/akn/gh/act/2012/843/eng%402012-05-18?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### E. Referral commissions should not form part of the initial campaign

I recommend **not introducing rent-linked commissions for rehabilitation supervisors or clinical staff**.

Such arrangements could create conflicts between an applicant's interests and a referrer's financial interests.

The campaign instead prioritises educational collaboration, transparent institutional relationships and voluntary referrals.

### **2.2 Separate the referral waiting list from the actual admissions process**

A waiting list entry must not be interpreted as:

- An admission approval.
- A reservation of a bed.
- Confirmation that a preferred location exists.
- Confirmation that the applicant is clinically ready to move.
- A commitment by the applicant to pay a particular amount.

I recommend four separate statuses:

1. Expression of interest received.
2. Preliminary assessment pending or completed.
3. Eligible for a particular house's interview.
4. House decision and move-in arrangements.

This prevents misunderstandings and gives the foundation a much clearer operational picture.

### **2.3 Do not make the referral commission part of the launch**

I recommend temporarily removing the proposed monthly referral commission.

Recovery housing referrals involve vulnerable people, and paying a supervisor a percentage of a client's rent could create conflicts of interest or pressure to refer unsuitable individuals.

Instead, build institutional relationships through:

- Professional presentations.
- Staff education.
- Recognition of institutional partners.
- Recovery education and collaboration.
- Transparent referral procedures.

Any future financial arrangement should undergo separate legal, ethical and governance review and must not influence clinical decisions or house admissions.

### **2.4 Make the sponsor's role clear**

The client must voluntarily agree to being referred.

The sponsor may support the referral, but should not be treated as the owner of the application or the decision-maker over the applicant.

The proposed process should require:

- Applicant consent.
- Permission to contact the applicant.
- Confirmation that the supervisor has discussed the opportunity with the applicant.
- Sponsor involvement only with the applicant's permission, where appropriate.

This distinction should be reflected in the form and the WhatsApp communication.

### **7.3 Relapse and return-to-house procedure**

This needs particular care.

The international Oxford House model requires a substance-free environment and does not permit a resident who returns to substance use to simply remain in the house.

Therefore:

- A relapse should trigger the response required by the current charter and house rules.
- Appropriate medical or detoxification services should be sought where necessary.
- A former resident should not be promised automatic reinstatement.
- Any subsequent application must follow the applicable admission process.
- Residents retain their decision-making responsibilities.

The house is not a medical detoxification facility.

### **9.1 Referral commissions**

I recommend removing monthly rent-based commissions from the initial programme.

The reasons are:

- Supervisors should recommend accommodation based on the applicant's interests, not financial compensation.
- Rehabilitation facilities may have policies prohibiting payments to individual staff for referrals.
- Financial incentives could undermine trust in the admission process.
- Oxford House admissions should remain independent of external financial influence.

Alternative recognition arrangements include:

- Institutional partnership certificates.
- Staff training and educational sessions.
- Recognition at annual recovery events.
- Reimbursement of approved institutional presentation expenses.
- Formal collaboration agreements.

- Say “chartered” only for the specific house for which current charter evidence has been confirmed. Explain the house’s relationship to Oxford House accurately and distinguish that from permission to give presentations.

## 1. Bottom line

Your plan has the right spine: supervisors learn about the house, the person agrees to be referred, a waiting-list entry is created, a video interview follows, then the house interview and resident vote. I kept that spine. I changed six things, and each change protects either the applicants, the Foundation, or the credibility of the campaign.

1. **The applicant gives consent, not only the sponsor.** An adult's own agreement is required. A sponsor's or supervisor's agreement is an addition to it, never a substitute.
2. **First contact collects less.** Date of birth, place of birth, discharge detail and sponsor contacts move to the interview stage. 3. **Verify before you claim.** Charter status of the Ashiaman house, current vacancies, rent, and the current house rules must be confirmed in writing before any message goes out. A wrong claim to a rehab supervisor costs more than a one-week delay.

5. **Explain the model; do not attack other providers.** Point 11 of your plan (benefits over unlicensed halfway houses) becomes a plain list of what a chartered, resident-governed house gives people: a written charter, resident votes, published rules, and accountability to Oxford House, Inc.
6. **Referral commissions are deferred.** Paying staff at treatment facilities per client creates a conflict of interest and can look like buying referrals from vulnerable people. Section 9 gives you safer alternatives. This one is your decision, but I recommend not launching with it.

## 7. Stage 3 — From interest to move-in

The order never changes, and no step can be skipped by anyone, including the Chairperson.

1. **Expression of interest.** The applicant submits the form, or a supervisor helps them with the applicant present and agreeing.
2. **First call (within 24 hours).** Confirm their consent and their safe way to be reached. Confirm they are 18 or older. Say plainly that this is recovery housing, not treatment or detox, and ask whether they want to continue.
3. **Information call.** Give the facts in this order: location and gender of the house, whether a bed is actually free, the monthly contribution and any deposit, house rules including testing, how the residents vote, and that acceptance is not guaranteed. Send the current rules and cost sheet securely.
4. **Preliminary video interview (20–30 minutes).** Held with any team member, scheduled with the applicant's agreement. The applicant must be able to speak privately. Ask about: why they want this kind of home, their expected move-in date, how they would contribute to the household, whether they are ready to follow the rules and accept testing, and what support they have. Do not ask about substances used, diagnoses, medication or treatment details. Refer clinical or detox questions to a qualified provider.
5. **House interview.** Arranged a few days before a possible move-in. The residents meet the applicant and ask their own questions. Offer reasonable accessibility support.
6. **Residents' vote.** Each resident votes under the charter's threshold. Tell the applicant the result privately and respectfully. If the answer is no or not yet, say what, if anything, can happen next, and ask whether they want updates about future houses.
7. **Admission.** Only after a yes vote, a confirmed free bed and agreed costs, give the written terms, rules, emergency and safeguarding contacts and an orientation. Take no money before the terms are written and agreed.
8. **After move-in.** The house governs itself under its rules. HopefulWay keeps only the support role it is authorised to hold.

**Relapse, testing and return.** Say this to every applicant: any resident may ask for a test; a failed or refused test, or any substance use, is handled under the house rules, which can include removal from the house, with the support of local authorities if safety requires it. A resident who has relapsed and completed detox may ask to return, and the residents decide by vote. The house is not a detox or treatment service. In a medical emergency, call emergency services or go to a clinic.

## Referral commission policy

If a referral commission is pursued, first obtain written approval from HopefulWay leadership and legal or compliance advice.

A safer model would be:

- A fixed administrative referral appreciation payment, paid only after a referred client completes a defined period of residence, such as 30 or 60 days.
- A transparent written agreement with each referring organisation.
- Clear disclosure to the client that a referral relationship exists.
- No payment based solely on client placement.
- No pressure on clients to choose HopefulWay.
- No commission arrangement that could interfere with clinical judgement, treatment decisions, or safeguarding.

A percentage of rent may create dependency on occupancy and can be perceived as an incentive to fill beds. A modest fixed payment after sustained residency is generally more defensible.
