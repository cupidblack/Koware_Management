One particularly useful recommendation in the previous responses is to distinguish between a referral and an actual application for residence.

I would formalise this distinction:

- **Expression of interest:** An individual indicates that they would like to learn more.
- **Referral:** An authorised person introduces an interested individual with their permission.
- **Preliminary assessment:** HopefulWay checks whether the individual wishes to proceed and explains the arrangements.
- **House application:** The individual applies to a particular residence.
- **House decision:** Existing residents conduct the membership interview and make their decision.
- **Admission:** The individual is accepted, completes the agreed arrangements and moves in.

This prevents a large waiting list from being mistaken for a list of people who are ready and eligible to move into a house.

### **5.3 Privacy and safeguarding requirements**

This programme will handle information that may reveal an individual's substance-use recovery history.

The Ghana Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843) treats health information as sensitive personal data. The Data Protection Commission also states that data controllers intending to process personal data must register with the Commission.&#x20;

GhaLII+1



Before launching the form:

1. Confirm Hopeful Way Foundation's Data Protection Commission registration and compliance position.
2. Identify the responsible data controller and authorised data processors.
3. Publish a privacy notice.
4. Restrict access to the Google Form response sheet.
5. Establish a retention and deletion policy.
6. Ensure that only designated team members can view applicant information.
7. Provide a means for applicants to request corrections or withdraw consent.

Do not collect medical diagnoses, medication lists, detailed treatment histories or clinical records through this initial form.

Do not send the full form responses to a shared email inbox. The email should simply notify the authorised team that a new submission has arrived.

### **9.4 Residence rules and relapse**

The public information pack must explain the rules before an individual proceeds to the house interview.

The rules should cover:

- Substance-free living.
- Shared responsibilities.
- House expenses and contributions.
- Democratic decision-making.
- House meetings.
- Membership interviews.
- Behaviour and conduct.
- Safeguarding.
- Emergency procedures.
- Complaints and dispute resolution.
- Substance testing, if permitted under the approved rules.
- Relapse and membership termination procedures.

There are two matters requiring formal resolution.

**First: testing.** Your proposal says any resident can request a test at any time. That procedure should be documented, including who can administer tests, confidentiality, refusal, disputed results and the applicable legal and safeguarding requirements. Do not advertise arbitrary testing powers until the policy is approved.

**Second: detoxification and return after relapse.** The proposal suggests that a resident may undergo immediate detoxification and return if voted in.

This needs reconciliation with the Oxford House charter. The official model requires immediate expulsion of a resident who returns to alcohol or illicit drug use.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.oxfordhouse.org\&sz=32)

Accordingly, I recommend:

- The house must follow its applicable charter requirements.
- A person requiring detoxification must be referred to a qualified clinical facility.
- The house should not be presented as a detoxification or treatment centre.
- Any subsequent application for residence must follow the applicable Oxford House rules and formal guidance.

Do not promise an automatic return following detoxification.

Based on the official Oxford House model and charter information.

## **12. Referral commission and partnership policy**

I recommend removing the proposed monthly percentage or fixed referral commission from the initial campaign.

There is a significant ethical distinction between paying for legitimate institutional services and paying an individual to direct vulnerable people towards paid accommodation.

A percentage of a client's rent could create a conflict of interest for a rehabilitation supervisor, counsellor or clinical professional.

The previous platform review independently identified this risk.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://raw.githubusercontent.com\&sz=32)

GitHub+1



Instead, consider:

- Institutional partnership agreements.
- Joint recovery education programmes.
- Sponsored presentations.
- Transparent reimbursement of legitimate institutional expenses.
- Approved training or educational service agreements.
- Corporate sponsorship of recovery housing.

Any financial arrangement should be transparent, independently reviewed and unrelated to whether a particular individual is admitted or how much rent they pay.

The primary incentive for a rehabilitation institution should be the availability of a credible post-treatment recovery housing option for suitable clients.

2. What Oxford House is and is not: peer-run recovery housing; not treatment, detox, or guaranteed accommodation.
3. Self-government, shared responsibilities, self-support and substance-free expectations, using only verified local rules.
4. HopefulWay's role and exact verified charter/residence status.
5. Personal testimony, with consent and no outcome promise.
6. Current Ashiaman opportunity and verified costs/availability/eligibility.
7. Referral/consent process and applicant privacy.
8. House interview and resident decision.
9. How to request a presentation or share the interest link.
10. Questions and a one-page contact/resource sheet.

The approved presentation letter can be shared with the initial outreach only if the signed version is available and the Foundation approves it for distribution. It is reasonable to attach it to the first personalized institutional message, alongside one concise current one-page factsheet; avoid a large bundle. Do not attach internal admin files or ask institutions to forward the letter as an admission guarantee. For institution-specific letters, create a separately approved cover letter addressed to the actual recipient and institution, with the institution's real address and current date. Preserve the signed authorization unchanged.

## 10. Compensation, expansion and housing viability

Do not pay supervisors, sponsors, staff at treatment facilities, or referrers per applicant, per admission, or as a percentage of rent in this campaign. Such payments create conflict-of-interest, safeguarding, procurement and trust risks, especially where people are vulnerable. If a future institutional service agreement is considered, obtain independent legal/ethics review, written Foundation approval, transparent terms, no influence over clinical discharge or house admission, and disclosure to applicants. A non-financial partnership, staff education, and transparent expense reimbursement are safer pilot options.

Do not promise a female house or a house in another town based on a waiting list alone. Assess sustained voluntary demand, safe separate premises, charter/organizational approval, resident leadership, local legal requirements, staffing/support boundaries and a viable budget first. Do not combine genders in accommodation while the programme does not support co-ed houses.

Create a weekly Ashiaman viability dashboard for the Chairperson and residents: verified occupancy/capacity; voluntary qualified interest; interviews booked/completed; offers/declines; monthly income and costs at aggregate level; lease decision; and safe contingency options. The stated target of six residents by 31 December must be treated as an internal management target only, not used to pressure applicants. Make no move-in commitment without actual bed availability and a viable lease.

## 11. Step-by-step implementation schedule

### Days 1–2: decision and verification

- Chairperson convenes house residents and Foundation decision-makers.
- Verify charter and the exact authorized public description; house rules; costs; vacancy; occupancy; lease facts/deadlines; current contact and signatory; and who owns each action.
- Decide whether Ashiaman is actively accepting referrals now and what to tell applicants if the house closes or changes.
- Approve the consent, privacy, safeguarding, relapse/emergency and complaints process. Obtain Ghana privacy/legal review as needed.

### Days 2–5: minimum viable system

- Foundation privacy lead approves the notice, retention schedule, access list and response workflow.
- Build the minimal Google Form using Section 5.2, under the Foundation-controlled account; configure notification-only email to support@buzzjuice.net and lock the response sheet.
- Publish the form on the canonical landing page with current facts, video, official links and acknowledgement. Set up a redirect from the second domain only if its owner can configure it.
- Configure Amelia; if not ready, publish no false booking link and use reply/call scheduling.
- Create separate institution and restricted applicant registers; assign a named owner and backup for every new enquiry.
- Test from a phone as an applicant and as a supervisor; test acknowledgements, consent fields, access, email notification, deletion request, calendar timezone, and link redirects. Remove test entries securely.

### Days 5–7: staff readiness and pilot

- Review approved signed letter, current public brochure/factsheet, house rules, cost sheet, and official model language. Remove stale or unverified claims.
- Rehearse the 15–20 minute presentation and the questions about cost, testing, relapse, treatment, gender, privacy, house voting and availability.
- Compile and verify 5–10 Greater Accra institutions and the correct professional contact. Assign one owner to each and record source/permission.
- Send personalized pilot messages; record response, referral permission and next action. Do not use a group broadcast exposing numbers.

### Weeks 2–4: refine and scale

- Apply the Day 3/7/14 sequence only where appropriate; pause on opt-out or decline.
- Hold presentations by appointment; offer the interest form to individuals, not as a requirement for attendance.
- Review weekly funnel measures and applicant experience with the Chairperson and house representatives. Fix confusing form questions and page friction before expanding to additional institutions.
- Reconfirm Ashiaman lease and capacity weekly until its future is settled. If it cannot continue, contact every active applicant privately and offer only verified alternatives or an opt-in future-interest record.

### Monthly: governance and learning

- Review aggregate measures, privacy incidents/requests, complaints, opt-outs, follow-up completion, resident feedback, house viability and expansion demand.
- Delete expired records; review access; retrain staff; update the public factsheet and form when facts change.

## 12. Measurement and launch criteria

Measure: verified institutions approached; delivery/response rate; presentations requested/booked/completed; voluntary applicants; percentage with applicant consent confirmed; contact time; preliminary discussions; house interviews; resident decisions; admissions; withdrawals; opt-outs; and demand by location/gender in aggregate. Track the conversion funnel without setting quotas for vulnerable clients or rewarding referrers.

**Do not launch until all are true:** current residence status and rules verified; truthful cost/availability information available; signed authorization handled correctly; privacy notice and lawful collection process approved; restricted form and registers tested; Chairperson and backup assigned; response/withdrawal process tested; and messages approved by the Foundation and house representatives.

## Recommended direction

## Updated action plan

### 1. Prepare the offer before generating referrals

Create a one-page, dated referral brief that states:

- What HopefulWay offers and who the residence may be suitable for.
- The **currently available** house location, eligibility, verified vacancies, fees, and next steps.
- That an application or referral is **not a guarantee of placement**; availability and house-member decisions apply.
- What is and is not provided: for example, whether treatment, clinical supervision, transport, or employment support is available.
- How to ask questions, request a presentation, or refer someone—with consent.
- A named Chairperson contact and a backup contact.

Keep future locations and women’s accommodation clearly labeled as **possible depending on demand and resources**, not currently available. Explain that a person may need to consider an available location while a preferred area is unavailable.

### 2. Build a focused contact list

Start with a small, verified list of rehabilitation facilities and relevant educational or community organizations in Accra and nearby areas. Identify the appropriate supervisor or referral contact and their preferred channel. Contact institutions individually or through an opt-in list; avoid a WhatsApp group or broadcast that exposes recipients’ numbers.

The initial institutional message should ask permission to send the referral brief or arrange a short call. Send a tailored, addressed presentation letter after confirming the institution’s details. Do not rely on an old generic letter without checking its date, recipient, claims, and authorization.

### 3. Use a short first message and a clear next step

The first WhatsApp should be brief enough to read on a phone. It should not carry a long biography, multiple attachments, sensitive client information, or unsupported claims. Offer the official HopefulWay and Oxford House websites, the short video if it has been checked and approved, and one referral link.

**Draft message to a supervisor**

> Hello [Name], I’m [Chairperson’s name] from HopefulWay Foundation. We’re inviting interested adults to learn about our sober-living residence and its application process after treatment or through another referral source.  
>   
> Returning to the same environment after treatment can be difficult. I spent 3 years in an Oxford House while completing a diploma; I’ve now maintained 10 years of sobriety. This is my personal experience, not a promise of outcomes.  
>   
> Our current residence is in Ashaiman, and availability and admission are confirmed individually. With the person’s agreement, you can ask us a question, request a presentation, or refer someone here: [approved referral link].  
>   
> HopefulWay: https://hopefulwayfoundation.org/  
> Oxford House: https://www.oxfordhouse.org/  
> Video: https://youtu.be/OH2X5Ib8eIc?si=CRSmSvSIj-SvbjRN  
>   
> Would you prefer a brief call or a presentation for your team?

Use the recovery-story paragraph only with the storyteller’s explicit approval. If there is not room, omit it from the first message and offer it during a presentation. Include the presentation letter as an attachment only if it is finalized, correctly addressed where appropriate, and useful to the recipient.

### 4. Set up a privacy-conscious referral and interest form

A form should collect **interest**, not function as an admission decision or clinical assessment. Avoid asking for place of birth, full medical history, substance-use details, sponsor details, or exact date of birth unless there is a specific, justified need. Do not make sponsor permission a substitute for the prospective resident’s own agreement.

Suggested fields:

- Preferred name; surname optional at the initial-interest stage.
- Prospective resident’s phone number or preferred way to be contacted; optional if the supervisor will facilitate contact.
- Confirmation that the person knows about and agrees to this referral.
- Referrer’s name, organization, role, and work contact.
- Whether the person is currently in treatment, preparing for discharge, or enquiring from another source; no diagnosis or treatment details.
- Expected discharge or move-in timeframe, if relevant.
- Current available location(s) the person is willing to consider.
- Preferred area, free text; clearly state that it is not currently available unless confirmed.
- Preferred language and contact time, if useful.
- Optional request for a presentation or follow-up.
- Separate permission for HopefulWay to contact the prospective resident, and a way to withdraw that permission.

Provide a plain-language privacy notice naming who can see submissions, how they will be used, how long they will be retained, and how to request correction or deletion. Restrict access to a small, named team. Do **not** email the full form response to a shared inbox; send a notification that a new submission needs review. Before launch, check applicable Ghanaian data-protection requirements and the form provider’s handling of the data.

A Google Form may be a quick pilot, but only after its access controls, notification settings, privacy wording, and data-retention arrangements are checked. Test both proposed URLs on mobile before publishing:

- https://buzzjuice.net/hopefulway-oxfordhouse
- https://koware.org/hopefulway-oxfordhouse

Route notifications to the authorized team or `support@buzzjuice.net` without placing unnecessary sensitive information in email.

### 5. Separate presentation booking from client intake

Offer supervisors a simple presentation request with institution, contact person, preferred dates, format (in-person or virtual), estimated audience, and accessibility needs. Confirm availability manually at first; test the Amelia setup end-to-end before advertising it. Do not include client names or recovery details in the booking system.

The client’s supervisor can help coordinate a preliminary video interview, but the client should know what it involves and agree to participate. Only after that stage should the team explain the in-house interview, house-member vote, and any move-in requirements. Do not imply that a supervisor or sponsor can guarantee admission.

### 6. Use respectful, consent-based follow-up

Maintain a simple referral log with institution, contact person, date contacted, response, next step, and opt-out status. Do not record unnecessary client details in that log.

Suggested institutional cadence:

- **Day 0:** tailored introduction and ask whether they want the brief or a presentation.
- **Around Day 3:** one short follow-up if there has been no response.
- **Around Day 7:** one final message or call, if calling is appropriate and the contact details were provided for that purpose.
- **After Day 14:** pause. Re-contact only after a meaningful interval or a relevant update, and honor any request not to be contacted.

For an individual referral, contact the person only within the permission they gave. Do not send sensitive recovery information through WhatsApp unless the person has chosen that channel and understands the privacy implications.

### 7. Do not pay referral commissions to clinical or rehabilitation staff

I recommend **not** offering supervisors, clinicians, or institutional staff a percentage or fixed commission tied to a client’s rent. That can create conflicts of interest, undermine trust, and may raise legal or professional concerns. If the Foundation wants to compensate an organization for legitimate work, get local legal and ethics advice first, make it transparent, and ensure it is not contingent on a particular person’s placement or rent payments.

## Messaging guardrails

- Say “chartered” only for the specific house for which current charter evidence has been confirmed. Explain the house’s relationship to Oxford House accurately and distinguish that from permission to give presentations.
- Describe democratic house governance, sobriety expectations, shared responsibilities, fees, voting, testing, relapse procedures, and consequences in plain language **after confirming the current written rules**.
- Do not promise sobriety, employment, education, a specific location, immediate admission, or a return after relapse.
- Avoid framing a relapse as a moral failure. Explain the residence’s safety and return process calmly, and direct people needing detox or urgent clinical care to qualified services.
- Explain that residents’ votes and house rules matter, but also state how decisions, appeals or complaints, and safeguarding concerns are handled.

## Measure whether the campaign is working

Track institutional replies, presentation requests and completions, **consented** referrals, successful first contacts, interviews scheduled, applications, admissions, and reasons people could not proceed. Review these regularly alongside vacancies and house sustainability. Keep referral volume within the residence’s actual capacity; do not recruit people into a placement that is unavailable or not yet verified.

**Practical launch sequence:** verify the house and charter claims → approve the brief, letter, privacy notice, and form → test the links and workflow → contact a small pilot group of institutions → review responses and adjust before scaling.
