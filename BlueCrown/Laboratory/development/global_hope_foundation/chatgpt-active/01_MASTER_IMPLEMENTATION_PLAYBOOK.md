## 1. Decision brief

**Immediate objective:** Preserve a suitable and safe recovery-housing opportunity in Ashiaman by converting **consented expressions of interest** into completed assessments, resident interviews and accepted move-ins. The stated baseline, **subject to reconfirmation on launch day**, is one active male residence, eight-person capacity, two occupants, and a **minimum six residents by 31 December 2026** to support continuation; the reported lease expiration is **31 January 2027**. Hence four additional admitted residents, rather than merely four form entries, are needed by the December checkpoint. Treat the six-person target as an internal sustainability constraint, **never a reason to pressure an applicant or waive admission requirements**.

**Longer-term objective:** Maintain a reusable, privacy-conscious interest and referral process for future houses, other regions, and separate women's houses when demand, finance, safe facilities and resident leadership justify opening. No co-ed placements are currently proposed.

**Core proposition:** After rehabilitation, some people benefit from a stable, substance-free, peer-governed environment in which they can practice independent living and re-enter employment, education or community life. An Oxford House is **not a treatment centre, detoxification facility, clinically supervised residence, or guaranteed relapse-prevention programme**. The national Oxford House model is resident-governed, resident-supported and alcohol/drug-free. The Foundation can explain that model; specific charter, licensing and service claims require documentation.

**Single funnel:** Verified institutional contact → permission-based information → interested adult's informed consent → minimal-data intake → first contact and eligibility conversation → preliminary video interview → current-house information and rules → in-house resident interview → **80% favorable house-membership vote under the published Oxford House model, unless locally applicable governing documentation calls for a different lawful process** → financial/practical arrangements → move-in, or an appropriately managed alternative/waiting state. No sponsor, referrer, officer or presentation speaker can bypass the house's membership decision.

**Launch rule:** Any failed gate blocks publication of the specific related claim or collection activity. Where a missing cost, charter or safety fact materially affects an informed housing choice, hold admission outreach until it is confirmed. Obtain a dated house-information sheet before the first interview.

**Charter wording:** Until individual charter proof is checked, say: *“Hopeful Way Foundation has authorised a team to present the Oxford House recovery-housing model and provides information about its peer-supported residence in Ashiaman.”* Only after verification may the organisation truthfully say *“The Ashiaman residence operates under a current Oxford House charter”* and cite the relevant charter. Do not compare Oxford House to “illegal” or “unlicensed” houses without independently checking each establishment's legal status.

**Clinical/legal boundary:** The Oxford House traditions discuss removal from membership for substance use or disruptive conduct. A test refusal/failure, illness, relapse, or threat to safety requires the verified house process plus Ghanaian legal obligations, safe handling and appropriate health/safeguarding referral; do not promise police-assisted or extrajudicial eviction. Re-admission after treatment is never automatic: it requires applicable house rules and resident acceptance. Drug testing methods and who may request tests must be verified against the active written rules, not improvised in campaign messages.

## 5. Audience targeting, relationship management and consent

### Priority order
1. Existing trusted recovery partners and men's residential rehabilitation programmes around Ashaiman/Tema, then Greater Accra. A former partner relationship is not a substitute for obtaining the appropriate supervisor's current permission.
2. Hospitals/psychiatric and substance-use services with discharge coordinators; approach through official patient-relations or clinical leadership channels.
3. Community recovery support, qualified counsellors, faith-based recovery services and social-welfare organisations.
4. Tertiary institution counselling offices, technical/vocational education and workplaces supporting adults in recovery. Avoid bulk targeting students or disclosing student health concerns.
5. Self-referred adults, families and sponsors **only with the adult's independent informed agreement**.

### Contact-register governance
Maintain a nonclinical **Institutions** register separate from **Applicants**. Record organisation, publicly listed location/phone, named supervisor only when confirmed, permission/contact source, last date, next action, status, opt-out and presentation outcome. Do not import personal client names or treatment information into the institution register. Where an institution has not opted in, initiate a brief professional inquiry to its public office and ask who handles discharge/referrals; do not immediately bulk-message thousands of mobile numbers. WhatsApp Business broadcasting requires contact opt-in and typically recipients saving the sender's number; ordinary one-to-one messages are safer for initial relationships. See https://faq.whatsapp.com/861663048350950 and https://faq.whatsapp.com/361005896189245/.

**First-message rule:** Ask the supervisor to share the **public interest URL with adults who want it**. A sponsor's willingness to pay may be relevant later, but it does **not replace an adult applicant's consent**. If a supervisor fills the form on the adult's behalf, verify the adult's personal consent at first contact; do not progress on supervisor attestation alone. Sponsors should receive only information authorised by that adult and may independently consent to financial arrangements.

### Follow-up service levels
- Day 0: individual institutional message plus the signed presentation authority **only if** institution details and approval are confirmed; otherwise offer to send it.
- Day 3: one short receipt check; ask whether a presentation or information sheet would be welcome.
- Day 7: one useful approved item or a call with permission; do not resend the same long pitch.
- Day 14: last discretionary phone contact or short closing message; stop repeated chasing.
- Day 30: targeted follow-up **only** if engagement was positive and more information was requested.
- Day 60–90: voluntary nurture if the contact agreed, a real update exists and no opt-out was registered.
- **Stop immediately** when declined or opted out; record suppression centrally so other team members do not contact the same person again.

## 8. Consented admission pipeline — SOP

| Stage / status | Owner | Minimum action and evidence | Target service level |
|---|---|---|---|
| NEW | Intake coordinator | Form receipt logged with unique reference; permissions check | Same working day |
| CONSENT-PENDING | Chairperson or delegate | Contact adult through safe authorised channel; verify interest, right to withdraw, age 18+ | Within 1 business day |
| CONTACTED | Intake coordinator | Explain actual location, gender-specific opportunity, available place, costs when verified, rules, and no guarantee | Within 2 business days |
| INFORMATION-SENT | Intake coordinator | Provide current approved house fact sheet and resident rules; invite voluntary questions | Before interview |
| VIDEO-INTERVIEW-SCHEDULED | Chairperson/delegate | Obtain adult's permission; referrer may coordinate time but cannot control answers | As feasible with discharge timeline |
| VIDEO-INTERVIEW-COMPLETE | Interviewer | Ask about voluntary readiness for a sober, shared, self-run household, practical timetable, expense sharing and accommodations; avoid diagnoses/history | Recorded securely |
| HOUSE-INTERVIEW-REQUESTED | House liaison | Ask resident members to offer interview times; verify capacity | Before any move-in commitment |
| HOUSE-INTERVIEW-COMPLETE | House members | Provide fair interview; note eligibility and decision process | Before vote |
| MEMBER-VOTE-PENDING | House members | Hold documented vote under current charter and rules, generally **at least 80% affirmative** for new Oxford House members | As soon as practical |
| ACCEPTED-PENDING-MOVE-IN | Resident house officers | Re-confirm space; give written financial terms/rules, emergency support arrangements, safeguarding contacts | Before admission |
| MOVED-IN | House officers | Record actual date, occupancy, payments to house accounts, induction and safety review | On move-in |
| DEFERRED / NOT ACCEPTED / WITHDRAWN | Intake lead | Respect decision; offer feedback/other safe options where possible; apply privacy procedure | Promptly |

**Interview prompts:** Why does the adult want a recovery residence? What does self-run daily living mean to them? Can they agree to no alcohol/drugs and the documented house rules? What shared chores, meeting and financial contributions can they reasonably make? What date do they need accommodation, and what support may be needed to travel or sustain continuity of care? Is direct contact safe? Who, if anyone, do they want copied on practical arrangements? Do not make the screening a substitute for a clinician's risk or detox assessment.

**Clinical safety:** People who need active withdrawal management, emergency treatment, or inpatient care should be connected to qualified services; the house should not undertake detox. For relapse, follow current house procedures, arrange safe support and emergency referrals where needed, and explain any later fresh application without guaranteeing acceptance. Protect confidentiality and use respectful, non-stigmatising language.

**Admissions governance:** One resident one vote. The usual Oxford House approval threshold is **80% of voting members for new admission**, not a simple majority. Other governance motions may use majority rules; verify written local charter and any legal constraints. The Chapter and Foundation must respect resident self-governance while maintaining lawful safeguarding, housing and privacy responsibilities.

## 10. Chairperson's institutional presentation — 20 minutes + questions

1. **Welcome and scope (2 min):** present Hopeful Way, signed presentation authorisation, current objectives, explicit limit: not a treatment or detox provider.
2. **What changes after discharge (3 min):** a return to previous environmental triggers can challenge recovery; acknowledge varied recovery pathways; no universal guarantees.
3. **What an Oxford House is (4 min):** history and established model, resident democracy, shared costs and responsibilities, alcohol/drug-free living, length-of-stay determined by continued compliance rather than arbitrary treatment periods.
4. **Hopeful Way's local opportunity (3 min):** Ashiaman men's residence, actual verified vacancies, expenses, intake steps, residence access, no co-ed offering; clearly distinguish housing interest elsewhere from existing beds.
5. **Personal testimony (3 min):** optional Chairperson account of 13 years of addiction, three in an Oxford House, a completed diploma, and a decade of sobriety afterward. Share only after testimonial release approval; avoid implying a typical success rate.
6. **How to refer (3 min):** give public interest URL; adult consent first; call/video screening, in-house interview, 80% vote and acceptance; explain sponsor role and privacy.
7. **Joint next step (2 min):** invite supervisor to share the flyer and discuss clients approaching discharge **without disclosing their identities**; request staff follow-up or a visit to future groups.
8. **Questions (10 min):** cost, location, house rules, date, clinical services boundary, charter and eligibility.

Recommended visual set: title/authority, post-discharge transition gap, Oxford House mechanics, current Ashiaman fact sheet, testimonial (consented), admission flow, house rules and safeguarding, links and contact. Do not add unsupported success percentages or unauthorised photographs.

## 11. Cost, sustainability and expansion

**Internal sustainability:** Project monthly expenditure = lease rent + utilities + household maintenance + reserve + safety/compliance costs; compare to the residents' approved equal-share contributions or other permitted model. Compute break-even residents by dividing recurring expenditure by verified net per-resident contribution; round up, then apply capacity limit. Six residents may be a governance target but is not automatically the break-even point. Record landlord discussions and a relocation/closure contingency **before 21 December**.

**Future houses:** Register independent interest from women and other towns but disclose that these are **not current placements**. A distinct women's house is a possible future project only after at least six genuinely interested prospective residents can sustain it, an appropriate building and lawful resources exist, and resident governance and an authorised charter pathway are arranged. Preference clusters can be analysed in aggregate monthly; no promises to launch a particular house on a given date.

**Referral commissions:** **Do not offer per-client or rent-linked financial incentives to clinical supervisors, hospital staff or rehab employees.** They create serious potential conflicts in client placement and may contravene employer, professional or legal duties. Offer transparent shared education, public recognition with consent, non-contingent training/collaboration, and optional written institutional agreements subject to legal review. Never pay an individual for bringing a patient into a particular residence.
