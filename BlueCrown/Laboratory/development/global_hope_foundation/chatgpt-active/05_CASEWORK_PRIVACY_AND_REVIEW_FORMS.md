# Restricted internal operating forms and privacy procedures
**Release:** 1.00 | **Do not publish or collect clinical records through the public interest form.**

## A. Applicant information consent sheet — to explain before interview

**About the programme.** Hopeful Way Foundation provides information about a peer-run, self-supported, alcohol- and drug-free recovery residence for adults. The currently reported Hopeful Way house is in Ashiaman and is designed for men. A separate women's house and other locations may be considered in the future, but interest in those locations does not mean a place is available. The residence is **not a detox or medical treatment service**.

**Your choice.** You may enquire, ask questions, decline a call, withdraw interest, or seek other housing without pressure. Staff, supervisors and sponsors must not submit your details unless you freely agree. A sponsor may discuss financial support only with your permission; the sponsor does not vote on admission.

**What admission means.** A form entry is not admission. You will receive written rules and actual costs before deciding whether to continue. A preliminary conversation may be conducted by video, followed by an in-house interview and a decision by current house residents. The published Oxford House model uses an 80% affirmative vote for new members; the actual house documents govern the local process subject to law. Appropriate housing may not be available when you need it.

**House expectations.** Members ordinarily share house duties and expenses, follow safety and safeguarding policies, respect other residents and maintain an alcohol- and drug-free living environment. Testing, responses to use/relapse, return after treatment and any removal from membership are governed by current written rules and applicable law; the Foundation must explain them to you before you agree.

**Privacy.** Hopeful Way Foundation processes the contact information necessary to reply and coordinate voluntary interview/housing decisions. Its approved small intake team and, when needed, lawful house decision-makers may receive appropriate information. The data may be stored in restricted Google Form/Sheet services. You may ask for information, correction or withdrawal through `support@buzzjuice.net`. Do not send medical records by email or WhatsApp. The proposed pilot deletes non-admitted inactive enquiry records **90 days after the last meaningful contact**, only after Foundation legal/privacy approval and subject to applicable retention obligations.

**Applicant agreement:** Staff record informed agreement via the private casework process and confirm the adult's personal decision directly. Do not treat a sponsor's or supervisor's checkmark as the adult's standalone consent. Retain evidence in authorised private systems only.

## B. Preliminary interview guide (20–30 minutes)

**Preparation:** Verify the applicant wants this call and can speak privately. Confirm adult age, safe contact route and no coercion. Confirm the applicant has received the cost and house-rule brief. A video call is optional when accessibility or privacy make it unsuitable; seek an equivalent approved method.

**Start:** Explain your role, intended use of notes, the ability to skip questions or withdraw, how interview decisions work, and that this is not medical care. Use non-stigmatising language.

**Questions:**
1. What interested you in shared recovery housing at this stage?
2. What kind of living arrangement or supportive routines would help you maintain your goals?
3. What does a self-run house mean to you? How would you contribute to chores and group decision-making?
4. Do you understand the no-alcohol/no-drugs expectation and agree to review the written testing, conduct and safeguarding rules?
5. When do you expect to need accommodation? Would Ashiaman work, or are you only expressing future interest elsewhere?
6. Have you read the current house costs and what financial arrangements may be required? Would you like a separate conversation about practical affordability?
7. What questions do you have about privacy, house-mate decision-making, visitors, emergencies, complaints or referral to medical support if needed?
8. Would you like a house interview and whom, if anyone, may we include when coordinating time or financial support?

**Do not request:** Substance-use history, diagnoses, tests, medication list, personal health papers or a full discharge summary as a routine first step. If a clear clinical safety concern arises, refer to qualified clinicians according to approved policy rather than improvising a medical assessment.

**Private recorded outcome:** Adult consent confirmed / Not confirmed; House rules sent / Needs explanation; Current accommodation feasible / Future interest only; Interview offered / Deferred / Declined; Safe next contact method and date. Use case reference rather than identifiable details in coordination spreadsheets.

## C. In-house interview and voting record — controlled private checklist

1. Confirm availability and safe capacity as of the planned move-in date.
2. Give all voting members a consistent, fair set of nonclinical admission criteria and the applicant's consent to the interview.
3. Explain shared expenses, chores, residents' equal voting rights, meeting process, alcohol/drug rules, substance testing policy, safeguarding, complaints, and practical support options.
4. Interview the applicant, allow their questions and explain how a decision will be communicated.
5. Conduct the documented house membership vote using the applicable current charter/bylaws. **Oxford House generally requires 80% approval for admitting new members**. Count eligible members and votes correctly; a simple majority is not the baseline for new admission.
6. Record the vote outcome in the **secure house minutes**, not WhatsApp or a public CRM. Share only the final outcome to the intake officer and applicant through a safe channel.
7. If accepted, issue the current written cost and occupancy agreement before taking payments or promising a date. Keep funds in the approved house financial system, not personal commission accounts.
8. If deferred/not accepted, provide respectful process information and any other referrals the person requests, without disclosing other residents' private remarks.

## D. Move-in and resident induction

- Written house rules and applicable documents received; resident understands voting, governance and responsibilities.
- Actual expense share, deposit/initial payment, billing, records, and refunds explained and recorded appropriately.
- Room/bed assignment and occupancy limit checked; essential safety, emergency exits, emergency contacts and house access protocols provided.
- House meeting orientation and resident officer contacts explained; complaint and safeguarding mechanisms available to everyone.
- Personal belongings and privacy respected. No assumption of medical information or mandatory sharing with sponsors.
- Recovery and clinical services, if desired/needed, remain with qualified providers. Keep continuity plans voluntary and accessible.
- Move-in date entered into private operational record; **only aggregate occupancy** shared for campaign reporting.

## E. Withdrawal and retention SOP

1. Receive request by `support@buzzjuice.net` or the applicant's safe agreed method; verify identity without demanding unnecessary sensitive data.
2. Immediately mark the case **WITHDRAWN** and suppress contact and referrals; notify the person through an agreed safe method.
3. Determine which records may lawfully be deleted immediately vs which must be retained for a legitimate documented reason.
4. Delete/restrict as required from **Google Forms response store, linked Google Sheet, pipeline, email notifications, exports, paper notes and backups as applicable**. Document completion in a minimal non-identifying deletion log.
5. Notify authorised processors/recipients as required. Never send deletion records to an institution without the adult's authority.
6. Apply the **proposed 90-day inactive nonadmitted lead retention** only after privacy/legal approval. Resident financial and legal documents require a different record-retention schedule.

## F. Incident and safeguarding triggers

- **Suspected intoxication or medical emergency:** follow house emergency rules; obtain appropriate clinical or emergency assistance. Do not attempt unsupervised withdrawal management.
- **Threats, violence or exploitation:** prioritise immediate safety; follow safeguarding escalation and Ghanaian legal obligations. Record incident privately and restrict disclosure.
- **Actual relapse:** follow the current lawful Oxford House and house rules, while preserving dignity and helping access clinical care. No promise of automatic readmission, and no unsupported threats about police intervention.
- **Resident dispute / complaint:** use the documented democratic process, plus a safe independent safeguarding contact for disputes that cannot reasonably be handled solely by resident vote.
- **Privacy breach:** restrict access, preserve evidence, notify the responsible privacy lead promptly, assess applicable legal reporting requirements, inform affected persons where required, and revise controls.
