# HopefulWay Oxford House — Outreach and Waiting-List Action Plan (v2)

Prepared Thursday 26 November 2026 · Campaign lead: the Chairperson · Replaces the 23-point proposal

## 1. Bottom line

Your plan has the right spine: supervisors learn about the house, the person agrees to be referred, a waiting-list entry is created, a video interview follows, then the house interview and resident vote. I kept that spine. I changed six things, and each change protects either the applicants, the Foundation, or the credibility of the campaign.

1. **The applicant gives consent, not only the sponsor.** An adult's own agreement is required. A sponsor's or supervisor's agreement is an addition to it, never a substitute.
2. **First contact collects less.** Date of birth, place of birth, discharge detail and sponsor contacts move to the interview stage. Someone's recovery status is sensitive personal information under Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843), so the form asks only what is needed to call the person back.
3. **Verify before you claim.** Charter status of the Ashiaman house, current vacancies, rent, and the current house rules must be confirmed in writing before any message goes out. A wrong claim to a rehab supervisor costs more than a one-week delay.
4. **Absolutes become house-rule language.** "Immediate eviction" and "always returns after detox" become "the house rules apply, including testing and removal for substance use, and a return after detox is decided by a resident vote." That matches how an Oxford House actually works and avoids promises you may not be able to keep.
5. **Explain the model; do not attack other providers.** Point 11 of your plan (benefits over unlicensed halfway houses) becomes a plain list of what a chartered, resident-governed house gives people: a written charter, resident votes, published rules, and accountability to Oxford House, Inc.
6. **Referral commissions are deferred.** Paying staff at treatment facilities per client creates a conflict of interest and can look like buying referrals from vulnerable people. Section 9 gives you safer alternatives. This one is your decision, but I recommend not launching with it.

## 7. Stage 3 — From interest to move-in

The order never changes, and no step can be skipped by anyone, including the Chairperson.

1. **Expression of interest.** The applicant submits the form, or a supervisor helps them with the applicant present and agreeing.
2. **First call (within 24 hours).** Confirm their consent and their safe way to be reached. Confirm they are 18 or older. Say plainly that this is recovery housing, not treatment or detox, and ask whether they want to continue.
3. **Information call.** Give the facts in this order: location and gender of the house, whether a bed is actually free, the monthly contribution and any deposit, house rules including testing, how the residents vote, and that acceptance is not guaranteed. Send the current rules and cost sheet securely.
4. **Preliminary video interview (20–30 minutes).** Held with any team member, scheduled with the applicant's agreement. The applicant must be able to speak privately. Ask about: why they want this kind of home, their expected move-in date, how they would contribute to the household, whether they are ready to follow the rules and accept testing, and what support they have. Do not ask about substances used, diagnoses, medication or treatment details. Refer clinical or detox questions to a qualified provider.
5. **House interview.** Arranged a few days before a possible move-in. The residents meet the applicant and ask their own questions. Offer reasonable accessibility support.
6. **Residents' vote.** Each resident votes under the charter's threshold. Tell the applicant the result privately and respectfully. If the answer is no or not yet, say what, if anything, can happen next, and ask whether they want updates about future houses.
7. **Admission.** Only after a yes vote, a confirmed free bed and agreed costs, give the written terms, rules, emergency and safeguarding contacts and an orientation. Take no money before the terms are written and agreed.
8. **After move-in.** The house governs itself under its rules. HopefulWay keeps only the support role it is authorised to hold.

**Relapse, testing and return.** Say this to every applicant: any resident may ask for a test; a failed or refused test, or any substance use, is handled under the house rules, which can include removal from the house, with the support of local authorities if safety requires it. A resident who has relapsed and completed detox may ask to return, and the residents decide by vote. The house is not a detox or treatment service. In a medical emergency, call emergency services or go to a clinic.

## 9. Referral payments and expansion

**Commissions.** I recommend not paying supervisors, sponsors or facility staff per client, per admission or as a share of rent in this campaign. A payment for sending vulnerable people to a house you benefit from can distort discharge decisions and damage trust with the very institutions you need. If you still want something for referrers, three safer options: a flat, transparent annual partnership contribution to the institution itself rather than to individuals; reimbursement of documented costs such as transport to a presentation; or free staff training sessions. If you ever introduce a payment, get written Foundation approval, take independent legal advice, disclose it to applicants, and make sure it cannot influence admission votes.

**New locations and women's housing.** The plan's rule of at least six consistent residents is sensible. Add three conditions before promising anything: a safe, separate property; resident leadership ready to run it; and a budget that covers rent and bills if numbers dip. Until then, describe these as demand research only, which is how the form and page already word it. Co-ed houses stay unsupported.
