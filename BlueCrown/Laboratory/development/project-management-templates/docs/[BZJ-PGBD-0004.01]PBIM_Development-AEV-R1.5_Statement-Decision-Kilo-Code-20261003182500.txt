# MINOR OBSERVATIONS (non-blocking; for the fresh R1.5 AEC to probe)

These are **not** conditions (R1.5 §71: "A material unresolved finding returns the
architecture to amendment"). Neither is an *unresolved architectural defect* — both are
*correct by inference* or *design choices* that are *appropriate* for the fresh AEC to
probe, not AEV conditions.

## OBS-1 (MINOR) — Verifier-of-the-Verifier terminates at CA: implied, not explicit
R1.5 §23 (anchor verification) and §15 (matrix serialization) *both* involve a *verifier*.
The *verifier's* independence ultimately *terminates* at CA (the root of trust, §4.1),
because CA is *above* all verifiers. This is *implied* by §4.1 + §8 (CA is the root; the
verifier is below CA; a compromised CA is CA-TRUST-BOUNDARY-COMPROMISED). But §23 and §15 do
*not* *explicitly* state "the verifier's independence terminates at CA; the verifier does
not self-verify." **Fresh-AEC attack:** "Does the verifier self-verify, or does its
independence terminate at CA?" Severity: *MINOR*. Correct by inference; appropriate for the
fresh AEC to confirm explicitly.

## OBS-2 (MINOR) — Concrete-mechanism-naming pattern: verify the boundary is maintained
R1.5 *names* concrete mechanisms (§34 CI file, §56 ref format, §15/§17 transaction
sequence). This is a *testable* design choice (good for the fresh AEC to check "does the
implementation match the canonical mechanism?"), but it *slightly* blurs the
architecture/implementation boundary. §2/§67/§68 *maintain* the boundary ("shall not claim
a control is ENFORCED merely because a document describes it"). **Fresh-AEC attack:** "Does
naming a mechanism (e.g., `.github/workflows/task-scope-check.yml`) risk being *read* as a
claim that the mechanism *exists*?" Severity: *MINOR*. The boundary is *maintained* by
§68; appropriate for the fresh AEC to confirm.

## OBS-3 (MINOR) — 72-hour emergency-TTL default is arguably long
R1.5 §42 sets a *default* emergency-delegation TTL of **72 hours** (for H0 unavailability),
"unless the constitutional authority explicitly establishes a stricter limit." For an H0-
unavailability emergency, 72 hours is a *long* window for a delegate to act without
re-confirmation. A *stricter* default (e.g., 24 hours) might be safer. But R1.5 *allows CA
to set a stricter limit* (the architecture doesn't *lock in* 72 hours; it *defaults* to 72
and lets CA *tighten*). **Fresh-AEC attack:** "Is the 72-hour default too long for H0-
unavailability emergencies? Should the default be 24 hours, or should CA *mandate* a
stricter limit?" Severity: *MINOR*. The *override path* is *correct* (CA can tighten); the
*default* is a *design choice* the fresh AEC can challenge.
