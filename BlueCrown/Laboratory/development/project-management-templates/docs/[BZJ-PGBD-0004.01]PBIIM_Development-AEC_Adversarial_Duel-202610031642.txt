# 1. Purpose

This document initiates the **PBIIM Architectural Engineering Challenge Duel** against the R1.2 architecture.

The purpose of this stage is not to improve, polish, rewrite or endorse the PBIIM architecture.

The purpose is to attempt to **break it**.

Each collaborating agent shall independently attack the architecture and attempt to demonstrate that one or more of its assumptions, controls, authority boundaries, lifecycle mechanisms or scalability claims fail under realistic conditions.

The challenge must therefore seek:

* contradiction;
* bypass;
* authority collapse;
* hidden authority;
* false verification;
* circular governance;
* deadlock;
* excessive ceremony;
* insufficient ceremony;
* scalability failure;
* operational failure;
* security failure;
* recovery failure;
* implementation/architecture boundary failure.

A successful challenge is valuable even when it invalidates a desirable part of the architecture.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The AEC stage asks:

> **"Under what conditions would this architecture fail?"**

It does not ask:

> "How can this architecture be made to look better?"

Agents must therefore prefer falsification over confirmation.

A control should be attacked at its weakest point.

The collaborating agents have reviewed R1.2.

Current dispositions:

**AEV APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS — for advancement to AEC**

Kilo also retained open questions concerning operational-readiness, requirement-change thresholds, empty template stubs and H0 instantiation.

Jules considers the architectural control model complete enough to proceed to the adversarial stage, while explicitly maintaining that implementation and production are not authorized.

### GitHub/Copilot

**AEV APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS / ADVANCE**

GitHub/Copilot approves advancement to the next independent challenge stage but does not grant implementation or production authorization. It specifically emphasizes that registry, repository permissions, drift detection, Task Packet enforcement, stop/resume, challenge independence and reset mechanisms still require implementation evidence later.

These conditions are deliberately carried into this challenge.

# 4. Challenge Disposition Classes

Every significant finding must be assigned one classification:

### PASS

The challenged condition survives the attack.

### WEAKNESS

The architecture remains valid but has a meaningful weakness.

### MATERIAL RISK

The architecture may remain valid only with explicit mitigation or controlled acceptance.

### BLOCKER

The architecture cannot advance until the finding is resolved or formally dispositioned by the appropriate authority.

### ARCHITECTURAL INVALIDATION

A load-bearing architectural assumption is demonstrated to be false.

### UNKNOWN

Evidence is insufficient to determine whether the architecture survives.

### FALSE POSITIVE

The apparent failure is demonstrated not to apply.

# 6. R1.2 Maturity Model Challenge

R1.2 distinguishes:

**DESIGNED → ENFORCEABLE → ENFORCED**

and uses:

**E0 → E1 → E2 → E3 → E4**

for evidence maturity.

Attack this distinction.

### Questions

1. Can a control be classified ENFORCEABLE when the proposed enforcement mechanism itself has not been verified?
2. Can an enforcement mechanism introduce a new authority bypass?
3. Can E2 automated enforcement falsely indicate correctness?
4. Can E4 independent verification itself be circular?
5. Can a governance control become operationally dangerous even though it reaches E4?
6. Who verifies that the evidence itself has not been manipulated?
7. Can an agent manufacture E3 evidence?
8. Can E4 become ceremonial rather than genuinely independent?

Determine whether the maturity model closes the original documentation-versus-enforcement problem or merely relocates it.

# 63. Evidence Requirement

Every BLOCKER or ARCHITECTURAL INVALIDATION must include evidence.

Evidence must be classified:

* FACT;
* INFERENCE;
* ASSUMPTION;
* PROPOSAL;
* RECOMMENDATION;
* RISK;
* UNKNOWN;
* DISPUTED.

If evidence is unavailable, classify the finding as UNKNOWN rather than inventing certainty.

# 64. Challenge Outcome Rules

### All agents PASS

Proceed to AECC.

### One or more agents identify MATERIAL RISK

AEC may proceed to AECC only if the risk is:

* contained;
* explicitly accepted by the appropriate authority;
* and does not affect a load-bearing assumption.

### One or more agents identify BLOCKER

Return to AEV unless the blocker can be conclusively demonstrated to be a false positive.

### One or more agents identify ARCHITECTURAL INVALIDATION

Return to AEA or initiate Architectural Reset depending on the failed assumption.

### Agents disagree

The disagreement must be preserved.

Consensus must not be manufactured merely to advance the project.

---

# 65. Prohibition on Premature Closure

The following do not constitute AEC PASS:

* three agents agreeing;
* a majority vote;
* absence of objections;
* confidence statements;
* "looks reasonable";
* "probably safe";
* "implementation can fix it later."

A PASS requires survival of the adversarial attack.
