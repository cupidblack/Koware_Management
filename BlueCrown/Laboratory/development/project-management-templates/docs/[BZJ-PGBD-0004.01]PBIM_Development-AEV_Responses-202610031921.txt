The **PBIM Architectural Engineering Verification Statement (R1.5)** presents a **mature, well-structured governance framework** that addresses material findings from the R1.4 challenge duel. The architecture demonstrates strong foundational principles around constitutional authority, privilege separation, evidence integrity, and operational readiness.

1. **Clear Authority Hierarchy**

   - Distinguishes governance authority from technical capability

2. **Integrity & Evidence Framework**

   - Out-of-band integrity anchors to prevent single-domain compromise
   - Raw evidence retrievability for independent verification

3. **Independence & Challenge Controls**
   - Explicit I1–I4 independence dimensions with verification requirements
   - Conflict-of-interest registers for AEC reviewers
   - Protection against fabricated independence in resource-constrained projects

4. **Control Enforcement**

   - Stop/resume mechanisms with distinct authorities
   - Baseline-drift detection requirement
   - Anti-evasion provisions (e.g., architectural reset cannot hide prior findings)

5. **Risk & Release Gates**
   - Cumulative risk assessment across related changes
   - Operational readiness checklist with blocking conditions
   - "Not Applicable" rule preventing circumvention of controls

## Critical Concerns & Gaps

### **1. Implementation Gap (Acknowledged)**
The document states (Section 2):
> "Architectural specification does not constitute implementation evidence."

**Impact:** The controls are architecturally sound but their actual enforcement remains unverified. The R1.5 design cannot be declared "implemented" without Implementation Verification.

**Required Action:** Full Implementation Verification (IV) phase must validate:
- [ ] Privilege Boundary is technically enforced in identity and access systems
- [ ] Protected governance resources prevent unilateral modification
- [ ] Task-scope-check.yml workflow actually blocks out-of-scope changes
- [ ] Authority–Permission Matrix serialization is enforced
- [ ] Baseline-drift monitor operates independently of execution path
- [ ] Stop states are machine-enforced (not advisory)
- [ ] Evidence retention is technically implemented and auditable

### **2. CA Instantiation & Succession**
**Concern:** Sections 5–7 require CA identity and succession procedures but do not specify:
- Who defines CA for the BZJ-PGBD project?
- Has CA been formally appointed?
- Is a succession procedure documented in the Governance Repository?

**Status Check Required:** Verify CA record exists before project reaches `CHARTERED`.

### **3. Registry Recovery & Emergency Paths**
**Concern:** Section 19 forbids "ungoverned emergency registry" but does not detail:
- How is the last-trusted registry revision identified?
- Who independently verifies integrity?
- How long can execution remain in `REGISTRY-BLOCKED`?
- Is the recovery SLA defined?

**Risk:** Operational pressure during outages may lead to out-of-governance workarounds.

### **4. Evidence Independence for High-Assurance Work**
**Concern:** Section 26 requires "independently protected evidence environment" for compromised evidence domains, but:
- No specification of what "equivalent external verification" entails
- No timeline for establishing independent evidence paths
- Unclear how to bootstrap initial evidence collection

### **5. Emergency Delegation TTL Enforcement**
**Concern:** Section 42 specifies 72-hour TTL for H0-unavailability delegation, but:
- No mechanism described for automatic revocation
- No escalation process if H0 remains unavailable after 72 hours
- Assumes alert and workflow systems are functioning

### **6. Organizational Commitment Outside PBIM**
**Concern:** Several requirements depend on organizational-level controls beyond PBIM authority:
- CA protection (Section 6)
- Independence verification (Sections 28–30)
- Conflict-of-interest management
- Operational readiness ownership

**Risk:** PBIM cannot force organizational compliance; architectural soundness depends on org commitment.
