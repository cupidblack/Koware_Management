## 4. THE AGENTS.md ISSUE — WHY THIS IS A CONDITION, NOT AN UNRESOLVED BLOCKER

My AEC classified AGENTS.md drift as **BLK-1** (a blocker). R1.1 does not *conform* the current AGENTS.md (F4 proves it still drifts on main). So why am I not returning `AEV RETURN` on the ground that "a live blocker remains"?

Because R1.1 changes the *class* of the problem from "unresolved contradiction" to "contained contradiction pending detector":

1. **§15 gives a precedence rule that resolves the conflict for any action.** The hierarchy puts the Approved Baseline above Repository-Level AGENTS.md, and states: "If contradiction exists: the lower-level instruction is invalid for that action. The agent must stop and escalate." So even *today*, with AGENTS.md still naming Claude/Cove, an agent that encounters the contradiction is *directed by the architecture to stop and escalate*, and the baseline governs the action. The failure mode is **bounded**.

2. **§16 requires the detector (governance-drift check) that will FIND and fix the drift.** It is specified, not yet built. That is a *condition*, not an *unresolved blocker*.

3. **§16 explicitly protects the good parts:** "Existing AGENTS.md requirements concerning payment/security/secret handling remain in force unless formally superseded through controlled architectural change." This prevents the baseline from silently discarding the repo's payment-security rules — important, and it answers the concern that "baseline-wins" could erase critical safety rules.

Therefore the present-tense AGENTS.md contradiction is **contained** (bounded by §15 stop-and-escalate) and **pending a detector** (§16, to be built). That is a *condition*, not an *unresolved blocker that prevents AECC*. §39 permits `APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS` exactly when "no unresolved blocker remains and all conditions are explicit, bounded and acceptable for AECC" — and this is bounded and acceptable for AECC (AECC closes the *challenge*; the detector build is a post-closure, pre-implementation item under B-02/B-06).

**However**, I flag one *new* internal tension R1.1 introduces (this is my §38 item 4 test — "create a new architectural conflict"):

> **NEW CONFLICT N-1:** §15 places the Approved Baseline *above* AGENTS.md, so a baseline amendment would win over AGENTS.md on any conflict. But §16 says AGENTS.md payment/security/secret rules "remain in force unless formally superseded." These are in tension: if a baseline amendment conflicts with an AGENTS.md security rule, §15 says the baseline wins, §16 says the security rule stays. The document does not say which of these two overrides the other. **Recommendation (condition):** reconcile by stating that *AGENTS.md security/secret/payment-safety rules sit at the same governance level as the Approved Baseline* (or are explicitly subsumed by it), so a baseline amendment cannot silently weaken a payment-safety rule. This must be resolved at AECC.

## 5. WHERE R1.1 IS STILL NOT SUFFICIENT (my preserved dissent)

I do not suppress dissent to manufacture agreement. The following remain genuine concerns I carry into AECC:

**D-1 — "Amended" ≠ "Enforced" (the §37 overstatement).** R1.1 §37 marks all 20 AEC findings "Amended" and §35 marks all 12 LA "AMENDED — VERIFY." I object to *reading* "Amended" as "resolved/enforced." Amended means *the design now specifies the control*. It does not mean the control operates. I accept "Amended" as accurate for the *design* but dissent against any downstream document treating the §37 "Amended" column as evidence the control now works. The B-01..B-14 list is the real gate; "Amended" is not. (This is the R1.1 version of my original AEC thesis, applied to R1.1's own status table.)

**D-2 — The single-registry concurrency problem is not actually solved at scale (my MAT-6 / Attack P).** §28 adds large-project *delegation* (good — addresses the Lead-Agent bottleneck I found), but the *registry* remains a single canonical artifact with "defined write authority" (§7.1). 10+ agents editing one registry still hits the concurrency/merge-conflict failure of my Attack B. R1.1 specifies "conflict detection" and "controlled modification" but does not specify a *merge-safe or sharded* registry model. I keep this OPEN: at large-project scale the registry is the first hard serialization point. Condition: specify the registry's concurrency model (serialized writes? CRDT? per-workstream sharding with a reconciler?) before it can be trusted at scale.

**D-3 — Four material gaps I keep OPEN (from §3):** MAT-8 (no distinct operational-readiness gate), MAT-10 (no crisp requirement-change materiality threshold), MAT-11 (no explicit "no empty stub sections" rule for the operating template), MAT-15 (evidence must be *pinned to commit-hash and re-run by CI*; R1.1 only addresses secret-redaction, not reproducibility-of-test-evidence). These are real, bounded, and I list them as conditions.

**D-4 — I did not independently verify the other agents' R1.1 dispositions.** R1.1 §2.2 records Jules = "AEC PASS WITH AMENDMENTS" and Copilot = "VIABLE ONLY WITH AMENDMENT," and my own "RETURN TO AEV." Per §36 of the AEC duel I do *not* use their dispositions as evidence for mine. I reached `APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS` from my own re-measurement (F1–F6) and my own per-finding re-verification (§3). The fact that I am now *more positive* than my AEC stance is not convergence-with-the-panel; it is because R1.1 *fixed the specific defect* (overstated maturity) that drove my `RETURN`. If R1.1 had merely re-described the same controls R1.0 described, I would still be at `RETURN`.
