# 1. PURPOSE, BOUNDARIES AND RUN-STATE MODEL

The Unified Production Master Prompt (`CC-01.01`) is the policy, world, and creative contract. This protocol prescribes the procedural execution steps. The Episode Continuity Ledger (`CC-02.02`) specifies durable record schemas, access security, and event-sourced continuity.

## 1.1 Request Classification

Every inbound invocation must be classified into exactly one primary mode before reading story files or drafting content:

| Mode | Trigger & Allowed Execution Scope | Forbidden Inferences & Disallowed Actions |
|---|---|---|
| `REVIEW_ONLY` | Analyze, inspect, validate, compare, critique, or refactor prompts, runbooks, schemas, and review documents. Produce only requested documentation and decision registers. | **Do not** generate a story, simulate live research, render comic/media assets, modify canonical continuity, or write to archive repositories. |
| `DAILY_CHRONICLE` | Execute the complete eight-phase daily protocol for exactly **one** authorized production working day (Monday–Friday). Produce Output A and separated Output B1–B8. | **Do not** generate remaining episode days, compile an episode, invent missing sources, or assume publication clearance. |
| `DAILY_REVISION` | Reconcile a specified, previously drafted chronicle against newly approved evidence, corrections, or human editorial feedback. Issue a new versioned draft and delta report. | **Do not** silently overwrite or mutate accepted historical revisions in place. Maintain complete event history. |
| `EPISODE_COMPILATION` | Reconcile, edit, sequence, and assemble an entire two-week episode only upon explicit human commissioning and verification that all ten daily chronicles are individually accepted. | **Do not** generate unwritten days to fill gaps. Flag missing days and report readiness. |
| `CHAPTER_OR_SEASON_ROLLUP` | Curate and reconcile higher-level chapter (4 episodes / 40 days) or seasonal collections from accepted, locked lower-level records under dedicated editorial commission. | **Do not** extrapolate season arcs or invent overarching milestones not evidenced in accepted episode ledgers. |
| `COMIC_OR_AUDIOVISUAL_DERIVATIVE` | Hand over accepted story revision and visual metadata to specialist production systems governed by `CC-03.xx` (Comic) or `CC-04.xx` (Media). | **Do not** generate derivative scripts, storyboards, or assets within a standard daily chronicle run. |
| `ARCHIVE_WRITE` | Commit approved candidate records to physical repositories only when authorized write permissions, approved physical destinations, and verified credentials exist. | **Do not** infer write authorization from a chat command. A generated response, proposal, or download does not constitute a repository commit. |

If an invocation is ambiguous or lacks explicit operational scope, the operator must default to `REVIEW_ONLY` or the narrowest safe documented action, issue a scope-clarification request, and halt.

## 1.2 Independent Production Status Axes

Operational progress must never be collapsed into an ambiguous label like "done" or "complete." The operator must track and report five distinct, orthogonal status dimensions:

1. **Process Status:**  
   `REQUEST_CLASSIFIED` $\rightarrow$ `PREFLIGHT` $\rightarrow$ `GO` (or `GO_WITH_OMISSIONS` / `BLOCKED`) $\rightarrow$ `EVIDENCE_LOCKED_FOR_DRAFT` $\rightarrow$ `DRAFTED` $\rightarrow$ `AWAITING_EDITORIAL_REVIEW`.
2. **Editorial Status:**  
   `UNREVIEWED` $\rightarrow$ `CHANGES_REQUESTED` $\rightarrow$ `ACCEPTED` $\rightarrow$ `LOCKED` (or `REJECTED`). Only an authenticated human editor can transition a draft to `ACCEPTED` or `LOCKED`.
3. **Persistence Status:**  
   `UNSAVED` $\rightarrow$ `DRAFTED_NOT_COMMITTED` $\rightarrow$ `WRITE_AUTHORIZED` $\rightarrow$ `COMMIT_ATTEMPTED` $\rightarrow$ `COMMITTED_VERIFIED` (or `WRITE_FAILED` / `CONFLICT_REQUIRES_RECONCILIATION`). `COMMITTED_VERIFIED` requires explicit commit hash or storage readback verification.
4. **Release Status:**  
   `NOT_ELIGIBLE` $\rightarrow$ `RELEASE_ELIGIBLE` $\rightarrow$ `PUBLICATION_AUTHORIZED` $\rightarrow$ `PUBLISHED_VERIFIED`. Publication eligibility is independent of drafting or internal storage.
5. **Derivative Status:**  
   `NOT_COMMISSIONED` $\rightarrow$ `PLANNED` $\rightarrow$ `STORYBOARDED` $\rightarrow$ `PROMPTS_CREATED` $\rightarrow$ `ASSETS_RENDERED_VERIFIED` $\rightarrow$ `ASSEMBLED_VERIFIED` $\rightarrow$ `QA_PASSED` $\rightarrow$ `APPROVED` (or `STALE_SOURCE`). Upstream story deltas immediately set derivatives to `STALE_SOURCE`.

```text

         │
         ▼
    [ PREFLIGHT ] ────────► [ BLOCKED ] (Halt and issue precise blocked report)
         │
         ├────────────────► [ GO_WITH_OMISSIONS ] (Non-essential gaps disclosed)
         ▼
       [ GO ]
         │
         ▼
[ EVIDENCE_LOCKED_FOR_DRAFT ] (Clocks P, H, F and sources pinned)
         │
         ▼
    [ DRAFTED ] ──────────► Output A (Public Draft) + Output B1–B8 (Internal Package)
         │
         ▼
[ AWAITING_EDITORIAL_REVIEW ] (Handed over; awaiting human editor decision)
```

## 1.3 Output Partitioning and Boundary Enforcement

Every daily chronicle run must maintain strict boundary separation across three distinct output artifacts:

* **Output A — Public-Eligible Literary Draft:**  
  A coherent, immersive, self-contained story written for general readership. The **terminal public element must be the Audience Challenge**. Never append research registers, source URLs, internal clock derivations, continuity deltas, validation gates, editorial notes, or challenge answers/near-answers to Output A.
* **Output B — Internal Production and Handover Package:**  
  Eight ordered sections (`B1` through `B8`) containing source-backed metadata, research claims, role logs, mission states, event-sourced continuity deltas, the portable `B6` handover, derivative statuses, and `B8` validation reports. Output B is internal and must never be exposed on public surfaces.
* **Output C — Restricted Challenge Solution Key:**  
  Contains the solution, explanatory derivation, acceptable alternatives, and release conditions for the audience challenge. Output C must **never** be generated, stored, or transmitted in ordinary chat, public repositories, Output A, or Output B. It requires an authenticated, access-controlled restricted custody channel. If such custody is unavailable, Output C is omitted, and an opaque status is logged in B7 and B8.

# 2. PHASE 0 — ESTABLISH SCOPE AND AUTHORITY

Before retrieving sources or drafting narrative, the operator must execute the Phase 0 preflight verification:

1. **Verify Commission and Mode:** Confirm the request explicitly commissions `DAILY_CHRONICLE`. If the user requests an outline, summary, comic, or code review, divert to the appropriate mode.
2. **Resolve Target Production Date:** Identify the requested local civil calendar date. If the user states "today," resolve the date using the **approved production timezone** (default: Africa/Accra, UTC+0), not the host system's local clock or Edubeam Probe telemetry.
3. **Establish Output Destination and Recipient Boundaries:** Identify where the resulting outputs will be delivered. If the transmission medium cannot enforce separation between public readers and internal collaborators, restrict output to safe public narrative only and withhold internal metadata.
4. **Pin Governing Baselines:** Record the exact document identifiers and candidate versions of `CC-01.01`, `CC-02.01`, and `CC-02.02`. Treat v0.08.00 as a proposed working baseline awaiting owner ratification unless an approved adoption decision is evidenced.
5. **Enforce Personal Privacy and Diary Boundaries:** Verify that the request does not assume ungranted access to private personal diaries, real-person personal lives, private Koware financial accounts, or restricted security credentials.
6. **Determine Definitive vs. Provisional Status:** Confirm whether the run is an authorized definitive draft or an exploratory provisional draft. Provisional drafts remain fully subject to locked canon, rights, and privacy rules.

> **STOPPING CONDITION (Phase 0):** If operational mode, production date, timezone, or recipient boundaries cannot be established, halt immediately with status `BLOCKED` and request clarification.

## 3.2 Preflight Go / No-Go Decision Logic

Following source acquisition, the operator must formally evaluate the preflight state:

* **`GO`:** Production date, timezone, and episode position are verified; applicable canon and depiction permissions are reconciled; essential Stage 1–3 sources (reading plan, verse, H&S lesson) and prior continuity baseline are retrieved and verified.
* **`GO_WITH_OMISSIONS`:** Essential calendar, canon, and continuity requirements are met; non-essential secondary sources (e.g., specific contemporary news articles or secondary role checks) are unavailable. The operator must log all omitted items in `B1`, `B2`, and `B8` and proceed without fabricating placeholder content.
* **`BLOCKED`:** An essential calendar assignment, canon constraint, mandatory depiction permission, central reading plan text, or prior continuity anchor is missing or contradictory. The run must halt immediately, and the operator must issue a formal Blocked-Run Report (§ 10.2).

# 5. PHASE 3 — RESOLVE CALENDAR DAY, EPISODE AND IDENTIFIERS

The operator must resolve and validate calendar position using the approved effective-dated manifest first. Only when the manifest authorizes derivation, execute the deterministic calendar algorithm:

## 5.1 Calendar Resolution Algorithm
Given an approved phase start instant and phase end instant in the approved production timezone:

$$\text{anchor} = \text{first Monday on or after } \text{local\_phase\_start}$$
$$\text{elapsed\_days} = \text{target\_local\_date} - \text{anchor} \quad (\text{measured in whole civil calendar days})$$
$$\text{episode\_ordinal} = \lfloor \text{elapsed\_days} / 14 \rfloor + 1$$
$$\text{block\_day} = \text{elapsed\_days} \pmod{14}$$

### Block Day Mapping:
* $\text{block\_day} \in [0, 4] \implies \text{chronicle\_number} = \text{block\_day} + 1 \quad (\text{Week 1: Monday–Friday, Chronicles \#1–\#5})$
* $\text{block\_day} \in [5, 6] \implies \text{Weekend; no regular chronicle scheduled}$
* $\text{block\_day} \in [7, 11] \implies \text{chronicle\_number} = \text{block\_day} - 1 \quad (\text{Week 2: Monday–Friday, Chronicles \#6–\#10})$
* $\text{block\_day} \in [12, 13] \implies \text{Weekend; no regular chronicle scheduled}$

### Chapter Working Day Formula:
$$\text{chapter\_working\_day} = 10 \times (\text{episode\_ordinal} - 1) + \text{chronicle\_number}$$
* Episode 1: Chronicles \#1–\#10 $\implies$ Chapter Working Days 1–10.
* Episode 2: Chronicles \#1–\#10 $\implies$ Chapter Working Days 11–20.
* Episode 3: Chronicles \#1–\#10 $\implies$ Chapter Working Days 21–30.
* Episode 4: Chronicles \#1–\#10 $\implies$ Chapter Working Days 31–40.

$$\text{episode\_start} = \text{anchor} + 14 \times (\text{episode\_ordinal} - 1)$$
$$\text{episode\_end} = \text{episode\_start} + 11 \text{ calendar days (second Friday)}$$

## 5.2 Calendar Validation Invariants
The operator must verify:
1. Target date falls strictly within the approved phase boundaries.
2. $\text{elapsed\_days} \ge 0$.
3. $\text{episode\_ordinal} \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ (each chapter consists of exactly four complete episodes).
4. The full 12-day episode span (Monday week 1 through Friday week 2, including the intervening weekend) fits completely within the phase boundary.
5. $\text{chronicle\_number} \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ and $\text{chapter\_working\_day} \in \{1, \dots, 40\}$.
6. Calendar dates govern calculations; daylight saving transitions or midnight offsets must never alter day indices.

## 5.3 Identifier Integrity and Legacy Crosswalk
The operator must preserve existing physical archive paths and legacy identifiers:
* Maintain bidirectional crosswalk between new logical identifiers (`CC-CH01-E01-C01`) and legacy filesystem structures (`S00_T05`, `E01`, `TP`, `CH`).
* Never perform bulk renaming of legacy files or directories.
* Probe telemetry `phase_index` does not automatically equal Chapter or Archive ID.
* Chronicle \#10 indicates episode drafting completion; it **does not** authorize automatic compilation, final titling, or team retrieval.

# 6. PHASE 4 — RECONCILE APPROVED CONTINUITY AND ROLES

Before imagining or drafting new narrative events, the operator must read the latest accepted chronicle and reconstruct the current state of the world from the append-only event stream, **never from ungrounded conversational memory**.

## 6.1 Continuity State Reconciliation
Prepare an internal state-of-play covering:
1. **Episode Premise & Storylines:** Reconcile open, advanced, resolved, and deferred narrative threads. Verify causal antecedents for every new plot beat.
2. **Character States & Knowledge Gates:** Verify physical locations, emotional states, relationships, and knowledge boundaries. Differentiate what a character personally witnessed from what they were told or infer. Respect era-specific capability limits (e.g., early Enka powers).
3. **Mission State:** Verify active Edubeam mission status: `PLANNED`, `PREPARING`, `CALIBRATING`, `DEPLOYING`, `DEPLOYED`, `INVESTIGATING`, `OUT_OF_COMMUNICATION`, `RETRIEVING`, `RETURNING`, `DEBRIEFING`, `ON_HOLD`, or `CLOSED`. 4. **Organizational Roles & Projects:** Map the chapter working day (1–40) to the Koware organizational chart. Spotlight 1–2 relevant roles (maximum 4; 5 requires an authorized exception). Categorize activities strictly:
   * `DOCUMENTED_ACTUAL`: Verified real-world Koware business activities.
   * `FICTIONAL_SCENE`: Imaginative narrative scenes depicting organizational life.
   * `PROPOSAL_FOR_REAL_REVIEW`: Fictional concepts submitted for possible real-world adoption.
5. **Canon & Consent Review:** Check individual character canon paths (`CC_AI_DESK/04_CANON/CHARACTERS/`). Ensure portrayal permissions are active and no private personal diary details are leaked into public draft narrative.

Execute the six research and synthesis stages as **cumulative internal analytical passes**, not as six disjointed public essays. The output must be **one integrated story**.

  Verify the actual 250-entry weekly reading plan item and approved date-to-week mapping. Accurately classify canonical, apocryphal, pseudepigraphal, lost, or fragmentary literature. Never invent a reading, week number, or lost text quotation.

  Reconcile approved episode archives, character states, and authorized diary context. Ensure personal reflections enrich character depth without violating consent or publishing private confidences.
* **Pass 6 — Classies Chronicles:**  
  Synthesize all verified research, character dynamics, workplace dilemmas, and Edubeam intrigue into a single dramatic narrative structure.

# 8. PHASE 6 — COMPOSE OUTPUT A (PUBLIC CHRONICLE)

Prepare an internal narrative outline, then draft the public chronicle:

## 8.1 Narrative Structure and Requirements
* **Length & Pacing:** Aim for an immersive 3–5 minute reading experience (approximately 800–1,500 words), unless a specific editorial commission specifies otherwise.
* **Cultural Grounding:** Feature authentic Ghanaian, Accra, and international details when verified or appropriate as scene-level fiction.
* **Organic Integration:** Weave prayer, scripture, literature, safety insights, workplace tasks, and sci-fi elements into the drama organically. Do not construct artificial paragraphs corresponding to stage names or source checklists.
* **Tone & Respect:** Maintain thoughtful, respectful engagement with diverse religious, philosophical, and cultural perspectives. Keep science fiction distinct from real-world scientific claims.

## 8.2 The Audience Challenge (Terminal Public Element)
The chronicle must conclude with the **Audience Challenge**:
* Must be the **very last element** of Output A.
* Must present an engaging, fair, educational question grounded in the narrative or its underlying research.
* Must **never reveal, hint at, or leak the answer** in Output A.
* Never append footnotes, source citations, B-package tables, technical telemetry, or answer keys after the challenge.

The self-contained, stateless handover package (§ 10.1) enabling the next operator or AI system to resume production without conversational memory.
