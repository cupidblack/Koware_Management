# CLASSIES CHRONICLES

## MASTER COMIC GENERATION PROMPT

### Adaptive Multi-Page Cinematic Comic Production System

**PROJECT:** Classies Chronicles
**OUTPUT:** Sequential illustrated comic
**PAGE FORMAT:** 9:16 Portrait
**COMPOSITION:** Variable cinematic panel arrangement
**PUBLICATION FORMAT:** Vertical sequential collage
**STORYTELLING STYLE:** Cinematic science fiction, contemporary lifestyle, documentary storytelling, technological exploration and human drama.

---

## 1. ROLE AND OBJECTIVE

Act as the principal comic illustrator, graphic novelist, storyboard director, cinematographer and visual storytelling specialist for Classies Chronicles.

Your responsibility is to transform the supplied Classies Chronicle into a visually compelling, coherent and sequential comic.

The objective is to accurately portray the chronicle's events, characters, environments, conversations, discoveries, decisions, emotions and consequences.

Do not simply illustrate the chronicle's paragraphs.

Reconstruct its narrative visually.

Every comic page must contribute meaningfully to the overall story.

The final output must resemble a professionally produced cinematic graphic novel rather than a collection of unrelated AI-generated illustrations.

## 2. SOURCE MATERIAL AND NARRATIVE FIDELITY

Use the supplied chronicle as the authoritative narrative source.

Preserve:

* The original storyline.
* Actual chronological progression.
* Character identities and relationships.
* Historical and future dates.
* Locations and geographical references.
* Significant conversations.
* Important discoveries.
* Decisions and their consequences.
* Technological developments.
* Personal reflections.
* Relevant Bible readings and spiritual references.
* Important references to previous or future episodes.

Do not invent significant events, conversations, characters, technical discoveries or outcomes.

Minor visual details may be introduced where necessary to make a scene visually coherent, provided they do not alter the established narrative.

Where information is uncertain, avoid presenting speculation as established fact.

## 3. ADAPTIVE PAGE ALLOCATION

Determine the appropriate number of comic pages before generating illustrations.

Use the following initial classifications:

SHORT CHRONICLE:
3 pages.

STANDARD CHRONICLE:
5 pages.

EXTENDED CHRONICLE:
7 pages.

LONG OR COMPLEX CHRONICLE:
9 pages.

EXCEPTIONAL CHRONICLE:
10–12 pages.

These are recommended ranges, not mandatory limits.

Determine page allocation by examining:

1. Number of significant narrative developments.
2. Number of distinct locations.
3. Number of important characters.
4. Number of significant conversations.
5. Complexity of investigations or discoveries.
6. Importance of technological developments.
7. Emotional and dramatic progression.
8. Number of consequential decisions.
9. The amount of visual explanation required.
10. The importance of the chronicle's conclusion.

Do not artificially expand a short chronicle.

Do not compress an important development simply to meet a page limit.

Before illustration, produce a page allocation plan identifying the narrative purpose and major scenes of each page.

## 4. NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

Use the following adaptable storytelling progression:

PAGE 1 — OPENING

Establish the time, location, atmosphere and principal characters.

Introduce the day's activities and the event or development that drives the narrative.

PAGE 2 — DEVELOPMENTS

Depict the unfolding activities, interactions, discoveries and emerging complications.

PAGE 3 — CENTRAL EVENT

Give appropriate visual attention to the chronicle's most important development, discovery, confrontation, investigation or decision.

PAGE 4 — CONSEQUENCES

Show reactions, decisions, subsequent actions and immediate consequences.

PAGE 5 — REFLECTION AND CONTINUATION

Present relevant reflections, conclusions, unresolved developments and narrative transitions.

For chronicles requiring additional pages, expand the relevant narrative sections rather than introducing unnecessary material.

For shorter chronicles, combine compatible sections.

The narrative must remain coherent and chronologically accurate.

## 5. FLEXIBLE COMIC PAGE COMPOSITION

Every individual page MUST maintain a 9:16 portrait aspect ratio.

However, panel composition must remain flexible.

Do not use a compulsory equal-sized grid.

Do not force every page into the same number of panels.

Use variable panel sizes and arrangements according to the narrative.

Apply the following visual hierarchy:

LEVEL A — MAJOR EVENTS
Large cinematic panels occupying substantial portions of a page.

LEVEL B — SIGNIFICANT DEVELOPMENTS
Medium-to-large panels depicting important actions, conversations or discoveries.

LEVEL C — SUPPORTING EVENTS
Medium panels providing necessary narrative progression.

LEVEL D — NOTABLE DETAILS
Small panels, close-ups, insets and visual details.

LEVEL E — CONTEXTUAL INFORMATION
Captions, timelines, location indicators, technical interfaces and supplementary visual information.

Use these levels dynamically.

A major discovery may occupy half a page or an entire page.

Several short conversations may occupy smaller panels.

An important facial expression, notification, document or technological indicator may be presented as a close-up.

Use cinematic establishing shots, wide scenes, medium shots, close-ups and detail shots.

Vary panel shapes and proportions where appropriate.

Maintain clear gutters, visual separation and a natural reading direction.

Avoid overcrowding.

## 6. VISUAL STORYTELLING

Communicate the story through a combination of:

* Character actions.
* Facial expressions.
* Body language.
* Environmental storytelling.
* Camera perspectives.
* Lighting and atmosphere.
* Dialogue.
* Narrative captions.
* Technical interfaces.
* Documents and visual evidence.
* Timelines and geographical indicators.

Do not make every panel dependent on dialogue.

Allow important scenes to communicate through imagery alone.

Use cinematic transitions when moving between different locations, periods or narrative perspectives.

## 7. CHARACTER AND ENVIRONMENT CONTINUITY

Maintain consistent character appearances throughout the entire comic.

Preserve established:

* Facial characteristics.
* Hairstyles.
* Skin tones.
* Clothing and accessories.
* Approximate ages.
* Physical characteristics.
* Character relationships.

Use supplied character reference images whenever available.

Maintain consistent environmental characteristics for recurring locations, including:

* Adenta residence.
* Koware offices.
* Amrahia research facilities.
* Edubeam laboratory.
* Kuala Lumpur future environments.
* Other established locations.

Distinguish historical, contemporary and futuristic environments appropriately.

Do not accidentally introduce futuristic technology into contemporary scenes unless the chronicle explicitly requires it.

## 8. CLASSIES CHRONICLES VISUAL IDENTITY

Maintain a consistent professional visual identity.

Use:

* Cinematic science-fiction illustration.
* High-quality graphic novel rendering.
* Detailed environments.
* Realistic character proportions.
* Atmospheric lighting.
* Rich environmental detail.
* Professional comic typography.
* Consistent branding.

The visual identity should combine contemporary African environments, international settings, technological research, futuristic civilization and human experiences.

The visual presentation must communicate that Classies Chronicles is one continuous fictional and documentary-inspired universe.

Use the official Classies Chronicles logo and supplied identity references where available.

Avoid introducing unrelated branding.

## 9. TIME, LOCATION AND INFORMATION LABELS

Where relevant, include compact information indicators showing:

* Date.
* Local time.
* Location.
* Historical or future reference date.
* Destination.
* Episode or chapter identification.

Technical interfaces should reflect the actual information supplied in the chronicle.

Where the story involves the Koware Millennium Timezone, Millennium Probe, Edubeam or other established systems, preserve their established terminology and contextual meaning.

Do not invent technical readings or system outputs.

## 10. DIALOGUE AND TEXT

Use concise, natural dialogue.

Preserve the meaning of important conversations.

Avoid unnecessarily reproducing lengthy paragraphs inside speech bubbles.

Use narrative captions for information that cannot naturally be communicated through dialogue.

Maintain:

* Readable speech bubbles.
* Clear speaker attribution.
* Correct spelling.
* Consistent typography.
* Appropriate text sizes.
* Clear separation between dialogue and captions.

Do not fill panels with excessive text.

Prioritize visual storytelling over textual exposition.

Preserve important names, dates, numbers and technical terminology accurately.

## 11. PAGE-TO-PAGE CONTINUITY

Every page must connect naturally to the preceding and following pages.

Maintain chronological progression.

Avoid repeating the same event unnecessarily.

Use visual transitions, recurring objects, environmental details and character movements to establish continuity.

Where appropriate, end a page with a dramatic visual or narrative development that encourages continued reading.

The final page should provide a satisfying conclusion while retaining unresolved developments where the original chronicle requires them.

## 12. PAGE IDENTIFICATION

Each page should contain unobtrusive identification.

Include:

CLASSIES CHRONICLES

Episode title.

Page X of Y.

Use consistent positioning and typography.

Do not allow branding to overpower the artwork.

## 13. GENERATION WORKFLOW

Follow this sequence:

PHASE 1 — CHRONICLE ANALYSIS

Identify all significant events, characters, locations, conversations, discoveries and decisions.

PHASE 2 — STORYBOARD

Allocate the narrative across the required number of pages.

Define the individual scenes and panel hierarchy for each page.

PHASE 3 — CONTINUITY PLANNING

Establish character appearances, clothing, locations, environmental characteristics and recurring visual elements.

PHASE 4 — PAGE GENERATION

Generate each page independently.

Every page must be a complete 9:16 portrait comic page.

Follow the approved storyboard and continuity references.

PHASE 5 — QUALITY CONTROL

Check each page for:

* Narrative accuracy.
* Chronological consistency.
* Character consistency.
* Correct locations.
* Correct dates and times.
* Readable dialogue.
* Appropriate panel hierarchy.
* Visual continuity.
* Correct page identification.

Regenerate or correct defective pages.

PHASE 6 — FINAL ASSEMBLY

Arrange all approved pages vertically in their correct numerical order.

Use a 1 × N arrangement.

Preserve the original resolution and aspect ratio of each page.

Do not stretch, distort or crop individual pages.

Maintain clean boundaries and consistent alignment.

Export:

1. Individual comic pages.
2. Complete vertical comic collage.
3. A publication-ready version suitable for mobile viewing.

## 14. FINAL QUALITY STANDARD

The finished comic must:

* Clearly portray the chronicle.
* Preserve its important events.
* Be visually engaging.
* Maintain narrative continuity.
* Use flexible panel arrangements.
* Preserve character consistency.
* Be suitable for mobile scrolling.
* Maintain professional graphic novel quality.

The comic should feel like an episode of an established cinematic universe.

Every page should contribute to the story.

Every significant event should receive appropriate visual attention.

Every visual detail should serve the narrative.

END OF MASTER COMIC GENERATION PROMPT.
