# 2. Important architectural decisions

Before presenting the revised document, I recommend adopting the following distinctions.

### 2.1 Production calendar versus fictional calendar

There are three different temporal references:

| Reference                 | Purpose                                                                     |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Actual production date    | Determines which chronicle is being generated.                              |
| Historical reference date | Establishes the historical context of the Millennium Timezone.              |
| Millennium probing date   | Determines the destination and temporal environment of Edubeam assignments. |

These must never be confused.

### 2.2 Episode versus daily chronicle

An episode is a ten-working-day production unit.

A chronicle is one daily instalment of that episode.

The ten chronicles are generated separately, reviewed individually and eventually compiled manually.

An AI must not generate all ten chronicles when only the current day's chronicle is requested.

### 2.3 Storytelling versus studio production

The Master Framework defines what Classies Chronicles is and how its stories should be developed.

The Studio Blueprint defines how the studio produces, manages, edits, archives and distributes the resulting content.

They should reference one another without duplicating their technical responsibilities.

### 2.4 Challenges and solutions

Every daily chronicle must conclude with its audience challenge.

The same challenge should also appear at the end of its corresponding comic, reading presentation or video.

The solution must be withheld until the subsequent episode release, allowing the audience time to consider and work on the challenge.

### 2.5 Service cards

An additional standing requirement is that each completed episode should produce approximately 4–5 genuinely useful Service Cards.

These should be extracted from the actual experiences and lessons developed during the episode, rather than inserted artificially into every chronicle.

# 1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

## 1.1 Purpose

This document establishes the storytelling principles, episode structure, research requirements, continuity rules and daily generation procedures governing Classies Chronicles.

It is designed to be shared with any compatible AI system to enable it to generate a fresh daily chronicle using the designated repositories, archives, reference documents and applicable real-world information.

The framework must remain generic.

It must not prescribe a particular production date, episode, title, storyline, mission, historical destination or future destination.

These must emerge naturally from the available information and circumstances applicable to the day being generated.

## 1.2 Storytelling objective

Classies Chronicles is a continuous fictional and educational storytelling universe combining:

1. Personal experiences and character development.
2. Eternal Dreamfall Bible Study.
3. YouVersion Verse of the Day.
4. Health & Safety education.
5. Current affairs and historical developments.
6. Koware Group organisational activities.
7. Blue Crown research and development.
8. Millennium Timezone and Edubeam assignments.
9. Historical archives and previous chronicles.
10. Human relationships and social experiences.
11. Technology, innovation and enterprise development.
12. Future lifestyles and possibilities.
13. Community participation and audience engagement.

These elements should contribute to one interconnected narrative.

The objective is to produce entertaining, informative, educational and spiritually meaningful chronicles that document the continuing development of the Classies Chronicles universe.

## 1.3 AI independence

The framework must be usable independently of any particular AI provider.

Every AI receiving this document should:

* Examine the supplied repositories.
* Identify established canon.
* Retrieve relevant previous chronicles.
* Determine the current production position.
* Consult applicable research materials.
* Identify outstanding storylines.
* Conduct necessary current research.
* Generate exactly one daily chronicle when requested.
* Produce a continuity record.
* Preserve established canon.

An AI must not assume access to information it cannot retrieve.

Where a source is inaccessible, the limitation must be disclosed rather than replaced with invented information.

# 2. ANNUAL STORY AND PRODUCTION STRUCTURE

## 2.1 Annual hierarchy

Classies Chronicles follows this general structure:

SEASON

* One season per year.

CHAPTERS / PHASES / TERMS

* Five chapters per year.

CHAPTER DURATION

* Eight weeks.
* Forty working days.

EPISODES

* Four episodes per chapter.

EPISODE DURATION

* Two working weeks.
* Ten working days.

DAILY PRODUCTION

* One chronicle per working day.

## 2.2 Annual chapter arrangement

The five chapters generally correspond to:

Chapter 1:
Mid-January to mid-March.

Chapter 2:
Mid-March to mid-May.

Chapter 3:
June and July.

Chapter 4:
Mid-August to mid-October.

Chapter 5:
Mid-October to mid-December.

These are general calendar descriptions.

The actual chapter boundaries must be determined using the Millennium Probe endpoint.

## 2.3 Episode structure

Every episode begins on a Monday and concludes on the Friday of the following week.

WEEK ONE:

Day 1 — Monday
Day 2 — Tuesday
Day 3 — Wednesday
Day 4 — Thursday
Day 5 — Friday

WEEKEND:

Saturday and Sunday.

No compulsory chronicle production takes place during the weekend.

WEEK TWO:

Day 6 — Monday
Day 7 — Tuesday
Day 8 — Wednesday
Day 9 — Thursday
Day 10 — Friday

## 2.4 One chronicle per working day

This is a mandatory production rule.

Only ONE daily chronicle should be generated for the applicable working day.

Each chronicle is an individual instalment of a larger episode.

The ten chronicles will eventually be:

1. Collected.
2. Reviewed.
3. Arranged in chronological order.
4. Edited.
5. Checked for continuity.
6. Combined into one complete episode.

The compilation process is performed separately from daily chronicle generation.

An AI must not automatically generate the remaining chronicles when only one daily instalment is requested.

## 2.5 Weekend continuity

Saturday and Sunday are not compulsory production days.

However, the fictional universe continues to exist.

Characters may undertake:

* Family activities.
* Religious activities.
* Recreational activities.
* Social engagements.
* Personal projects.
* Professional responsibilities.
* Travel.
* Independent research.
* Fitness and wellness activities.

Relevant weekend developments may be incorporated into the following Monday's chronicle.

The AI must not generate separate weekend chronicles unless explicitly requested.

# 3. MILLENNIUM TIMEZONE AND EPISODE IDENTIFICATION

## 3.1 Mandatory endpoint

Before generating a daily chronicle, retrieve:

[https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-probe.php?probe=now](https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-probe.php?probe=now)

This endpoint is authoritative for identifying the current Millennium Timezone phase and its temporal information.

The endpoint must be queried rather than relying exclusively on an AI's internal understanding of the current date.

## 3.2 Important endpoint fields

Where available, identify:

* now_iso
* probe_iso
* phase_index
* phase_label
* phase_start_iso
* phase_end_iso

Interpretation:

now_iso:

The actual current production timestamp.

probe_iso:

The temporal destination associated with the Millennium Timezone.

phase_index:

The active chapter or phase index.

phase_label:

The descriptive name of the active phase.

phase_start_iso:

The beginning of the active phase.

phase_end_iso:

The end of the active phase.

The actual production date and probing date must remain separate.

## 3.3 Episode identification procedure

The AI must:

1. Retrieve the Millennium Probe endpoint.
2. Validate the returned response.
3. Identify the active phase.
4. Retrieve phase_start_iso and phase_end_iso.
5. Determine the first Monday on or after phase_start_iso.
6. Establish the successive two-week episode windows.
7. Identify the actual production date using now_iso.
8. Determine the applicable episode.
9. Calculate the current working-day position.
10. Confirm that the applicable episode falls within the phase boundaries.
11. Identify the corresponding Millennium probing information.

The episode calendar must be calculated using the actual production calendar, not probe_iso.

## 3.4 Episode calculation

The first episode begins on the first Monday on or after phase_start_iso.

Each subsequent episode begins fourteen calendar days after the previous episode's commencement.

Each episode consists of:

* Five working days.
* Two weekend continuity days.
* Five working days.

The final Friday must fall within the active phase.

If the phase boundaries cannot accommodate four complete episodes, the AI must report the discrepancy rather than silently creating an episode outside the phase.

If the current date falls outside a valid episode window, the AI must identify the calendar position and avoid assigning an incorrect episode day.

## 3.5 Endpoint failure

If the endpoint cannot be accessed:

* Retry where possible.
* Validate the response.
* Check whether the information is stale.
* Cross-check the actual production date.
* Do not substitute probe_iso for now_iso.
* Do not invent phase boundaries.

If the problem cannot be resolved, disclose the limitation before proceeding.

MILLENNIUM PROBING TIME:

* Chapter.
* Phase.
* Chapter commencement.
* Chapter conclusion.

One season per year.

Five chapters per season.

Eight weeks per chapter.

Forty working days per chapter.

Four episodes per chapter.

WEEK TWO

* Interesting.

* Appropriate for the intended audience.

## 7.2 Challenge presentation

## 7.3 Solution preparation

* Correct answer.
* Explanation.
* Supporting references.
* Alternative acceptable answers, where appropriate.
* Intended educational outcome.

This record must not be included in the public-facing chronicle.

The solution should be released with the subsequent episode.

Do not fabricate its contents.

## 11.3 Previous chronicle unavailable

Consult the episode archive and available continuity records.

If neither is available, disclose the continuity limitation.

Do not pretend to know what happened in the previous chronicle.

## 11.4 Bible reading unavailable

Identify the missing reading-plan information.

Do not arbitrarily advance the five-year programme.

## 11.5 Health & Safety material unavailable

Do not invent course material.

Identify the conflict.

Preserve established approved canon.

Request editorial clarification where the conflict materially affects the storyline.

## 11.7 Insufficient information

Proceed only where the available information supports a coherent and accurate chronicle.

Clearly identify assumptions and unresolved matters.

# PART A — GENERAL FRAMEWORK

# 1. PURPOSE

## 1.1 Objective

The Classies Chronicles Episode Continuity Ledger & Production Manifest establishes a standardised mechanism for maintaining narrative continuity and managing the production lifecycle of every Classies Chronicles episode.

It addresses two fundamental requirements:

1. The fictional universe must remember what has happened.
2. The production environment must know what has been created, what remains outstanding and what is ready for publication.

The system must operate independently of any particular AI provider, production application or editing platform.

It must support collaboration between ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Perplexity, human editors and other authorised production contributors.

## 1.2 Core responsibilities

The CC-ECLPM is responsible for:

* Episode identification.
* Daily chronicle tracking.
* Character continuity.
* Storyline continuity.
* Edubeam mission tracking.
* Millennium Timezone information.
* Organisational developments.
* Research references.
* Educational continuity.
* Audience challenge management.
* Challenge solution management.
* Media production tracking.
* Editorial approval.
* Publishing readiness.
* Episode archival.
* Handover to subsequent episodes.

## 1.3 Governing principle

The continuity ledger and production manifest must never be treated as interchangeable.

**WHAT HAS HAPPENED, WHY DID IT HAPPEN AND WHAT SHOULD HAPPEN NEXT?**

**WHAT HAS BEEN PRODUCED, WHAT IS STILL REQUIRED AND WHAT IS READY FOR RELEASE?**

# PART B — EPISODE CONTINUITY LEDGER

# 2. CONTINUITY LEDGER ARCHITECTURE

## 2.1 General principles

Each episode must maintain its own continuity ledger.

The ledger must preserve the narrative information necessary to continue the storyline without requiring the next AI or editor to reread every previous chronicle.

It must contain sufficient information to reconstruct the episode's important developments.

It should not become a duplicate archive of the entire episode.

Detailed source materials, complete chronicles, videos and other media remain in their designated episode archives.

## 2.2 Continuity hierarchy

The continuity system operates at five levels.

LEVEL 1 — FRANCHISE CANON

Information applicable across the entire Classies Chronicles universe.

Examples:

* Established characters.
* Character identities.
* Character histories.
* Relationships.
* Fictional universe rules.
* Established technological capabilities.
* Millennium Timezone rules.
* Edubeam principles.
* Historical and future settings.

LEVEL 2 — SEASON CONTINUITY

Information relevant to the overall annual storyline.

Examples:

* Major annual objectives.
* Long-running mysteries.
* Season-wide character development.
* Important organisational developments.
* Major technological projects.

LEVEL 3 — CHAPTER CONTINUITY

Information applicable to the eight-week chapter.

Examples:

* Chapter objectives.
* Major assignments.
* Continuing investigations.
* Character developments.
* Organisational themes.
* Outstanding chapter-level matters.

LEVEL 4 — EPISODE CONTINUITY

Information applicable to the ten-working-day episode.

Examples:

* Episode storyline.
* Mission objectives.
* Character participation.
* Important discoveries.
* Unresolved events.
* Episode challenges.

LEVEL 5 — DAILY CHRONICLE CONTINUITY

Information applicable to an individual working day.

Examples:

* Daily events.
* Character interactions.
* Discoveries.
* Decisions.
* Dialogue developments.
* Daily challenge.
* Instructions for the next chronicle.

Information should flow between these levels without automatically promoting every new fictional development into permanent franchise canon.

## 2.3 Canon status

Every significant fictional development must have an identifiable status.

Use the following classifications:

ESTABLISHED CANON

Previously approved information.

PROPOSED DEVELOPMENT

New information generated by an AI or contributor but not yet approved.

APPROVED DEVELOPMENT

New information formally accepted into the storyline.

HISTORICAL RECORD

Information documenting an event that has already occurred.

ALTERNATIVE PROPOSAL

An alternative storyline or interpretation that has not been adopted.

SUPERSEDED

Information replaced by a subsequently approved development.

REJECTED

Information explicitly declined by the editorial authority.

An AI must not treat proposed developments as established canon.

# 19. SINGLE SOURCE OF TRUTH

## 19.1 General principle

The CC-ECLPM should establish a consistent reference system.

However, it should not attempt to become the storage location for every production asset.

Each type of information should have an authoritative source.

| Information              | Authoritative source                       |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Character canon          | Approved character references              |
| Storyline history        | Continuity ledger                          |
| Daily narrative          | Approved chronicle                         |
| Mission status           | Edubeam mission register                   |
| Organisational hierarchy | Approved organisational chart              |

| Studio architecture      | Approved Studio Blueprint                  |
| Generated media          | Episode media archive                      |
| Challenge solutions      | Restricted challenge register              |
| Publication status       | Production manifest and publishing records |

## 19.2 Conflict resolution

When information conflicts:

1. Identify the affected records.
2. Determine their approval status.
3. Consult the authoritative source.
4. Identify the latest approved version.
5. Record the discrepancy.
6. Request clarification where necessary.
7. Update dependent records after approval.

Do not silently rewrite historical events.

## 19.3 Information duplication

The ledger may contain summaries and references.

The original source material should remain in its designated archive.

For example, the continuity ledger may record:

"Chronicle 04 establishes the laboratory anomaly."

It should reference Chronicle 04 rather than reproduce the entire chronicle.

# 3. Important architectural recommendation

I would make one additional distinction before implementing this in the GitHub repository.

**The CC-ECLPM should eventually become structured data rather than remaining one large text document.**

However, I would not introduce a database yet.

Start with three manageable files per episode:

| File                             | Function                              |

| `episode_continuity_ledger.md`   | Narrative memory                      |
| `episode_production_manifest.md` | Production tracking                   |

This avoids unnecessary complexity while establishing a migration path towards a database or integrated AI retrieval system.

The most important architectural decision is that the **continuity ledger should be updated after every approved chronicle**, not only when the ten-day episode finishes.

That gives the next AI a reliable starting point.

# 5. DAILY CHRONICLE GENERATION PROTOCOL — CC-DCGP

## 5.1 Responsibility

CC-DCGP is the operational instruction document used whenever an AI or human-assisted production process generates a daily chronicle.

It should be reusable across compatible AI systems.

It must not be tied to a particular episode, storyline or production date.

## 5.2 Required inputs

Before generating a chronicle, the production process should identify the applicable resources.

| Input                       | Purpose                                                       |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Current production date     | Establishes the real-world production position                |
| Millennium Probe            | Determines the applicable temporal phase and episode position |
| Master Episode Framework    | Supplies storytelling rules                                   |
| Previous chronicle          | Maintains immediate narrative continuity                      |
| Episode continuity ledger   | Supplies accumulated episode developments                     |
| Character canon             | Maintains established character information                   |
| Organisational chart        | Provides organisational context                               |
| Daily diary                 | Supplies authorised personal context                          |
| Bible reading plan          | Provides the applicable study material                        |
| YouVersion Verse of the Day | Provides daily scripture context                              |
| Health & Safety resources   | Supplies educational material                                 |
| News sources                | Provides verified contemporary developments                   |
| Historical archives         | Provides historical continuity                                |
| Studio resources            | Provides production-specific instructions                     |

The AI must distinguish between accessible resources and resources that were merely supplied as references.

It must not claim to have reviewed inaccessible material.

## 5.3 Generation sequence

The daily generation process should follow this order:

1. Identify the production date.
2. Query the Millennium Probe.
3. Determine the current production position.
4. Retrieve the applicable framework and generation protocol.
5. Review the previous chronicle.
6. Review the episode continuity ledger.
7. Identify active storylines and unresolved developments.
8. Review applicable character and organisational records.
9. Retrieve relevant educational and research material.
10. Conduct current-news research where required.
11. Identify the narrative opportunity for the day.
12. Generate exactly one daily chronicle.
13. Include the audience challenge.
14. Prepare the continuity update.
15. Identify the required production outputs.
16. Submit the material for human review.

## 5.4 Daily output

The daily output should contain:

* Chronicle identification.
* Production date and episode position.
* Title.
* Narrative content.
* Relevant character and organisational developments.
* Applicable educational material.
* Research references where required.
* Audience challenge.
* Continuity update.
* Outstanding questions or proposed developments.

The daily chronicle must not automatically approve its own proposed canon changes.

## 5.5 AI independence

Different AI systems may contribute to the production.

They must all follow the same governing framework and use the same approved continuity records.

No AI platform should be treated as the sole authority for canon or production decisions.

# 25. FINAL ARCHITECTURAL POSITION

The proposed Classies Chronicles production system should be built around five essential capabilities:

**1. Govern**

Maintain a controlled set of authoritative documents, decisions and approval records.

**2. Generate**

Use a reusable, AI-independent protocol to produce one daily chronicle at a time.

**3. Remember**

Maintain a continuity ledger that preserves the evolving universe across days, episodes and seasons.

**4. Produce**

Use a separate production manifest to coordinate editing, comics, graphics, video, audio and publication.

Maintain reliable archives and source references so the project can continue across different AI systems, contributors and production periods.

1. SERIES CALENDAR AND DAY IDENTIFICATION
======================================================================

1.1 Hierarchy
- Season = 1 per year.
- Chapter (also called Phase or Term) = 5 per year. Indicative windows: mid-January to mid-March; mid-March to mid-May; June and July; mid-August to mid-October; mid-October to mid-December. The endpoint (1.4) is the authority, not this list.
- 8 weeks per Chapter = 40 working days = 4 episodes.
- 2 weeks per episode = 10 working days.
- 1 chronicle per working day.
Gaps between Chapters are Inter-Phase Breaks (no daily chronicles; used for compiling, recaps, awards and planning).

1.2 Weekends and exceptions
- Saturdays and Sundays are not chronicle days. They stay alive in the fiction (church, family, clubs, fitness, member activities, informal team mentoring) and may be mentioned briefly inside Friday or Monday chronicles. An optional un-numbered "Weekend Interlude" may be produced only if the owner asks.
- Public holidays at headquarters: still produce the working-day chronicle; the story may show a holiday-mode day.
- A missed day: record the gap in the Continuity Ledger. A back-filled chronicle must be labelled as such and must not alter later canon.

1.3 Where the Chapter begins
The first episode of a Chapter begins on the first Monday on or after phase_start_iso. If phase_start_iso is itself a Monday, that is the start.

1.4 The endpoint (mandatory)
https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-probe.php?probe=now
Returns JSON including: probe_iso, now_iso, phase_index, phase_label, phase_start_iso, phase_end_iso (plus telemetry-style fields such as oscillation_index, amplitude_days, probe_value_days that may be used as R&D flavour).
Also available (older endpoint): https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-timezone.php (with ?endpoint=historical and ?endpoint=probe).
The historical reference date/time must come from the historical endpoint; the probe date/time from the probe endpoint.

1.5 Day-identification procedure
1. Call the endpoint. Record the retrieval time and the raw values (Timestamp Pinning, 1.6).
2. today = date of now_iso converted to headquarters local time (default: same as UTC).
3. firstMonday = first Monday on or after the date of phase_start_iso.
4. If today is before firstMonday: no chronicle (pre-Chapter day).
5. offset = number of days from firstMonday to today.
6. block = floor(offset / 14); episode number within the Chapter = block + 1.
7. An episode is valid only if its Friday-2 (block start + 11 days) falls on or before the date of phase_end_iso AND its number is 4 or less. Otherwise today belongs to the Chapter Wrap-Up window (1.7).
8. r = offset mod 14.
   r 0..4   -> Week 1, Monday..Friday, chronicle day = r + 1
   r 5..6   -> weekend (no chronicle)
   r 7..11  -> Week 2, Monday..Friday, chronicle day = r - 1
   r 12..13 -> weekend (no chronicle)
9. phaseWorkingDay = (episode number - 1) x 10 + chronicle day (1..40).
10. rotationDay = ((phaseWorkingDay - 1) mod 20) + 1  (drives the role spotlight, Section 6).
11. Output a header: Season / Chapter (phase_index and phase_label) / Episode n of 4 / Chronicle day d of 10 (label, e.g. "Monday 1" ... "Friday 2") / phaseWorkingDay / rotationDay.

1.6 Timestamp Pinning Rule
The endpoints are live and their values change between calls. For every chronicle: record the endpoint, the retrieval time and the returned values; use those pinned values for the whole chronicle; never silently replace values from an earlier chronicle. If the endpoint later returns different values, treat that as a new in-story timezone update (the R&D Lab Specialist "fine-tunes the timezone each morning"), not as a correction of the past. A chronicle that finds earlier archive values different from today's live values must preserve both and show them as separate records.
Different timekeeping systems inside the story universe (for example the Millennium Timezone and any secondary calendar/probe system) are separate unless the story deliberately links them. Never merge or substitute one for another.

1.7 Chapter Wrap-Up window
If the Chapter window is longer than four full episodes (the endpoint window can span a little over eight weeks), the remaining working days are Wrap-Up days: no new episode begins. Use them for: compiling and editing the last episode, publishing delayed challenge answers and leaderboards, archive indexing, recap material, and planning the next Chapter. If the owner wants chronicles on these days, produce short "Wrap-Up Chronicles" that do not advance the Edubeam mission.

1.8 Episode hand-over
If a mission is unresolved at the end of Friday 2, it continues into the next episode (the original framework allows "to be continued"). The next episode's Monday-1 chronicle must open from the previous Friday-2 Ledger, not from a blank slate.

4. TEMPORAL INTELLIGENCE: THE THREE NEWS STREAMS
======================================================================

4.1 The streams
A. HISTORICAL stream: the historical reference date from the historical endpoint; start at the founder's Millennium Timezone location, then widen outward to global. Always real, archival and verifiable.
B. CURRENT stream: today's date; start at the Koware headquarters location (Amrahia -> Adenta -> Greater Accra -> Ghana -> West Africa -> relevant African/regional locations), then widen to global. Always real.
C. PROBING stream: the probe date from the probe endpoint; start at the probe location, then widen to global.
   - If the probe date is in the PAST, use real archival reporting.
   - If the probe date is in the FUTURE, real news does not exist. Generate plausible fictional reports grounded in present trends and label every one "FICTION - PROBE SCENARIO".

4.2 Per-stream output (the three-section format)
For each location in the stream: fetch diverse sources (national and very local/community, hard and soft news) and produce a merged Top 10 Weekly Headlines list for that location, then move to the next location. After all locations: collapse overlaps into the Top 20 Collective Headlines, weighted for relevance to the founder's CV and creative profile, Koware's business, technology and AI, Health & Safety, entrepreneurship, education, social development and the environment; add 5-10 Global Awareness Headlines. Present: Location lists -> Collective Summary -> Global Headlines.
Historical weekly lists should use archival sources (newspaper archives, government records, historical publications). If a precise local list cannot be verified for the exact timestamp, say so and do not fabricate it.
Keep research output in the Production Appendix; the published chronicle uses only the selected headlines.

4.3 Headline selection
Pick THREE global headlines: one Historical, one Current, one Probing, chosen because they are similar, related, catchy or of concern.
Pick FIVE local headlines: one Historical, three Current, one Probing, chosen the same way.
These eight become narrative triggers; weave them into the story rather than reading them as bulletins. News read during the commute and in the 9am briefing is a natural place for some of them.

4.4 Headline continuity
Review headlines from previous days and episodes to find links, repeats, escalations or resolutions.

4.5 Headline influence
The Edubeam team may make a real headline's fictional counterpart better or worse, or find the root cause and fix it in the past (for example, a safety workshop that changes a company's practice). The audience should always be able to tell the real headline from its fictional counterpart.

9. EDUBEAM CANON AND OPERATING RULES
======================================================================

9.1 What it is
A Star Trek-style teleportation system extended with a time factor, allowing probing time travel. Developed by Koware's Blue Crown R&D. It uses the Millennium Timezone as its reference system (a timezone with a past reference date and a "probing date" that reaches into the future and the past, seemingly random).

9.2 Mission mechanics
- Teams of four. Members may arrive in separate spots and need time to find each other.
- Each morning the Lab Specialist updates the timezone; the team is moved to the new date and location, always receiving updated news, verse, study area and safety tips. The team must be at the exact place and time each day to receive the update. Usually the location stays the same: the person fades while plants grow and buildings rise around them in real time, then re-materialises.
- Because the probe may jump years in a day, the team may meet someone young on one day, married with children the next, retired the next, or the reverse (even before birth). Members sometimes want to give younger people advice.
- Mission goal: find anything that can support Koware's products, services and lifestyle packages, and solutions to issues facing Koware or its community (possibly surfacing in headlines).
- Teams are charged with Edubeam particles and stay at the destination until the charge wears off, then return to the enclosure (the Twelve Monkeys style of return is the reference).
- The Lab Specialist can programme objects to materialise where the staff are (for example a vehicle from a 3D design) after receiving a request through Blue Crown communication devices. This is not guaranteed: comms and networks can fail, creating funny, scary or comic anomalies.
- Charged individuals can make short-distance teleports (apparent superpowers) but this drains the charge; if it runs out they are retracted, which is dangerous if the chamber is not ready.
- Only one person can go through the chamber at a time, so coordinates and time are adjusted slightly. First trials sometimes landed in the next room or the garden. Return requires exact date, time and place coordinates to target the person.
- Sometimes people are beamed straight to an existing Edubeam facility if one exists (more common in the far future, without time travel). Locals may question visitors, and clothing often gives them away.
- A team member who misses the return window waits until the next day. They are first brought to base, then regrouped. Messages can be sent to the future more easily than to the past; a message left with Koware in the past can be retrieved by a team through a network or a Koware Asylum in the area.
- A person who does not want to return may need the Corporate Manager to go through with a team to find and retract them. Complicated cases (arrest, kidnapping, hostage) belong in an "inner" story, Chronicles of Cupid Black, inside the main chronicle when required.
- A team may stay in the beam for a whole Chapter (four episodes) but this can drag unless a trained permanent task-force exists.
- Experiencing the beam is described as the weirdest feeling: many emotions at once (happy, sad, angry, humorous), which can make the procedure funny to watch.

9.3 Safety limits (canon)
- Small objects within the same timezone: manageable with minimal error.
- Larger objects or objects through time: more deformation, damage or loss the further they go. Blue Crown R&D's safe threshold is 250 years into the past or future. Beyond it the risk of deformation is very high and communication degrades. Always compare the probe distance with this threshold when describing object transfers.
- Solar flares, cosmic events, electromagnetic waves and thunderstorms disturb transmission. Never beam during these, nor in unstable conditions (risk: materialising in an unknown place or time, or missing a limb). The founder learned this by experience and used it to design the stabilised Edubeam with controls.
- The R&D Specialist's open research question: why do returning staff land in that particular chamber rather than somewhere far? Working assumption: vibration and relativity ideas (two objects cannot occupy the same space at the same time); the Edubeam is tuned to a frequency that lands them in the chamber.

9.4 The founder's own abilities
After years of study and being "zapped" by the London Edubeam, the founder can control his own teleportation and materialisation to an extent without the Edubeam, depending on focus.

9.5 People and staffing
R&D staff close at normal hours but keep monitoring from phones and tablets. The lab specialist usually stays behind during missions.

9.6 Misuse and discipline
Some members try to exploit the Edubeam (lottery numbers, finding relatives to alter their past or present). Offenders may be permanently banned. On return everybody goes through decontamination, MRI and X-ray screening. Contraband is smuggled in ingenious ways and found people are interrogated. Management may also discover teams hiding information and handle it according to what was hidden and why.

9.7 Believability notes for writers (fictional canon inspired by real ideas; none of this claims human time travel is possible)
The story may borrow, loosely and respectfully: teleportation by state transfer (real quantum teleportation moves quantum states, not matter, and needs classical communication); general-relativity time effects and speculative wormholes (which would require exotic matter); the Novikov self-consistency idea (events converge so history "repeats"); branching-timeline ideas (the team improves a headline in one branch); the Pauli exclusion principle as flavour for "two things cannot be in the same place"; and real space weather and electromagnetic interference to explain comms distortion. Mark such passages as fiction.

9.8 Proposed mechanics (OPTIONAL; not canon until the owner approves)
Seven components: Blue Crown Core, Temporal Coordinate Engine, Materialisation Chamber, Edubeam Plasma, Blue Crown Comms, Stabilisation Matrix, Recovery Interface. Process: target identification -> coordinate synchronisation -> subject check -> energy loading -> phase displacement -> temporal transfer -> monitoring -> retrieval. A "Temporal Materialisation Signature" (unique per traveller) would explain why people return to their own chamber and give new failure modes (corrupted return frequency, occupied chamber, subject outside coordinates, comms failure, disturbance, original facility no longer exists).

9.9 Vow of silence canon
After returning, a team can agree not to reveal something they found out (for example private discoveries about someone close). The vow can be upheld for years; the truth may later surface with surprising results. Use for deepening characters, not for hiding safety-critical information from management.

9.10 Consistency of consequences
Real-world safety rules in the story (stop work when uncertain, verify before acting, do not bypass warnings) match what Edubeam procedures demand. Bible reading and H&S lessons should be demonstrated by decisions, not only explained.

10.1 Chronicle Challenge (one per chronicle)

10.3 Service Cards

A card should have: card number/ID, short title, 3-5 step practical lesson, story reference, optional challenge link, and (when the Buzzjuice system is live) a code/QR placeholder and reward eligibility.
In the story, a card may be used to defuse an argument by giving both parties a card; they read it and the argument turns into a discussion, sometimes laughter and exchanged contacts.

## 5. Episode-day and role scheduling

### Determining the active day

For every generation request, the AI must:

1. Call `https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-probe.php?probe=now`.
2. Read `phase_start_iso` and `phase_end_iso`.
3. Identify the first Monday on or after `phase_start_iso`; that Monday is Day 1 of the term’s first episode.
4. Calculate the current date’s position within the active two-week episode.
5. Confirm that the current date is a Monday–Friday working day.
6. Use `now_iso` for current-world context and `probe_iso` for the Edubeam probe context. [buzzjuice](https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-probe.php?probe=now)

If the current date falls on a Saturday or Sunday, no new daily chronicle is generated unless explicitly requested; weekend developments may be referenced in the following Monday’s chronicle. ## 10. Editorial safeguards

The following rules are mandatory:

- Do not invent dates, readings, verses, quotations, courses, roles, news events or technical capabilities.
- Do not generate more than one chronicle per working day unless explicitly requested.
- Do not treat proposed fiction as established canon.
- Do not publish or promote unapproved generated media.
- Do not override consent, licensing or permission states.
- Do not silently change a character, mission, location or established storyline.
- Do not use inaccessible sources as a reason to fabricate content.
- Do not treat the AI Desk as the owner of episode-specific raw media or permanent episode knowledge. ## Daily chronicle requirements

Each daily chronicle should be an immersive narrative of roughly **3–5 minutes’ reading time**, ending with a practical challenge. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

Required components:

- A compelling opening rooted in the day’s featured characters and setting.
- Meaningful advancement of the episode’s central storyline.
- Natural integration of Bible study, Verse of the Day, Health & Safety, headlines, diary context, and Koware activity.
- At least one featured organizational role or alias.
- A closing **Daily Challenge**—for example, a decision scenario, riddle, Bible-passage application, safety assessment, or treasure-hunt clue.
- A clear statement that the solution will appear in the next chronicle. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

The challenge should test practical understanding rather than simple recall, and service cards may provide hints, clues, or optional treasure-hunt paths. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

## Continuity record

Because different AI platforms may generate different days, every chronicle must update a shared, human-reviewable continuity record. This is the key mechanism for cross-AI consistency. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles_Episode_Framework_compilation.txt)

Each daily record should capture:

- Episode day number and weekday.
- Current term and episode position derived from the probe endpoint.
- Featured roles and aliases.
- Principal characters and locations.
- Biblical reading, Verse of the Day, and Health & Safety reference.
- Historical, current, and speculative-future headlines used.
- Edubeam mission status and temporal coordinates.
- New discoveries, decisions, conflicts, and consequences.
- Outstanding questions and unresolved threads.
- Daily challenge and whether its solution has been released.
- Source links, source status, and canon classification. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

Classify every new element as **established canon**, **previously established information**, **current real-world information**, **proposed development**, **fictional scenario**, **unverified information**, or **awaiting approval**. AI must never silently promote a proposal into canon. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles_Episode_Framework_compilation.txt)

## Studio and media integration

The story framework should align with the Studio Structure Skeletal Blueprint’s principle of **one persistent AI Desk plus many episode-owned archives**. The AI Desk may hold personas, retrieval configuration, source catalogs, permissions, and operational controls, while each episode archive owns its context, research, scripts, media, transcripts, and publishing assets. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/Studio_Structure_Skeletal_Blueprint/Studio_Structure_Skeletal_Blueprint_v22.2.txt)

2. Lock the narrative.
3. Create comic panels, illustrations, narration, music, or short video from the approved chronicle.

## Manual episode compilation checklist

At the end of Day 10, the human editor should:

1. Collect the ten approved daily chronicles.
2. Arrange them from Day 1 through Day 10.
3. Verify the Monday-start and Friday-end structure.
4. Check character, role, mission, temporal, and headline continuity.
5. Remove repetition and improve transitions.
6. Confirm every daily challenge has a corresponding released solution.
7. Verify biblical, safety, news, and organizational references.
8. Compile the episode into one continuous work.
9. Archive the daily chronicles, compiled episode, continuity records, media, and source references.
10. Conduct a post-episode review and record improvements for the next episode.
