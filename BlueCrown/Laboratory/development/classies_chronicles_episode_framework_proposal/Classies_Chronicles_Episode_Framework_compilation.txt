# Classies Chronicles — Review, Analysis and Updated Episode Framework

**Recommended document:** Classies Chronicles – Master Episode Generation Framework
**Proposed version:** CC-MEGF-0.02.00
**Status:** Proposed Update — For Review and Approval
**Scope:** Generic, reusable framework for generating ten daily chronicles that collectively form one Classies Chronicles episode.

I have reviewed the original v0.01.05 proposal, the compiled contributions from ChatGPT, Claude, GitHub and Copilot, and the available project resources.

My recommendation is to retain the original concept but substantially reorganise it into a **repository-driven, AI-independent storytelling framework**.

There are three particularly important distinctions that the updated framework must enforce:

1. An episode spans ten working days, beginning on Monday and ending on the Friday of the following week.
2. Only ONE chronicle is generated per working day. The ten individual chronicles are subsequently compiled, reviewed and edited manually into one complete episode.
3. The framework must be generic. It must not prescribe a particular episode, date, storyline, mission, mystery, destination or outcome. Every AI generating a chronicle must discover those details from the supplied repositories, archives and current information.

### Main weakness

The original framework is primarily a daily activity schedule.

It describes what characters should do but does not sufficiently explain how those activities should contribute to one evolving storyline.

For example, the morning routine, Bible study, news, staff meetings and time-travel missions could become repetitive if they were simply reproduced every day.

The solution is to establish a central narrative structure that connects these activities.

## 2. Important improvements from the compiled proposals

The contributions from the different AI systems provide several important improvements.

### A. Introduce a central narrative spine

Each ten-day episode should have an identifiable overarching storyline.

The storyline should develop progressively rather than presenting ten unrelated stories.

Recommended narrative progression:

* Establish the circumstances.
* Introduce the central development.
* Begin investigation or exploration.
* Introduce complications.
* Develop character relationships.
* Discover additional information.
* Introduce consequences.
* Reach a significant decision or turning point.
* Resolve, partially resolve or transform the central situation.
* Conclude the episode while preserving appropriate continuity.

The central storyline must come from the actual project archives and current circumstances.

An AI must not automatically introduce a mystery, warning, malfunction or time-travel complication simply because those elements appeared in previous episodes.

### B. Separate the framework from individual episode generation

This was one of the strongest recommendations in the compiled proposals.

The framework should define the rules of the Classies Chronicles universe.

The daily generation instructions should explain how an AI applies those rules to produce one particular chronicle.

This separation allows the same framework to work with:

* ChatGPT
* Claude
* GitHub Copilot
* Other AI systems
* Future AI agents

It also prevents the storytelling universe from becoming dependent on a particular AI platform.

### C. Strengthen continuity management

Every generated chronicle should respect previously established information.

The AI should distinguish between:

* Established canon.
* Previously documented events.
* Current real-world information.
* New fictional developments.
* Proposed developments.
* Unverified information.

This is especially important for the time-travel storyline and the established character histories.

### D. Integrate the different story elements

The strongest proposals recommend treating the Bible study, Health & Safety, news, diary entries, organisational activities and fictional missions as interconnected elements.

I agree.

These should not appear as six unrelated sections in the story.

Instead, they should influence the characters' decisions, experiences, conversations and activities.

For example, a biblical principle concerning responsibility could influence a character's decision during an expedition. A corresponding Health & Safety principle could provide practical guidance, while a current news event could introduce a related challenge.

This creates a much more meaningful reading experience.

### E. Introduce structured continuity and mission records

The compiled proposals correctly identify the need for continuity records.

These should capture:

* Character developments.
* Mission assignments.
* Locations.
* Temporal coordinates.
* Equipment and technology.
* Important discoveries.
* Organisational developments.
* Outstanding questions.
* Decisions and consequences.

These records will become particularly important when different AI systems generate different daily chronicles.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

## 1.1 Purpose

The purpose of this framework is to establish a consistent, reusable and AI-independent approach to generating Classies Chronicles.

Classies Chronicles is a serialized storytelling universe integrating:

1. Personal experiences and character development.
2. Eternal Dreamfall Bible Study.
3. YouVersion Verse of the Day.
4. Health & Safety education.
5. Current and historical news.
6. Koware Group activities and organisational developments.
7. Time-travel exploration and research through the established Ejibeam technology.
8. Previous chronicles and archived storylines.
9. Community participation and other relevant project activities.

These elements should contribute to one interconnected narrative.

The objective is to create entertaining, informative, educational and spiritually meaningful chronicles that collectively document the continuing development of the Classies Chronicles universe.

## 1.2 Episode duration and production arrangement

Every episode consists of ten working days.

The production arrangement is:

WEEK ONE

* Monday — Day 1
* Tuesday — Day 2
* Wednesday — Day 3
* Thursday — Day 4
* Friday — Day 5

WEEKEND

* Saturday and Sunday — No compulsory daily chronicle production.

WEEK TWO

* Monday — Day 6
* Tuesday — Day 7
* Wednesday — Day 8
* Thursday — Day 9
* Friday — Day 10

An episode always begins on a Monday and concludes on the Friday of the following week.

The ten working days constitute one complete episode.

## 1.3 Daily production rule

Only ONE chronicle shall be generated for each working day.

Each daily chronicle represents one instalment of the larger episode.

The ten chronicles shall eventually be:

1. Collected.
2. Reviewed.
3. Arranged in chronological order.
4. Edited for continuity.
5. Combined into one complete episode.
6. Prepared for the appropriate publication formats.

The daily generation process must not automatically generate all ten chronicles.

Unless explicitly requested otherwise, the AI must generate only the chronicle for the current production day.

## 1.4 Generic application

This framework is not tied to any particular episode, season, calendar date, mission, character assignment, destination or storyline.

Every generating AI must independently establish the relevant circumstances by examining the supplied repositories, archives, current information and applicable continuity records.

Previous episodes should inform the next chronicle without forcing it to repeat their storylines.

# 3. NARRATIVE ARCHITECTURE

## 3.1 Central storyline

Every episode should have one overarching storyline.

The storyline should emerge from the available archives, current circumstances, character development and relevant project activities.

The AI should identify:

* The central situation.
* The principal characters involved.
* The relevant locations.
* The primary objective or challenge.
* The associated subplots.
* The expected narrative progression.
* Outstanding questions.
* Potential consequences.

The storyline should develop naturally over the ten working days.

## 3.2 Narrative integration

The following elements should be integrated into the central storyline.

### A. Personal experiences

Use relevant personal experiences and diary entries to develop realistic characters.

These may include:

* Domestic responsibilities.
* Work schedules.
* Relationships.
* Personal challenges.
* Family interactions.
* Fitness activities.
* Social activities.
* Professional responsibilities.
* Spiritual development.

Not every activity needs to appear every day.

### B. Biblical development

The Eternal Dreamfall Bible Study and YouVersion Verse of the Day should contribute to the spiritual and moral development of the story.

The biblical material should influence:

* Character decisions.
* Conversations.
* Reflections.
* Leadership.
* Relationships.
* Responses to challenges.
* Personal development.

Avoid turning the chronicle into a disconnected sermon.

### C. Health & Safety

Health & Safety should provide practical educational value.

Where appropriate, identify one principal lesson or safety topic for the day.

Integrate the lesson naturally through an incident, discussion, workplace activity, expedition or personal experience.

### D. News and world events

Current news should be researched using reliable sources.

Where possible, curate relevant developments from different geographical levels:

* Local communities.
* Greater Accra.
* Ghana.
* West Africa.
* Africa.
* International developments.

The geographical selection should depend on the storyline and the available news.

News may influence the characters' activities, research, discussions and decisions.

Real-world news must remain distinguishable from fictional broadcasts and events.

Historical news should be sourced from the relevant historical period.

Future news must be explicitly identified as fictional speculation.

### E. Koware Group activities

The story should reflect the actual activities of the Koware Group.

Characters may participate in:

* Meetings.
* Research.
* Project development.
* Business activities.
* Product development.
* Staff training.
* Community initiatives.
* Operational challenges.
* Organisational planning.

These activities should contribute to both the fictional storyline and the development of the wider Koware ecosystem.

### F. Ejibeam missions

The Ejibeam provides the established mechanism for relevant time-travel and research activities.

Its capabilities, limitations and operational procedures must follow established project canon.

The AI must consult existing records before introducing new technical capabilities.

Time-travel missions should have meaningful objectives, not simply serve as opportunities to visit different locations or periods.

# 4. TEN-WORKING-DAY NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

The following structure defines the general purpose of each production day.

It does not prescribe the actual events that must occur.

## WEEK ONE — ESTABLISHMENT AND DEVELOPMENT

### DAY 1 — MONDAY

Episode commencement and establishment.

Primary objectives:

* Establish the opening atmosphere.
* Introduce the relevant characters.
* Establish the current circumstances.
* Present the central storyline.
* Introduce relevant organisational activities.
* Conduct the appropriate weekly briefing.
* Establish any new assignments or missions.
* Introduce the day's biblical and educational themes.

Where appropriate, the opening may begin before sunrise, following the characters' morning routines.

The first day should give readers sufficient context to understand the developing episode.

### DAY 2 — TUESDAY

Investigation and exploration.

Primary objectives:

* Develop the established storyline.
* Follow assigned personnel.
* Explore the relevant environments.
* Introduce discoveries and encounters.
* Develop the day's educational themes.
* Integrate relevant news.
* Show the consequences of Monday's developments.

Formal meetings are not compulsory.

### DAY 3 — WEDNESDAY

Discovery and complication.

Primary objectives:

* Advance the central storyline.
* Introduce meaningful developments.
* Explore relationships between characters.
* Present unexpected information or circumstances where supported by the story.
* Develop the research or expedition activities.
* Introduce relevant organisational and personal developments.

The storyline should become more developed without introducing unnecessary complications.

### DAY 4 — THURSDAY

Review and integration.

Primary objectives:

* Review significant developments.
* Conduct appropriate follow-up discussions.
* Connect discoveries to the wider organisational ecosystem.
* Reassess assignments and objectives.
* Develop the biblical and Health & Safety applications.
* Prepare relevant personnel for subsequent activities.

Where appropriate, conduct follow-up meetings.

### DAY 5 — FRIDAY

First-week conclusion.

Primary objectives:

* Conclude the first week's activities.
* Review significant discoveries and developments.
* Address immediate operational responsibilities.
* Prepare relevant reports.
* Establish weekend activities.
* Develop any outstanding storyline elements.

The first week should conclude naturally without prematurely resolving the entire episode.

## WEEKEND — CONTINUITY PERIOD

Saturday and Sunday are not compulsory chronicle-production days.

However, the fictional world continues to exist.

Where relevant, characters may:

* Spend time with family.
* Participate in social activities.
* Attend religious activities.
* Exercise.
* Travel.
* Undertake personal projects.
* Participate in recreational activities.
* Continue independent research.

Weekend developments may be incorporated into the following Monday's chronicle.

Do not generate additional daily chronicles unless specifically requested.

## WEEK TWO — CONSEQUENCES AND CONCLUSION

### DAY 6 — MONDAY

Second-week commencement.

Primary objectives:

* Re-establish the storyline after the weekend.
* Review relevant developments.
* Introduce new information.
* Continue outstanding assignments.
* Conduct the appropriate weekly briefing.
* Reassess personnel and mission responsibilities.

The second week should feel like a continuation, not an entirely new episode.

### DAY 7 — TUESDAY

Further investigation and development.

Primary objectives:

* Advance the storyline.
* Explore outstanding questions.
* Develop character interactions.
* Introduce relevant discoveries.
* Connect the story to current organisational activities.
* Develop the educational themes.

### DAY 8 — WEDNESDAY

Convergence and consequences.

Primary objectives:

* Bring important storylines closer together.
* Explore the consequences of previous decisions.
* Develop significant character interactions.
* Reassess mission objectives.
* Prepare for the episode's principal turning point.

### DAY 9 — THURSDAY

Critical developments and decision-making.

Primary objectives:

* Address the episode's major outstanding questions.
* Present significant discoveries or developments.
* Introduce important decisions.
* Resolve or transform relevant conflicts.
* Prepare for the episode's conclusion.

The AI should avoid forcing an artificial resolution.

### DAY 10 — FRIDAY

Episode conclusion and handover.

Primary objectives:

* Conclude the ten-working-day storyline.
* Review the principal developments.
* Document important discoveries.
* Complete appropriate reports and briefings.
* Integrate relevant findings into Koware operations.
* Identify outstanding questions.
* Establish continuity for future episodes.

Where appropriate, introduce future assignments, personnel or story developments.

The conclusion should provide narrative satisfaction while allowing the wider universe to continue developing.

* Not every role must appear in every episode.

# PART 4 — IMPORTANT OBSERVATIONS AND RISKS

There are four areas I would pay particular attention to before approving the framework.

### 1. The distinction between an episode and a daily chronicle

This is the most important structural correction.

An episode is not ten independently generated stories.

It is one continuous storyline produced through ten daily instalments.

The daily chronicles must therefore preserve the episode's direction without requiring every daily instalment to provide a complete resolution.

### 2. Avoid repetitive daily routines

The original proposal gives considerable attention to the 4:00 a.m., 4:30 a.m., 5:00 a.m. and 6:00 a.m. routines.

These are useful for establishing familiarity.

However, reproducing every alarm and activity every day would make the chronicles unnecessarily repetitive.

My recommendation is to retain the routines as available storytelling material, allowing the AI to select the scenes that contribute meaningfully to that day's story.

### 3. Protect the established fictional canon

The compiled proposals correctly identify the danger of introducing unsupported technology capabilities and changing established storylines.

This is particularly important with the Ejibeam.

The AI should be a storyteller operating within the established universe, not an authority independently rewriting its rules.

### 4. Improve repository accessibility

One limitation encountered during this review was that the Health & Safety repository's directory contents could not be independently retrieved through the available browsing interface.

Consequently, I have included mandatory verification requirements rather than assuming particular courses or lessons exist.

I recommend eventually introducing a central source index containing direct links to important files, particularly:

* Character canon.
* Organisational chart.
* Episode archives.
* Daily diary.
* Ejibeam technical documentation.
* Health & Safety course index.
* Bible study materials.

This would substantially improve the reliability of generation across different AI platforms.

Updated Classies Chronicles Episode Framework Proposal (Generic / Reusable)

This revision synthesizes the strongest ideas from both the original proposal and the compilation, while removing all specific dates, episode numbers, and production-restricted references. It is designed so that any AI can use the same framework to generate a fresh chronicle for any episode, as long as it follows the shared world-building rules, daily rhythm, and mission structure.

1. Core Purpose

The Classies Chronicles episode is a 10-working-day narrative cycle that follows:
- the daily life of the Koware Group base and its personnel,
- the routines and reflections of the main characters,
- the ongoing Edubeam mission in a probe location,
- the broader continuity of the world and the mission’s mystery.

Each episode:
- begins on a Monday,
- runs for 10 working days,
- ends on the following Friday,
- produces one chronicle for one day only,
- preserves a unified story arc across the full 10-day period.

The final episode is not a single long narrative; it is a manually curated compilation of ten daily chronicles, each written as a stand-alone but continuity-aware chapter.

2. Required Constraints

The following rules must always be respected:

- No specific date or episode number should be included.
- The episode must be generic and reusable.
- The story must always begin on a Monday and end on the following week’s Friday.
- Only one chronicle should be generated for each day.
- The episode must span exactly 10 working days:
  - Week 1: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday
  - Week 2: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday
- No day outside the 10-day cycle should be treated as a story day.
- Each day should feel like a natural continuation of the previous day and a setup for the next.
- The narrative must preserve continuity with prior episodes and archives, while allowing each new AI to generate fresh material.
- The story should feel grounded in:
  - daily domestic life,
  - mission operations,
  - R&D and Edubeam procedures,
  - local and global news resonance,
  - biblical or reflective reading,
  - health and safety lessons,
  - organizational interactions,
  - continuity and mission logs.

3. Episode Structure

The episode is organized into two key layers:

A. Base Layer: Koware Group life
This is where the story anchors the human and organizational realities:
- morning routines
- family and domestic management
- leadership conversations
- staff roles and responsibilities
- meetings, briefings, and daily operations
- health and safety practices
- reflections and prayer
- investigations and action planning

B. Probe Layer: Edubeam expedition
This is the story layer where the mission unfolds:
- field team in the probe location
- discoveries, encounters, conversations, warnings
- investigation of the time/probe environment
- connection to historical/current/global headlines
- clues, anomalies, and unresolved threats
- strategic decisions about what to do next

4. Narrative Principles

4.1 The episode should alternate between:
- the daily life of the base,
- the mission life of the expedition team,
- a reflective interpretive layer that links both.

4.2 Each day should focus on a different organizational perspective.
The proposal’s strongest idea is to rotate focus among staff roles so the story grows through a wider cast, rather than concentrating only on one lead character.

Suggested rotation:
- Day 1: Founder / Corporate Manager / primary lead
- Day 2: R&D or lab specialist
- Day 3: operational support or technical personnel
- Day 4: administrative or finance or project oversight
- Day 5: field team and mission impact
- Day 6: another executive/leadership role
- Day 7: a support/communications function
- Day 8: a field specialist or scientific investigator
- Day 9: cross-functional review and decision point
- Day 10: summary, closure, and continuation

4.3 The story should always include
- one human emotional center,
- one organizational problem,
- one technical or mysterious anomaly,
- one safety concern,
- one relevant reflective or faith-based insight.

4.4 A single episode may revolve around one main mission question, for example:
- a warning received from an unknown or unauthorized source,
- an unexplained instruction,
- a communication anomaly,
- a dangerous pattern emerging across timelines,
- a clue that connects the probe location to the base,
- a risk that the team may be returning to the wrong outcome or wrong place,
- a decision to stop, verify, or continue.

5. Daily Chronicle Template

Each chronicle should follow the same daily rhythm, but with different emphasis and developments.

5.1 Morning Opening
The day begins before sunrise or at first light.

Include:
- the atmosphere of home or base,
- the domestic routine,
- the environment, weather, and city soundscape,
- the main character’s state of mind,
- the sense that the day may carry significance.

5.2 Eternal Dreamfall Reading
At the start of the day:
- activate the Eternal Dreamfall reading plan,
- identify the relevant biblical text for the day,
- connect the reading to the mission or emotional state of the episode.

This is not just background. It should shape the tone of the chapter.

5.3 Verse of the Day
The verse should be:
- briefly read and reflected on,
- used to establish the moral, spiritual, or interpretive lens of the day.

This should not be a stand-alone devotional add-on. It should feed directly into the day’s mission, safety insight, and relationships.

5.4 Health & Safety Reflection
At least one health and safety principle must be introduced each day.

The safety lesson should:
- be linked to the day’s reading or mission,
- reflect practical safety awareness,
- be realistic and relevant to daily operations,
- apply to the Edubeam team, base operations, or broader world conditions.

Examples of themes:
- stop and verify before acting,
- do not bypass warning systems,
- maintain clear communication,
- preserve evidence,
- ensure safe return conditions,
- monitor energy, charge, and operational status,
- distinguish between urgency and authorization.

5.5 Morning Base Activities
The story should move into the working day:
- routines at the Koware Group base,
- preparation for operations,
- staff arrival,
- monitoring of equipment,
- updates from R&D,
- relevant domestic tasks,
- a sense of community and routine.

5.6 Briefing or Non-Briefing Day
The daily structure should follow the week’s rhythm:
- Monday: mission briefing and focus on goals
- Tuesday: follow-up and deeper probe activity
- Wednesday: discovery and complication
- Thursday: cross-functional check-in and review
- Friday: report-out, wrap-up, continuation, or closing tension

Some days may be quiet or have no formal briefing. The absence of a meeting can be meaningful.

6. Weekly Flow

Week 1: Mission Launch and Discovery
Week 1 Monday:
- introduce the main mission,
- initialize the base rhythm,
- define the probe objective,
- begin with atmosphere and routines,
- reveal the purpose of the episode.

Week 1 Tuesday:
- R&D or field team becomes active,
- new findings emerge,
- a clue or anomaly appears.

Week 1 Wednesday:
- deeper exploration,
- the narrative broadens,
- the team meets obstacles or new evidence.

- the operational review takes place,
- cross-functional discussion,
- the mystery deepens,
- the team must decide whether to continue, pause, verify, or return.

Week 1 Friday:
- the first full-week arc closes,
- some findings are consolidated,
- new tension remains unresolved,
- the weekend creates anticipation.

Week 2: Continuation, Pressure, and Decision
Week 2 Monday:
- revisit mission status,
- integrate findings,
- turn the story toward escalation or consequence.

Week 2 Tuesday:
- another clue, another challenge,
- distinct focus on one key role or team member.

Week 2 Wednesday:
- the situation becomes more dangerous or ambiguous,
- evidence requires interpretation.

Week 2 Thursday:
- high-level review or critical briefing,
- return procedure decision,
- actions must be formalized.

Week 2 Friday:
- decisive moment or near-resolution,
- integration of findings into the Koware business or organizational plan,
- setup for next episode or open-ended continuation.

7. Daily Story Engine

Each chronicle should include the following narrative rhythm:

- Opening atmosphere and character grounding
- Daily reflection from scripture
- Safety-oriented lesson
- Base operations / organizational function
- Mission status update
- Discovery or anomaly
- Human reaction
- Consequence or decision
- Closing reflection

This keeps the story grounded while preserving momentum.

8. Edubeam and Probe Mission Logic

The Edubeam must remain a central organizing force.

The mission logic should include:
- a target date or time layer,
- a probe location,
- a team assigned to investigate,
- a purpose related to discovery, learning, problem-solving, or anomaly resolution,
- an operational limitation such as return status, charge management, or communication uncertainty,
- an anomaly that is not immediately explained.

The Edubeam should operate like a serious research tool, not a casual adventure system:
- it can support discovery,
- it can expose danger,
- it can create tension between mission urgency and safe return,
- it can open moral questions about intervention, witness, and responsibility.

The technology should be mysterious but structured:
- return routes are not always safe,
- signals may be falsified, duplicated, delayed, or unexpected,
- unauthorized or incomplete instructions must be treated as serious risks,
- the team cannot simply act on the basis of novelty or urgency.

9. News Integration

The story should remain connected to current realities, historical references, and fictional future developments.

Each day should include a curated article or headline theme, but not necessarily a full news list.

Preferred news structure:
- one historical echo,
- one current-world relevance,
- one probe or futuristic parallel,
- three to five local/related headlines or patterns that can be woven into the chapter.

The headlines should not be random; they should mirror the episode’s underlying theme.

Examples of thematic connections:
- transport and communication safety,
- industrial risk,
- environmental impact,
- infrastructure reliability,
- public trust and accountability,
- medical, research, or security concerns,
- governance and oversight.

The purpose is not just “news in the background.” The headlines should illuminate the moral, technical, or social tension of the day’s mission.

10. Archive and Continuity Integration

Every chronicle should be informed by prior episodes, diary notes, or archive material.

The AI should:
- reference the continuity ledger,
- incorporate previous unresolved questions,
- introduce a new clue without overwriting earlier developments,
- keep the story moving by connecting past events to current anomalies.

At the end of each daily chronicle, include a concise mission log or continuity ledger entry with:
- field status
- key discoveries
- unresolved issue
- safety status
- next action
- continuity note

This ensures the episode can be assembled manually into a larger narrative without losing coherence.

The story should continuously rotate through the Koware organizational chart, introducing:
- leadership,
- R&D,
- administration,
- operations,
- technical support,
- finance,
- legal or compliance,
- security,

- field personnel,
- community-facing roles.

This gives the episode texture and makes the world feel lived-in. The team need not always be the same people; the organizational structure should support a broad and believable cast.

12. Tone and Style

The tone should be:
- reflective,
- atmospheric,
- grounded,
- thoughtful,
- sometimes suspenseful,
- occasionally contemplative and spiritual,
- always human.

The prose should blend:
- domestic realism,
- mission tension,
- subtle mythic or symbolic language,
- practical management details,
- and a sense that the world is larger than the characters can fully understand.

The mood should move from quiet routine to urgency without becoming chaotic.

13. Required Closing Pattern

Each day should close with:
- a human reflection,
- a mission update,
- a sense of unresolved tension,
- or a brief question that invites continuation.

The final day of the 10-day episode should:
- complete a meaningful phase,
- resolve enough to feel earned,
- leave a persistent open thread for the next episode or continuation,
- avoid forcing a false full resolution.

14. Output Requirements for Each Daily Chronicle

Each generated daily chronicle should include:
- a heading identifying the day in the episode cycle,
- the primary focus of the day,
- the main character or role spotlight,
- the base activity,
- the Edubeam or probe activity,
- the scriptural reflection,
- the health and safety insight,
- the relevant mission or continuity log,
- the closing tension or question.

The day’s narrative should read as a self-contained chapter, but also as a piece of a larger 10-day structure.

15. Final Recommended Version of the Proposal

The episode should be written as a 10-working-day framework in which:
- every day has a structured rhythm,
- every day includes domestic life and mission life,
- every day advances the mystery,
- every day deepens the world,
