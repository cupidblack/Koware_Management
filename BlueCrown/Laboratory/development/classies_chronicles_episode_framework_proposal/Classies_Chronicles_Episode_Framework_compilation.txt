### Main weakness

The original framework is primarily a daily activity schedule.

It describes what characters should do but does not sufficiently explain how those activities should contribute to one evolving storyline.

For example, Bible study, morning routines, news, meetings, staff assignments and Edubeam missions could become repetitive if reproduced mechanically every day.

The updated framework should therefore introduce a **central narrative structure** that connects these activities.

## 2. Improvements identified in the compiled proposals

The compiled proposals provide several important recommendations.

### A. Central narrative development

Every episode should have one overarching narrative.

However, the framework must not prescribe what that narrative should be.

The AI should discover appropriate storylines from:

* Previous chronicles.
* Current real-world developments.
* The Bible study programme.
* The Millennium Timezone.
* Koware's organisational activities.
* Edubeam research.
* Historical and future developments.
* Character experiences and personal diaries.

A storyline should develop progressively rather than presenting ten unrelated daily stories.

### B. Separation of framework and generation instructions

This is particularly important.

The framework should establish:

* The universe.
* The production structure.
* The storytelling principles.
* The research requirements.
* The continuity rules.

The daily generation instructions should explain how an AI applies those rules to produce one chronicle.

This separation makes the framework reusable across ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Perplexity and future AI systems.

### C. Continuity management

The AI must distinguish between:

1. Established canon.
2. Previously documented events.
3. Current real-world information.
4. New fictional developments.
5. Proposed developments.
6. Unverified information.

This is especially important for the Millennium Timezone, Edubeam missions and established character histories.

### D. Integration of storytelling elements

The strongest proposals recommend integrating the different components rather than presenting them as unrelated sections.

For example:

A biblical principle concerning responsibility could influence a character's decision during an expedition.

A corresponding Health & Safety principle could provide practical guidance.

A contemporary news development could introduce a related challenge.

The three elements become part of the same story.

### E. Continuity records

Every chronicle should contribute information that helps the next AI continue the narrative.

This includes:

* Character developments.
* Mission assignments.
* Locations.
* Temporal coordinates.
* Equipment and technology.
* Discoveries.
* Organisational developments.
* Important conversations.
* Decisions and consequences.
* Unresolved issues.

These records are particularly important when different AI platforms generate different daily chronicles.

The compiled proposals contain additional useful recommendations concerning weekend continuity, research, role rotation, episode compilation and quality assurance. I have incorporated those into the proposed revision.

# 1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

## 1.1 Purpose

This document establishes the principles, storytelling structure, research requirements and production procedures governing the generation of Classies Chronicles.

It provides a reusable framework that can be shared with any compatible AI system to generate a fresh daily chronicle using the designated repositories, archives, reference materials and current information.

The framework must remain generic.

It must not prescribe a particular:

* Episode.
* Production date.
* Episode title.
* Storyline.
* Mission.
* Historical setting.
* Future destination.
* Mystery.
* Conflict.
* Character outcome.

These must emerge naturally from the available resources and circumstances applicable to the day being generated.

## 1.2 Core storytelling objective

Classies Chronicles is a continuous storytelling universe integrating:

1. Personal experiences and character development.
2. Eternal Dreamfall Bible Study.
3. YouVersion Verse of the Day.
4. Health & Safety education.
5. Current affairs and historical developments.
6. Koware Group organisational activities.
7. Blue Crown research and development.
8. Millennium Timezone and Edubeam missions.
9. Previous chronicles and historical archives.
10. Community participation and social activities.
11. Research, innovation, technology and enterprise development.
12. Human relationships and future possibilities.

These elements should contribute to one interconnected narrative.

The objective is to produce entertaining, informative, educational and spiritually meaningful chronicles that document the continuing development of the Classies Chronicles universe.

## 1.3 AI independence

The framework must work with different AI platforms.

An AI generating a chronicle must independently:

* Examine the supplied resources.
* Identify established canon.
* Determine the current production position.
* Review relevant previous chronicles.
* Identify outstanding storylines.
* Conduct necessary research.
* Generate exactly one daily chronicle.
* Preserve continuity for subsequent contributions.

An AI must not assume access to information it cannot retrieve.

# 2. ANNUAL STORY AND PRODUCTION STRUCTURE

## 2.1 Annual hierarchy

Classies Chronicles follows this general production hierarchy:

SEASON

* One season per year.

CHAPTERS / TERMS

* Five chapters per year.

CHAPTER DURATION

* Eight weeks per chapter.

EPISODES

* Four two-week episodes per chapter.

DAILY PRODUCTION

* One chronicle per working day.

## 2.2 Annual chapter arrangement

The five chapters generally cover:

Chapter 1:

* Mid-January to mid-March.

Chapter 2:

* Mid-March to mid-May.

Chapter 3:

* June and July.

Chapter 4:

* Mid-August to mid-October.

Chapter 5:

* Mid-October to mid-December.

These are general calendar descriptions.

The actual chapter boundaries must always be established using the Millennium Probe endpoint.

## 2.3 Episode duration

Every episode consists of ten working days.

An episode always begins on Monday and concludes on the Friday of the following week.

WEEK ONE:

Day 1 — Monday
Day 2 — Tuesday
Day 3 — Wednesday
Day 4 — Thursday
Day 5 — Friday

WEEKEND:

Saturday and Sunday — No compulsory chronicle production.

WEEK TWO:

Day 6 — Monday
Day 7 — Tuesday
Day 8 — Wednesday
Day 9 — Thursday
Day 10 — Friday

## 2.4 Daily production rule

Only ONE chronicle must be generated per working day.

Each chronicle represents one instalment of the larger episode.

The ten chronicles will subsequently be collected, reviewed, edited and manually compiled into one complete episode.

The AI must not automatically generate all ten chronicles when the requirement is to produce the current day's instalment.

The production of each daily chronicle must be treated as an independent deliverable within the larger narrative.

## 2.5 Weekend continuity

Saturday and Sunday are not compulsory production days.

However, the fictional universe continues to exist.

Characters may:

* Spend time with family.
* Participate in social activities.
* Attend religious activities.
* Exercise.
* Travel.
* Undertake personal projects.
* Participate in recreational activities.
* Continue independent research.
* Handle professional responsibilities.

Relevant weekend developments may be incorporated into the following Monday's chronicle.

The AI must not generate separate weekend chronicles unless explicitly requested.

# 3. CALENDAR IDENTIFICATION AND MILLENNIUM TIMEZONE

## 3.1 Authoritative calendar endpoint

Before generating a daily chronicle, the AI must retrieve:

[https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-probe.php?probe=now](https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-probe.php?probe=now)

This endpoint provides the actual current timestamp, active phase information and current Millennium Timezone probing coordinates.

The returned JSON must be interpreted before generating a chronicle.

## 3.2 Important endpoint fields

The AI must identify:

* now_iso
* probe_iso
* phase_index
* phase_label
* phase_start_iso
* phase_end_iso

Their responsibilities are different.

`now_iso`:
Identifies the actual production date and time.

`probe_iso`:
Identifies the temporal destination associated with the Millennium Timezone and Edubeam storyline.

`phase_index`:
Identifies the active chapter/term index.

`phase_label`:
Provides the descriptive name of the active phase.

`phase_start_iso`:
Identifies the beginning of the active term.

`phase_end_iso`:
Identifies the end of the active term.

The actual production date must never be confused with the probing date.

## 3.3 Episode identification procedure

The AI must:

1. Retrieve the Millennium Probe endpoint.
2. Validate the returned JSON.
3. Identify the active phase.
4. Read phase_start_iso and phase_end_iso.
5. Determine the first Monday on or after phase_start_iso.
6. Establish the successive two-week episode windows.
7. Determine the current production date using now_iso.
8. Identify the applicable episode.
9. Calculate the current working-day position within that episode.
10. Verify that the production date falls within the applicable term.
11. Establish the corresponding Millennium Timezone probing date.

Episode calculations must use the actual production calendar, not probe_iso.

## 3.4 Episode calculation

The first episode begins on the first Monday on or after the phase start.

Each subsequent episode begins fourteen calendar days after the previous episode's commencement.

Each episode comprises:

* Five working days.
* Two weekend continuity days.
* Five working days.

The final Friday must fall within the active phase.

If the phase boundaries do not accommodate four complete episodes, the AI must identify the discrepancy rather than silently creating an episode outside the term.

If the current date falls before the first episode commencement or outside a valid ten-working-day window, the AI must explain the calendar position and avoid assigning an incorrect episode day.

## 3.5 Endpoint reliability

If the endpoint is unavailable:

* Retry the request where possible.
* Validate the returned timestamp.
* Check whether the response is stale or cached.
* Cross-check the actual production date against a reliable clock.
* Do not substitute the probing date for the production date.
* Do not invent phase boundaries.

If the discrepancy cannot be resolved, disclose the limitation before generating the chronicle.

## 3.6 Internal calendar record

Before writing, establish:

* Season.
* Chapter/term.
* Episode position.
* Working-day number.
* Production date.
* Production time.
* Millennium Timezone probe date.
* Probe location, where established.
* Relevant previous chronicle.

These details should guide generation but must not turn the generic framework into a document tied to a particular episode.

# 5. NARRATIVE DEVELOPMENT

## 5.1 Central narrative principle

Every episode should have one overarching storyline.

The ten daily chronicles must contribute to the development of that storyline.

The storyline must emerge from available information.

The AI must not automatically introduce:

* A mystery.
* A crisis.
* A malfunction.
* A conspiracy.
* A conflict.
* An emergency.
* A time-travel complication.

These developments should only occur when justified by the established universe and the circumstances of the story.

## 5.2 Narrative progression

The ten-day structure should support a natural progression.

The following is a flexible narrative guide, not a mandatory plot formula.

DAY 1 — ESTABLISHMENT
Introduce relevant circumstances, characters, developments and objectives.

DAY 2 — DEVELOPMENT
Expand the storyline and introduce relevant activities or discoveries.

DAY 3 — EXPLORATION
Develop research, relationships, observations and investigations.

DAY 4 — PROGRESSION
Advance the storyline through character decisions and organisational activities.

DAY 5 — FIRST-WEEK REVIEW
Review developments, address outstanding matters and establish weekend continuity.

DAY 6 — RESUMPTION
Re-establish the narrative after the weekend.

DAY 7 — FURTHER DEVELOPMENT
Advance the storyline through new information, research or experiences.

DAY 8 — CONSEQUENCES
Explore the consequences of previous decisions and discoveries.

DAY 9 — PREPARATION
Prepare relevant reports, decisions, returns or next-stage developments.

DAY 10 — CONCLUSION
Conclude or transform the episode's central development while preserving appropriate continuity.

These stages may overlap or occur in a different order when the actual storyline requires it.

## 5.3 Narrative continuity

Every chronicle must:

* Connect with relevant previous developments.
* Introduce meaningful progression.
* Respect established character histories.
* Avoid unnecessary repetition.
* Preserve unresolved matters.
* Establish appropriate transitions.

An episode may conclude without resolving every storyline.

Classies Chronicles is a continuing universe, not a collection of disconnected stories.

# PART 3 — IMPORTANT IMPLEMENTATION OBSERVATIONS

There are several matters I recommend addressing before treating this document as an approved production standard.

### 1. The Millennium Probe should be the calendar authority

This is probably the most important technical improvement.

The endpoint provides two different temporal dimensions:

| Field             | Purpose                                 |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| `now_iso`         | Actual production date                  |
| `probe_iso`       | Fictional/historical/future destination |
| `phase_start_iso` | Beginning of the active chapter         |
| `phase_end_iso`   | End of the active chapter               |

The AI must never use `probe_iso` to determine which chronicle to generate.

I also recommend that the endpoint be accessed without caching, where possible. A stale response could cause an AI to generate the wrong daily chronicle.

### 2. There is a calendar alignment issue worth resolving

Your annual structure specifies:

* Five terms per year.
* Eight weeks per term.
* Four episodes per term.
* Fourteen calendar days per episode.

That works mathematically when the term boundaries align with the episode boundaries.

However, if `phase_start_iso` falls in the middle of a week, the first Monday on or after it may shift the episode schedule.

The framework therefore needs to validate that four complete episodes fit inside the actual phase boundaries.

It should not silently extend an episode into another term.

### 3. Organisational role rotation needs a separate tracking mechanism

The organisational chart contains role-check days extending from Day 1 through Day 20.

This is useful because it allows organisational representation to continue across consecutive episodes.

I recommend eventually introducing a simple role-tracking register containing:

| Field               | Purpose                             |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Role                | Organisational position             |
| Alias               | Character representing the position |
| Scheduled check day | Expected spotlight                  |
| Last featured       | Most recent appearance              |
| Last reviewed       | Last organisational assessment      |
| Outstanding matters | Issues requiring attention          |
| Status              | Pending, featured, reviewed         |

This would prevent roles from being overlooked when different AI systems generate the chronicles.

### 4. Separate storytelling from editorial compilation

I strongly recommend preserving three distinct production artefacts:

**A. Master Episode Framework**

Defines the permanent rules and structure.

**B. Daily Chronicle**

The actual story generated for one working day.

**C. Episode Continuity Record**

Records the information required to maintain continuity between daily chronicles.

This separation will become increasingly important as Classies Chronicles develops into a larger multimedia production.

# PART 4 — ADDITIONAL RECOMMENDATIONS

There are five improvements I would strongly recommend implementing alongside the updated proposal.

## 1. Introduce a Chronicle Continuity Ledger

This is probably the most valuable addition for long-term production.

Rather than relying exclusively on previous stories, maintain a structured record that every AI can consult.

For example:

| Field                | Description                          |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Episode ID           | Unique episode reference             |
| Production Day       | Day 1–10                             |
| Principal Characters | Characters featured                  |
| Current Location     | Actual story location                |
| Probe Coordinate     | Millennium timezone coordinate       |
| Mission Status       | Preparing, deployed, returning, etc. |
| Discoveries          | Important findings                   |
| Decisions            | Decisions made                       |
| Unresolved Matters   | Outstanding storylines               |
| Canon Changes        | Approved changes                     |
| Source References    | Supporting resources                 |

This becomes particularly important when switching between AI platforms.

**Recommendation:** Eventually maintain this as a repository file, preferably a structured JSON document alongside human-readable Markdown records.

## 2. Establish a distinction between the production calendar and fictional calendar

The Classies Chronicles universe operates across different periods.

Consequently, every chronicle should maintain three distinct temporal references:

| Temporal reference        | Purpose                                                                   |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Production date           | When the chronicle is generated                                           |
| Millennium probe date     | When and where the fictional expedition occurs                            |
| Historical/reference date | Relevant date associated with a character's memories or historical events |

This prevents inconsistencies when the team moves between historical and future environments.

## 3. Introduce a research-to-story quality gate

Before generating a chronicle, the AI should establish that it has sufficient information.

For example:

* Has the correct production day been established?
* Has the latest archive been consulted?
* Is the Bible reading correct?
* Has the Verse of the Day been verified?
* Is the Health & Safety lesson authentic?
* Are the characters' responsibilities accurate?
* Does the storyline respect established canon?

If an important source is unavailable, the AI should disclose the limitation rather than inventing information.

## 4. Establish a standard narrative length

I recommend retaining the proposed three-to-four-minute reading duration.

This is approximately 450–600 words, depending on dialogue and sentence structure.

It is a good balance between:

* Daily reading.
* Voice narration.
* Comic adaptation.
* Social media distribution.
* Eventual episode compilation.

The length should be flexible when a particular day's developments genuinely require more or less space.

## 5. Introduce a production pipeline

The long-term workflow should be:

**RESEARCH → DAILY CHRONICLE → REVIEW → APPROVAL → VISUAL PRODUCTION → ARCHIVING → EPISODE COMPILATION → PUBLICATION**

This separates creative development from editorial approval.

It also creates opportunities for future automation without making the storytelling process dependent on any particular AI platform.

10. EDUBEAM CANON (RULES OF THE FICTIONAL TECHNOLOGY)
======================================================================

The Edubeam works somewhat like Star Trek teleportation with an added time dimension that allows probing time travel. Everything here is FICTION unless stated.

10.1 Components (use these names consistently; proposal)
  Blue Crown Core (processing/control); Temporal Coordinate Engine (target date, time, place); Materialisation Chamber (enclosure); Edubeam Plasma (energy/charge); Blue Crown Comms (communication across time); Stabilisation Matrix (materialisation and retrieval); Recovery Interface (emergency retrieval and monitoring).

10.2 Operation
  - Only one person goes through the chamber at a time; date, time and coordinates are adjusted slightly between people.
  - The team's mission: find and bring back anything that could support Koware's products, services and lifestyle packages. Teams may materialise far apart and need time to find each other; sometimes a member turns up on TV or in a controversy and is rescued just in time.
  - Staff are charged with Edubeam particles and remain in the probe zone until the charge wears off; they then return to the enclosure (Twelve Monkeys strategy).
  - The R&D lab specialist stays behind to monitor equipment and signals. He can programme objects to materialise near the team (for example a vehicle from a 3D design) when asked over the Blue Crown comms. This is not always instant: networks fail, signals garble, and anomalies (funny, scary or comic) follow.
  - Devices must be charged; Edubeam plasma can recharge them. If the charge runs out the team is retracted automatically to the chamber. If the chamber is not prepared, the person could land in limbo or in uncontrolled conditions. Self-induced retraction exists but can take years to master.
  - Short-distance teleportation is possible while charged: it looks like a superpower but drains the charge.
  - The founder, after years of study and repeated exposure to the London Edubeam, can now control his own teleportation and materialisation to a limited extent without the chamber (depends on focus).

10.3 Why people return to the same chamber (proposed explanation for approval)
  The Edubeam is set to a specific vibration frequency that lands people in the chamber. Date, time and coordinates must target the person at their exact location and time. Researchers assume that two objects cannot occupy the same space at the same time (real-science analogy: the Pauli exclusion principle for identical fermions). Optional canon device: every traveller receives a Temporal Materialisation Signature (TMS) linking identity, charge, origin chamber, coordinates and stabilisation frequency; the return chamber searches for that signature. Failure modes include: corrupted frequency, occupied chamber, traveller outside the designated coordinates, communication failure, disturbance, or the chamber no longer existing. Sometimes people can be beamed directly into an existing Edubeam facility in the future if one exists at that location (Edubeam is more common in the far future, but without time travel); locals may then question the team suspiciously, and their clothing often gives them away if they prepared poorly.
  The team must be at the exact location at the exact time each day to receive the update and be beamed to the next date and location. When they stay in the same place they see plants growing and buildings rising in real time before materialising. Travellers report feeling every emotion at once, which can make a beaming look funny. Early trials landed subjects in the next room or the garden, never very far.

10.4 Distance, objects and the 250-year threshold
  - Small objects within the same timezone: manageable, minimal errors.
  - Larger objects, or any object pushed through time: the further into the past or future, the more risk of deformation, damage or loss.
  - Blue Crown R&D's operating threshold: 250 years either way is the safe limit for objects. Beyond it, risks are very high (malformed materialisation). It is a fictional operating limit.
  - Communication also degrades with distance. Never use the Edubeam during solar flares, cosmic events, electromagnetic disturbance or thunderstorms. Outcomes could be catastrophic (an unknown place, an unknown time, even a missing limb).
  - If the probe date lies beyond 250 years, the story must acknowledge the elevated risk (special authorisation, no objects, shorter stays, or a deliberately dangerous mission).

10.5 Daily timezone update
  Each morning the R&D lab specialist updates the timezone from the probe endpoint. The team, still deployed, is instantly moved to the new probe date. They receive updated details, news headlines, the verse of the day, Bible-study areas and safety tips. Because the probe can move through several years in a day: a person met young may be married with children the next day, retired the day after, then old and grey; or the reverse, even before they were born. Team members may want to advise a younger person about their future (governed by Section 10.8).

10.6 Messages and comms
  Messages into the future are easy; messages into the past need the Edubeam and careful targeting of place and time. A message left with Koware in the past can be retrieved by the team through a network or a Koware Asylum in their area. Solar and electromagnetic disturbances distort comms.

10.7 Plausibility framework (to make it believable; inspiration only)
  The founder wants the time-travel factor explained with reference to existing research. Real concepts the stories may borrow, clearly flagged as inspiration rather than proof that such travel is possible: relativistic time dilation; closed timelike curves and the self-consistency principle; Hawking's chronology protection conjecture (a natural reason the technology has limits); many-worlds / branching timelines; wormhole and exotic-matter theory; quantum teleportation (which transfers quantum state, not matter) and entanglement (which cannot send information faster than light); the Pauli exclusion principle (10.3). Use them in R&D dialogue, never as claims in the Verification Register.

10.8 Timeline model (proposed canon decision, needs approval)
  "Mutable but resistant": small interventions can change outcomes; large ones are damped; the team's origin anchor (Section 10.3) is how they come back to the version of the present they left. This supports the founder's example in which a team, after seeing a worse future headline (an oil-rig disaster with a major spill), returns to the past, hosts safety workshops with the companies involved, and comes home to a better headline. Rules for interventions: help is welcome, harm is prohibited; no personal gain; keep a low profile; every intervention is logged for the report.

10.9 Return, screening and rule-breaking
  On return, staff go through decontamination, then MRI and X-ray screening. Teams found with illegal objects or information are interrogated. Smuggling attempts (swallowed items, items under the tongue, other places) and misuse are recurring story material: trying to get lottery numbers, to find or change one's own family or past, and so on. Some members end up banned from the Edubeam for breaching the terms and conditions.
  Vows of silence may exist between team members. A truth kept quiet (for example, someone finds out a partner cheated but chooses to say nothing) may surface in later episodes, with surprises. Management may uncover hidden information and respond according to the content and the reason it was hidden.
  On the weekend between handover and next departure, outgoing and incoming teams may meet and share dos and don'ts and open issues that may never reach the official report (smuggled notes, requests to check on someone or something at a given time and place; not always possible if the probe is years off).

10.10 Missed windows and extended stays
  If a team member misses the daily Edubeam window, they wait until the next day; they are usually brought back to base before regrouping. A message can be sent to the future if the team is in the future. If a member genuinely does not want to return, the Corporate Manager may go through the Edubeam with a team to find and retract them. Complications (arrest, kidnapping, hostage) are handled in the "Chronicles of Cupid Black", a nested story inside the main episode, only when needed.
  A team could, in principle, spend a whole chapter (four episodes) in the Edubeam, but this can slow the story unless the team is a trained permanent taskforce and regulators.

10.11 Safety rules for the stories (use them as dramatic constraints)
  Never beam in unstable conditions. Never proceed on incomplete or unauthenticated instructions. Monitor charge. Keep contact with the R&D specialist. Never assume the return chamber is ready without confirmation. Report missing members immediately. Never carry unauthorised objects. A single communication channel must not be able to authorise a return without independent verification.

Overview

The key design principles are:

- The framework is generic and must not be tied to a specific date, season, episode, event, mystery, or destination.
- Each episode lasts ten working days and always begins on a Monday and ends on the following Friday.
- Only one chronicle is generated for each working day.
- The ten daily chronicles are later collected, ordered, edited, and compiled into one complete Classies Chronicles episode.
- The story must be driven by a single evolving storyline rather than by repetitive daily routines.
- The bible study, verse of the day, health and safety lessons, news, Koware activities, diary records, and Edubeam missions must all contribute to one integrated narrative.

Core production structure

The proposed structure of the Classies Chronicles is generally:

- 1 season per year
- 5 chapters/terms per year
- 8 weeks per chapter
- 2 weeks per episode
- 1 day per part

This means an episode is not a single monolithic story; it is a sequential set of ten daily chronicles, each covering one working day within the two-week episode.

Production rule

An episode always begins on a Monday and ends on the Friday of the following week.

The ten working days are:

- Week 1: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday
- Week 2: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday

No daily chronicle is generated for Saturday or Sunday unless expressly requested for a special purpose. Those days remain part of the narrative continuity but are not compulsory production days.

Only one chronicle should be produced for each working day. The daily chronicles are then compiled into a single episode later through curation, editing, and continuity checking.

## 3. Calendar and episode positioning

### 3.1 Season and chapter structure

The general Classies Chronicles structure is:

- One season per year.
- Five chapters/terms per year.
- Eight weeks per chapter.
- Two weeks, or ten working days, per episode.
- One day per chronicle part.

The five annual chapter windows are broadly:

1. Mid-January to mid-March  
2. Mid-March to mid-May  
3. June and July  
4. Mid-August to mid-October  
5. Mid-October to mid-December [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

### 3.2 Millennium Probe requirement

Before writing a chronicle, the generating AI must call:

`https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-probe.php?probe=now`

This endpoint returns, among other values:

- `now_iso`: the current date and time.
- `phase_start_iso`: the start of the current term/chapter.
- `phase_end_iso`: the end of the current term/chapter.
- `phase_index` and `phase_label`: the current term identifier. [buzzjuice](https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-probe.php?probe=now)

The AI must use `phase_start_iso` to identify the **first Monday** of the current term. That Monday is the start of Episode 1 of the term.

Then the AI must calculate the current episode and day as follows:

1. Identify the first Monday on or after `phase_start_iso`.
2. Group working days in blocks of ten: Monday–Friday, then the following Monday–Friday.
3. Determine which ten-working-day block contains `now_iso`.
4. Determine the current day number from 1 to 10.
5. Use that day number as the chronicle’s position within the episode.

For example, if the first Monday of the term is Day 1, then the following Monday is Day 6, and the Friday after it is Day 10. The weekend between the two Fridays is a continuity period, not a chronicle-production period. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

### 3.3 Probe date and historical date

The chronicle must also consult the Millennium Timezone endpoints:

- Historical date: `https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-timezone.php?endpoint=historical`
- Probe date: `https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-timezone.php?endpoint=probe`
- Probe-specific date, where applicable: `https://buzzjuice.net/shared/millennium-timezone/millennium-timezone.php?endpoint=probe&probe_iso={probe_iso}` [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

These values establish the historical reference context, the current Edubeam probe context, and the temporal setting for the expedition storyline. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

- Five related local headlines: one historical, three current, and one probe-related. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

## 8. Role spotlight and organizational checks

The organizational chart assigns aliases and episode check days to Koware roles. A check day indicates when a role should be placed in the narrative spotlight or audited through the story. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/Organizational_Chart_Structure-Aliased_Checks.txt)

The format is:

`Role [Alias] Days`

For example, `Corporate Manager [Alias: Enka Yamoah] Day 4` means the Corporate Manager is a featured role on Day 4 of an episode. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/Organizational_Chart_Structure-Aliased_Checks.txt)

Rules:

- Use the check-day value to determine which role or roles receive narrative focus.
- A day may focus on one or two roles; use up to four only when the story genuinely requires it. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)
- Do not force every role into every episode.
- A role may recur when the storyline, news, or mission makes that appropriate. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)
- Check days beyond Day 10 refer to the equivalent day in the next episode: Day 11 means Day 1 of the next episode, Day 15 means Day 5, and Day 20 means Day 10. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)
- The Corporate Manager’s routine should remain flexible and diary-driven rather than identical every day. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

## 9. Edubeam missions

The Edubeam is the established fictional mechanism for teleportation and temporal research. Its capabilities, limitations, risks, and procedures must follow the existing Classies Chronicles and Koware Management canon. Each mission should have:

- A clear research or operational objective.
- Assigned personnel.
- A probe location and temporal coordinate.
- A deployment status: preparing, deployed, continuing, returning, retrieved, or under review.
- Communication and equipment status.
- Discoveries, incidents, and unresolved matters. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

## 11. Continuity record

At the end of every daily chronicle, produce a structured continuity record containing:

- Production day and episode-day number.
- Current term and episode position.
- Principal characters and spotlighted roles.
- Locations and temporal coordinates.
- Bible reading and Verse of the Day references.
- Health & Safety lesson.
- Historical, current, and probe headlines used.
- Diary-derived story elements.
- Mission status and discoveries.
- Organizational developments.
- Open questions and unresolved threads.
- New canon proposals, clearly marked as proposals rather than established canon. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

## 12. Mandatory prohibitions

The generating AI must not:

- Generate all ten chronicles unless explicitly requested.
- Assume a specific episode, date, mission, or storyline.
- Invent Bible readings, verses, Health & Safety courses, organizational roles, or historical facts.
- Misquote scripture or fabricate news reports.
- Present fictional future events as real news.
- Contradict the organizational chart or established character canon.
- Introduce unsupported Edubeam capabilities.
- Silently convert a proposed development into established canon.
- Repeat prior content without advancing the episode. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

***

## 13. Manual episode compilation

After all ten chronicles exist, a human editor should:

1. Arrange them in working-day order.
2. Check character, mission, temporal, and organizational continuity.
3. Remove repetition and improve transitions.
4. Verify all factual, biblical, safety, and news references.
5. Confirm that unresolved threads are intentional.
6. Compile the ten parts into one polished episode.
7. Archive the daily chronicles, continuity records, sources, and final episode. [raw.githubusercontent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cupidblack/Koware_Management/refs/heads/main/BlueCrown/Laboratory/development/classies_chronicles_episode_framework_proposal/Classies_Chronicles-Proposed_Episode_Framework_v0.01.05.txt)

***

## 14. Governing principle

The Classies Chronicles universe belongs to its established canon, archives, characters, and community—not to whichever AI generates a particular part. Each daily chronicle is one narrative building block; the manually compiled episode is the complete work.
