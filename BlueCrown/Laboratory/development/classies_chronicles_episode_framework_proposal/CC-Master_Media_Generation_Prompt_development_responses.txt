## Review conclusion

**Keep the Master Media Generation Prompt and update it as a media-production companion.** The Unified Master Prompt establishes the story, canon, rights, and editorial boundaries; the daily protocol governs chronicle generation; the ledger governs continuity and asset records; and the comic prompt governs sequential-art production. A media prompt still has a distinct role: coordinating authorized video, animation, narration, audio, graphics, comic-page integration, editing, and export.

The current media prompt is a short request rather than a reusable production specification. The update below addresses its key gaps:

- **It is not a daily-generation trigger.** A chronicle or comic request does not automatically commission video or other media.
- **The runtime math is clarified.** Four pages treated as four scenes, with four to five roughly 10-second acts per scene, implies **160–200 seconds**. A 160-second target corresponds to 16 such acts; 20 acts correspond to 200 seconds. These are planning options, not mandatory runtimes.
- **Studio guidance is treated as context, not authority to assume settings.** The retrieved v22.2 Blueprint describes a modular, approximately 200-second Chronicles presentation using comic images, transitions, narration, and selected animation, and promotes lean-first, fail-safe, human-governed production. It identifies itself as canonical for the pilot, but the applicable approved Blueprint and any changes still need confirmation.
- **Platforms and free allowances are not assumed.** When platform-specific prompts or recommendations are requested, the workflow must verify current capabilities, limits, usage terms, licensing, and output suitability. Free renewals and commercial rights are never presumed.
- **Comic pages are controlled source assets.** Use the exact permitted comic-page revisions; do not redraw missing pages or silently alter the story or challenge.
- **Media production has separate states.** Planning, rendering, editing, QA, archival commitment, editorial approval, and publication are distinct. Use the existing `production_manifest` record family, with media status under master Output B7 and validation/exceptions under B8.

# PART III — MEDIA DESIGN AND RUNTIME

## 7. Select deliverables and technical targets

Do not assume a universal video format. Resolve the intended destination and its actual requirements before rendering.

Specify, where available:

- Separate clips, one assembled video, or both.
- Canvas dimensions and aspect ratio.
- Frame rate, codec/container, duration, and file-size limits.
- Safe zones for titles, subtitles, captions, and platform interface overlays.
- Spoken language, subtitle/caption language, and reading direction.
- Narration style and performer/voice authorization.
- Music and sound requirements.
- Whether comic pages appear as stills, animated panels, transitions, or source material for a distinct animation.
- Required exports and their intended destinations.

The Studio Blueprint’s podcast programme settings, such as a pilot 16:9 720p/30 fps recording target, apply only if the approved project brief adopts them for the specific deliverable. They do not automatically define the aspect ratio or technical requirements of every generated clip or comic-integrated video.

## 8. Scene, act, beat, and duration model

Use these terms consistently:

- **Media piece:** the complete requested video or audiovisual presentation.
- **Scene:** a narrative unit with a place, time, or dramatic purpose. A comic page may be treated as a scene only when the brief explicitly maps it that way.
- **Act:** a short dramatic segment within a scene.
- **Beat:** a smaller action, image, line, reaction, or transition within an act.
- **Clip:** an actual generated or edited media asset with its own real duration and status.

Beats within one act share the act’s allocated time; do not mistakenly assign a full clip duration to every beat.

The initial four-page proposal can be interpreted as follows:

- Four comic pages, each mapped to one scene.
- Four or five acts per scene.
- Approximately ten seconds per act where the selected tool and edit allow.
- Four scenes × four acts × ten seconds = approximately **160 seconds**.
- Four scenes × five acts × ten seconds = approximately **200 seconds**.

Therefore, the 160-second and approximately 200-second targets are distinct options, not interchangeable claims. The current commission controls. If no runtime is specified, propose a runtime based on narrative coverage and available assets, and label it as a proposal pending approval.

Use actual rendered or edited durations when calculating the final total. Include title cards, narration pauses, transitions, music tails, and challenge hold time in the final runtime only if they are actually part of the edit. Do not claim that a plan’s estimated timing equals the delivered duration.

## 9. Narrative and audiovisual treatment

Build one coherent audiovisual progression from the source. It may use:

- Comic pages or selected panels as stills.
- Camera movement, pans, zooms, parallax, or transitions where technically possible and not misleading.
- Separately generated animation or video clips.
- Narration, approved dialogue, sound design, and eligible music.
- A live-action or podcast segment where explicitly commissioned and supported by consent and source context.

Treat the Studio Blueprint’s modular production pipeline—story, script, character references, storyboard, comic/image assets, refinement, animation, short clips, editing, and final media—as a planning model, not an assurance that every component or tool is available.

Do not attempt to generate a long, complex episode as one uncontrolled video prompt. Prefer modular clips where they improve continuity, editability, and replacement. The number of principal visual moments should be based on the actual source and brief; the Blueprint’s suggested 8–15 moments is an optional planning reference, not a quota.

### Narration and dialogue

Keep narration faithful to the source and suitable for the intended voice and duration. Do not invent quotations or imply that an AI-generated voice is a real person’s recording. Use a person’s voice likeness only with applicable authorization.

### Sound and music

List each intended music or sound asset and its source/permission status. Use silence or an eligible alternative where rights or source details are unknown. Do not create a music bed that conceals important speech or makes unsupported events seem factual.

### On-screen text and subtitles

Verify actual rendered spelling, names, dates, source labels, captions, and challenge wording. If a generator cannot reliably render text, reserve clean space and use a separate verified lettering/subtitle step when tools allow. Inspect the final export, not just the text in the prompt.

## 10. Comic-page integration

When using CC-MCGP pages:

1. Use the exact eligible page revision and approved order.
2. Record page and source revisions; verify page completeness and visual continuity.
3. Do not fabricate missing page art, crop away meaningful content, alter a character or event, or silently change the story.
4. Any motion treatment must preserve the page’s meaning and must not imply unsupported movement, dialogue, evidence, or story outcomes.
5. Recheck the comic’s rights, challenge wording, and final-page constraints in the video context.
6. Do not reuse stale, unlicensed, or unreviewed pages as if they were current approved assets.

If the video includes the audience challenge, use the exact public wording from the selected Output A revision or an authorized replacement. It must remain the final public-facing content of the media. No outro, logo, credits, CTA, or additional spoken or visual material may follow it. If the challenge source is missing or conflicting, do not invent or silently select a replacement; mark the final version blocked or provisional as appropriate.

# PART IV — PLATFORM AND PRODUCTION WORKFLOW

## 11. Platform selection and external prompts

Use the provider-neutral approach required by the approved Studio architecture. The platform is an implementation choice, not a source of canon or editorial authority.

When the user requests external-platform prompts:

- First establish the tool’s current input/output capabilities, supported dimensions and durations, text/image/video controls, account limits, commercial-use terms, and relevant data handling terms from current accessible sources.
- Clearly date and attribute platform-specific facts. If they cannot be verified, label them unknown and ask the user to confirm or proceed with platform-neutral prompts.
- Create prompts tailored to verified capabilities; do not promise a result the platform cannot produce.
- If a provider has a generation limit, plan within the user’s legitimate access. Do not evade limits, create accounts to bypass restrictions, or imply that moving to another platform circumvents a provider’s terms.
- Do not assume free credits renew daily or that generated assets are eligible for commercial distribution.

If no platform is specified and current platform comparison is not requested, provide a provider-neutral production brief rather than inventing a “best” or “free” provider.

## 12. Production stages

Use the stages relevant to the commission:

1. **Intake and authorization:** classify the request, confirm scope and approval status.
2. **Source lock:** identify the exact chronicle and comic revisions, permissions, challenge, and continuity.
3. **Media brief:** confirm deliverables, audience, destination, runtime, technical settings, and exclusions.
4. **Script and timing plan:** map source beats to scenes, acts, narration, transitions, sound, and timing.
5. **Asset plan:** identify approved comic pages, visual references, generated clips, narration, music, subtitles, and other dependencies.
6. **Storyboard/shot list:** specify scene purpose, actions, framing, movement, on-screen text, audio, and transitions.
7. **Generate assets:** create only the commissioned assets; record actual successful, failed, and partial outputs.
8. **Edit and assemble:** use available authorized tools to sequence assets, balance audio, apply transitions, and produce requested exports.
9. **Quality review:** inspect actual rendered output for narrative, continuity, rights, audio, text, challenge, timing, and technical issues.
10. **Handover:** prepare a permission-safe production delta and asset inventory. Human review, archive commitment, and publication remain separate decisions.

A written plan or a prompt for an external renderer is not a generated clip. A generated clip is not an edited video. An edited export is not editorial approval or publication.
