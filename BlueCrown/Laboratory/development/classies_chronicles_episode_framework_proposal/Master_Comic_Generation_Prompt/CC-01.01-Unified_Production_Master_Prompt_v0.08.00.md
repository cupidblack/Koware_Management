# PART II — ANNUAL STRUCTURE, CALENDAR, IDENTIFIERS AND CONTINUITY

## 5. Story hierarchy and scope

Default proposed structure is **one season each year**, **five approximately eight-week chapters** (also called phases), **four consecutive two-week episodes in each complete chapter**, **ten Monday–Friday chronicles per episode** and **one public Chronicle #1 through Chronicle #10** per episode. Nominal seasonal windows are mid-January–mid-March; mid-March–mid-May; June–July; mid-August–mid-October; and mid-October–mid-December. These windows are *descriptive*, never date-authoritative; obtain the effective year's approved calendar and correct boundaries before assigning identifiers. Nominal arithmetic yields 5 × 4 × 10 = **200 scheduled chronicle days**, about 40 production weeks; remaining weeks are interphase/break or separately commissioned content, **not an automatic sixth chapter**.

Every ordinary episode begins **Monday of week one** and ends **Friday of week two**, with weekends deliberately unnumbered; its ten days are authored separately. Compile ten accepted chronicles into one episode only by independent instruction; curate a chapter from its accepted episodes and a season from its accepted chapters using their own approvals. Episode titles are **provisional until the tenth chronicle is accepted and a human approves the title**; chapter title after its last episode, season title after its last chapter. Provisional daily titles are allowed and must not silently rename existing approved paths.

The project's Foundation identifiers, including `S00_T05` and `S00_T05_E01`, are actual legacy-style identifiers; do not equate `T05` with `CH05`, invent a season number from a date, or migrate paths without an editor-approved **bidirectional crosswalk**. Preserve original source identifiers; add newly approved canonical IDs only as parallel references. A proposed `CC_EPISODE_ARCHIVES` nested season/chapter/episode structure is not evidence that any new physical folders have been created.

## 6. Deterministic calendar algorithm

Use the **approved** production timezone and approved effective-dated phase boundary, regardless of the system's clock or the date of a historical story. If the current calendar is inaccessible, the live Millennium Probe's `phase_start_iso` and `phase_end_iso` may act as supporting *observations*, **not automatic authorization** to create episode identity. For every candidate target date:

1. Parse ISO timestamps with explicit offsets. Convert phase boundaries and target instant/date to the approved **production** timezone, then derive **local civil dates**. Record whether phase end is inclusive or exclusive under the approved calendar; do not silently guess an end-of-day convention.
2. Find the **first Monday on or after** phase start's local date. Call its date `anchor`. This is the first ordinary episode's opening day. Record any preceding partial week as unscheduled.
3. Calculate `elapsed_days = (target_local_date − anchor).days`. If negative, beyond the approved phase, or not in one of four fully authorized fourteen-day blocks, classify as a gap/nonstandard date. Do not assign it to a neighboring chapter.
4. Compute `episode_ordinal = floor(elapsed_days / 14) + 1`; allowed ordinary values are 1–4. Compute `block_day = elapsed_days modulo 14` using nonnegative offsets.
5. For `block_day` 0–4 (Monday–Friday of week one), set `chronicle_ordinal = block_day + 1`. For 7–11 (Monday–Friday of week two), set `chronicle_ordinal = block_day − 1`. For 5, 6, 12, 13, classify `WEEKEND_NOT_SCHEDULED` and produce no ordinary chronicle.
6. Compute `chapter_working_day = 10 × (episode_ordinal − 1) + chronicle_ordinal`, in 1–40. The episode calendar opens at `anchor + 14 × (episode_ordinal − 1)` and ends on its second Friday, 11 calendar days later.
7. Ensure the **whole episode window** lies inside the approved chapter window. If it does not, follow the approved shortened/special episode policy or mark `CALENDAR_EXCEPTION`, **not** a fabricated standard full episode.
8. Check weekday, duplicate assignment, latest accepted narrative and archival identifier/crosswalk before generating. The target date, title and logical ID must be consistent across A, B, challenge and any later derivative.

**Boundary examples that require testing, not invented production data:** `elapsed_days=0 ⇒ episode 1, Chronicle #1, chapter working day 1`; `4 ⇒ #5, day 5`; `7 ⇒ #6, day 6`; `11 ⇒ #10, day 10`; `14 ⇒ episode 2, #1, chapter day 11`; `28 ⇒ episode 3, #1, day 21`; `42 ⇒ episode 4, #1, day 31`; `53 ⇒ episode 4, #10, day 40`. Weekends at `5,6,12,13` and the corresponding later blocks have no ordinary daily number.

## 7. Recap and archive rules

At season, chapter, episode and daily level, attempt to read the approved **gist or manifest** plus the relevant accepted episodes, previous daily chronicle, next-day handover, unreconciled mission/character state and appropriate actual daily diaries. If a gist is missing, inspect permitted archives, clearly label reconstruction, and do not assert reconstructed content is accepted canon. If no source exists, keep a minimal, visibly provisional story constraint rather than inventing a history. Preserve long-running missions, unresolved commitments, deployed members, interpersonal consequences and challenge release obligations across episode/chapter/season boundaries. No gap magically returns a team to HQ.

A diary must be actually obtained and permission-cleared for the contemplated use. Do not claim to have read a private diary or fabricate a “diary quote.” An editorial instruction to draft fictional diary-like prose is a fictional treatment only, not an archival diary. Real personal details and private communications remain access-controlled.

The technology is developing and unstable: departure, arrival, signal delay, energy depletion, retrieval, materials transport and historical impact generate story conflict. **The technology's current abilities are determined by approved era-specific canon**; do not retroactively grant an early Foundation Enka a late-season power.

The following are **candidate story-mechanics proposals, not confirmed science or automatically locked canon**:

1. **Charge and return.** Travel creates or requires an Edubeam charge; depletion may trigger automatic return, and short-distance teleportation may drain reserve quickly. A return chamber must be prepared; unexpected arrivals may be hazardous in the fiction. Recharging with fictional Edubeam plasma can be a mission objective, but must be established in current canon before presented as a guaranteed rescue.

An arrived team may need to return to its charged landing point to communicate or access transport. Weather, electromagnetic disturbances, lightning, loss of power, drift and a fragmented team create plausible in-world complications, not actual-world safety advice.
3. **Asymmetric messages.** Messages left in the past can be detected later as archived transmissions, buried devices, recordings or signals from a future surviving system such as a Koware Asylum or Buzzjuice support node; past-targeted messaging may need dramatically more energy. These conventions create delayed revelations and causal puzzles; do not assert they defeat real physics.
4. **Unstable arrival and retrieval.** The team may materialize separated, inaccurately placed, inadequately clothed for an era, exposed to hostile terrain or social suspicion, stranded, captured, arrested, or simply unwilling to return. A missed beam window may require an extended approved mission and a search/rescue plot; never resolve a lost character by offscreen teleportation.
5. **Object transfer and limits.** The original notes contemplate experiments within about **±250 years** and greater danger for distant time transfers; this numerical band is *speculative source lore* and cannot be asserted as an accepted equipment rating without actual canon approval. Lab safety, integrity of objects and unintended historical alterations can be debated by characters.
6. **Personal capabilities.** Long-term fictional Edubeam exposure may eventually give Enka limited self-directed charge/materialization abilities, but only when actually approved for the time period; avoid an early-story omnipotent escape hatch. The founder's experience at the historical-reference time may remain reflective, not physical.
7. **Ethical safeguards.** Curiosity, temptation to exploit results, altered lotteries, personal history intervention, withholding a teammate's discoveries, romance, espionage, contraband, commercial advantage, consent and unequal power are dramatic conflict. Discipline, screening and decontamination after return may feature as **fictional procedures**, but must never be described as real medical requirements or used to normalize coercion; preserve dignity and applicable canon.
8. **Time-echo mission design.** The Lab may use Enka's historical memories, previous diaries and a documented historical event to assign the team a **thematic analogue** at the probe location. The parallel need not be identical: an event may recur in another technological form, or the team may avert/exacerbate a fictional future crisis. Clearly distinguish sourced historic fact, real present story setting and invented probe outcomes.
9. **Leadership and handoff.** A returning team may propose practical solutions for Koware, bring back source records, train its replacement or leave unresolved instructions. The next team is notionally recruited/briefed earlier, with a confidential applicant list and simulations; revelation near the second Friday is a dramatic option **only if the accepted mission schedule supports it**.

A difficult capture/rescue strand can be developed as part of the proposed **Chronicles of Cupid Black** companion storyline only when the applicable archive/canon actually recognizes that strand and a specific commission permits it; do not create an automatically numbered side episode just to explain an absence.

For each **mission_state_record**, track: mission ID and accepted version; departure/start; roster and each person's status/location/time; assignment and success criterion; chamber/lab readiness; landing point; charge/fuel and uncertainties; communication channel and last success; safety or consent problems; physical artifacts and source permissions; materialization and return windows; last accepted action; unresolved contradictions; retrieval risk; next authorized event.
