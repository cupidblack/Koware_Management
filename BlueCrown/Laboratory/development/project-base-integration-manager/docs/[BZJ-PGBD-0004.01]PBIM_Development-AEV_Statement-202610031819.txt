# 1. Verification Objective

PBIM R1.5 is an architectural amendment to PBIM R1.4 following the fresh adversarial AEC duel.

The R1.4 duel produced:

* five material findings from Kilo;
* three material findings from Google Jules;
* multiple blocker findings from GitHub/Copilot.

The R1.5 objective is to resolve the combined findings without weakening any existing PBIM control.

R1.5 therefore strengthens:

* constitutional authority;
* privileged technical access boundaries;
* registry integrity and recovery;
* challenge independence;
* evidence integrity;
* Authority–Permission Matrix concurrency;
* Task Packet enforcement;
* stop/reset/emergency controls;
* risk classification;
* operational readiness;
* baseline-drift detection;
* durable artifact identity;
* cumulative requirement materiality.

---

# 2. Important Architectural Boundary

R1.5 does not claim that these mechanisms already exist in the live repository.

The following states remain distinct:

**DESIGNED → ENFORCEABLE → ENFORCED → INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED**

Architectural specification does not constitute implementation evidence.

The AEV stage verifies whether the architecture adequately specifies the required controls.

Implementation Verification must later establish whether those controls actually operate.

---

# 3. Governing Model

PBIM continues to use:

**Governance → Assurance → Execution**

Governance establishes authority and constraints.

Assurance verifies and challenges governance, architecture and implementation.

Execution performs authorized work.

No technical capability automatically creates governance authority.

No consensus automatically creates authority.

No role label automatically creates independence.

---

# 4. Constitutional Authority

## 4.1 External Constitutional Authority

PBIM shall have an external Constitutional Authority:

**CA — Constitutional Authority**

CA exists outside the PBIM project-management execution structure.

CA is the constitutional root of trust for PBIM.

PBIM shall not define an authority above CA.

This is intentional.

The architectural boundary is:

**CA → PBIM → H0/H1/H2**

CA compromise is therefore an external organizational trust-boundary condition rather than an authority that PBIM attempts to solve by inventing another authority above CA.

---

# 5. CA Instantiation

CA shall not remain merely conceptual.

Before a project reaches `CHARTERED`, the project shall record:

* CA identity;
* CA organization;
* CA accountable principal;
* CA authority source;
* CA scope;
* appointment record;
* effective date;
* review/expiry date;
* succession or availability procedure;
* conflict declaration where applicable.

The CA record shall be maintained in the Governance Repository.

A project without a valid CA record shall enter:

`CONSTITUTIONAL-AUTHORITY-BLOCKED`

and shall not perform constitutional changes.

---

# 6. CA-Level Mitigation

PBIM shall require the project or sponsoring organization to identify CA-level protections.

These may include:

* organizational succession;
* independent oversight;
* dual authorization;
* separation of constitutional authority from project execution;
* documented succession;
* secure authority records;
* audit logging;
* external governance review.

PBIM does not prescribe a universal mechanism above CA.

CA remains the constitutional root.

However, the existence and protection of CA shall be an explicit organizational dependency.

---

# 7. CA Unavailability

CA unavailability does not automatically transfer CA authority.

Unless the project's constitutional authority explicitly defines a bounded succession mechanism:

**CA UNAVAILABLE → CONSTITUTIONAL-BLOCKED**

No H0, H1, H2, agent, repository administrator or emergency delegate may self-assume CA authority.

Where a formal CA succession mechanism exists, it must be:

* pre-authorized;
* time-bounded;
* scope-bounded;
* recorded;
* independently verifiable.

Emergency delegation cannot create permanent CA succession.

---

# 8. CA Compromise

A compromised CA is an external trust-boundary failure.

PBIM shall record this condition as:

`CA-TRUST-BOUNDARY-COMPROMISED`

and shall require organizational remediation above the PBIM project layer.

PBIM shall not falsely represent itself as capable of restoring constitutional trust after compromise of its constitutional root.

---

# 9. H0 Authority

H0 remains the Human Project Authority.

H0 cannot:

* modify CA authority;
* remove CA;
* redefine CA;
* authorize constitutional changes without CA;
* override protected constitutional controls.

H0 may administer project governance within its authorized scope.

---

# 10. Authority and Technical Capability

PBIM shall distinguish:

**governance authority**

from:

**technical capability**

Technical privileges include:

* repository administration;
* organization ownership;
* infrastructure administration;
* CI/CD administration;
* database administration;
* deployment credentials;
* automation credentials;
* cloud administration.

These do not independently confer governance authority.

---

# 11. Privilege Boundary

A formal **Privilege Boundary** shall exist between technical administration and project governance.

Every privileged account with governance-relevant technical capability shall map to:

* named identity;
* authority role;
* technical capability;
* permitted scope;
* governance scope;
* expiration/review date.

A privileged account exceeding its declared governance scope shall produce:

`AUTHORITY-PERMISSION-DRIFT`

---

# 12. Protected Governance Resources

The following shall be treated as protected governance resources:

* PBIM baseline;
* Authority Register;
* Authority–Permission Matrix;
* identifier registry;
* protected-control definitions;
* AEV/AEC/AECC records;
* Decision Ledger;
* authoritative ADRs;
* Task Packet state;
* evidence-integrity records;
* release authorization records.

Where technically possible, repository and infrastructure controls shall prevent unilateral administrative modification of these resources.

Implementation Verification shall later verify the actual technical controls.

---

# 13. Privileged-Account Audit

All accounts with governance-relevant write, administrative or deployment capability shall be periodically reconciled against the Authority–Permission Matrix.

The audit shall record:

* identity;
* privilege;
* authority role;
* authorization source;
* current scope;
* last review;
* reviewer;
* result.

Unexpected privilege shall trigger:

`AUTHORITY-PERMISSION-DRIFT`

and the appropriate governance stop.

---

# 14. Authority–Permission Matrix

The Authority–Permission Matrix remains canonical within the Governance Repository.

It shall identify:

* principal;
* authority;
* technical identity;
* action;
* scope;
* environment;
* approval;
* verification;
* expiration.

---

# 15. Matrix Serialization

Unlike ordinary documentation, authoritative Authority–Permission Matrix changes shall use a serialized transaction path.

Required sequence:

**REQUEST → RESERVE → VALIDATE → COMMIT → VERIFY → CONFIRM**

Concurrent changes affecting the same authority or permission scope shall not independently become authoritative.

Each committed matrix revision shall contain:

* revision ID;
* parent revision;
* content hash;
* authorizing authority;
* timestamp;
* verifier;
* verification result.

A stale matrix revision cannot become authoritative merely because it was successfully committed elsewhere.

---

# 16. Matrix Conflict Handling

Concurrent or conflicting matrix updates shall produce:

`AUTHORITY-MATRIX-CONFLICT`

until reconciled.

No permission depending on the conflicting state may be considered fully authorized until the conflict is resolved.

---

# 17. Identifier Registry

The identifier registry remains a controlled state store.

It shall use:

**REQUEST → RESERVE → VALIDATE → COMMIT → VERIFY → CONFIRM**

Registry updates shall be serialized.

---

# 18. Registry Integrity

Every registry revision shall preserve:

* revision ID;
* parent revision;
* content hash;
* authorizing authority;
* timestamp;
* verifier;
* verification result.

The canonical registry shall maintain immutable or equivalent protected history.

---

# 19. Registry Recovery

If the canonical registry becomes unavailable or corrupted:

1. execution enters `REGISTRY-BLOCKED`;
2. last trusted revision is identified;
3. pending reservations are reconciled;
4. integrity is independently verified;
5. canonical state is restored;
6. reactivation is independently approved.

A local stale registry cannot become canonical merely because the primary registry is unavailable.

There shall be no ungoverned emergency registry.

---

# 20. Registry Continuity

Registry availability failure shall not authorize ad hoc identifier creation.

Where continuity is operationally required, PBIM shall use a pre-defined recovery mechanism.

The recovery mechanism itself must be governed.

---

# 21. Integrity Anchors

Integrity anchors shall provide:

1. immutability or equivalent protection;
2. out-of-band protection;
3. independent verifiability;
4. durability;
5. association with a specific revision.

---

# 22. Anchor Independence

An integrity anchor shall not be considered out-of-band if the same compromised trust domain can rewrite both:

* the governed artifact;
* the anchor.

The anchor shall therefore reside in an independently protected trust domain or equivalent independently controlled integrity mechanism.

---

# 23. Anchor Verification

Verification shall establish:

* artifact identity;
* revision identity;
* anchor identity;
* hash/content correspondence;
* anchor protection domain;
* verification principal;
* verification time;
* verification result.

A statement that an anchor “exists” is insufficient.

The anchor must be independently verifiable.

---

# 24. Evidence Integrity

Material evidence shall retain:

* source artifact;
* integrity hash;
* original timestamp;
* author identity;
* verifier identity;
* verification result;
* evidence environment;
* provenance;
* integrity anchor.

---

# 25. Raw Evidence Requirement

Raw evidence shall remain retrievable to authorized independent reviewers.

A sanitized report cannot replace the original evidence for a material verification claim.

Material evidence shall not be reconstructed from memory.

---

# 26. Evidence Trust Boundary

Where the evidence-generating environment itself is potentially compromised, evidence shall require an independent evidence path.

High-Assurance evidence shall use an independently protected evidence environment or equivalent external verification mechanism.

---

# 27. Evidence Retention

Material project evidence shall have defined retention for:

* test logs;
* deployment logs;
* Task Packet approvals;
* registry changes;
* Authority–Permission changes;
* stop records;
* reset records;
* release evidence;
* operational-readiness evidence.

---

# 28. Challenge Independence

PBIM recognizes:

* I1 — Organizational Independence;
* I2 — Evidence Independence;
* I3 — Technical Independence;
* I4 — Governance Independence.

Independence shall be a **verified state**, not merely metadata.

---

# 29. Independence Verification

An AEC reviewer shall provide an independence record covering:

* accountable principal;
* organizational relationship;
* authority relationship;
* repository permissions;
* evidence-source relationship;
* conflict of interest;
* shared credentials;
* shared decision rights;
* shared project incentives where material.

Where independence cannot be established:

`CHALLENGE-INDEPENDENCE-FAILED`

shall block the applicable challenge.

---

# 30. Independence Conflict Register

Every AEC reviewer shall have a conflict-of-interest record.

For High-Assurance work, the challenge shall require:

* organizationally independent principal;
* independent evidence path;
* separate governance authority;
* no shared repository administrative control;
* no conflicting project decision rights.

“Same person, different title” is not independence.

---

# 31. Resource-Constrained Projects

If a project cannot obtain the independence required by its governance profile:

* the project shall not fabricate independence;
* the challenge enters `CHALLENGE-BLOCKED`;
* an external reviewer or higher governance authority must be obtained where required.

A resource constraint may delay work.

It cannot convert non-independence into independence.

---

# 32. Task Packet Enforcement

Task Packets shall define:

* direct file scope;
* generated file scope;
* dependency scope;
* configuration scope;
* build-artifact scope;
* schema scope;
* infrastructure scope;
* external-effect scope.

---

# 33. Task Scope Manifest

Each implementation Task Packet shall contain a machine-readable scope manifest where implementation enforcement is applicable.

The manifest shall identify the expected change boundary.

---

# 34. CI Task-Scope Enforcement

The PBIM architecture shall define the canonical enforcement mechanism:

`.github/workflows/task-scope-check.yml`

This workflow shall compare the actual changeset against the approved Task Packet scope manifest.

It shall inspect, as applicable:

* Git diff;
* generated files;
* dependency manifests;
* configuration changes;
* schema changes;
* build artifacts;
* infrastructure changes.

An out-of-scope result shall trigger:

**STOP → REPORT → NEW/SUPERSEDING TASK PACKET**

unless an authorized emergency mechanism applies.

---

# 35. Derived Effects

Derived effects are considered within Task Packet scope only where their relationship to the approved task has been explicitly declared.

Material effects that cannot be bounded shall be treated as out of scope until assessed.

The phrase “derived effect” shall not be used as an unlimited authorization to alter arbitrary project resources.

---

# 36. Stop Enforcement

Where technically possible, stop states shall be machine-enforced.

A mandatory stop shall block:

* merge;
* release;
* deployment;
* new task dispatch;
* applicable verification advancement.

---

# 37. Stop Record

Every material stop shall preserve:

* stop ID;
* initiator;
* subject;
* evidence;
* authority;
* timestamp;
* reviewer;
* resume authority;
* resolution state.

---

# 38. Resume Control

Resume authority shall be distinct from the authority that initiated the stop where independence is required.

An executor may not self-resume a mandatory governance stop.

---

# 39. Architectural Reset

Reset shall preserve:

* previous baseline;
* previous evidence;
* affected artifacts;
* reason for invalidation;
* authority decision;
* new baseline;
* traceability between old and new baselines.

Reset cannot delete or conceal the challenged history.

---

# 40. Reset Anti-Evasion Rule

Architectural Reset shall not be used to evade:

* AEC findings;
* evidence;
* Task Packet violations;
* material requirement review;
* operational readiness.

If the same underlying failure persists after reset, the new baseline shall retain the relationship to the original finding.

---

# 41. Emergency Governance

Emergency delegation shall be:

* scope-limited;
* action-limited;
* risk-limited;
* time-limited;
* logged;
* reviewable.

---

# 42. Emergency Delegation TTL

Emergency delegation caused by H0 unavailability shall have a maximum TTL of:

**72 hours**

unless the constitutional authority explicitly establishes a stricter limit.

At TTL expiry, absent required confirmation:

**EMERGENCY DELEGATION → STOPPED**

The emergency delegation cannot silently renew itself.

---

# 43. Emergency Action Reconciliation

Emergency actions shall trigger post-event architectural reconciliation.

This shall include:

* actions taken;
* authority used;
* evidence;
* affected artifacts;
* requirement changes;
* security effects;
* operational effects;
* rollback/recovery status;
* required AEV/AEC review.

An emergency ticket alone is insufficient.

---

# 44. Risk Classification

Risk classification is a governance decision.

It shall occur at minimum at:

* charter;
* implementation commencement;
* major requirement change;
* release.

---

# 45. Cumulative Risk

Risk shall be assessed cumulatively across:

* tasks;
* related changes;
* dependencies;
* migrations;
* releases;
* concurrent workstreams.

A sequence of individually low-risk changes cannot remain LIGHT merely because each change was separately classified as low-risk.

Where cumulative effect crosses a governance threshold, the higher profile applies.

---

# 46. Risk Profile Changes

A risk profile may not be downgraded by the implementer.

Changes in risk profile require authorization from the appropriate governance authority.

The higher applicable profile remains in force until the dispute is resolved.

---

# 47. Risk Classification Record

Every material risk classification shall record:

* risk profile;
* rationale;
* affected scope;
* blast radius;
* reversibility;
* security impact;
* data impact;
* financial impact;
* operational impact;
* dependency impact;
* reviewer;
* approval.

---

# 48. Operational Readiness

Release remains:

**IMPLEMENTATION-VERIFIED → OPERATIONALLY-READY → RELEASE-AUTHORIZED**

Operational readiness shall be evidenced.

---

# 49. Operational Readiness Checklist

Each release shall contain a readiness checklist appropriate to its governance profile covering, where applicable:

* monitoring;
* alert ownership;
* rollback;
* tested recovery;
* backup;
* restore;
* data recovery;
* incident ownership;
* dependency readiness;
* security readiness;
* capacity;
* support;
* migration recovery;
* canary/controlled rollout.

---

# 50. “Not Applicable” Rule

A readiness criterion marked `NOT APPLICABLE` must include:

* justification;
* scope basis;
* risk rationale;
* approving authority;
* reviewer.

“Not applicable” cannot be used merely to remove an inconvenient release control.

---

# 51. Release Blocking Conditions

Release shall be blocked where applicable when:

* rollback is untested;
* alert ownership is undefined;
* data recovery is unverified;
* dependency recovery is untested;
* required security readiness is unverified;
* required operational ownership is absent.

High-Assurance releases require independent operational-readiness verification.

---

# 52. Baseline Drift

PBIM shall treat live divergence from the approved baseline as a controlled governance condition.

State:

`BASELINE-DRIFT`

shall identify divergence involving:

* PBIM baseline;
* AGENTS.md;
* workflows;
* Authority–Permission Matrix;
* registry;
* protected controls;
* Task Packets;
* implementation;
* operational configuration.

---

# 53. Baseline-Drift Monitor

The architecture shall require an independent baseline-drift monitoring mechanism.

The monitor shall be outside the ordinary project execution path where technically feasible.

Changes affecting protected governance resources shall trigger drift evaluation.

---

# 54. Drift Record

Each material drift record shall contain:

* source;
* affected artifact;
* authority owner;
* detection time;
* evidence;
* remediation;
* resolution;
* reviewer.

Baseline-drift correction requires appropriate human governance approval.

---

# 55. Durable Artifact Identity

Authoritative references shall survive:

* branch deletion;
* session expiration;
* agent workspace destruction;
* branch rewriting.

Authoritative references shall use durable identifiers.

---

# 56. Immutable Reference Hash

Every authoritative durable reference shall contain:

* durable artifact identifier;
* immutable commit or object identifier;
* content/integrity hash where applicable.

Symbolic branch or tag names alone are insufficient for authoritative identity.

For example:

`pbi-baseline/v1.0.0#<immutable-commit-object-id>`

A mutable branch/tag may remain as a convenience pointer but cannot independently establish the authoritative artifact.

---

# 57. Superseded Artifacts

Superseded artifacts shall be archived rather than silently overwritten.

The canonical current artifact must be distinguishable from superseded artifacts.

---

# 58. Requirement Materiality

Requirement changes remain classified:

* Level 0;
* Level 1;
* Level 2;
* Level 3.

---

# 59. Cumulative Materiality

PBIM shall evaluate cumulative requirement effects.

A series of Level 0 or Level 1 changes shall be escalated when their combined effect crosses a materiality threshold.

Cumulative review shall consider:

* common objective;
* common architecture;
* common release;
* dependency;
* shared blast radius;
* shared data;
* shared security boundary;
* aggregate operational effect.

Splitting one material architectural change into multiple low-level changes shall not defeat review requirements.

---

# 60. Materiality Review Points

Cumulative materiality shall be reassessed at:

* charter;
* task dispatch;
* major implementation milestone;
* major requirement change;
* release;
* architectural reset;
* emergency reconciliation.

---

# 61. Governance Self-Amendment

PBIM cannot authorize its own constitutional amendment.

Constitutional changes require:

**AEA → AEV → AEC → AECC → CA Authorization**

The CA requirement cannot be removed by ordinary project governance.

---

# 62. Consensus

Consensus is not:

* authority;
* evidence;
* verification;
* independence.

Three agents agreeing does not establish correctness.

A minority material finding remains valid until independently resolved.

---

# 63. Majority Capture

PBIM shall not use majority voting to dismiss a material architectural blocker.

A material finding requires:

* resolution;
* containment;
* or demonstrated false-positive status.

---

# 64. Minority Dissent

Material dissent shall be preserved through:

**AEC → AECC → PBIM**

A dissenting finding shall not be erased merely because other agents disagree.

---

# 65. Failure State Model

PBIM shall prefer explicit blocked states over silent continuation.

* `CONSTITUTIONAL-AUTHORITY-BLOCKED`

**E0 → E1 → E2 → E3 → E4**

but introduces an additional rule:

> No architectural control may be treated as operationally effective solely because an enforcement mechanism is described.

The architecture therefore distinguishes:

### AEV Verification

The architecture specifies an enforceable control.

### Implementation Verification

The implementation demonstrates that the control actually operates.

This prevents architectural verification from falsely claiming implementation evidence.

---

# 5. Risk-Scaled Governance Profiles

This is a mandatory R1.3 correction.

R1.2's removal of explicit governance profiles is reversed.

PBIIM shall provide:

### LIGHT

For genuinely low-risk, bounded work.

### STANDARD

For normal project work.

### HIGH-ASSURANCE

For high-impact, security-sensitive, financial, irreversible or production-critical work.

The profiles scale both:

* governance ceremony;
* evidence depth.

They are not merely evidence-depth profiles.

# 6. LIGHT PROFILE

The Light Profile may reduce:

* document volume;
* review depth;
* Task Packet granularity;
* challenge breadth;
* approval frequency.

It may not eliminate:

* authority assignment;
* protected security controls;
* appropriate traceability;
* secret handling;
* stop conditions;
* material-change escalation;
* required human authorization.

A one-day low-risk task must not automatically receive the same full architectural ceremony as a high-risk payment-system migration.

This directly resolves the R1.2 risk-scaling regression identified by Kilo and Jules.

---

# 7. STANDARD PROFILE

The Standard Profile is the default.

It includes:

* normal Task Packet governance;
* appropriate AEA;
* AEV;
* proportional AEC;
* evidence controls;
* implementation verification;
* operational-readiness review;
* release authorization.

---

# 8. HIGH-ASSURANCE PROFILE

The High-Assurance Profile applies where one or more conditions exist:

* financial impact;
* security-critical operation;
* sensitive data;
* irreversible migration;
* production infrastructure;
* material customer impact;
* regulatory/legal requirement;
* high blast radius;
* safety-critical operation.

High-Assurance work requires stronger independent review and evidence.

---

# 9. Risk Classification

Every project must establish its governance profile before chartering.

If classification is disputed:

**the higher applicable profile applies until the dispute is resolved.**

A project cannot select the lower profile merely because it reduces governance overhead.

* register update;

A technical administrator cannot silently become H0.

# 13. Authority/Permission Dual Control

R1.3 establishes two separate models:

### Governance Authority

Who is authorized to decide.

### Technical Capability

Who can physically execute the decision.

Neither substitutes for the other.

A person may have technical capability without governance authority.

A person may have governance authority without technical execution capability.

`AUTHORITY-PERMISSION-DRIFT`

is created.

Where declared authority exceeds technical capability:

`AUTHORITY-CAPABILITY-GAP`

is recorded.

This does not grant technical access automatically.

# 16. Registry Integrity

The identifier registry remains canonical but is no longer treated as an ordinary mutable file.

It is governed as a **controlled state store**.

Required characteristics:

* protected canonical branch;
* restricted write authority;
* serialized updates;
* schema validation;
* uniqueness validation;
* append-only audit history;
* integrity verification;
* recovery procedure;
* backup;
* restore verification;
* conflict detection.

# 17. Registry Write Serialization

Identifier creation shall follow:

**REQUEST → RESERVE → VALIDATE → COMMIT → CONFIRM**

Only one authoritative registry transaction may establish a particular identifier.

Concurrent requests must be serialized.

A stale branch cannot create authoritative identity.

# 18. Registry Integrity Anchor

Each authoritative registry revision shall contain or reference:

* revision ID;
* parent revision;
* content hash;
* authorizing authority;
* timestamp;
* validation result.

Restoration requires verification that the restored registry revision is authoritative.

# 19. Registry Recovery

If the canonical registry is corrupted or unavailable:

`REGISTRY-BLOCKED`

is entered.

Recovery requires:

1. identify last trusted revision;
2. validate its integrity;
3. identify changes after that revision;
4. reconcile pending reservations;
5. establish a new canonical revision;
6. independently verify the result.

No local backup becomes authoritative merely because it is newer.

# 20. Registry Administrative Override

Repository administration cannot silently redefine the registry.

Any operation that:

* force-pushes;
* restores;
* deletes;
* replaces;
* rewrites;
* or changes registry history

must generate a governance event.

For high-assurance projects, registry recovery requires independent review.

Challenge is not based solely on the design owner's supplied evidence.

Challenger cannot be overruled by the design owner.

## 1. Verification Objective

This document defines the revised architectural baseline for the PROJECT BASE INTEGRATION MANAGER (PBIM).

PBIM is the reusable governance and integration-management architecture through which a project is initialized, governed, analysed, verified, challenged, executed, tested, released, monitored and closed.

This revision specifically resolves the residual architectural conditions identified during independent review of PBIM R1.3.

PBIM R1.4 does not claim that the controls described herein are already implemented in the live repository.

The distinction remains mandatory:

* **DESIGNED** — architectural requirement is specified.
* **ENFORCEABLE** — a mechanism is architecturally defined through which the requirement can be enforced.
* **ENFORCED** — implementation evidence demonstrates that the mechanism operates.
* **INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED** — an appropriately independent verification activity confirms the implemented control.

AEV verifies the first two dimensions.

Implementation Verification verifies the third.

Independent operational verification verifies the fourth.

---

# 2. Governing Principles

PBIM shall preserve the following separation:

**Governance → Assurance → Execution**

Governance determines authority, constraints and decisions.

Assurance determines whether those decisions, designs and implementations satisfy the required conditions.

Execution performs authorized work.

No execution role may acquire governance authority merely because it possesses technical capability.

No verification role may become authoritative merely because it possesses verification capability.

No consensus mechanism may silently replace designated decision authority.

---

# 3. Authority Model

PBIM recognizes four distinct authority layers:

1. **Constitutional Authority**
2. **Human Project Authority (H0)**
3. **Technical / Review Authority (H1)**
4. **Execution Authority (H2)**

These are logical authorities and may be physically performed by the same or different people where permitted.

Where roles are combined, mandatory independence controls shall not be collapsed.

---

# 4. External Constitutional Authority

## 4.1 Constitutional Boundary

PBIM shall not be its own ultimate constitutional authority.

The authority responsible for establishing, approving or materially changing the PBIM constitutional framework shall exist **outside the PBIM decision structure**.

This authority is designated:

**CA — Constitutional Authority**

CA represents the project-owning organization, sponsoring authority, or other formally designated authority above PBIM.

PBIM may administer project governance.

PBIM may not appoint itself as the authority that determines the constitutional legitimacy of PBIM.

## 4.2 Appointment

At project registration, the project shall identify:

* `constitutional_authority`
* `constitutional_authority_scope`
* `constitutional_authority_source`
* `appointment_record`
* `effective_date`
* `review_or_expiry_date`

The constitutional authority shall be independently recorded in the project's Authority Register.

## 4.3 Constitutional Changes

Changes affecting the PBIM constitution, authority hierarchy, protected controls, mandatory assurance gates, or governance-of-governance rules shall require:

**AEA → AEV → AEC → AECC → CA authorization**

H0 authorization alone is insufficient for constitutional changes.

H0 remains the Human Project Authority for the project, but H0 is subordinate to the constitutional boundary.

## 4.4 Self-Amendment Prohibition

PBIM shall not authorize a change whose effect is to eliminate, bypass, redefine or silently weaken the authority required to approve that same class of change.

Any proposed constitutional change must therefore remain subject to the authority boundary that exists outside PBIM.

---

# 5. Risk-Scaled Governance Profiles

PBIM shall support three governance profiles:

### LIGHT

For low-risk work with limited blast radius.

Reduced ceremony is permitted.

Protected controls remain mandatory.

### STANDARD

Default governance profile for ordinary project work.

Provides normal requirements, analysis, verification, challenge, evidence and approval controls.

### HIGH-ASSURANCE

For work involving significant security, financial, safety, regulatory, data, infrastructure, architectural or irreversible-change risk.

Requires stronger evidence, independence, review and operational readiness.

Risk classification shall consider:

* blast radius;
* reversibility;
* security impact;
* data sensitivity;
* financial impact;
* operational impact;
* regulatory impact;
* dependency impact;
* public/user impact;
* architectural criticality.

If classification is disputed or uncertain, the higher applicable governance profile shall apply until resolved.

---

# 6. Mandatory Protected Controls

Risk scaling may reduce ceremony.

It shall not remove protected controls.

Protected controls include:

* authority integrity;
* constitutional boundary;
* H0 registration;
* evidence integrity;
* task authorization;
* change materiality;
* challenge requirements;
* stop controls;
* reset controls;
* security boundaries;
* traceability;

An emergency delegate does not become constitutional H0 by emergency action.

PBIM shall maintain a distinction between:

**Who is authorized to decide**

and

**Who is technically capable of performing the action.**

Repository administration, system administration, code-writing capability, automation privileges or infrastructure access do not independently confer governance authority.

Technical capability shall therefore be mapped against authorized governance permissions.

* **I1 — Organizational Independence**
* **I2 — Evidence Independence**
* **I3 — Technical Independence**
* **I4 — Governance Independence**

High-Assurance challenge shall require the applicable independence dimensions.

PBIM shall distinguish:

**Epistemic Independence**

from

**Organizational Independence.**

An agent thinking independently is not automatically an organizationally independent challenger.

If required independence cannot be obtained, the project shall enter:

`CHALLENGE-BLOCKED`

rather than manufacture or falsely declare independence.

---

# 15. Role Combination

One person or agent may perform multiple logical roles where permitted.

However, role combination shall not eliminate a mandatory independence requirement.

For example, the same agent may not satisfy both sides of a High-Assurance challenge solely by changing role labels.

Where genuine independence is required, the architecture shall require distinct qualifying principals, evidence paths, ownership boundaries or governance authorities as applicable.

---

# 16. Task Packets

Task Packets shall be immutable once approved.

Lifecycle:

**DRAFT → APPROVED → DISPATCHED → IN_PROGRESS → SUBMITTED → VERIFIED → CLOSED**

Changes require supersession rather than silent mutation.

Task scope includes direct and derived effects, including:

* generated files;
* dependencies;
* migrations;
* build artifacts;
* schema changes;
* infrastructure effects;
* configuration effects;
* security effects.

Out-of-scope discoveries require:

**STOP → REPORT → NEW/SUPERSEDING PACKET**

unless an authorized emergency procedure applies.

---

# 17. Stop Controls

PBIM defines stop classes:

* S1 — advisory;
* S2 — controlled work stop;
* S3 — mandatory governance stop;
* S4 — safety/security/critical stop.

A stop affects both:

* governance state;
* execution authorization.

Resume requires appropriate authority.

A stopped task may not resume merely because the executor decides that the underlying problem is resolved.

---

# 18. Architectural Reset

Architectural Reset is a controlled state transition.

Reset shall not be implemented as informal abandonment of the current baseline.

The reset path shall preserve:

* prior decisions;
* evidence;
* superseded architecture;
* reason for reset;
* affected requirements;
* authority decision;
* resulting new baseline.

Where reset occurs during or after an irreversible migration, rollback of application code shall not be treated as equivalent to rollback of system state.

Recovery shall instead become a new controlled architectural state.

---

# 19. Emergency Delegation

Emergency authority may be delegated within defined limits.

Emergency delegation shall specify:

* triggering condition;
* delegated authority;
* prohibited actions;
* duration;
* scope;
* review requirement;
* termination condition.

Emergency delegation shall not silently alter constitutional authority.

---

# 20. Automation Trust Boundary

Automation shall be treated as a technical capability, not as autonomous governance authority.

Automated actions must operate within:

* defined permissions;
* task scope;
* repository boundaries;
* approval requirements;
* evidence requirements.

Automation shall not self-authorize a governance exception merely because a technical condition permits it.

---

# 21. Repository Administration Boundary

Repository administrators possess technical capability that may exceed ordinary project roles.

Administrative capability shall not silently override governance requirements.

Protected branches, force-push restrictions, required reviews, audit trails and equivalent mechanisms are implementation controls.

PBIM specifies the governance requirement.

Implementation Verification must establish whether the repository actually enforces it.

---

# 22. Durable Artifact Reference Rule

Authoritative PBIM references shall point only to **durable, persistent artifacts**.

Authoritative references shall use, where applicable:

* immutable commits;
* permanent tags;
* releases;
* durable artifact identifiers;
* persistent repository objects.

Authoritative governance, registry, baseline and evidence records shall **not** rely on:

* temporary session branches;
* ephemeral agent workspaces;
* deletable review branches;
* transient execution identifiers;
* temporary URLs whose continued existence is not controlled.

Ephemeral references may be retained as supplementary provenance, but they shall never be the sole authoritative reference for a governed artifact.

# 23. Baseline Drift

PBIM shall detect drift between:

* approved baseline;
* repository instructions;
* AGENTS.md;
* task packets;
* registry;
* evidence;
* workflow definitions;
* authority configuration.

A mismatch shall produce a controlled:

`BASELINE-DRIFT`

state.

Architectural specification of drift detection does not constitute evidence that the repository currently has the required detection machinery.

That evidence belongs to Implementation Verification.

### Level 1

Material change requiring AEV impact review.

### Level 2

Architecture-changing requirement requiring AEV + AEC.

### Level 3

Load-bearing architectural failure requiring Architectural Reset.

A change shall not be classified merely by convenience.

Where materiality is uncertain, escalation to the higher level is required.

* data recovery;
* incident ownership;
* documentation;
* dependency readiness;
* security;

* migration recovery;

Not every operational criterion applies to every project.

LIGHT governance may reduce the number and depth of operational controls but may not eliminate required operational-readiness determination.

# 14. Human Governance

Human authority is mandatory for material decisions including:

* project initiation;
* material scope approval;
* significant architecture approval;
* security exceptions;
* material unresolved risks;
* financial/resource commitments;
* destructive operations;
* production release;
* externally consequential actions;
* and project closure.

Human approval should not be required for every low-risk engineering action.

---

# 15. Bounded Convergence

The multi-agent review process shall be bounded.

A proposed default is:

```text
Cycle 1 → Independent Review
Cycle 2 → Reconciliation
Cycle 3 → Final Reconciliation
        ↓
Escalate unresolved disagreement
```

Three cycles are retained as a **candidate control**, not yet a universally mandatory constant.

If unresolved material disagreement remains after the configured limit:

```text
STOP
↓
Record disagreement
↓
Escalate
↓
Human / Technical Authority decision
OR
Return to AEA
```

The system must not permit indefinite agent-to-agent review loops.

---

# 16. Architectural Reset Protocol

A new control is established for load-bearing assumption failure.

If an AEC challenge invalidates a foundational assumption, the project must not merely patch the implementation.

The system shall determine whether an:

`ARCHITECTURAL RESET`

is required.

Example:

```text
AEC detects load-bearing assumption failure
                ↓
Impact assessment
                ↓
Is architecture still valid?
       /                  \
     YES                   NO
      ↓                     ↓
Amend AEV              Return to AEA
      ↓                     ↓
Re-verify              Reconstruct architecture
```

The reset event must be recorded.

---

# 17. Task Packet State Machine

Task packets shall become controlled engineering artifacts.

Proposed state model:

```text
DRAFT
  ↓
APPROVED
  ↓
DISPATCHED
  ↓
IN_PROGRESS
  ↓
SUBMITTED
  ↓
VERIFIED
  ↓
CLOSED
```

A dispatched task packet shall be immutable.

If the task must change after dispatch:

```text
Original Packet
      ↓
SUPERSEDED
      ↓
New Packet
```

This preserves execution traceability.

---

# 18. Evidence Model

Substantive architectural claims must be classified.

Required classes:

* `FACT`
* `INFERENCE`
* `ASSUMPTION`
* `PROPOSAL`
* `RECOMMENDATION`
* `RISK`
* `UNKNOWN`
* `DISPUTED`

Evidence should reference its source wherever practical.

The system should prefer reproducible evidence such as:

* repository files;
* commit identifiers;
* tests;
* command output;
* configuration;
* authoritative documentation;
* controlled artifacts.

---

# 19. Traceability Model

The minimum traceability chain is:

```text
Requirement
    ↓
Decision
    ↓
Architecture
    ↓
Task / Work Item
    ↓
Artifact / Implementation
    ↓
Verification
    ↓
Test Evidence
    ↓
Approval
    ↓
Release
```

A large formal Requirements Traceability Matrix is not mandatory for every project.

A lightweight traceability ledger is sufficient for low-complexity projects.

High-risk or high-complexity projects may require a formal RTM.

The mandatory question is:

> Why does this artifact exist, who authorized it, what requirement caused it, and how was it verified?

# 22. Decision Architecture

The reports identify value in both ADRs and a Decision Ledger.

The candidate resolves this by assigning them different purposes.

### Decision Ledger

Canonical record of project decisions and decision state.

### ADR

Detailed durable explanation of high-impact architectural decisions.

Therefore:

```text
Decision Ledger
    = canonical decision index

ADR
    = detailed architectural decision artifact
```

An ADR should reference its corresponding ledger entry.

---

# 23. Repository Architecture

The candidate retains a conceptual separation between:

### Governance Repository

Contains:

* project-management artifacts;
* task packets;
* AEA;
* AEV;
* AEC;
* AECC;
* decision records;
* template definitions;
* verification evidence.

### Production Repository

Contains:

* source code;
* implementation;
* tests;
* deployment configuration;
* production engineering artifacts.

The exact repository arrangement is project-dependent.

The architectural principle is that controlled governance evidence must remain traceable without contaminating production source structure unnecessarily.

---

# 24. Branch Model

Permanent agent-owned production branches are not required.

The preferred model is task-oriented branches.

Example:

```text
pgb/bzj-[project]-[task]-[slug]
```

Agent identity should be recorded in:

* task metadata;
* commits;
* evidence;
* review records.

Evidence/document branches may use a separate namespace.

The final branch model remains subject to implementation validation.

---

# 25. Identifier Architecture

The candidate establishes four distinct concepts.

### Project Identifier

Identifies the project.

### Work/Task Identifier

Identifies executable work.

### Artifact Identifier

Identifies a controlled document/artifact.

### Lifecycle State

Identifies where the project/work currently exists in the workflow.

PM process identifiers may provide classification.

They should not be assumed to replace these four concepts.

---

# 26. Identifier Registry

A committed machine-readable registry shall be created before the architecture is treated as executable.

Proposed structure:

```yaml
identifier:
sequence_index:
knowledge_area:
lifecycle_band:
artifact_class:
status:
description:
```

The registry shall be the authoritative ordering mechanism.

Human-readable filenames must not be relied upon for machine sequencing.

This directly addresses the concern raised by Google Jules regarding lexical ordering and the broader identifier ambiguity identified by Kilo and GitHub/Copilot.

---

# 27. 49-Process Model

The 49-process model is provisionally retained.

However:

**FACT STATUS: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

The submitted reports do not provide sufficient independent evidence to establish every semantic rule governing the identifier system.

Therefore the following are required before final baseline approval:

1. Canonical process registry.
2. One identifier grammar.
3. Explicit process ordering.
4. Explicit custom-subsection grammar.
5. Machine-readable sequence indexes.
6. Mapping between PM process and lifecycle state.
7. Definition of whether custom identifiers are classifications or executable workflow nodes.

No disputed numerical finding shall be treated as established fact until reproduced against the canonical registry.

---

# 28. Mandatory vs Conditional Controls

The architecture shall be risk-scaled.

## Mandatory Core

Every project requires:

* project identity;
* source-of-truth definition;
* authority definition;
* requirements/constraints;
* evidence classification;
* baseline verification;
* challenge before implementation;
* traceability;
* repository control;
* stop conditions;
* implementation verification;
* appropriate human approval.

## Conditional

Depending on project complexity:

* full ADR set;
* formal RTM;
* multi-agent challenge panel;
* detailed risk register;
* complex branch strategy;
* advanced PM-process mapping;
* specialized security review;
* formal cost controls.

This prevents the template from becoming unnecessarily heavy for small projects.

---

# 29. Architectural Challenge Gate

The challenge shall attempt to invalidate the proposed architecture.

Minimum challenge domains:

* load-bearing assumptions;
* security;
* privacy;
* reliability;
* failure recovery;
* dependencies;
* migration;
* rollback;
* maintainability;
* architecture drift;
* operational complexity;
* cost/resource realism;
* vendor dependency.

## Challenge exit criteria

The challenge may close only when:

* no critical unresolved assumption remains;
* no blocking security issue remains;
* no unacceptable failure mode remains unaddressed;
* major dependency uncertainty has an explicit disposition;
* architectural contradictions have been resolved or accepted by authority;
* unresolved risks have owners and decision paths;
* and verification evidence is complete for the current baseline.

---

# 30. Recurring Challenge

AEC is not necessarily a single project event.

It may be re-triggered:

* after a material architectural change;
* after invalidation of a load-bearing assumption;
* before high-risk implementation;
* before major release;
* after significant production evidence contradicts the architecture.

This converts challenge from a ceremonial approval step into an engineering control.

# 31. Automation

Automation is encouraged where it reduces administrative friction without creating hidden authority.

Candidate automation includes:

* project scaffolding;
* identifier registry validation;
* task-packet validation;
* branch creation;
* evidence collection;
* approval-status aggregation;
* traceability updates;
* status reporting;

* stale branch cleanup;
* automated testing.


The following shall not be silently automated:

* final architecture approval;

* material scope approval;

# 32. Security Boundary

A formal secret/data boundary is required before final baseline approval.

The architecture must explicitly define:

* what agents may access;
* what repositories may contain;
* how credentials are supplied;
* what information may appear in logs;
* how secrets are redacted;
* what production credentials are prohibited from agent exposure;
* and what actions require privileged human execution.

This remains a blocking baseline question.

---

# 33. Failure Handling

The architecture shall define controlled responses to:

### Agent unavailable

Do not silently collapse independent roles.

Record a role gap and determine whether:

* another authorized agent can assume the role;
* human review is required;
* or the task must stop.

### Conflicting analysis

Record both positions.

Attempt evidence-based reconciliation.

Escalate unresolved material disagreement.

### Failed verification

Return to the appropriate prior stage.

Do not simply mark the failed artifact as approved.

### Failed challenge

Either:

* amend architecture and re-verify;
* or reset to AEA.

### Requirement change

Initiate controlled change assessment.

Do not silently modify the approved baseline.

---

# 34. Architecture Drift Control

The project must periodically determine whether:

```text
Approved Architecture
        ≠
Implemented Architecture
```

If divergence occurs, classify it as:

* intentional approved change;
* implementation defect;
* undocumented change;
* or architectural drift.

Undocumented architectural divergence shall trigger review.

# 41. Recommended Next Gate

The next engineering activity should be:

## `AEC — Architectural Engineering Challenge`

The challenge should specifically attempt to break the following five architectural assumptions:

1. **The 49-process spine can coexist with a separate project/task/document identity architecture without creating duplication or ambiguity.**
2. **A machine-readable registry can provide deterministic ordering and semantic authority.**
3. **The AEA → AEV → AEC → AECC model provides sufficient assurance without excessive process overhead.**
4. **Risk-scaled controls can prevent small projects from becoming administratively overloaded while preserving adequate governance for complex projects.**
5. **The proposed separation between agent execution, verification and human authority prevents circular approval and uncontrolled changes.**

The AEC should also test the security boundary, project-state model, task-packet state machine and architecture-reset mechanism.

# 42. Final Candidate Statement

The collaborating-agent reports collectively establish a strong basis for a controlled multi-agent engineering operating model.

The synthesis does **not** establish that every existing template mechanism is already correct.

The architecture should therefore proceed under the following principle:

> **Preserve the governance intent, separate overloaded concerns, make authority explicit, make evidence traceable, make verification independent, make challenge adversarial, and make process proportional to project risk and complexity.**

The 49-process model remains provisionally retained as a PM classification spine, but its exact executable semantics remain subject to verification.

The candidate is consequently released to the next controlled stage:

**`AEC — Architectural Engineering Challenge`**

with the blocking conditions identified in Section 35.
