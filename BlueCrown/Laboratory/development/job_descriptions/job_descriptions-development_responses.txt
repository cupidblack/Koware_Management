## **4. Important design considerations**

The job descriptions should be developed with the following principles.

### **A. Preserve the organisational hierarchy**

A position must not acquire authority simply because its job description sounds senior.

For example:

- The Corporate Manager should retain the management responsibilities assigned by the organisational chart.
- The Administrative Auditor should have appropriate independent review responsibilities.
- The Accounts & Finance Executive should not automatically acquire authority to approve all financial transactions.
- The Quality Assurance & Compliance Manager should have sufficient independence to identify and report non-compliance.
- The Programme Manager and Director of Operations should have distinguishable responsibilities.

The descriptions must reinforce, rather than rewrite, the chart.

### **B. Separate responsibility from authority**

Each document should distinguish between:

- What the employee is responsible for.
- What the employee can independently decide.
- What requires supervisory approval.
- What must be escalated.
- What the employee is prohibited from doing.

This is particularly important for finance, auditing, corporate administration, employment matters and technical operations.

### **C. Avoid inventing qualifications**

A position should have reasonable qualifications and competency requirements, but these should not become unnecessarily restrictive.

For instance, a Graphic Designer may need a demonstrable portfolio and appropriate design capabilities. A university degree should not automatically become mandatory if equivalent professional experience is sufficient.

Likewise, professional certifications should only be mandatory where justified by the position or applicable requirements.

### **D. Make KPIs measurable**

Each position should have measurable performance indicators.

Examples:

| Position                     | Possible performance indicators                              |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Corporate Secretary          | Accuracy and timeliness of corporate records                 |
| Accounts & Finance Executive | Financial reconciliation accuracy and reporting timeliness   |
| Graphic Designer             | Design quality, delivery times and approved revisions        |
| Platform Developer           | Platform reliability, defect resolution and delivery quality |
| Health & Safety Technician   | Inspection completion and incident reporting                 |
| Academic Registrar           | Accuracy of student records and academic administration      |
| Marketing Manager            | Campaign performance and qualified audience engagement       |
| Facility Manager             | Maintenance completion and facility condition                |

These are proposed indicators, not established targets. Numerical targets should be determined from actual operating capacity and approved policies.

### **E. Design for a developing organisation**

Koware Group is a developing enterprise in which the founder may initially perform multiple management and operational functions.

Consequently, the descriptions should be written for the permanent position, not for a particular individual.

The alias names in the organisational chart should be retained as reference metadata where appropriate, but they should not become requirements for the position.

This will allow the documents to remain valid when:

- A position is filled by a new employee.
- Responsibilities are delegated.
- Work is outsourced.
- A position becomes full-time.
- An intern transitions into a permanent position.
- The organisational structure evolves.

# Koware Group — Job Description Document Standard

**Document ID:** `KOWARE-HR-JD-STANDARD`  
**Version:** `0.3.0`  
**Status:** Draft — awaiting authorised human review  
**Applies to:** Individual role records under `BlueCrown/Employment_Resources/Job_Descriptions/`

## 1. Purpose and status

This standard governs the drafting, review and maintenance of the role descriptions in this collection. A job description explains the purpose and expected work of a position; it is not an employment contract, appointment, professional licence, governance instrument, approved policy or delegation of authority. No draft becomes effective until reviewed and approved through the organisation's authorised process.

## 2. Source hierarchy and traceability

Use the latest formally approved organisational chart as the source for position title, placement, reporting relationships and charted direct reports. The current development collection was mapped to `Organizational_Chart_Structure-Aliased_Checks.txt` on `main`, blob SHA `7b6ed7f1c9ce4d162832f96eadb4c6140c5211f8`. Verify that source before adoption and update each affected record if the chart changes.

For decisions beyond the chart, use applicable bylaws/constitution and governance resolutions, approved policies and written delegations, applicable law, and professional regulation—in that order as applicable. A title, hierarchy, alias, prior platform response or draft does not itself confer authority or prove a person's identity, appointment, experience, qualification or licence. Keep aliases out of job requirements and do not infer personal characteristics from them.

Every record must identify its stable document ID, exact chart title, functional area, version/status, source chart and source line, reporting position, charted direct reports, responsibilities, outputs, authority boundary, role-relevant competencies, review route and revision history. Preserve source title wording where practical; explain a filename slug separately if necessary.

## 3. Minimum content for each individual record

1. **Document control:** stable ID, title, function, version, status and source provenance.
2. **Purpose and placement:** the organisational need served, reports-to relationship, charted direct reports, and key internal/external working interfaces.
3. **Accountabilities:** role-specific duties expressed as observable actions. Distinguish recurring work, coordination, supervision and specialist work as appropriate; do not copy generic duties in place of the role's actual remit.
4. **Outputs and service standards:** concrete records, services, decisions or work products; accuracy, timeliness, confidentiality and handover expectations.
5. **Authority and escalation:** routine discretion only within documented delegation; approvals required; matters to escalate; prohibited or reserved decisions. State explicitly where the role must not self-approve, perform regulated work without credentials, or bind the organisation.
6. **Requirements and competencies:** proportionate education, training, experience and skills. Accept equivalent demonstrated capability where appropriate. Require credentials only where assigned duties or law require them, and verify those credentials before assignment.
7. **Performance review:** role-specific evidence-based indicators. Targets must be set after the workload, resources, baseline and service commitments are understood; do not invent numerical targets.
8. **Conduct, safety and information:** apply approved conduct, safeguarding, privacy, records, health and safety, accessibility and incident-reporting policies relevant to the position.
9. **Employment conditions and approval:** refer hours, location, employment classification, pay, benefits, travel and contract terms to separately approved instruments; include review/approval fields and change history.

## 4. Drafting controls

- Describe the enduring position, not a named incumbent.
- Preserve the chart's exact parent-child relationships. Indentation denotes the reporting tree; two roles at the same indentation under a parent are peers, not automatically a supervisor/subordinate pair.
- Separate responsibility from decision authority. Never infer budget, signing, hiring, disciplinary, legal, clinical, academic-award, data-access or policy-making authority from a title.
- Keep independent assurance independent in practice. A reporting line does not permit the auditor to operate or approve the controls being audited or to audit their own work.
- Avoid claiming that a policy, service, facility, system, licence or employment arrangement is already in place unless verified. Where operation depends on approved controls, say so.
- Use respectful, accessible and non-discriminatory language. For domestic, cleaning, grooming and other service work, define safe tasks and boundaries without diminishing dignity; no role should be assigned unsafe, intimate or out-of-scope work without consent, training and applicable safeguards.
- Treat interns as supervised learners. Specify learning, supervision and escalation; do not make an intern accountable for decisions reserved to an authorised employee.
- Do not use the development schedule/check-day information in the chart as a work schedule or employment condition.

## 5. Review and approval

Recommended sequence: source-chart verification → role supervisor/departmental review → Human & Employment Resources review → legal, finance, safety, academic or other specialist review where relevant → approval by the authorised governance body/office → controlled publication. Confirm Ghanaian employment, tax, data-protection, safeguarding, occupational-safety and relevant professional requirements before use. Employee acknowledgement, if collected, records receipt only unless a separate lawful agreement provides otherwise.

## 6. Versioning

Use `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`: major for mandate/reporting changes; minor for substantive responsibility or control changes; patch for editorial corrections. Draft status remains `DRAFT_AWAITING_HUMAN_REVIEW` until formal approval. Record date and material change in each file's revision history and keep the index synchronized.

## 7. Collection-specific controls

The chart has 62 individual positions on source lines 5–66. Stakeholders and shareholders are constituencies, not employee positions. Members Representative is represented by a separate mandate/charter; Governing Committee and Board of Directors are collective bodies and use charters, not individual employee descriptions. Do not invent a second Liaison Assistant position or make the Corporate Manager's Liaison Assistant report to the Liaison Officer.
