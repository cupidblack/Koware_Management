# 3. Core Architectural Decisions

The following decisions are now definitive for Version 19.0.

### Decision 1 — ENVY is the master broadcast workstation

The HP ENVY m7 remains the central studio control machine.

It should handle:

- OBS
- primary camera
- host presentation
- slides
- media playback
- programme recording
- Pavilion programme feed
- live scene control
- master recording
- final programme monitoring

It should **not** simultaneously carry unnecessary AI generation, heavy browser workloads, virtual machines or intensive rendering.

---

### Decision 2 — Pavilion is the producer / AI / guest workstation

The Pavilion handles:

- Blue Yeti
- remote guests
- producer communication
- AI interaction
- research
- REAPER stem recording
- secondary camera
- backup video
- AI TTS
- guest-call management

The Pavilion's 8 GB RAM is the most significant current workstation bottleneck.

**Recommended upgrade: 16 GB RAM.**

---

### Decision 3 — S24 Ultra is the mobile production unit

The Samsung S24 Ultra should remain primarily independent from the fixed studio.

It becomes:

> **Dynamic Camera / Mobile Production Unit**

Use it for:

- mobile podcasting
- field interviews
- short status posts
- live streams
- meetups
- B-roll
- spontaneous discussions
- vehicle/drive recordings
- alternative camera angles

---

### Decision 4 — OBS is the programme recorder

OBS provides:

> **Master Programme**

---

### Decision 5 — REAPER is the stem recorder

REAPER provides:

> **Independent Pavilion-originating audio where technically possible**

This distinction is important.

OBS preserves the programme.

REAPER preserves recoverable components.

---

### Decision 6 — V8S remains supplemental

The V8S is formally:

> **Stereo Programme Mixer / Secondary Audio Source**

It is not assumed to provide independently addressable microphone channels.

---

### Decision 7 — 44.1 kHz remains the commissioning baseline

Because the existing hardware chain has already been tested successfully at 44.1 kHz, the first complete studio commissioning should remain at:

> **44,100 Hz**

The studio can later test a controlled 48 kHz migration.

For the pilot:

> **stability takes precedence over theoretical optimization.**

---

### Decision 8 — Physical routing comes before virtual routing

Do not introduce:

- Voicemeeter
- VB-Cable
- NDI
- network audio
- complex virtual mixers

until the physical system demonstrates an actual requirement for them.

---

### Decision 9 — AI gets operating modes

AI should not simply be "on" or "off".

The studio uses:

1. **LISTEN**
2. **ASSIST**
3. **CO-HOST**
4. **AUTONOMOUS**

with an independent:

> **AI MUTE**

control.

---

### Decision 10 — Producer always retains emergency control

AI may become conversationally autonomous within the production environment, but the producer must always be able to immediately mute or remove its audio from the programme.

# 8. Microphone Hierarchy

Based on actual testing rather than theoretical specifications:

## ENVY host

**Primary:**

> Conexant SmartAudio HD Microphone Array

## Pavilion producer

**Primary:**

> Blue Yeti

**Backup:**

> Intel Smart Sound Technology Digital Microphone Array

## V8S condenser

Use when:

- speaker can remain approximately 1 inch away
- controlled environment is available
- close-range recording is practical

Do not compensate for distance by excessive gain.

Testing has already demonstrated that this introduces:

- noise
- static
- distortion

---

# 9. Audio Gain Philosophy

The studio should solve weak audio primarily through:

1. microphone placement
2. microphone distance
3. appropriate source gain
4. room control
5. clean routing

rather than aggressive post-recording amplification.

A clean slightly quiet recording is recoverable.

A clipped or heavily distorted recording is not.

---

## ENVY

Do not blindly make Daakro the Windows default playback device.

The correct configuration depends on the monitoring topology.

The critical requirement is:

> Pavilion programme audio must reach the Daakro input and therefore the ENVY.

OBS should explicitly select the desired recording devices wherever practical rather than relying entirely on Windows defaults.

---

## Pavilion

The Blue Yeti should remain the:

> **Playback device**

because the existing architecture deliberately uses its headphone output as the programme-feed point.

This is a key part of the working topology.

- disable Game Bar/background recording

# 19. Critical OBS Clarification

OBS does **not** magically separate individual microphones simply because multiple recording tracks are enabled.

A source must actually exist as a distinct input.

Therefore:

```text
OBS Track 3
```

can preserve the Pavilion feed as a separate OBS source, but:

```text
Pavilion Blue Yeti
Guest
AI
```

cannot automatically become separate ENVY tracks if the ENVY only receives them as one stereo feed.

That is why the Pavilion-side REAPER project remains important.

# 29. AI Co-Host Operating Modes

- listens
- transcribes
- observes

## MODE 1 — ASSIST

- listens
- suggests questions
- identifies useful facts
- summarizes
- provides producer notes

Only the producer decides what goes on air.

---

## MODE 2 — CO-HOST

AI:

- listens
- responds
- asks questions
- comments
- participates

AI may:

- initiate relevant questions
- respond spontaneously
- challenge the host
- react to guests
- introduce useful observations
- participate without requiring sentence-by-sentence approval

The term "autonomous" means:

> **autonomous conversational initiative inside the production environment**

It does not mean bypassing platform safety controls.

# 52. Consent Revocation

When a member disables participation:

1. stop future harvesting
2. exclude the user from future episode processing
3. delete staging copies that no longer have a valid processing basis
4. remove them from active AI-generation datasets
5. prevent future story generation using revoked material
6. preserve only information that must legitimately be retained

The system should distinguish:

> **withdrawal from future processing**

from:

> **removal of previously published content**

Those are different operational events.
