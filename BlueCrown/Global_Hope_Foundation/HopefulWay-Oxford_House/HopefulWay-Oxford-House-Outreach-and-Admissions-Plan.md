# HopefulWay Oxford House: Outreach, Referral and Admissions Plan

**Version:** 1.0  
**Prepared:** 26 November 2026  
**Owner:** HopefulWay Foundation Chairperson  
**Purpose:** Turn the approved authority to give Oxford House presentations into a safe, respectful, trackable referral pathway that helps interested adults make an informed application for the currently available HopefulWay Oxford House.

## 1. Executive recommendation

Run this as a **resident referral and informed-choice programme**, not as a high-pressure recruitment campaign. The immediate priority is to confirm whether the Ashiaman men's house is continuing, its true available capacity and costs, and whether it can safely accept a new resident. Then make it easy for a person who is interested to request contact, with their own consent, and guide them through screening, a preliminary interview, the house interview and the residents' decision.

The approved presentation letter gives HopefulWay permission to present; it is not itself proof that a particular residence is licensed, inspected, individually chartered, clinically staffed, or guaranteed to meet every applicant's needs. Describe the Foundation's charter only in the precise wording supported by the current charter and Oxford House authorization. Do not describe a house as licensed or individually certified unless that has separately been verified.

The material supplied includes the proposed campaign, a link to the unsigned presentation-letter PDF, the Oxford House folder, and a prior-responses text file. The prior-responses file returned only partially in the available repository view, and the folder listing contains many binary files that could not be read as text here. Accordingly, this plan integrates the visible prior recommendations and the facts in the request; it does not claim to have audited the full contents of every PDF, DOCX, image, or video. Before external use, compare the final claims and letter against the signed approval, current charter, actual house bylaws, and current fees/capacity.

## 2. Decisions and corrections to the original proposal

1. **Verify the time-sensitive house situation first.** The supplied plan says there are two residents, maximum capacity of eight, a target of six by the end of December, and a lease ending at the end of January. As of this plan's preparation date, confirm these figures, the year and deadlines, lease renewal options, current vacancies, move-in date, and whether the house is actually accepting applications. Do not advertise a deadline or availability until the Chairperson and house confirm it.
2. **Advertise only the real current option.** The currently described option is a male residence in Ashiaman. Do not imply that female or co-ed housing, other locations, or future houses are available. Record other location requests as non-binding demand information; never promise a house will open.
3. **Use a two-stage, minimal-data intake.** First collect only enough information to ask the adult applicant how they want to be contacted, whether they consent to the referral, and whether they want to hear about Ashiaman or another location. Collect sensitive recovery, health, identity, sponsor, and emergency-contact details only later if necessary, with a stated purpose and appropriate privacy controls.
4. **The adult applicant's consent is primary.** A supervisor may share the link, help someone complete it, or submit a referral only after the person has explicitly agreed. Sponsor awareness can be encouraged where the applicant wants it, but a sponsor's consent must not replace the adult applicant's own consent or become an unnecessary gate to asking for information. For minors or people whose legal capacity is in question, pause and obtain qualified safeguarding/legal guidance before collecting information or making decisions.
5. **Do not pay referral commissions tied to rent, admission, or occupancy.** Such payments create conflicts of interest in referrals involving people in recovery and may conflict with organizational policies, applicable law, or Oxford House principles. Offer non-financial partnership, information sessions, training, and acknowledgement instead. Obtain legal and Oxford House guidance before adopting any incentive.
6. **Do not promise a clinical or guaranteed recovery outcome.** Oxford House is recovery housing, not a substitute for treatment, detoxification, medical care, or emergency services. Do not say that a person will automatically return after a relapse or that a test automatically results in eviction unless the current approved bylaws say exactly that and the resident has received the policy in advance. Explain the actual safety, testing, relapse, appeal, and re-entry procedures plainly and consistently.
7. **Separate presentation requests from applications.** One request form can offer two clear routes, but presentation bookings must not collect applicant health information, and an applicant should not have to attend a presentation to ask about residence.
8. **Use individual, permission-based WhatsApp outreach.** Do not put supervisors or clients in a group, expose recipients' phone numbers, or send repeated unsolicited messages. Contact institutional decision-makers individually, respect opt-outs, and pause follow-up when they respond.

## 3. Pre-launch gate: confirm the service before inviting referrals

The Chairperson and house representatives should complete and approve a one-page current availability sheet before the first campaign message. It must state:

- Whether the Ashiaman house is open and accepting applications, the verified number of places, and the earliest realistic move-in date.
- Eligibility requirements, including age, gender, readiness and any conditions that are actually in the current house rules.
- Current rent, dues, deposits, payment timing, utilities, food arrangements, and what happens if a resident cannot pay. State amounts clearly on the application page or in an information sheet; do not make a person apply before learning the costs.
- House address/location information appropriate for public sharing, transport/access considerations, and whether a visit is possible. Protect resident privacy; do not publish residents' names or private contact details.
- The current bylaws and policies: substance-free expectations, testing process and costs, relapse response, resident responsibilities, conflict resolution, safeguarding, complaints, emergency response, and discharge/appeal procedures.
- What support the Foundation provides and what it does not provide. Identify the route to clinical care and emergencies. Do not imply staff are available around the clock unless that is true.
- The accurate charter relationship and any limits on what the charter covers, using the charter's own current language.
- Who receives referrals, who conducts interviews, who can access records, the response-time commitment, and a backup contact.

Resolve any mismatch between the approved presentation letter, public website, charter, house rules and this sheet before publication. If the lease/continuation decision is unresolved, pause admission marketing or state transparently that continuity is under review and offer an interest-only request, not a promised placement.

## 4. Referral journey and operating responsibilities

**Institution → informed adult → consented interest form → acknowledgement → eligibility/availability check → preliminary conversation → house interview → resident vote → written decision → move-in orientation.**

- **Chairperson / campaign owner:** approves claims and letters, leads institutional relationships, owns the weekly dashboard, and handles escalations.
- **Referral coordinator:** checks the queue on business days, acknowledges requests, updates status, and follows up only through the applicant's chosen channel.
- **Interview team:** conducts a respectful preliminary conversation, explains costs/rules and records only relevant decisions.
- **House members:** conduct the in-house interview and make the admission decision under the applicable bylaws. The Foundation must not promise a positive vote.
- **Privacy lead:** controls access, notices, retention/deletion, correction requests and any suspected data incident. Assign this role before collecting personal information.

Set a realistic service standard, such as acknowledgement within two business days and an initial status update within five business days. If the team cannot meet it, publish a more accurate standard. Do not send medical or referral details through ordinary WhatsApp or broad email notifications.

### Applicant statuses

Use one controlled status field in a restricted tracker: **New; Consent/Contact Check; Awaiting Applicant; Availability Review; Preliminary Conversation; House Interview; House Vote Pending; Offered; Waitlisted by Applicant Choice; Deferred; Not Proceeding; Moved In; Closed/Withdrawn.** Record the next action and due date. A waitlist is not an offer or guarantee. Tell applicants why they are deferred or not proceeding where safe and appropriate, and give them a route to ask questions or withdraw.

## 5. Landing page and form implementation

### Public entry point

Use `https://buzzjuice.net/hopefulway-oxfordhouse` as the preferred campaign page, with the Koware.org route redirecting to the same canonical page if maintained. The supplied routes are proposed routes, not verified live endpoints; test them on mobile and desktop before distributing. Make the page explain the opportunity first and provide two distinct buttons: **Ask about residence** and **Request a presentation**.

The page should contain: current availability and costs; a concise explanation of the Oxford House peer-supported model; the Foundation's precise charter statement; the Chairperson's personal recovery story (only with their approval); the application and admission steps; the rules and limitations; FAQs; the Oxford House and Foundation links; the supplied video; contact/privacy information; and a clear statement that submitting a request does not guarantee admission. Do not publish the unsigned letter as authorization. Share only the approved, appropriately addressed letter with the intended institution.

### Interest form: first stage

Use a Google Form under a Foundation-controlled Google Workspace account, not a staff member's personal account. Configure response access for only the named referral team, require appropriate account security and multi-factor authentication, and do not expose the response sheet publicly. Avoid sending full form answers to a shared mailbox. Send `support@buzzjuice.net` a minimal notification that a new request arrived, with a secure link accessible only to authorized staff.

Ask only:

1. Applicant's name (preferred name may be used).
2. Applicant's age band confirming they are an adult, rather than full date of birth at this stage.
3. Applicant's own phone or email and preferred safe contact method/time. Ask whether it is safe to leave a message and what name to use.
4. Current town/region (optional if not needed for contact).
5. Interest: current Ashiaman men's house; another location (write-in); or information only. Clearly label other locations as expressions of interest, not current availability.
6. Preferred language/accessibility or communication support (optional).
7. Referrer institution and supervisor contact (optional, and only if the applicant agrees to involve them).
8. A required applicant checkbox: “I am making this request for myself, or I am completing it with the applicant's direct permission. I understand this is an enquiry, not an offer or guarantee of residence. I agree that HopefulWay Foundation may use the details I submit to respond to this enquiry and explain the next steps. I can ask to correct or withdraw my information.”
9. A separate optional checkbox: “I agree that HopefulWay may contact the named supervisor/referrer about this enquiry.” Do not preselect it.
10. A separate optional checkbox for the applicant to authorize sponsor involvement, if wanted. Do not require sponsor name/number or sponsor permission to submit an initial enquiry.

Do **not** request diagnosis, substance history, treatment notes, medication, full birth date, national ID, sponsor details, emergency contact, or detailed relapse history in this first form. Do not ask a supervisor to certify sobriety or clinical suitability. Gather only information essential to the next step, through an appropriately protected process, after explaining why it is needed.

Show a privacy notice immediately before submission: who controls the information; purposes; who can access it; how long it is retained; how to request access/correction/deletion; and a contact route. Have a qualified Ghana data-protection adviser review the notice, the lawful basis/consent process, retention period and the proposed Google Workspace processing before launch. The information is sensitive; minimize it, restrict access, and establish a deletion schedule. Do not claim that email is confidential or that the form is an emergency service. Provide an urgent-care/emergency direction approved for the local context.

### Presentation request form / Amelia

Create a separate Amelia service called “HopefulWay Oxford House information presentation.” Make requests subject to confirmation, rather than automatically promising a slot. Collect institution, contact person's name/role, institutional email/phone, venue or virtual preference, preferred date/time options, approximate audience size, accessibility needs and questions. Do not request client names or clinical information. Notify the Chairperson and `support@buzzjuice.net`; confirm only after checking team availability. Test cancellation, rescheduling, time zone, duplicate bookings, and notifications.

### Tracker and reporting

Maintain a restricted institutional CRM and a separate applicant tracker. Institution CRM fields: institution, type, area, public contact/source, decision-maker, first contact date, consent/permission notes, last contact, next action/date, presentation status, referrals count (aggregate only where possible), and opt-out/do-not-contact status. Applicant tracker uses a unique internal reference, status, consent scope, chosen contact method, next action, and minimal relevant information. Do not use shared spreadsheets with open links, personal-device downloads, or WhatsApp groups to manage applicants.

## 6. Copy-ready supervisor outreach

Send individually to a supervisor or authorized institutional contact. Use the approved personalized letter as an attachment only after verifying it is the signed version and the recipient details are correct. Do not forward the unsigned repository PDF as the approval letter.

### Initial WhatsApp message

> Good morning. I am contacting you on behalf of HopefulWay Foundation. We are inviting referrals of adults who may want to learn about recovery housing after treatment. The current opportunity we can discuss is a men's HopefulWay Oxford House in Ashiaman, subject to current availability and the house's interview and resident decision.
>
> A supportive, substance-free peer community can help some people practise independent living and reintegration after treatment; it is not a substitute for treatment, and residence is not guaranteed. I spent about 13 years in addiction, with several recovery attempts. After living in an Oxford House for three years, I completed a diploma and have maintained ten years of sobriety since returning home. I would be glad to explain the model and answer your team's questions.
>
> Information and the enquiry/presentation request routes: https://buzzjuice.net/hopefulway-oxfordhouse
> HopefulWay Foundation: https://hopefulwayfoundation.org/
> Oxford House: https://www.oxfordhouse.org/
>
> If useful, may I send the approved presentation letter or arrange a short staff presentation? Please share the enquiry link only with an adult who is interested, and let them decide whether to contact us. We will not contact a client through your referral without their permission.
>
> Kind regards, HopefulWay Foundation

This copy is ready only after the landing route is live, the Ashiaman availability statement is confirmed, and the personal story is approved by the Chairperson. If any fact changes, edit the message before sending. Keep the first contact short; send the video (`https://youtu.be/OH2X5Ib8eIc?si=CRSmSvSIj-SvbjRN`) and approved letter when requested or after the supervisor agrees to receive materials. The website and Oxford House links are sufficient in the initial message.

### Follow-up schedule

- **Day 3:** “Good morning. I am following up once on the HopefulWay recovery-housing information I shared. Would a brief call or staff presentation be useful? If this is not relevant, let me know and I will close the follow-up.”
- **Day 7:** If there has been no response and no opt-out, send one useful item only: the short video or the approved letter, with a one-sentence context and a question about whether the material is useful. Do not send both plus a long explanation.
- **Day 14:** Make one brief call during appropriate business hours only if the contact details are public/authorized and prior messages were not declined. Ask whether they received the information, whether a presentation would help, and whether they prefer no further contact. Log the outcome.
- **After Day 14:** Stop routine chasing. Set one optional check-in around 90 days only for contacts who did not opt out and where this is appropriate. Any “stop,” “not interested,” or equivalent response means immediately record do-not-contact and end outreach. No calls to clients based solely on a supervisor's submission.

Residents live in a peer community with shared responsibilities and house rules. Applicants are told the current costs, expectations and available support before deciding whether to continue. Admission is subject to availability, an interview and the residents' decision; an enquiry does not guarantee a place. Other locations are not currently being offered. You can contact HopefulWay yourself; you do not need a supervisor or sponsor to submit an initial enquiry.

## 8. Presentation content and safeguards

Use a concise 15–20 minute presentation followed by questions. Suggested order:

1. The transition after treatment and the importance of a safe, supportive living environment (avoid claiming that relapse is inevitable or that housing alone prevents it).
2. What Oxford House is: explain the peer-supported, substance-free, democratically operated model using current official Oxford House material.
3. HopefulWay's role and the exact scope of its charter, supported by the approved charter documents.
4. The actual Ashiaman offer: current gender, vacancies, costs, rules, support and limitations.
5. The Chairperson's personal story, told as personal experience, not a guarantee or typical outcome.
6. Enquiry and admissions steps, including applicant consent, preliminary conversation, house interview, resident vote, possible deferral and no guarantee of admission.
7. Questions, private follow-up options, and the two links for self-directed enquiry and presentation requests.

Use the official Oxford House website and video supplied by the Foundation. Verify that testimonials are authentic, have permission for reuse, and are not edited to imply typical outcomes. Do not compare the chartered model with “unlicensed” houses in a way that implies all other homes are unsafe or unlawful. Explain verifiable governance and accountability differences, not disparagement.

## 9. Interview and admission safeguards

1. Acknowledge the enquiry and ask the applicant what they want to know; confirm the contact channel is safe.
2. Explain current availability, rent/dues, rules, required contributions, testing policy, relapse response, privacy, and the resident decision process before screening for fit.
3. Conduct a brief preliminary conversation with the applicant directly. Involve a supervisor or sponsor only with the applicant's permission. Do not ask for clinical records unless a qualified adviser has established a legitimate need and secure process.
4. If there may be an immediate medical, detoxification, withdrawal, mental-health or safety need, refer to appropriate qualified local services. Do not use an admission interview to make clinical decisions.
5. If mutually appropriate, schedule a house interview. Explain who will attend, what the house will decide, and how the applicant will be informed. House members vote under the current bylaws; no staff member should promise the outcome.
6. Communicate the decision privately and respectfully. If offered a place, provide written terms and a move-in checklist. If deferred or declined, explain the decision where appropriate and offer other information/resources without suggesting a future place is guaranteed.
7. At move-in, orient the resident to the current signed house rules, resident rights, rent/dues, conflict and complaint process, emergency/safeguarding arrangements, and contacts. Obtain acknowledgements without presenting them as a waiver of legal rights.

Before use, have the house and a local legal/safeguarding adviser review drug/alcohol testing, testing consent, searches, eviction, relapse, detoxification, return-to-house, safeguarding, use of local authorities, and grievance procedures. Any policy must be lawful, consistently applied, non-discriminatory, communicated in advance, and aligned with the actual Oxford House charter and bylaws. Do not state that refusal or a positive test always means immediate eviction unless the approved policy establishes that process and any applicable safety/decision safeguards.

## 10. Institution targeting and launch cadence

Begin with a small, relationship-led pilot in Greater Accra rather than a mass broadcast. Prioritize rehabilitation and treatment providers with adult discharge planning; then recovery-support organizations, community/faith organizations, social services and educational counselling/welfare offices where relevant. Contact only appropriate institutional staff using public or permission-based contact details. Maintain a source and date for every contact.

**Days 1–3:** confirm house continuity/availability, costs, policies, charter wording and safeguarding; identify the Chairperson and backup; approve the signed letter and content.  
**Days 4–7:** build and test the landing page, consented interest form, presentation request workflow, privacy controls, confirmation emails, tracker and dashboard. Run test submissions and deletion/access checks.  
**Week 2:** create a verified list of 10–15 Greater Accra institutions; personalize letters; contact a small pilot cohort individually; record responses and objections.  
**Weeks 3–4:** deliver requested presentations, respond to applicant enquiries, review the funnel weekly, and adjust copy based on supervisor feedback. Expand only when staff can respond safely and consistently.  
**Ongoing:** continue permission-based follow-up, review occupancy and costs, and use aggregate location requests to evaluate future demand. Do not open a new gender/location house until funding, sustainable demand, staffing/governance, housing, charter and legal requirements are confirmed.

## 11. Weekly dashboard

Report counts and conversion rates, not personal stories or identifiable health details:

- Institutions verified, approached, responded, opted out, and agreed to a presentation.
- Presentations requested, confirmed, delivered, and cancelled.
- Applicant enquiries received; applicant-consented referrals; acknowledgement time; enquiries awaiting applicant response.
- Preliminary conversations, house interviews, offers, declines, deferrals, withdrawals and move-ins.
- Current house occupancy / verified capacity; expected vacancies; rent/dues status at an aggregate level; and lease/continuity decision.
- Enquiry source and preferred location, reported only in aggregate when group sizes are large enough to protect privacy.
- Complaints, safeguarding or privacy incidents, and resolution status, reported through a restricted governance channel.

Do not treat form submissions as the principal success metric. The meaningful measures are informed enquiries, completed interviews, safe and voluntary admissions, sustained occupancy, resident wellbeing and compliance with the house model.
